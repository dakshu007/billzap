A late GST return costs you in two ways. The **late fee** is charged for every day the return is late, even if you owe no tax. **Interest** is charged on tax paid after the due date. Many pages still quote the per-day fee written into the law (₹200 a day) or the old 24% interest on wrong credit, and neither is what applies today.

This guide gives the figures as they apply in November 2026: the caps for each return, how the fee and interest are worked out and paid, five worked examples, the waivers that have come and gone, and the three-year bar after which a return cannot be filed at all.

## Quick answer

- **GSTR-3B and GSTR-1:** **₹50 a day** (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST), capped per return by last year's aggregate turnover: **₹2,000** up to ₹1.5 crore, **₹5,000** from ₹1.5 crore to ₹5 crore, **₹10,000** above ₹5 crore.
- **Nil return:** **₹20 a day** (₹10 + ₹10), capped at **₹500** per return.
- **GSTR-4:** ₹50 a day, capped at **₹2,000**, or **₹500** if no tax is payable. **CMP-08** has no late fee.
- **GSTR-9:** **₹50 a day** up to ₹5 crore turnover and **₹100 a day** from ₹5 crore to ₹20 crore, capped at **0.04%** of turnover in the state; **₹200 a day** above ₹20 crore, capped at **0.5%**. Where GSTR-9C applies, the fee runs until **both** are filed.
- **Interest:** **18% a year** on tax paid late, on the **cash** part only. On input tax credit **wrongly availed and used**, also **18%**; the old 24% was replaced retrospectively in 2022.
- **Payment:** late fee and interest come **only from the electronic cash ledger**, never from input tax credit.
- **Three-year bar:** a return **cannot be filed** three years after its due date, and the portal now enforces it. The GSTR-9 for FY 2022-23 cannot be filed after **31 December 2026**.

## Late fee for each return in 2026

Section 47 sets the late fee at **₹100 a day under each Act** (₹200 a day for CGST and SGST together), up to ₹5,000 per Act per return, and the same daily rate for the annual return up to 0.25% of turnover in the state per Act. The government has reduced these by notifications under Section 128. The reduced figures below are what the portal charges.

### GSTR-3B and GSTR-1

From Notifications **19/2021** (GSTR-3B) and **20/2021** (GSTR-1), in force since June 2021. "Turnover" is your **aggregate turnover in the previous financial year**.

| Your situation | Late fee per day (CGST + SGST) | Maximum per return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nil return (no tax payable in 3B; no sales in GSTR-1) | ₹20 (₹10 + ₹10) | ₹500 (₹250 + ₹250) |
| Turnover up to ₹1.5 crore | ₹50 (₹25 + ₹25) | ₹2,000 (₹1,000 + ₹1,000) |
| Turnover above ₹1.5 crore up to ₹5 crore | ₹50 (₹25 + ₹25) | ₹5,000 (₹2,500 + ₹2,500) |
| Turnover above ₹5 crore | ₹50 (₹25 + ₹25) | ₹10,000 (₹5,000 + ₹5,000) |

The same caps apply to quarterly returns under QRMP. The GSTR-3B late fee is worked out by the portal and paid when you file that return. The late fee for a late GSTR-1 is added to your **next GSTR-3B**, so keep cash ready for it. Filing steps are in our guides to [GSTR-3B](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-3b) and [how to file GSTR-1](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-file-gstr-1).

### Composition dealers: GSTR-4 and CMP-08

| Return | Late fee per day | Maximum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GSTR-4 (annual, due 30 April) with tax payable | ₹50 (₹25 + ₹25) | ₹2,000 |
| GSTR-4 with no tax payable | ₹20 (₹10 + ₹10) | ₹500 |
| CMP-08 (quarterly) | No late fee | No late fee |

The GSTR-4 caps are from Notification **21/2021**, for FY 2021-22 onwards. The GST portal's FAQ states that CMP-08 has no late fee, but interest applies to tax paid late through it. More in our [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide.

### GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C

For **FY 2022-23 onwards**, Notification **07/2023** reduced the annual return late fee:

| Aggregate turnover | Late fee per day (CGST + SGST) | Maximum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to ₹5 crore | ₹50 (₹25 + ₹25) | 0.04% of turnover in the state |
| Above ₹5 crore up to ₹20 crore | ₹100 (₹50 + ₹50) | 0.04% of turnover in the state |
| Above ₹20 crore | ₹200 (₹100 + ₹100) | 0.5% of turnover in the state |

**GSTR-9C** has no separate fee. CBIC Circular **246/03/2025-GST** clarifies that where 9C is required (above ₹5 crore), the annual return is complete only when both are filed, so the fee keeps running until 9C is in. Our [GSTR-9](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-9) guide covers who must file.

**Counting days:** the fee runs for each day after the due date up to and including the day you file. Once it reaches the cap it stops growing; interest on unpaid tax does not.

## Interest under Section 50

### 18% on tax paid late, on the cash part

Under **Section 50(1)**, tax paid after the due date carries interest at **18% a year**, from the day after the due date to the day of payment. Two rules cut what this costs:

- **Net cash liability only.** A proviso to Section 50(1), effective from 1 July 2017, charges interest only on the tax paid in **cash** when a return is filed late, not on the part set off from credit. It does not help where the return is filed after proceedings under Section 73 or 74 have started.
- **Cash already in your ledger.** Since **10 July 2024**, a proviso to **Rule 88B(1)** (Notification 12/2024-Central Tax) excludes any amount that was in your **electronic cash ledger on the due date** and is debited when you file. Deposit the tax on time and, even if the return is late, you pay the late fee but no interest on that deposit.

### 18% on credit wrongly availed and used, not 24%

**Section 50(3)** covers input tax credit you were not entitled to. The Finance Act, 2022 changed it with effect from **1 July 2017**:

- Interest applies only to credit **wrongly availed and utilised**. Wrong credit that sits unused and is reversed attracts none. Under **Rule 88B(3)**, credit counts as utilised when your credit ledger balance falls below the wrongly availed amount.
- The rate is **18%**. The same Act amended Notification 13/2017-Central Tax retrospectively to replace 24% with 18% for Section 50(3). Many pages still quote 24%.

The portal shows a system-computed interest figure in GSTR-3B, which you can check and correct. Tax found later, such as an unreported sale, is paid through Form DRC-03 with interest.

## Worked examples

### 1. Late GSTR-3B with tax to pay

A shop with ₹80 lakh turnover files its **September 2026 GSTR-3B**, due 20 October 2026, on **4 November 2026**, paying **₹18,000** in cash.

| Item | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days late | 21–31 Oct (11) + 1–4 Nov (4) | 15 |
| Late fee | 15 × ₹50 | **₹750** (₹375 CGST + ₹375 SGST) |
| Interest | ₹18,000 × 18% × 15 ÷ 365 | **₹133.15** |
| **Cost of filing late** | | **₹883.15** |

If ₹10,000 had been deposited in the cash ledger by 20 October, interest would run only on the other ₹8,000: ₹8,000 × 18% × 15 ÷ 365 = **₹59.18**. The late fee stays ₹750.

### 2. Late GSTR-1 that hits the cap

A shop with ₹1.2 crore turnover files its **October 2026 GSTR-1**, due 11 November 2026, on **31 December 2026**: 19 days in November + 31 in December = **50 days**. 50 × ₹50 = ₹2,500, capped at **₹2,000**, which is added to the next GSTR-3B.

### 3. Nil GSTR-3B filed two months late

60 days × ₹20 = ₹1,200, capped at **₹500**; the cap is reached on day 25. A late nil return usually cannot be filed by SMS, because the fee is a pending liability, so file online and pay it in cash. Our [nil GST return](https://billzap.app/blog/nil-gst-return) guide has the steps.

### 4. GSTR-9 for a ₹6 crore business

The **FY 2025-26 GSTR-9 and 9C**, due 31 December 2026, are filed on **31 March 2027**: 31 + 28 + 31 = **90 days**. At ₹100 a day, the fee is **₹9,000**, well under the cap of 0.04% of ₹6 crore = ₹24,000. Filing GSTR-9 on time but 9C on 31 March would cost the same ₹9,000.

### 5. Interest on credit used wrongly

A shop claimed **₹40,000** of credit it was not entitled to, and its credit ledger fell below that amount on **20 August 2026**. It pays the amount back through DRC-03 on **18 November 2026**.

- Days: 21–31 August (11) + September (30) + October (31) + 1–18 November (18) = **90 days**.
- Interest: ₹40,000 × 18% × 90 ÷ 365 = **₹1,775.34**. At the old 24% it would have been ₹2,367.12.
- Had the wrong credit never been used, reversing it would cost no interest.

## How to pay the late fee

- **Cash ledger only.** Add money through a PMT-06 challan by UPI, net banking, card, NEFT/RTGS or over the counter; our [GST payment online](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-payment-online) guide shows the steps.
- **Right head.** The fee is split between CGST and SGST, each paid from its own head. Money under the wrong head has to be moved with Form PMT-09 first.
- **No credit back.** A late fee is not tax and never becomes input tax credit.
- **The portal will not file** until the cash ledger covers the tax, interest and late fee shown.

## The three-year bar: when late becomes never

The Finance Act, 2023 added a time limit: a return **cannot be filed after three years from its due date**. It covers GSTR-1 (Section 37(5)), GSTR-3B and GSTR-4 (Section 39(11)), GSTR-9 and 9C (Section 44(2)) and GSTR-8 (Section 52(14)), in force since **1 October 2023**.

GSTN announced in 2025 that the portal would block such returns, and after several advisories and revised dates the block took effect in late 2025. It now rolls forward every month. Dates coming up:

| Return | Due date | Cannot be filed after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GSTR-3B for November 2023 (monthly filer) | 20 December 2023 | 20 December 2026 |
| GSTR-9 for FY 2022-23 | 31 December 2023 | 31 December 2026 |
| GSTR-3B for December 2023 (monthly filer) | 20 January 2024 | 20 January 2027 |

A barred return stays unfiled for good, but the tax for that period can still be assessed with interest and penalty, and continued non-filing puts the registration at risk of cancellation. If you have a return pending for more than two and a half years, file it this month.

## Waivers: what was offered, and what is open

Late fees can only be waived by a notification under Section 128, usually after a GST Council recommendation; an officer cannot waive one on request.

| Waiver | What it covered | Window |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2021 amnesty (Notification 19/2021) | GSTR-3B for July 2017 to April 2021: fee capped at ₹500 per return with no tax, ₹1,000 otherwise | Filed 1 June to 30 November 2021 (after extension) |
| 2023 GSTR-9 amnesty (Notification 07/2023) | Pending GSTR-9 for FY 2017-18 to 2021-22: fee capped at ₹10,000 per Act (₹20,000 in all) | 1 April to 31 August 2023 (after extension) |
| GSTR-9C waiver (Notification 08/2025) | Late fee for pending 9C for FY 2017-18 to 2022-23; no refund of fee already paid | 9C filed by 31 March 2025 |

All of these windows have closed; the permanent June 2021 caps continue. A separate scheme under **Section 128A** waived interest and penalty on certain tax demands for FY 2017-18 to 2019-20 where the tax was paid by 31 March 2025, but not late fees. Do not hold back a filing in the hope of another amnesty: interest keeps running, and the three-year bar does not wait.

## FAQ

### What is the late fee for GSTR-3B in 2026?

₹50 a day (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST), capped at ₹2,000 per return if last year's turnover was up to ₹1.5 crore, ₹5,000 up to ₹5 crore and ₹10,000 above that. For a nil return it is ₹20 a day, capped at ₹500.

### Is there a late fee on GSTR-1?

Yes, the same as GSTR-3B: ₹50 a day with the same caps, or ₹20 a day up to ₹500 for a nil GSTR-1. The portal adds it to your next GSTR-3B.

### Can the GST late fee be paid from input tax credit?

No. Late fee and interest can only be paid from the electronic cash ledger.

### What is the interest rate on late GST payment?

18% a year under Section 50(1), on the tax paid in cash after the due date. On input tax credit wrongly availed and used, the rate is also 18%; the earlier 24% was replaced retrospectively from 1 July 2017.

### Can I file a GST return after three years?

No. The law bars filing a return more than three years after its due date, and the GST portal now blocks such returns. The GSTR-9 for FY 2022-23 cannot be filed after 31 December 2026.

### Is there a GST late fee waiver in 2026?

The one-time waivers of 2021, 2023 and 2025 have closed; the reduced caps from June 2021 continue. Any new waiver would come through a GST Council recommendation and a CBIC notification.

## The bottom line

For most small businesses, the GST late fee is **₹50 a day, up to ₹2,000 a return** (₹20 a day, up to ₹500, for a nil return), and **interest is 18%** on the cash part of tax paid late. Both are paid in cash. The fee is capped; interest and the three-year bar are not. If anything is pending, deposit the tax by the due date even if the return will be late, file the oldest return first, and clear anything near three years old before the portal closes it for good.
