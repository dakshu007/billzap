Most shop owners find out about GST on rent the hard way: their CA asks why the rent paid to the building owner has no reverse charge entry in GSTR-3B. Since **10 October 2024**, a GST-registered business that rents a shop or godown from an owner who is not registered has to pay **18% GST on that rent itself**, under reverse charge. The landlord does nothing different. The tenant pays.

That one rule sits beside several others: a different rule if the landlord is registered, an exemption for composition dealers, separate rules for flats and for PGs, and a ₹20 lakh limit that decides when the landlord must register. This guide sorts them by situation, with a table you can check your own case against and worked examples with real numbers.

## Quick answer

- **Rate:** GST on renting commercial property is **18%** (9% CGST + 9% SGST, or 18% IGST).
- **Registered landlord:** the landlord adds 18% to the rent bill (forward charge). You pay it to the landlord and claim it as input tax credit.
- **Unregistered landlord, registered tenant:** the **tenant pays 18% under reverse charge (RCM)**, in cash, through GSTR-3B. In force since **10 October 2024** (Notification 09/2024-Central Tax (Rate)).
- **Composition dealer as tenant:** **no RCM** on commercial rent from an unregistered landlord since **16 January 2025** (Notification 07/2025-Central Tax (Rate)).
- **Residential flat for living:** exempt. But if a **registered person** rents a flat for the business, the tenant pays **18% RCM** (since 18 July 2022), unless a proprietor rents it in his personal capacity as his own home.
- **Unregistered tenant:** pays GST only if the landlord is registered and charges it.

## Commercial rent: shop, godown, office

Renting out property is a supply of service under GST. Commercial property (anything other than a home: a shop, office, godown, factory shed, a plot used for business) is taxed at **18%**. What changed in October 2024 is **who** pays it.

### When your landlord is registered

The landlord issues a tax invoice with 18% GST and pays it to the government. You pay the rent plus GST and claim the GST back as input tax credit, as long as the invoice shows up in your GSTR-2B.

**Example:** shop rent ₹40,000 a month from a registered landlord.

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Rent | ₹40,000 |
| CGST 9% | ₹3,600 |
| SGST 9% | ₹3,600 |
| **Paid to landlord** | **₹47,200** |
| Credit you claim | ₹7,200 |

Your real cost stays ₹40,000, because the ₹7,200 comes back as credit against the GST on your sales.

### When your landlord is not registered: RCM since 10 October 2024

Most small landlords are individuals who are not registered, and before October 2024 their rent carried no GST. The 54th GST Council meeting recommended putting this rent under reverse charge, and **Notification 09/2024-Central Tax (Rate), dated 8 October 2024**, did so with effect from **10 October 2024**. The entry covers renting of any property other than a residential dwelling, by an unregistered person, to a registered person.

So you pay the landlord the agreed rent, without GST, and pay 18% of it to the government yourself. There is no minimum: a ₹6,000 shop rent attracts RCM just as a ₹60,000 one does. The full mechanics of reverse charge are in our guide to the [reverse charge mechanism](https://billzap.app/blog/reverse-charge-mechanism).

**Check your landlord's GSTIN before assuming.** If your landlord runs a business of his own and holds a GSTIN in your state, he is a registered person and should charge 18% on his rent bill instead. You can check any GSTIN with our [GST number search](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-number-search) guide.

### What you have to do each month under RCM

1. **Pay the rent** to the landlord as agreed, without GST.
2. **Make a self-invoice** for the rent, since the landlord cannot issue a tax invoice. Under Rule 47A, in force since 1 November 2024, it must be issued within 30 days of receiving the service. You also make a payment voucher when you pay.
3. **Report the tax** in Table 3.1(d) of [GSTR-3B](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-3b) and **pay it in cash**. Tax under reverse charge cannot be paid from your input tax credit balance.
4. **Claim it back** as credit in Table 4(A)(3) of the same return, if you use the shop for your taxable business.

GST becomes due when you pay the rent, or 60 days after the date of the landlord's bill if you pay later than that. Pay it late and interest at 18% a year applies.

## Worked example: a kirana shop paying RCM on rent

Meena runs a regular GST-registered kirana store in Pune. She rents the shop from its owner, a retired teacher who is not registered, for **₹25,000 a month**.

| Step | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rent paid to owner | | ₹25,000 |
| RCM liability | ₹25,000 × 18% | ₹4,500 |
| CGST / SGST | ₹4,500 ÷ 2 | ₹2,250 each |
| Paid in cash via GSTR-3B | | ₹4,500 |
| Credit claimed in the same return | | ₹4,500 |

Over a year, that is ₹3,00,000 of rent and ₹54,000 of RCM.

**Does it cost her anything?** Look at a month in which her sales carry ₹12,000 of output GST and her purchase bills give ₹7,000 of credit.

| | Without RCM | With RCM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output GST on sales | ₹12,000 | ₹12,000 |
| Credit from purchases | ₹7,000 | ₹7,000 |
| Credit from rent RCM | | ₹4,500 |
| GST on sales paid in cash | ₹12,000 − ₹7,000 = ₹5,000 | ₹12,000 − ₹11,500 = ₹500 |
| RCM paid in cash | | ₹4,500 |
| **Total cash to government** | **₹5,000** | **₹5,000** |

The cash outgo is the same. RCM is cost-neutral for Meena because her output tax is large enough to use the credit in the same month.

It is **not** neutral in two cases. If your purchase credit already covers your output tax (common for shops selling mostly 5% goods bought at 5%), the rent credit piles up unused in your ledger while the ₹4,500 has left your bank. And if your sales are exempt or you are a composition dealer, there is no credit at all. Our guide to [input tax credit](https://billzap.app/blog/input-tax-credit-gst) covers the conditions and the reversal rules for mixed use, such as a building that is partly shop and partly home.

## Composition dealers: no RCM on commercial rent

When the October 2024 rule came in, composition dealers were caught by it, and they could not even claim the credit back. The 55th GST Council meeting (December 2024) recommended taking them out. **Notification 07/2025-Central Tax (Rate), dated 16 January 2025**, excluded composition taxpayers from the commercial rent RCM from that date. CBIC Circular 245/02/2025-GST then regularised the period from 10 October 2024 to 15 January 2025 "on as is where is basis": tax already paid for that period is not refunded, and tax not paid is not demanded.

Two limits on this relief:

- It covers **commercial property only**. A composition dealer who rents a **residential flat** for the business (as a godown or office, say) still pays 18% RCM on it.
- If the landlord is **registered**, he charges 18% on his bill, and a composition dealer pays it as a cost, since composition dealers cannot claim input tax credit. On ₹20,000 rent, that is ₹23,600 a month.

More on how the scheme works in our guide to the [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme).

## Residential rent: flats and houses

**Renting a home for people to live in is exempt.** A family or a salaried tenant pays no GST on house rent, whoever the landlord is.

The exception, since **18 July 2022** (Notification 05/2022-Central Tax (Rate)): when a residential dwelling is rented to a **registered person**, the tenant pays **18% under RCM**, whether the landlord is registered or not. This catches a shop owner who rents a flat as a godown, a firm that rents a flat for staff, and a business run from a rented flat.

A fix followed for sole proprietors. From **1 January 2023** (Notification 15/2022-Central Tax (Rate)), the exemption covers a registered person who is the proprietor of a proprietorship concern and rents a flat **in his personal capacity for use as his own residence**, on his own account and not that of the business. So a shopkeeper with a GSTIN who rents a flat for his family to live in pays no GST, provided the lease is in his own name and the rent is not paid or booked by the business.

**Example:** a registered electrical shop rents a flat for ₹15,000 a month to store stock. RCM is ₹15,000 × 18% = **₹2,700** a month (₹1,350 CGST + ₹1,350 SGST), paid in cash and claimed back as credit.

## PG and hostel rent

PGs and hostels are not treated as a residential dwelling; they are accommodation services. Two rules apply:

- **Exempt** if the charge is **up to ₹20,000 per person per month** and the stay is **at least 90 continuous days**. This exemption applies from 15 July 2024, and Circular 228/22/2024-GST extended the same treatment to the earlier period back to 1 July 2017.
- **Taxable** otherwise. Since **22 September 2025**, accommodation valued at up to **₹7,500 per unit per day** is taxed at **5% without input tax credit** (it was 12% before; many pages still quote 12%). The PG owner charges it only if registered.

A PG owner whose total turnover stays under the registration limit does not register and charges no GST at all. If you run a PG with meals, short stays or rents above ₹20,000 a head, confirm the treatment with your CA.

## The landlord's side: when must a landlord register?

A landlord has to register under GST when aggregate turnover crosses **₹20 lakh** in a financial year (**₹10 lakh** for services in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura). Our guide to the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) has the full table.

| Landlord | Annual rent | Registration? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owns 2 shops at ₹30,000 a month each | ₹7,20,000 | Not required |
| Owns 6 shops at ₹30,000 a month each | ₹21,60,000 | Required: crosses ₹20 lakh |

A landlord who only rents homes to families makes only exempt supplies and does not need to register at all.

Some landlords argue that since their tenants pay under RCM, they never need to register (citing Notification 5/2017-Central Tax). Opinion is divided; the safer reading is that once commercial rent crosses ₹20 lakh, the landlord registers and charges 18% himself.

## Common mistakes with GST on rent

- **Missing RCM entirely.** Rent never passes through your billing, so it is easy to forget in GSTR-3B.
- **Paying RCM from credit.** It must be paid in cash.
- **Paying RCM and also accepting GST from a registered landlord.** If the landlord charges 18% on his bill, you do not pay RCM again.
- **Composition dealers still paying RCM** on commercial rent after 16 January 2025, or forgetting that the relief does not cover a rented flat.
- **Booking the proprietor's home rent through the business.** That brings it back under RCM.
- **Quoting 12% for PGs.** That rate ended on 22 September 2025.

## FAQ

### What is the GST rate on rent?

18% on commercial property such as shops, offices and godowns, and 18% under RCM where a registered person rents a residential flat for business. Homes rented for living are exempt. PGs and hostels outside the ₹20,000 exemption are taxed at 5% without credit, for room values up to ₹7,500 a day.

### Is GST payable on shop rent if the landlord is not registered?

Yes, if you as the tenant are registered under GST as a regular taxpayer. You pay 18% under reverse charge, in cash, through GSTR-3B, and can usually claim it back as input tax credit. If you are not registered, or you are a composition dealer, no GST applies.

### Is there a minimum rent below which RCM does not apply?

No. RCM applies to any amount of rent. The ₹20 lakh limit is for the landlord's registration, not for the tenant's reverse charge.

### Do composition dealers pay GST on rent?

Not on commercial rent from an unregistered landlord, since 16 January 2025. They still pay 18% charged by a registered landlord, as a cost, and RCM on a residential flat rented for business.

### Is GST payable on house rent?

No, when the house is rented for living, whoever the tenant is, with one exception: a registered person renting it for the business pays 18% under RCM. A registered proprietor renting a flat in his own name as his own home is exempt.

### Can I claim input tax credit on rent?

Yes, if you are a regular taxpayer and use the property for your taxable business. Credit is available both on GST charged by a registered landlord and on RCM you pay yourself. Composition dealers cannot claim it.

## The bottom line

For most shop owners, the rule fits in one line: **if you are registered and your landlord is not, you pay 18% on the rent yourself, in cash, and take it back as credit.** If your landlord is registered, he charges it instead. Composition dealers are out of the commercial rent RCM since January 2025. Homes for living are exempt, and long-stay PGs up to ₹20,000 a head are exempt too. Add the rent entry to your GSTR-3B checklist this month, so a routine expense does not turn into interest later.
