The customer scans your QR, enters the PIN, and the screen says **"Payment pending"**. Their bank SMS says ₹1,850 debited. Your soundbox says nothing, and the queue is getting longer.

It happens to every shop and every customer sooner or later. The money is almost never lost: RBI rules set a deadline for the refund and a penalty the bank pays if it is late. This guide explains what is going on, how long each case can take, how to complain, and what a shop should do at the counter.

## Quick answer

- **"Pending" means the final result has not reached your app yet.** Most pending payments settle as success or failure within minutes; some take a few hours. **Do not pay again** until the first one is settled, or you may be charged twice.
- **Failed but debited:** the money comes back automatically. Under RBI's turnaround-time circular of **20 September 2019**, a failed UPI transfer where the receiver's account was not credited must be reversed by **T+1** (the day after the transaction). A failed payment **to a shop** must be reversed by **T+5**.
- **Late refund = compensation.** If the bank misses the deadline, it owes you **₹100 for each day of delay**, credited without you having to ask.
- **Complain in this order:** your UPI app (Raise dispute / UPI Help), then your bank, then NPCI's UPI complaint form, then the **RBI Ombudsman** at cms.rbi.org.in if the bank has not resolved it in 30 days. Keep the **12-digit UTR** number.
- **Shops:** never hand over goods on a "pending" screen or a screenshot. Wait for your soundbox, merchant app or bank credit. If it does not arrive, take payment another way and tell the customer the debited amount will come back to them automatically.

## What "pending", "failed" and "success" actually mean

| What the payer sees | What has happened | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Success**, receiver confirms credit | Done | Nothing |
| **Success**, receiver says nothing arrived | Usually a delayed credit or settlement on the receiver's side | Receiver checks bank statement; if absent after a day, payer raises a dispute with the UTR |
| **Pending** | Your bank has debited, but the final answer from the receiver's bank or the network has not come back | Wait; do not repay. Check status in the app after some minutes |
| **Failed**, money not debited | Nothing moved | Pay again if you want |
| **Failed**, money debited | Debit happened, credit did not | Refund comes automatically by T+1 (transfer) or T+5 (shop payment); complain if it does not |

## Why UPI payments get stuck

A UPI payment passes through your UPI app, your bank, NPCI's switch and the receiver's bank in seconds. It goes pending when one of them does not answer in time:

- **The receiver's bank is slow or down**, so your bank has debited but has no confirmation of credit.
- **Your bank's server** times out after debiting.
- **Your phone loses network** after the PIN, so the app never gets the result even though the payment went through.
- **Peak hours**, such as festival evenings and salary days.

NPCI tightened the rules in 2025: banks and apps must respond faster, and apps may check a payment's status only a limited number of times, at set intervals. Most pending payments now resolve within minutes. When a bank's answer never comes, the transaction is reconciled later and ends up either credited to the receiver or reversed to the payer.

## What to do if your payment is pending or failed (customer)

1. **Do not pay again straight away.** Check the status in your UPI app after 5 to 10 minutes. If it turns to success, you are done; if it turns to failed and you were debited, the refund clock is running.
2. **Note the details.** Open the transaction in the app and note the **UTR** (a 12-digit reference number), amount, date, time and the receiver's UPI ID. Take a screenshot.
3. **Check with the receiver.** At a shop, ask the shopkeeper to check the soundbox, merchant app or bank SMS. Sometimes the money has arrived and only your app has not updated.
4. **Wait for the deadline.** For a failed transfer to a person, the reversal is due by the next day. For a failed payment to a shop or merchant, it is due within five days.
5. **If the deadline passes, raise a complaint** (next sections). The bank also owes you ₹100 a day for the delay.

## The RBI deadlines and the ₹100-a-day compensation

RBI's circular **"Harmonisation of Turn Around Time (TAT) and customer compensation for failed transactions using authorised Payment Systems"** (DPSS.CO.PD No.629/02.01.014/2019-20, dated **20 September 2019**, in force from 15 October 2019) sets the outer limits. For UPI:

| Situation | Deadline | Compensation if late |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your account debited, the receiver's account **not credited** (transfer of funds) | Auto-reversal by the receiver's bank by **T+1** | **₹100 per day** of delay beyond T+1 |
| Your account debited, but the **shop or merchant did not get confirmation** (payment to merchant) | Auto-reversal within **T+5** | **₹100 per day** of delay beyond T+5 |

**T** is the day of the transaction. The circular says compensation must be credited to the customer's account **on the bank's own initiative, without waiting for a complaint or claim**. It also says these timelines are the **outer limit**; banks are expected to resolve failures faster.

**Worked example.** Priya pays ₹1,850 at a medical store on **3 November** (T). Her account is debited, the shop gets nothing, and the app shows failed. The deadline for a merchant payment is T+5, which is **8 November**. The refund reaches her on **11 November**, three days late. Her bank owes her 3 × ₹100 = **₹300** in compensation on top of the ₹1,850. If it is not credited, she can cite the circular in her complaint.

**What this does not cover:** a payment that succeeded but went to the wrong person (you typed the wrong UPI ID or number), or a payment you were tricked into making. Those are not "failed" transactions, so there is no automatic reversal. Report a mistaken transfer to your bank at once; report fraud on the cyber crime helpline **1930** or cybercrime.gov.in as well.

## How to raise a complaint, step by step

Go up one step at a time, and keep every complaint number.

| Step | Where | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. UPI app | Transaction history, open the payment, tap **Raise dispute**, **Help** or **UPI Help** | As soon as the deadline passes, or straight away for a pending payment older than a day |
| 2. Your bank | Customer care, net banking grievance section, or the branch. Quote the UTR | If the app does not resolve it in a few days |
| 3. NPCI | npci.org.in, under the UPI complaint / Get in touch section. You get a complaint reference number | If the app and bank do not resolve it |
| 4. RBI Ombudsman | **cms.rbi.org.in**, or call **14448** | If the bank has not replied within **30 days**, or rejected your complaint |

**About UPI Help:** NPCI's "UPI Help" (launched on BHIM in 2021) lets you check a pending payment's status and raise a complaint from the transaction screen. Labels differ by app: look for "Raise dispute", "Report a problem" or "UPI Help".

**About the Ombudsman:** RBI's **Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026** applies to complaints filed from **1 July 2026**. You must first complain to your bank, with proof. If the bank does not reply within 30 days, or you are not satisfied with its reply, you can file on the CMS portal. Under the 2026 scheme the window is **90 days** from the bank's last reply or the end of the reply period, whichever is later. Many pages still say one year; that was the 2021 scheme. Filing is free and you do not need a lawyer.

**In any complaint, give** the UTR, amount, date and time, the receiver's UPI ID, what the app showed, and what you want: "refund of ₹1,850 plus ₹100 a day compensation under RBI circular dated 20 September 2019."

## For shop owners: what to do at the counter

The rule is simple: **you have been paid when the money reaches you, not when the customer's screen says so.**

| Customer's screen | Your soundbox, merchant app or bank | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Success | Credit received for the right amount | Hand over the goods |
| Success or pending | **Nothing received** | Wait 2-3 minutes; refresh your merchant app |
| Pending, still nothing after a few minutes | Nothing | Ask for cash or another payment; explain the pending amount will be refunded to them automatically |
| Failed, money debited | Nothing | Same as above; give them the RBI deadline (5 days for a shop payment) |
| Any screen | Credit received **after** you took a second payment | Refund the extra on the spot or the same day, to the same UPI ID |

**At a busy counter:**

- **Do not accept a screenshot or the customer's phone screen** as proof. It is the most common way shops are cheated; see our guide to [getting a UPI QR code for your shop](https://billzap.app/blog/upi-qr-code-for-shop) for the five checks that stop fake payments.
- **A soundbox is the fastest check**: it only speaks when the money has reached your merchant account. Settlement to your bank account may still follow later on the provider's cycle, so check your merchant app for "received" rather than waiting for the bank SMS on every sale.
- **For regular customers you trust**, you can let them take the goods and record the bill as unpaid until the money lands. Write it down at once, with the UTR, so it does not turn into forgotten udhaar. Our guide to [udhaar management](https://billzap.app/blog/udhaar-management) has a simple system.
- **For a stranger and a big amount**, wait or take another mode.
- **Large amounts that fail repeatedly** may be hitting the customer's bank limit, not a technical fault. Our guide to the [UPI transaction limit](https://billzap.app/blog/upi-transaction-limit) explains the per-day and per-payment caps.

### When a pending payment turns up later

Sometimes the customer pays cash after a pending UPI, and two hours later the UPI payment succeeds. You now have the money twice.

1. **Check your merchant app or statement** for the credit with that UTR.
2. **Refund it** to the same UPI ID, or with your merchant app's refund option, with a note such as "refund of double payment, bill 412".
3. **Tell the customer** on WhatsApp, with the refund's reference number.
4. **Record it in your [cash book](https://billzap.app/blog/cash-book-format)** so the day's totals match.

If you do not, the customer may raise a chargeback and your provider may hold the amount from your settlement. Refunding first keeps the customer.

### Refund scams to watch for

A stranger says he "paid you by mistake" and asks for the money back, often with a screenshot. **Check your own bank statement first.** If nothing came, there is nothing to refund. If it did, refund only to the UPI ID it came from, never to a different number. And never enter your UPI PIN to "receive" money: a PIN is only needed to send.

## Refunds for returns and exchanges

A refund for returned goods is a new payment from you to the customer, not a reversal. Send it by UPI to the same ID the customer paid from, or in cash, and note it against the original bill. For a GST-registered shop, a return after the bill has gone into your GST return is usually handled with a [credit note](https://billzap.app/blog/credit-note-in-gst); ask your CA. And never approve a payment request on your own UPI app in the belief that it is a refund to you: approving a request with your PIN sends money out.

## FAQ

### How long does a pending UPI payment take?

Most settle within minutes; some take a few hours. If it ends as failed and you were debited, the reversal is due by the next day (T+1) for a transfer to a person, and within five days (T+5) for a payment to a shop or merchant.

### UPI payment failed but money debited: will I get it back?

Yes. The money is reversed automatically, by T+1 for transfers and T+5 for merchant payments, under RBI's 2019 turnaround-time circular. If it is late, your bank owes you ₹100 for each day of delay, without you having to claim it.

### Should I pay again if the payment is pending?

Not until the first payment is settled. If you pay again and the first one succeeds later, you will have paid twice and will need the receiver to refund you. At a shop, pay by cash or another mode if you cannot wait, and let the pending amount reverse.

### Where do I complain about a failed UPI transaction?

Start in your UPI app (Raise dispute or UPI Help on the transaction), then your bank, then NPCI's UPI complaint form at npci.org.in. If the bank has not resolved it within 30 days, complain to the RBI Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in or call 14448.

### What is the time limit to complain to the RBI Ombudsman?

Under the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026, which applies to complaints filed from 1 July 2026, you must file within 90 days of the bank's last reply or of the end of the 30-day reply period, whichever is later. You must complain to the bank first.

### The customer's screen says success but I have not received the money. What should I do?

Do not hand over the goods yet. Wait a few minutes and check your merchant app. If nothing arrives, ask for another mode of payment; the customer's bank will reverse the debit if the payment really failed. A screen on the customer's phone is not proof of payment.

## The bottom line

A pending or failed UPI payment is almost always a delay, not a loss. Customers should wait, avoid paying twice, keep the UTR and know the deadlines: T+1 for a transfer, T+5 for a shop payment, and ₹100 a day if the bank is late. Shops should count money as received only when their own soundbox, merchant app or bank says so, keep unpaid bills on record until the credit lands, and refund double payments the same day.
