## What a good GST billing app has to do

For a shop in India, the bar is:

1. **A tax invoice that holds up.** Your GSTIN and the buyer's, HSN or SAC codes on every line, the rate per item, and the tax shown separately as CGST and SGST or as IGST. Get it wrong and the bill gets reissued.
2. **The right tax split, decided for you.** Same state as the place of supply means CGST plus SGST; a different state means IGST. A good app reads it from the place of supply and shows you which one it picked before you save.
3. **Speed at the counter.** A saved catalogue, saved customers, and a bill in well under a minute.
4. **A way to get paid on the spot.** A UPI QR printed on the bill, so the customer pays before they leave, and a PDF you can send on WhatsApp in two taps.
5. **Something your accountant can use.** A month-by-month GST summary laid out for GSTR-1, a profit and loss statement, and exports as PDF or CSV.
6. **Honest pricing and honest offline behaviour.** Know what stops working when the free trial ends, and what stops working when the signal does.

## Free versus paid: what the money actually buys

Most apps here have a free tier and then ask ₹3,500 to ₹4,500 a year. Be precise about what that buys, because for many shops the answer is "nothing I use".

**Multi-device sync and team access.** This is the single biggest reason to pay. If two people bill from two phones, or the owner wants to see the day's sales from home, you need a synced, account-based app such as Vyapar, myBillBook or Swipe. A single counter with a single phone does not.

**E-way bills and e-invoicing.** Both are compliance features with thresholds. E-invoicing (an IRN from the government portal) is mandatory only for businesses whose aggregate turnover crossed ₹5 crore in any year since 2017-18. An e-way bill is needed when you move goods worth more than ₹50,000. A neighbourhood shop that sells over the counter usually needs neither. A distributor needs both, and should pay for an app that does them well (GimBooks, myBillBook Pro Max, Vyapar or TallyPrime).

**Removing the app's branding.** Vyapar's free invoices carry "Vyapar" until you pay. A fair trade for a free product, but know it is there.

**Accounting depth.** Ledgers, vouchers, bank reconciliation and Tally export matter when your CA works from them. If your CA just wants a GSTR-1 summary and a profit and loss statement every month, a billing app's export is enough.

What the money does not buy is a better tax invoice. A correct GST invoice is table stakes, and it is available free.

## GST details that decide whether a bill holds up

Whichever app you choose, check these four things before you trust it with a real bill:

**Place of supply.** The app should decide CGST plus SGST against IGST from the place of supply, not from where the customer's phone number is registered, and it should show its choice. On a ₹3,740 sale at 18%, that is CGST ₹336.60 plus SGST ₹336.60 inside your state, or IGST ₹673.20 across a state line.

**HSN digits.** Since April 2021, businesses with turnover up to ₹5 crore must show at least a 4-digit HSN code on B2B invoices, and above ₹5 crore, 6 digits on every invoice. Services use SAC codes, which begin with 99. Save the code once against the item and let the app print it.

**Current rates.** Since 22 September 2025, most goods and services sit at 5% or 18%, with 40% for luxury and sin goods and 0% for exempt essentials. You want to set the rate per item.

**Your exports.** Ask your CA what they want each month and check the app produces it: usually a GSTR-1 summary of taxable value, CGST, SGST and IGST, plus sales and profit reports, as PDF or CSV. ## FAQ: GST billing apps

### Which is the best GST billing app for a small shop?

For a single shop billing at a counter, BillZap is the strongest fit: it is free with no paid tier, works fully offline, needs no account, puts a UPI QR on every unpaid bill and exports a GSTR-1 summary. If several people bill from different phones, or you need e-way bills, choose a synced paid app such as Vyapar or myBillBook instead.

### Is there a completely free GST billing app?

Yes. BillZap is free with no paid tier, no trial and no invoice limit, and Zoho Invoice is free for Indian businesses with unlimited invoices. Vyapar, Swipe and Khatabook have free tiers, but they keep features such as multi-device sync, e-way bills or branding removal for paying users.

### Which GST billing app works without internet?

BillZap works fully offline: every bill, customer and report is stored on the phone, and nothing waits to sync. Vyapar is also offline-first, saving your data on the device and syncing it on paid plans once you are back online, and TallyPrime is desktop software that does not need a connection to bill. Cloud apps such as myBillBook, Zoho Invoice, Swipe and GimBooks are built around an internet connection.

### Do I need e-invoicing in my billing app?

Only if your aggregate turnover has crossed ₹5 crore in any financial year since 2017-18; below that, e-invoicing (generating an IRN on the government portal) is not mandatory. Most small shops do not need it. If you do, pick an app that supports it, such as GimBooks, myBillBook Pro Max, Swipe's e-invoicing plan, Vyapar or TallyPrime.

### Can I make a GST invoice on my phone?

Yes. Every app on this list makes GST invoices on a phone except TallyPrime, which is desktop software. A proper GST invoice from a phone carries your GSTIN and the buyer's, HSN codes per line, the rate per item, and CGST and SGST or IGST shown separately. You can share it as a PDF on WhatsApp or print it to a Bluetooth printer.

### What should a GST bill contain?

Under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, a tax invoice shows your name, address and GSTIN, a serial number unique for the financial year, the date, and the buyer's name and address (and GSTIN if registered). It also shows HSN or SAC codes with description, quantity and unit, the taxable value after discount, and the rate and amount of CGST and SGST or IGST. For sales to another state, it adds the place of supply. Our [GST guide](https://billzap.app/#guide) walks through each part.
