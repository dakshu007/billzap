A customer walks in, buys dal, oil and a packet of biscuits, pays in cash and wants "a bill". In most Indian shops, what they get is a **cash memo**: a small slip torn from a printed pad, with the items, the total and the shop's stamp. It is the most common business document in the country, and also one of the most misunderstood, because the word "cash memo" means different things to a kirana owner, an accountant and a GST officer.

This guide gives you a clean cash memo format you can copy, three filled-in samples with every total checked, and the one GST rule that decides whether a cash memo is enough for your shop or whether you need a proper tax invoice.

## Quick answer

A **cash memo** is a bill issued for a sale paid on the spot, in cash or by UPI. It shows the seller, the date, a serial number, the items, quantities, rates and the total, and it doubles as proof that the customer has paid.

- **Shops not registered under GST** can use a simple cash memo for every sale.
- **GST-registered shops** cannot replace a tax invoice with a cash memo. Their "cash memo" must be a **tax invoice** (if they charge GST) or a **bill of supply** (if they don't), simply marked **"Payment: Cash"**.
- **Small-sale relief:** a registered shop need not issue a separate invoice for a sale **under ₹200** to an unregistered customer who does not ask for one, as long as it issues **one consolidated tax invoice at the end of the day** (Rule 46).
- **Must-have fields:** shop name and address, memo number, date, item, quantity, rate, amount, total, and "Paid" or "Received with thanks".
- **Keep copies** for at least **72 months** from the due date of the annual return for that year, if you are GST-registered.

## What is a cash memo?

The cash memo meaning is simple: it is a memorandum of a cash sale. A credit sale gets an invoice and is paid later; a cash sale is paid at the counter, so the bill and the acknowledgement of payment are the same piece of paper.

That is why cash memos are everywhere in retail:

- kirana and general stores billing walk-in customers,
- medical stores, hardware shops and stationery shops,
- sweet shops, bakeries and small restaurants,
- repair shops and tailors collecting payment on delivery.

"Cash" here really means *paid now*. A sale paid by UPI, card or cash at the counter is the same kind of sale, and the same memo works for all three. Just write how it was paid.

## Cash memo vs tax invoice vs bill of supply vs receipt

These four get mixed up constantly. The difference matters, because GST law only recognises some of them.

| Document | Who issues it | What it says | Is GST charged on it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cash memo** | Any shop, usually one not registered under GST | Items sold and paid for on the spot | No |
| **Tax invoice** | GST-registered seller charging GST | Items, HSN, taxable value, CGST/SGST or IGST | Yes |
| **Bill of supply** | GST-registered seller not charging GST (exempt goods, or a composition dealer) | Items and value, no tax shown | No |
| **Receipt** | Anyone receiving money | Money received, against what | Not a bill for goods at all |

A **receipt** is the one people confuse most. It acknowledges money, not a sale: an advance, a part-payment on an udhaar account, rent. Under GST, an advance for a future supply needs a *receipt voucher*, which is a different document again. A cash memo already includes the "paid" acknowledgement, so you don't issue a separate receipt for a counter sale.

## When a cash memo is not enough: the GST rule

Here is the part most template sites skip.

**If your shop is not registered under GST**, a plain cash memo is fine. You charge no GST, you show no GST, and the memo must not say "Tax Invoice" or show a GSTIN. Most small kirana stores are in this position until turnover crosses the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit).

**If your shop is GST-registered**, the law (Section 31 of the CGST Act) requires a **tax invoice** for every taxable sale, with the fields listed in Rule 46: your GSTIN, a consecutive serial number, HSN code, taxable value, the rate and amount of CGST and SGST (or IGST), and so on. Calling it a "cash memo" does not change that. A pad that says "Cash Memo" with only items and a total is **not a valid tax invoice**, even if the shop collected GST inside the price.

So for a registered shop, a correct "cash memo" is simply a tax invoice with **Payment mode: Cash**. The [GST invoice format](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-invoice-format) guide lists every field.

Two exceptions are worth knowing:

1. **Bill of supply instead.** A registered shop selling only exempt goods (loose rice, fresh vegetables, milk) or a [composition dealer](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) issues a **bill of supply** under Rule 49. A composition dealer must also print "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies" on it.
2. **The ₹200 relief.** Under Section 31(3)(b) and the proviso to Rule 46, a registered shop may skip the individual invoice when **all three** are true: the sale is under ₹200, the buyer is not registered, and the buyer doesn't ask for a bill. You then issue **one consolidated tax invoice at the close of each day** covering all such sales. If the customer asks for a bill, you must give one.

## What a cash memo format must contain

Whether it is a non-GST cash memo or a tax invoice paid in cash, these are the fields to print.

**For every cash memo**

1. Shop name, address and phone number.
2. The words "Cash Memo" (or "Bill") at the top.
3. A **serial number** that never repeats, for example `CM-0412`.
4. The **date** of the sale.
5. Customer name, optional for walk-in sales, useful for regulars.
6. A table of **item, quantity, rate and amount**.
7. **Total**, with any discount shown before the total.
8. **Amount in words**, which stops a ₹500 total being "corrected" to ₹1,500.
9. **Payment mode**: cash, UPI or card.
10. "Received with thanks" and the shop's signature or stamp.

**Add these if you are GST-registered**

- Your **GSTIN** and the heading "Tax Invoice" (or "Bill of Supply").
- **HSN code** for each item.
- **Taxable value**, GST rate, and **CGST + SGST** for a sale within your state, or **IGST** for a sale to another state.
- **Place of supply**, and the customer's GSTIN if they are registered.

## Sample 2: Cash memo from a GST-registered shop (done properly)

This is the same kind of counter sale from a registered general store in Karnataka. Because it charges GST, the document is headed **Tax Invoice** and marked paid in cash. ## Sample 3: Medical store cash memo

Pharmacies price at MRP, which already includes GST, so the bill shows the MRP amount and works the tax backwards. They also print the **batch number and expiry date** for every medicine. For prescription-only medicines, they record the patient's and the doctor's names as the drug rules require. Most medicines are at **5% GST** since September 2025, but check the rate for each product's HSN.

**City Care Pharmacy**, MG Road, Pune · GSTIN 27ABCDE1234F1Z9 · **Tax Invoice** No. **CCP-2210** · Date: 25-10-2026 · Patient: R. Kulkarni · Dr. S. Patil · Payment: **UPI**

| # | Item | Batch | Expiry | Qty | MRP (₹) | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paracetamol 650 mg, strip of 15 | PC2611 | 08/2028 | 2 | 31.50 | 63.00 |
| 2 | Cough syrup, 100 ml | CS0925 | 03/2028 | 1 | 126.00 | 126.00 |
| 3 | Vitamin C tablets, strip of 15 | VC1402 | 11/2027 | 2 | 52.50 | 105.00 |
| 4 | ORS sachet | OR7730 | 06/2028 | 6 | 21.00 | 126.00 |
| 5 | Crepe bandage roll | BD0418 | 12/2029 | 1 | 105.00 | 105.00 |
| | **Total (incl. GST)** | | | | | **525.00** |

Tax included at 5%: taxable value ₹500.00, CGST 2.5% ₹12.50, SGST 2.5% ₹12.50. Rupees five hundred twenty-five only.

To work out the tax inside an MRP, divide by 1 plus the rate: ₹525 ÷ 1.05 = ₹500 taxable, so the GST is ₹25.

## How long to keep cash memos

If you are GST-registered, Section 36 of the CGST Act requires you to keep your accounts and records, which includes copies of every invoice and bill, for **72 months (six years) from the due date of the annual return** for that year. For a sale in October 2026, that runs well into 2033. Income tax rules also expect business records to be kept for several years.

Practical habits:

- Keep carbon books in order, by serial number, in a dry box labelled by month.
- **Never tear out or reuse a number.** If a memo is spoiled, write "Cancelled" across it and keep both copies.
- If you bill on a phone, **back up** regularly so a lost phone does not mean lost records.

## Common cash memo mistakes

1. **A registered shop using a "Cash Memo" pad with no GST details.** It is not a tax invoice, however neat it looks.
2. **An unregistered shop printing "Tax Invoice" or adding "GST" to the bill.** You cannot collect GST without a GSTIN.
3. **Gaps or repeats in serial numbers**, usually from two pads used at once.
4. **Discount taken after tax.** On a tax invoice, the discount comes off the taxable value before GST is worked out.
5. **No amount in words**, which makes figures easy to alter.
6. **No payment mode**, so cash and UPI sales can't be matched at the end of the day.
7. **Skipping the end-of-day consolidated invoice** when relying on the ₹200 relief.

## FAQ: Cash memo format

### What is the meaning of a cash memo?

A cash memo is a bill issued when goods or services are paid for on the spot. It lists what was sold, the quantity, rate and total, and it also acts as the customer's proof of payment.

### Is a cash memo valid under GST?

For a shop not registered under GST, yes: a cash memo is a perfectly valid bill. For a GST-registered shop, no: every taxable sale needs a tax invoice with the Rule 46 details, or a bill of supply if no tax is charged. A registered shop's "cash memo" should be a tax invoice marked "Payment: Cash".

### What is the difference between a cash memo and an invoice?

A cash memo is for a sale paid immediately. An invoice can be for a sale paid now or later, and a GST tax invoice must carry GST details such as GSTIN, HSN and the tax split. In everyday use, a cash memo is a simple invoice for a cash sale.

### Is a cash memo the same as a receipt?

Not quite. A receipt only acknowledges money received, for example an advance or a payment against an old credit bill. A cash memo records a sale and acknowledges the payment for it, so a counter sale needs no separate receipt.

### Do I have to give a bill for a sale under ₹200?

If you are GST-registered, you may skip the individual bill when the sale is under ₹200, the buyer is unregistered and doesn't ask for one, but you must issue one consolidated tax invoice for all such sales at the end of the day. If the customer asks for a bill, you must issue it.
