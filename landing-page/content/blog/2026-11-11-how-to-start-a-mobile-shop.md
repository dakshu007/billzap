Every street with a bus stop seems to have a mobile shop, and most of them look the same from outside: phones in a glass counter, covers on the wall, a repair bench at the back. The ones that last are not the ones with the most phones. They are the ones that understood early that phones bring customers in, while accessories and repairs pay the rent.

This guide is for someone planning a first mobile shop in a city or town. It covers which kind of shop to open, what it costs (with a budget you can redo with your own quotes), the licences and GST registration, the GST rates, where the profit actually comes from, how to source stock safely, and the IMEI rules for used phones that came in late 2025 and that most guides do not mention yet.

## Quick answer

- **Pick a model:** new phones, accessories, repair, recharge and bill payments, or used phones. Most successful shops combine **phones + accessories + repair**.
- **Investment:** our sample budget for a small shop in a tier-2 town comes to about **₹7.4 lakh** with phone stock, or **₹4.4 lakh** for accessories and repair only. Your rent and stock decide the real figure.
- **Licences:** Shop and Establishment registration, a trade licence where your city requires one, and **GST registration** once turnover crosses ₹40 lakh for goods only, or **₹20 lakh if you also do repairs** (lower in some states).
- **GST:** **18%** on mobile phones, chargers, earphones, power banks and most accessories, and **18%** on repair services.
- **Margins:** thin on new phones, much higher on accessories and repair labour. Plan your profit around those.
- **Used phones:** since the Telecom Cyber Security amendment rules of **October 2025**, sellers of second-hand phones must **check the IMEI against the government's blacklist database** before selling. Keep a buy-sell register with the seller's ID.

## Step 1: Choose your mobile shop model

| Model | What you sell | Capital needed | Margin profile | Skill needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **New phones (multi-brand retail)** | Smartphones and feature phones from brand distributors | High: stock is expensive | Thin per phone; brand schemes and targets matter | Sales, product knowledge |
| **Accessories** | Covers, screen guards, chargers, cables, earphones, power banks, memory cards | Low to medium | High per item | Buying well, display |
| **Repair** | Screen and battery replacement, charging port, software | Low: tools and parts | Labour is mostly margin | A trained technician |
| **Recharge, DTH and bill payments** | Prepaid recharges, DTH, electricity and other bill payments | Very low: a wallet balance | Small commission per transaction | None; brings footfall |
| **Used and refurbished phones** | Phones bought from customers and resold | Medium | Can be good, but legal and quality risk | Testing and pricing |

A new shop usually starts with **accessories + repair + recharge**, which needs the least money, and adds new phones once it has a distributor and steady footfall. A phones-only shop competes with online sellers on a margin that leaves little for rent.

## Step 2: Location and shop size

A mobile shop lives on footfall: near bus stands, stations, colleges and markets. 150 to 250 sq ft is enough for counters, a wall display and a repair bench. Before you sign, check the power supply and earthing, signboard space, the landlord's consent for registrations, and how many mobile shops are within 200 metres. Two or three is normal; ten means a price war.

### The GST registration limit, with a catch for repair shops

The ₹40 lakh limit (₹20 lakh in some smaller states) applies to businesses supplying **only goods**. A mobile shop that also does **repairs is supplying services too**, so the limit becomes **₹20 lakh** (₹10 lakh in some north-eastern states). Turnover counts all your sales together. Our guide to the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) has the state-wise table.

Many mobile shops register from day one anyway: some distributors deal only with registered retailers, and registration lets you claim back the GST on purchases.

The composition scheme (1% on goods, with services such as repairs allowed up to 10% of last year's turnover or ₹5 lakh, whichever is higher) is possible, but you cannot claim credit on purchases or collect GST from customers. Check the numbers with your CA; our [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide explains the conditions.

### Buy only BIS-registered electronics

Mobile phones, chargers and adapters, and power banks are covered by BIS's Compulsory Registration Scheme. Such products must carry the BIS standard mark with a registration number (R-number). Avoid unmarked chargers and power banks.

## Step 5: GST rates for a mobile shop

| Product or service | HSN / SAC | GST |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mobile phones | 8517 | 18% |
| Chargers and adapters | 8504 | 18% |
| Earphones, headphones, speakers | 8518 | 18% |
| Power banks, phone batteries | 8507 / 8504 | 18% |
| Memory cards, pen drives | 8523 | 18% |
| Covers, screen guards, holders | As on supplier's bill | Mostly 18%; check the HSN on the supplier's bill |
| Repair and maintenance of phones | 9987 (998716 commonly used) | 18% |

These rates are as in force after the GST rate changes of **22 September 2025**, which did not change the 18% rate on phones.

**Used phones:** a registered dealer who buys used phones from individuals (without claiming input tax credit) can usually pay GST only on the margin, the difference between the selling and buying price, under Rule 32(5) of the CGST Rules. Confirm with your CA before you price on that basis.

**Billing a repair:** if you replace a screen, the part and the labour are usually billed together as a repair service at 18%. Show the part and labour as separate lines on the bill so the customer sees what they paid for.

| New phones | 6,00,000 | 5% | 30,000 |
| Accessories | 1,50,000 | 30% | 45,000 |
| Repairs | 60,000 | Parts cost ₹25,000 | 35,000 |
| Recharge and bill payments | (commission only) | | 4,000 |
| **Total** | **8,10,000** | | **1,14,000** |

| Monthly expense | Amount (₹) |
| --- | --- |
| Rent | 15,000 |
| One helper | 14,000 |
| Electricity | 3,000 |
| Internet, software, miscellaneous | 4,000 |
| **Total expenses** | **36,000** |

**Profit before the owner's own salary and income tax: ₹1,14,000 − ₹36,000 = ₹78,000.**

Phones are ₹6,00,000 of ₹8,10,000 in sales, about 74%, but only ₹30,000 of the ₹1,14,000 gross profit, about 26%. Accessories and repairs bring ₹80,000, about 70% of the gross profit, from under a quarter of the sales. That is why shop owners sell a cover and screen guard with every phone.

## Step 7: Sourcing stock

- **New phones:** buy through the brand's authorised distributor for your area. Ask about credit terms, schemes, demo units, price protection when prices drop, and the brand's process for dead-on-arrival handsets. Brands usually route retailers through distributors rather than selling directly.
- **Accessories:** wholesale electronics markets in big cities (Delhi's Gaffar Market is the best known) and online B2B suppliers. Buy small lots first, test quality, and insist on a GST bill.
- **Repair parts:** reliability matters more than price. Tell customers honestly whether a part is original or compatible.
- **Avoid:** phones sold "without bill", grey imports, refurbished phones sold as new, and chargers without the BIS mark.

## Step 8: Used phones, IMEI checks and record-keeping

Every phone has a 15-digit IMEI number (two on dual-SIM phones), and stolen phones are often resold through small shops.

**What the rules say now.** The Department of Telecommunications notified amendments to the Telecommunications (Telecom Cyber Security) Rules on **22 October 2025**. Under them, businesses buying and selling **used mobile phones must check each IMEI against a central database of blacklisted IMEIs** before the sale, and verification can carry a fee per IMEI. DoT has also warned that tampering with an IMEI is an offence under the Telecommunications Act, 2023. The procedure for dealers to get database access was still being rolled out, so ask your local mobile dealers' association or check the Sanchar Saathi portal for the current process.

**Free checks anyone can do today.** On the Sanchar Saathi portal or app, the "Know Your Mobile" service shows whether an IMEI is genuine and whether it is blocked as lost or stolen (you can also SMS "KYM" followed by the IMEI to 14422). Run it every time you buy a used phone, and keep a screenshot.

**Local police orders.** Some district magistrates and police commissioners issue orders requiring mobile dealers to keep registers of every used phone bought and sold, with the seller's and buyer's ID. For example, the Anand district administration in Gujarat issued one in March 2025. Ask your local police station whether such an order applies to you.

**A used-phone register to keep, whatever the local rule:**

| Column | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Date bought | 11-11-2026 |
| Brand, model, storage, colour | Brand X, Model Y, 128 GB, black |
| IMEI 1 / IMEI 2 | 35xxxxxxxxxxxxx / 35xxxxxxxxxxxxx |
| Seller's name, phone, address | |
| ID type and number (copy kept) | Aadhaar (masked), voter ID or driving licence |
| Original bill or box seen? | Yes / No |
| IMEI check result and date | Not blocked, 11-11-2026 |
| Price paid | ₹8,000 |
| Date sold, buyer's name and phone, sale bill number | |

Do not buy if the seller has no ID, the IMEI does not match the box, or it shows as blocked.

## Step 9: Warranty and after-sales

- **New phones** carry the manufacturer's warranty, honoured at the brand's authorised service centres. Your bill is the customer's proof of purchase, so it must show the date, model and your shop details. Note the IMEI on your bill or in your register so you can trace the handset later.
- **Dead-on-arrival handsets** follow the brand's DOA policy, usually within a short window after purchase. Learn your distributor's process before your first sale.
- **Don't promise what the brand doesn't.** Write "warranty as per manufacturer" on the bill.
- **Your repairs:** give a written warranty on your own work, for example 30 days on a screen you fitted, excluding physical and water damage. Write it on the repair bill.

## Step 10: Recharge and bill payments

Recharges, DTH top-ups and bill payments earn a small commission but bring people in daily. You sign up through an operator's retailer app or a multi-service distributor and keep a wallet balance. How GST applies to the commission depends on the distributor's arrangement, so ask your CA.

## FAQ

### How much investment is needed to start a mobile shop?

It depends on rent and stock. Our sample budget for a small shop in a tier-2 town comes to about ₹7.4 lakh including ₹3 lakh of phone stock, or about ₹4.4 lakh for accessories and repair only. Collect local quotes for each line before you decide.

### What is the profit margin in a mobile shop?

Margins on new phones are thin, often in single digits, while accessories and repair labour earn much more. In our worked example, phones were about 74% of sales but only about 26% of the gross profit.

### What licences do I need to open a mobile shop?

Shop and Establishment registration, a trade licence where your municipal body requires one, and GST registration once you cross the limit (₹20 lakh if you also do repairs, since that is a service). A current account and Udyam registration are useful too.

### What is the GST rate on mobile phones and accessories?

18% on mobile phones, chargers, earphones, power banks and most accessories, and 18% on repair services. Check the HSN code on your supplier's bill for less common items.

### Is a mobile repair shop a good business?

It needs little capital and most of the labour charge is margin, but it depends on a skilled technician and honest parts. Many shops run repair alongside accessories, because a customer who comes for a repair often buys a cover or charger too.

### Do I need to check the IMEI when buying a used phone?

Yes. Since October 2025, sellers of used phones must check the IMEI against the government's database of blacklisted IMEIs before selling. Use the Sanchar Saathi "Know Your Mobile" check every time, and keep a register with the seller's ID.

## The bottom line

Start with what needs the least money and builds footfall: accessories, repairs and recharges, then add new phones once a distributor will work with you. Register under your state's Shops Act, get GST registration early if you repair phones, bill at 18%, and plan profit around accessories and labour rather than handsets. If you handle used phones, check every IMEI and keep a register.

*Rules on IMEI verification and local dealer registers were still being rolled out in late 2026, and investment and margin figures here are illustrative assumptions. Check current requirements with your local police, DoT's Sanchar Saathi portal and your chartered accountant.*
