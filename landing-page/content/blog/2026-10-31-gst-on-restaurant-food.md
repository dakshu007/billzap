Ask ten people what GST a restaurant charges and you will hear "12% without AC, 18% with AC" from at least half of them. Some well-known finance websites still say the same in their FAQs. That rule died in **November 2017**. Since then the AC, the liquor licence and the star rating on the signboard have had nothing to do with the GST on your food bill.

This guide settles the rate for every common kind of eating place, shows a restaurant bill worked out line by line, and covers the questions that trip up owners: Swiggy and Zomato orders, service charge, hotel restaurants, composition restaurants and the counter that sells packed snacks next to the dining area.

## Quick answer

- **5% GST without input tax credit** applies to almost every restaurant in India: standalone restaurants (AC or non-AC), cafes, dhabas, cloud kitchens, food courts, takeaway and home delivery, and restaurants in hotels whose rooms are not "specified premises".
- **18% GST with input tax credit** applies only to restaurants located inside **specified premises**: hotel premises that supplied a room at more than **₹7,500 a night** in the previous financial year, or whose hotelier has declared them specified.
- **AC or non-AC makes no difference.** The old 12% / 18% split ended on 15 November 2017.
- **Swiggy and Zomato orders:** the app pays the 5% GST on the food, under Section 9(5), since 1 January 2022.
- **Alcohol** is outside GST. A bar bill carries state VAT on liquor and 5% or 18% GST on the food.
- **Composition restaurants** pay 5% of turnover from their own pocket and cannot show GST on the bill. They issue a bill of supply.

## The restaurant GST rate, by type of outlet

| Where and how food is served | GST rate | Input tax credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standalone restaurant, AC or non-AC, dine-in or takeaway | **5%** | Not available |
| Cafe, dhaba, QSR, food court, mall restaurant | **5%** | Not available |
| Cloud kitchen and home delivery | **5%** | Not available |
| Restaurant in a hotel that is **not** specified premises | **5%** | Not available |
| Restaurant **inside specified premises** (hotel, see below) | **18%** | Available |
| Outdoor catering (weddings, parties) by a caterer outside specified premises | **5%** | Not available |
| Food served together with renting a hall or venue for a function | **18%** | Available |
| Canteen or mess run on contract for an office, factory, school or college | **5%** | Not available |
| Food on trains and platforms by IRCTC or its licensees | **5%** | Not available |

On an intra-state bill, 5% appears as **2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST**, and 18% as **9% CGST + 9% SGST**. The service code (SAC) for restaurant service is **996331**.

### The 5% comes without input tax credit, and you cannot opt for 18%

"Without ITC" means the GST you pay on rent, kitchen equipment, packaging, gas and raw material cannot be set off against the 5% you collect. That tax is a cost, and it belongs in your menu pricing.

Many owners ask whether they can choose 18% to get credit back. A standalone restaurant cannot. The 18% rate follows the premises, not the owner's preference, and only a supplier of hotel accommodation can declare a premises "specified". The Karnataka GST department's FAQ on the April 2025 change gives the exact example: a mall owner cannot declare the mall specified, and restaurants in the mall outside a declared hotel premises "will continue to pay GST at the rate of 5% without ITC".

## What counts as "specified premises" from 1 April 2025

The definition was rewritten by Notification 05/2025-Central Tax (Rate), effective **1 April 2025**. A premises is specified for a financial year if:

1. the hotel supplied any unit of accommodation at a **value above ₹7,500 per unit per day** in the **previous financial year** (actual price charged, not the published tariff), or
2. a registered hotelier filed a declaration (Annexure VII) between **1 January and 31 March** of the previous year, declaring the premises specified, or
3. a new hotelier applying for registration filed a declaration (Annexure VIII) within **15 days** of the registration acknowledgement.

If any of these applies, every restaurant inside that premises charges **18% with ITC** for the whole year. A hotel crossing ₹7,500 for even one room-night is automatically in, with no declaration needed. Before April 2025 the test was the *declared tariff* of a room; dynamic room pricing made that unworkable, so the law now looks at what was actually charged last year.

For hotels below the line, the rooms themselves became cheaper on 22 September 2025: accommodation up to ₹7,500 a night moved to **5% without ITC**. Their restaurants stay at 5%.

## A restaurant bill, worked out

Here is a family dinner at a standalone restaurant in Pune. The restaurant is GST-registered and on the regular scheme, so the bill is a **tax invoice**.

| # | Item | SAC | Qty | Rate (₹) | Amount (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Veg thali | 996331 | 2 | 180.00 | 360.00 |
| 2 | Paneer butter masala | 996331 | 1 | 235.00 | 235.00 |
| 3 | Butter roti | 996331 | 4 | 28.00 | 112.00 |
| 4 | Sweet lassi | 996331 | 2 | 65.00 | 130.00 |
| | **Taxable value** | | | | **837.00** |

| Summary | Amount (₹) |
| --- | --- |
| Taxable value | 837.00 |
| CGST @ 2.5% | 20.93 |
| SGST @ 2.5% | 20.93 |
| Round off | +0.14 |
| **Amount payable** | **879.00** |

Check it yourself: 2.5% of ₹837 is ₹20.925, which rounds to **₹20.93** for each of CGST and SGST, so the tax is ₹41.86. ₹837 + ₹41.86 = ₹878.86, rounded to **₹879**. The lassi carries the same 5% as the thali: inside a restaurant, a drink served with the meal is part of the restaurant service, not a separate sale of goods.

What else the tax invoice needs, under Rule 46: the restaurant's name, address and **GSTIN**, a serial number, the date, the SAC, the taxable value, the rate and the tax amounts, and the place of supply. If the diner is a registered business and gives you its GSTIN, add their name, address and GSTIN too. ## Swiggy, Zomato and other food delivery apps

Since **1 January 2022**, restaurant service supplied through an e-commerce operator is covered by **Section 9(5)** of the CGST Act. The app, not the restaurant, collects and pays the **5% GST** on the food and issues the bill to the customer.

What this means for you:

- **Do not charge GST** again on the food value of app orders, and do not pay it in your own GSTR-3B. Report these sales in the separate table for Section 9(5) supplies in GSTR-1, as your CA will show you.
- **The app's commission to you** is a separate service, billed by the app at **18%**. Because your own sales are at 5% without ITC, you generally cannot claim that 18% back. It is a cost.
- **The delivery fee** the app charges the customer for local delivery also became subject to 18% GST, paid by the app under Section 9(5), from 22 September 2025.

Your own phone and WhatsApp orders, delivered by your own staff, are normal sales: 5% on your bill, paid by you.

## Service charge: voluntary, and taxed if paid

A restaurant **cannot add a service charge automatically** or by default. The Central Consumer Protection Authority's guidelines of July 2022 say so, and the **Delhi High Court upheld them in March 2025**, confirming that service charge must be voluntary and cannot be collected under another name. The CCPA has since acted against restaurants that kept levying it.

If a customer chooses to pay a service charge and it is billed, it is part of the value of your restaurant service, so **GST at the same rate (5%) applies to it**. A tip handed directly to staff and never billed is not your supply.

## Bars, alcohol and the two-tax bill

Alcoholic liquor for human consumption is **outside GST**. States tax it through excise and **VAT**. A bar or a restaurant with a liquor licence therefore runs two taxes on one table:

- Food and soft drinks: **5% GST** (or 18% inside specified premises).
- Beer, wine and spirits: **state VAT**, at your state's rate, with no CGST or SGST.

Keep them as separate sections, or separate bills, so that the GST figures in your returns match your GST bills exactly. The liquor licence no longer pushes your food to 18%; that was part of the pre-2017 rule.

## Packed goods at the counter versus restaurant service

Many restaurants also sell sealed products over a counter: bottled water, cold drinks, packed namkeen, chocolates. Whether that is "restaurant service" or "sale of goods" decides the rate.

- **Served as part of a meal or order** at the restaurant: restaurant service, **5%**.
- **Sold over a separate retail counter** as sealed goods, with no service element: sale of goods at the **goods rate**. Aerated drinks are **40%**, packed namkeen and chocolates **5%**, and so on. See our [GST rates list](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-rates-list) for the item-wise rates.

Sweet shops and bakeries with a few tables sit on the line. Since September 2025 most sweets, bakery items and ice cream are taxed at **5%** as goods, so for most items the rate is the same either way. Where an item carries a different goods rate, or you have a separate takeaway counter and dining area, ask your CA which side each sale falls on. A 2021 CBIC circular already held that an ice cream parlour selling factory-made ice cream is selling goods, not restaurant service, which shows how the line is drawn.

## Composition scheme for restaurants

Small restaurants can opt for the **composition scheme**, which is simpler but changes the bill completely.

| | Regular scheme | Composition scheme |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tax rate | 5% on each bill | **5% of total turnover** (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) |
| Who bears the tax | The customer, shown on the bill | **The restaurant**, from its own margin |
| Document | Tax invoice | **Bill of supply**, no tax shown |
| Turnover limit | None | Up to **₹1.5 crore** in the previous year (₹75 lakh in some special category states) |
| Returns | GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B | CMP-08 quarterly, GSTR-4 annually |
| Input tax credit | No (at 5%) | No |

Under composition, the restaurant **must not collect any tax** from the customer. The same dinner would look like this:

| # | Item | Qty | Rate (₹) | Amount (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Veg thali | 2 | 180.00 | 360.00 |
| 2 | Paneer butter masala | 1 | 235.00 | 235.00 |
| 3 | Butter roti | 4 | 28.00 | 112.00 |
| 4 | Sweet lassi | 2 | 65.00 | 130.00 |
| | **Total** | | | **837.00** |

The heading is **Bill of Supply**, and it must carry the words *"composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies"*. The customer pays ₹837. The restaurant later pays 5% of that, **₹41.85**, to the government through CMP-08. That is why composition restaurants usually price their menus a little higher. ## Do you need GST registration at all?

Restaurant service is a **service**, so the threshold is **₹20 lakh** of aggregate turnover in a year (₹10 lakh in special category states), not the ₹40 lakh limit for goods. A small dhaba or tea stall below that line does not have to register. If it is not registered, it **must not charge GST**: its bill is a plain bill, and calling it a tax invoice would be collecting tax it has no right to collect. ## Common mistakes on restaurant bills

- **Charging 12% or 18% at a standalone AC restaurant.** It is 5%. Overcharging GST is still collecting tax wrongly.
- **Claiming ITC on rent or equipment** while charging 5%. The 5% rate comes without credit.
- **Adding service charge by default.** It must be optional, and if billed, GST applies to it.
- **Mixing liquor into the GST total.** Liquor carries VAT, not GST.
- **Charging GST on app orders** that Swiggy or Zomato already taxed under Section 9(5).
- **A composition restaurant showing "GST 5%" on the bill.** Composition dealers cannot collect tax.
- **No FSSAI number on the bill.** It is mandatory for food businesses.

## FAQ

### What is the GST rate on restaurant food in 2026?

5% without input tax credit for almost all restaurants, including AC restaurants, cafes, dhabas, cloud kitchens and takeaway. Only restaurants inside specified hotel premises charge 18% with input tax credit.

### Is GST on an AC restaurant 18%?

No. The 18% rate for AC restaurants ended on 15 November 2017. An AC restaurant outside specified premises charges 5%, the same as a non-AC one.

### What GST applies to food in a hotel restaurant?

It depends on the hotel. If the hotel supplied any room at more than ₹7,500 a night in the previous financial year, or has declared itself a specified premises, its restaurant charges 18% with ITC. Otherwise the restaurant charges 5% without ITC. Room service food follows the same rate as the hotel's restaurant.

### Who pays GST on Swiggy and Zomato orders?

The app. Since 1 January 2022 the food delivery platform collects and pays the 5% GST on restaurant orders placed through it, under Section 9(5). The restaurant should not charge GST again on those orders.

### Is GST charged on service charge?

Service charge is voluntary and cannot be added by default. If a customer agrees to pay it and it appears on the bill, GST applies to it at the same rate as the food, normally 5%.

### Can a restaurant claim input tax credit?

Not at the 5% rate. Only restaurants in specified premises charging 18% can claim input tax credit. For everyone else, GST paid on rent, equipment and purchases is a cost.

### How does a composition restaurant bill its customers?

It issues a bill of supply showing no GST, with the words "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies". It pays 5% of its turnover to the government itself, every quarter, through CMP-08.

## The bottom line

Restaurant GST in 2026 is simpler than its reputation: **5% without ITC** almost everywhere, **18% with ITC** only inside specified hotel premises, and nothing on your bill depends on the AC. Get the SAC code and the 5% split right once, keep liquor and app orders out of your GST totals, never force a service charge, and your bills will be correct every time.
