Search "reverse charge mechanism in GST" and you get long tables: services by an arbitral tribunal, recovery agents, members of an RBI committee, lending of securities, insurance agents. All correct, and almost none of it is anything a kirana, hardware store or medical shop will ever pay for.

The question a shop owner actually has is narrower: **does RCM affect my shop, and if it does, what do I do each month?** This guide covers the handful of cases a small business really meets, the self-invoice and cash payment steps, GSTR-3B reporting and the composition dealer's position.

## Quick answer

- **Reverse charge (RCM)** means the **buyer pays the GST** to the government instead of the seller. It applies only to supplies the government has **notified**, not to every purchase from an unregistered person.
- **Cases a small shop meets:** freight paid to a **goods transport agency (GTA)** that does not charge GST itself (5%); **shop or godown rent from an unregistered landlord** (18%, regular taxpayers only); **advocate fees** (18%, unless last year's turnover was within the registration threshold); **security guards** from a non-company agency (18%); and some **scrap** bought from unregistered sellers.
- **Steps:** make a **self-invoice within 30 days** if the supplier is unregistered, issue a **payment voucher**, **pay the tax in cash** (input tax credit cannot be used), report it in **GSTR-3B Table 3.1(d)**, then claim it back as credit in **Table 4A(3)**.
- **For a regular taxpayer, RCM is mostly a cash-flow step**; for a **composition dealer it is a real cost**, because there is no credit. Composition dealers are excluded from RCM on rent from unregistered landlords (since 16 January 2025) and on security services, but not on GTA freight.

## What is the reverse charge mechanism in GST?

In a normal (forward charge) sale, the seller collects GST from you and pays it to the government. Under reverse charge, the seller bills you **without GST**, and you, the recipient, work out the tax and pay it yourself.

There are two legal routes into RCM:

| Provision | What it covers | Does it touch a small shop? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Section 9(3)** | A notified list of goods and services on which the recipient pays | Yes, a few entries (below) |
| **Section 9(4)** | Purchases by a registered person from **unregistered** suppliers, but only for notified classes of recipients | Today limited to real estate promoters; not to shops |

The second row causes most of the confusion. From 1 July to 12 October 2017, every purchase from an unregistered person attracted RCM. That was withdrawn, and since 2019 Section 9(4) has been notified only for real estate promoters. **A shop buying stock, stationery or repairs from an unregistered local supplier does not pay RCM**, unless the item is on the notified list.

## Does RCM affect my shop? The cases that actually come up

Out of the full RCM list of services and goods, these are the entries a small business meets.

| What you pay for | Is it RCM? | Rate | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Freight to a transporter who issues a consignment note (GTA)** | Yes, if the GTA has not opted to charge GST itself | 5% | If the GTA's bill already charges 5% or 18% GST, you do not pay RCM |
| **Tempo or truck hired directly from a driver, no consignment note** | No | Nil | Road transport by a non-GTA is exempt |
| **Shop or godown rent, unregistered landlord** | Yes, if you are a regular taxpayer | 18% | Composition dealers excluded from 16 January 2025 |
| **Shop rent, registered landlord** | No | 18% forward charge | Landlord charges GST on the rent bill |
| **Advocate or law firm fees** | Yes | 18% | Exempt if your turnover last year was within the registration threshold |
| **Security guards from an agency that is not a company** | Yes, for regular taxpayers | 18% | Composition dealers excluded; a company agency charges GST itself |
| **Metal scrap from an unregistered seller** | Yes, since 10 October 2024 | As per the scrap's rate | |
| **Plastic, tyre or e-waste scrap, or used cooking oil, from an unregistered seller** | Yes, from **1 November 2026** | As per the item's rate | New entries; see below |

The full list also covers sponsorship paid by companies and partnership firms, and directors' fees paid by companies; a proprietorship meets neither. Two things decide most of the rest: **whether the supplier is registered** and **whether you are a regular or composition taxpayer**. If your shop is an unregistered proprietorship (below the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit)), RCM generally does not touch you at all.

### GTA freight: the one most shops meet

A goods transport agency is a transporter that issues a **consignment note** (the bilty or LR) for goods it carries by road. If your distributor sends stock "freight to pay" through a transport company and you pay the freight, you are the recipient of a GTA service.

Since the GST rate changes of 22 September 2025, a GTA can either:

- **charge GST itself (forward charge)** at 5% without input credit or 18% with it, so its bill shows GST and you pay it like any other bill; or
- **not opt for forward charge**, so its bill carries no GST and the recipient pays **5% under RCM**.

RCM on GTA applies when the recipient is a registered person, a factory, a society, a body corporate, a partnership firm or a registered casual taxable person. The old exemption for small consignments (up to ₹1,500 per carriage or ₹750 per consignee) was withdrawn from 18 July 2022.

### RCM on rent: the newest and most expensive

From **10 October 2024**, renting any property **other than a residential dwelling** (a shop, office, godown) **by an unregistered person to a registered person** came under RCM at 18%. Many shopkeepers rent from a relative or a local owner who is not registered, so this caught a lot of small businesses.

The **55th GST Council** then recommended excluding composition taxpayers, and from **16 January 2025** they are out of this entry. Composition dealers who paid RCM on rent between 10 October 2024 and 15 January 2025 had that regularised "as is where is", meaning no refund and no demand.

If your landlord is registered, there is no RCM; the landlord charges 18% on the rent invoice. Residential property rented by a registered person has its own rules. Our guide to [GST on rent](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-on-rent) covers every combination.

### Advocate fees

Legal services by an advocate or law firm to a business are under RCM. But they are **exempt** if the business's aggregate turnover in the preceding financial year was within the limit for exemption from registration. So a small shop below the threshold usually pays nothing, while a business above it pays 18% under RCM on its lawyer's bill.

### Goods under RCM

The goods list is short and mostly agricultural: cashew nuts not shelled or peeled, bidi wrapper (tendu) leaves, tobacco leaves and raw cotton supplied by an agriculturist; silk yarn; lottery; used vehicles and seized goods sold by the government; and **metal scrap** bought from an unregistered person (since 10 October 2024).

The latest addition: by **Notification No. 04/2026-Central Tax (Rate)** dated 27 October 2026, from **1 November 2026** RCM also applies when a registered person buys **used cooking oil (heading 1518), plastic waste and scrap (3915), tyre waste and scrap (4004) and e-waste (8549)** from an unregistered person. If you buy old electronics, tyres or plastic from kabadiwalas for resale, check this with your accountant.

## How to pay RCM, step by step

### 1. Find the date the tax becomes due (time of supply)

- **Services:** the earlier of the date you pay the supplier, or the 61st day after the supplier's invoice date.
- **Goods:** the earliest of the date you receive the goods, the date you pay, or the 31st day after the supplier's invoice date.

For rent or freight you pay promptly, that is simply the month you pay.

### 2. Make a self-invoice if the supplier is unregistered

When you receive a supply under RCM from an **unregistered** supplier, you must issue an invoice to yourself (Section 31(3)(f)). **Rule 47A**, in force from 1 November 2024, sets the time limit: **within 30 days from the date of receipt** of the goods or services.

It is a tax invoice in your own series: your GSTIN, serial number and date, the supplier's name and address, description with HSN or SAC, taxable value, rate and tax, and a note that tax is payable on reverse charge. Monthly rent means one self-invoice a month.

If the supplier is **registered** (most GTAs are), you do not self-invoice. The supplier's invoice carries "reverse charge: yes" and that is your document.

### 3. Issue a payment voucher

When you pay the supplier for an RCM supply, issue a payment voucher (Section 31(3)(g)) recording the amount paid.

### 4. Pay the tax in cash

RCM tax must be paid **through the electronic cash ledger**, not set off against input tax credit. Even a shop with a large credit balance must put real cash in.

### 5. Report it in GSTR-3B

| Table | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| **3.1(d)** Inward supplies liable to reverse charge | The value and the tax you are paying under RCM |
| **4A(3)** Inward supplies liable to reverse charge (ITC) | The same tax, claimed back as input tax credit, if eligible |

When a registered supplier such as a GTA reports the invoice as reverse charge, it flows into your GSTR-2B and auto-fills both tables. For an **unregistered** landlord or scrap seller, nothing appears in GSTR-2B, so you enter the figures yourself. Since August 2024 the portal's **RCM Liability/ITC Statement** tracks RCM paid against RCM credit claimed, so excess credit gets flagged. See our [GSTR-3B guide](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-3b) for the full return.

### 6. Claim the credit

RCM tax you paid is input tax credit like any other, provided the item is used for business and is not a blocked credit. You can claim it in the same return in which you pay it. Our guide to [input tax credit](https://billzap.app/blog/input-tax-credit-gst) covers the conditions.

## Worked example: GTA freight for a regular taxpayer

Sharma Traders, a regular GST taxpayer in Maharashtra, receives stock through a transport company in the same state. The GTA has not opted for forward charge, so its bill for **₹20,000** carries no GST.

| Item | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freight paid to the GTA | | ₹20,000 |
| CGST under RCM | ₹20,000 × 2.5% | ₹500 |
| SGST under RCM | ₹20,000 × 2.5% | ₹500 |
| **RCM tax paid in cash** | | **₹1,000** |

If the transport company were in another state, it would be IGST of ₹1,000 instead of CGST and SGST.

Now the full month. Sharma Traders collected **₹18,000** GST on its sales and has **₹12,000** of credit from purchase bills.

| | Without the RCM freight | With the RCM freight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GST on sales | ₹18,000 | ₹18,000 |
| Credit from purchases | ₹12,000 | ₹12,000 + ₹1,000 RCM = ₹13,000 |
| Cash for forward charge tax | ₹18,000 − ₹12,000 = ₹6,000 | ₹18,000 − ₹13,000 = ₹5,000 |
| Cash for RCM | Nil | ₹1,000 |
| **Total cash paid** | **₹6,000** | **₹6,000** |

The total is the same. For a regular taxpayer, RCM costs nothing extra; it only changes which table the cash goes through. Missing it is what costs money: unpaid RCM attracts interest at 18% a year.

### The same freight for a composition dealer

A composition dealer receiving the same ₹20,000 GTA freight still pays **₹1,000** under RCM, in cash, at the normal 5% rate (not the composition rate). There is no credit to claim, so the ₹1,000 is a real cost.

**Rent:** a regular taxpayer paying **₹25,000** a month to an unregistered landlord in the same state pays RCM of **₹4,500** (CGST ₹2,250 + SGST ₹2,250) and claims it back. A composition dealer in the same shop pays **nil** since 16 January 2025.

## RCM for composition dealers in short

If you are under the [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme):

- You **pay RCM** at the **normal GST rate**, with **no input tax credit**, and report it in **CMP-08** and the annual GSTR-4.
- You are **excluded** from RCM on rent from an unregistered landlord (from 16 January 2025) and on security services.
- You still pay it on GTA freight, advocate fees (subject to the turnover exemption) and the notified goods and scrap.

## Common mistakes

Paying RCM from the credit ledger (it must be cash); paying it on every unregistered purchase (only notified items count); a late or missing self-invoice; paying RCM when the GTA has already charged GST on its bill; and, for regular taxpayers, not claiming the credit back.

## FAQ

### Is RCM applicable on rent paid to an unregistered landlord?

Yes, for a shop, office or godown (anything other than a residential dwelling) rented by a registered tenant from an unregistered landlord, since 10 October 2024, at 18%. Composition dealers are excluded from 16 January 2025. If the landlord is registered, the landlord charges GST instead.

### Can I pay RCM using input tax credit?

No. Tax under reverse charge must be paid in cash through the electronic cash ledger. Once paid, it can be claimed as input tax credit, if eligible, in the same return.

### What is the time limit for a self-invoice under RCM?

Under Rule 47A, in force from 1 November 2024, within 30 days from the date you receive the goods or services from an unregistered supplier.

### Is RCM applicable on all purchases from unregistered dealers?

No. That general rule applied only from 1 July to 12 October 2017. Today RCM applies only to notified goods and services, plus Section 9(4) cases that are limited to real estate promoters.

### Is GST payable on freight paid to a tempo driver?

Not if the driver is not a goods transport agency, that is, does not issue a consignment note. Road transport of goods by a non-GTA is exempt. If a transport company issues a bilty and does not charge GST itself, you pay 5% under RCM.

### Do composition dealers pay RCM?

Yes, where an entry applies, at the normal GST rate and without input tax credit, reported in CMP-08. They are excluded from RCM on rent from unregistered landlords and on security services.

## The bottom line

Most of the RCM list is not about you. Check four things: whether your transporter charges GST on its bill, whether your landlord is registered, whether your lawyer's fees are taxable at your turnover, and whether you buy scrap from unregistered sellers. Where RCM applies, self-invoice within 30 days, pay in cash, and claim the credit back in the same GSTR-3B. Done that way, a regular taxpayer pays nothing extra.
