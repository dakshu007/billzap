Most pages on this topic are written by lenders selling their own unsecured loan, so they skip the most useful fact for a small shop: **banks are not allowed to ask a micro or small enterprise for collateral on a loan of up to ₹20 lakh.** That limit was ₹10 lakh until 31 March 2026, and many pages still quote the old figure.

This guide sets out every collateral-free route open to a kirana, a medical store, a tailoring unit or a street vendor in 2026, matches each to a loan size, shows what the interest difference costs in rupees, and explains what a bank looks at before it says yes.

## Quick answer

- **The RBI rule:** for loans to micro and small enterprises (MSEs) **sanctioned or renewed on or after 1 April 2026**, banks must not take collateral up to **₹20 lakh**, and may go up to **₹25 lakh** for units with a good track record.
- **Government-backed routes:** **Mudra** (up to ₹20 lakh), **PM SVANidhi** for street vendors (₹15,000, then ₹25,000, then ₹50,000), **Stand-Up India** for women and SC/ST first-time entrepreneurs (₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore, renewed to March 2031) and **PMEGP** for new manufacturing and service units, with a subsidy.
- **CGTMSE** is the guarantee behind many of these loans: up to **₹10 crore** since 1 April 2025, with a fee from **0.37% a year** on loans up to ₹10 lakh. The lender applies for it, not you.
- **NBFC and app loans** are faster but usually cost much more.
- **What gets you approved:** a clean credit record, a bank account that shows sales coming in, GST returns or sales records that match it, and a clear use for the money.

## What "without collateral" really means

Collateral is an asset you pledge that is separate from the business: a house, land, a fixed deposit or a third person's guarantee. A collateral-free loan does not ask for these. But the bank still takes a charge (hypothecation) on the stock or equipment bought with its money, you as proprietor remain personally liable, and a guarantee fee may be passed on to you. Pledging gold or silver **voluntarily** within the limit is allowed; nobody can make you do it.

## RBI's ₹20 lakh collateral-free rule (from 1 April 2026)

The Reserve Bank's Lending to MSME Sector (Amendment) Directions, 2026, dated **9 February 2026**, say:

- Banks are **mandated not to accept collateral security** for loans of up to **₹20 lakh** to units in the MSE sector (it was ₹10 lakh).
- Banks **may** raise this to **₹25 lakh** for units with a good track record and financial position.
- Banks are advised to give **collateral-free loans up to ₹20 lakh** to units financed under **PMEGP**.
- It applies to loans **sanctioned or renewed on or after 1 April 2026**.

To count as an MSE, register on **Udyam** (free). Retail and wholesale traders with Udyam count as MSMEs for priority-sector lending, so a shop's working capital loan generally falls under this rule; see our [Udyam registration](https://billzap.app/blog/udyam-registration) guide. If a branch insists on property papers for a loan below ₹20 lakh, ask politely, in writing, which rule it is applying.

## CGTMSE: the guarantee behind collateral-free loans

The **Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises** does not lend. It promises the lender that, if your loan goes bad, it will pay a large share of the loss. That promise is what lets a bank lend without your property.

| Feature | What applies now |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum guarantee | **₹10 crore** per borrower, since **1 April 2025** (was ₹5 crore) |
| Share of loss covered | Commonly **75%**; **85%** for small loans to micro enterprises; **90%** for women-led enterprises (slabs vary, ask your bank) |
| Annual guarantee fee | **0.37% a year** on loans up to ₹10 lakh; higher for bigger loans |
| Who applies | The **lender**, after sanctioning your loan |
| Who qualifies | Micro and small enterprises with Udyam; informal micro units through the Udyam Assist Platform |

**Example:** a bank sanctions an ₹8 lakh limit to a hardware shop under CGTMSE. The first year's fee is ₹8,00,000 × 0.37% = **₹2,960**. If the bank recovers it from you, count it as part of the loan's cost. Terms for retail traders have changed more than once, so ask your bank whether your shop's loan will be covered and who bears the fee.

## Mudra loans: the default for small shops

The **Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana** is the main collateral-free route for non-farm micro businesses, through banks, NBFCs, small finance banks and MFIs.

| Category | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Shishu | Up to ₹50,000 |
| Kishore | Above ₹50,000 up to ₹5 lakh |
| Tarun | Above ₹5 lakh up to ₹10 lakh |
| Tarun Plus | Above ₹10 lakh up to ₹20 lakh, only after repaying a Tarun loan |

Mudra loans are guaranteed by the **Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units**, which the Union Cabinet extended in November 2026 to 31 March 2031. There is no subsidy, and each lender sets its own rate. Our [Mudra loan](https://billzap.app/blog/mudra-loan) guide covers documents, EMIs and applying on JanSamarth.

## PM SVANidhi: for street vendors

**PM Street Vendor's AtmaNirbhar Nidhi** is for vendors on the street: vegetable carts, tea stalls, cobblers, kiosks. The Cabinet restructured it in **August 2025** and extended lending to **31 March 2030**.

- **Three loans in turn:** **₹15,000** (was ₹10,000), then **₹25,000** (was ₹20,000), then **₹50,000**, each after repaying the last.
- **7% a year interest subsidy**, credited quarterly while the loan is not in default.
- **Digital cashback** of up to ₹1,600, and a **UPI-linked RuPay credit card** (limit up to ₹30,000) after repaying the second loan.

You need a vending certificate or a letter of recommendation from your urban local body; coverage is widening in phases to census towns and peri-urban areas. Apply on the PM SVANidhi portal, at a bank or a Common Service Centre. ## Stand-Up India: for women and SC/ST first-time entrepreneurs

**Stand-Up India** gives bank loans of **₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore** to set up a **new (greenfield) enterprise** in manufacturing, services, trading or agri-allied activities, to a woman, SC or ST entrepreneur, or a firm in which such a person holds at least 51% and control.

The scheme lapsed on 31 March 2025. On **11 November 2026** the Union Cabinet approved its continuation to **31 March 2031**, with a credit guarantee corpus of **₹4,111 crore**. The revamped operating rules are still being rolled out, so confirm the margin money and guarantee fee at your bank or on the Stand-Up Mitra portal. It is for a **first** venture, not for expanding an existing shop.

## PMEGP: a loan with a subsidy

The **Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme**, run by KVIC, funds **new** micro enterprises, with part of the project cost paid as a subsidy.

| Applicant | Subsidy, urban | Subsidy, rural | Own contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General category | 15% | 25% | 10% |
| Special category (women, SC/ST, OBC, minorities, ex-servicemen, persons with disabilities and others) | 25% | 35% | 5% |

It is meant for new manufacturing and service units (under the 2022 guidelines, projects up to ₹50 lakh in manufacturing and ₹20 lakh in services); a plain trading shop is generally not what it is for. **Status:** PMEGP was approved for 2021-22 to 2025-26, and we could not confirm a formal extension beyond March 2026, so check the KVIC PMEGP portal before planning around the subsidy.

**Not for shops:** the **Mutual Credit Guarantee Scheme for MSMEs** (60% guarantee on loans up to ₹100 crore for equipment and machinery, revised from 24 February 2026 to add services) is for factories and larger units, not for stocking a shop.

## NBFC and fintech unsecured business loans

Apps and NBFCs advertise "business loan without collateral in 24 hours". They are genuine options when the lender is regulated, but you pay for speed. **Typical terms (illustrative, not offers):** interest from about **12% to 28% a year** or more, processing fees often **1% to 4%**, tenure of 12 to 36 months.

What the rate costs on a **₹3,00,000** loan over **24 months**:

| Rate a year | Monthly EMI | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12% (bank MSE or Mudra loan, illustrative) | ₹14,122 | ₹38,929 |
| 24% | ₹15,861 | ₹80,672 |
| 30% | ₹16,774 | ₹1,02,572 |

The 30% loan costs **₹63,643 more** in interest than the 12% one, before fees. Before accepting any digital loan, check the app in **RBI's directory of digital lending apps** (live since 1 July 2025, under Citizen's Corner on rbi.org.in), read the **Key Fact Statement** for the full annual percentage rate including fees, and make sure money moves only to and from the lender's own account.

## Documents to keep ready

1. **KYC:** PAN and Aadhaar of the proprietor (and partners or directors).
2. **Proof of business:** Udyam, shop registration or trade licence, GST certificate if registered.
3. **Bank statements:** last 6 to 12 months of the account your sales go into.
4. **GST returns:** GSTR-3B and GSTR-1 for 6 to 12 months, if registered.
5. **Income tax returns:** one to two years, for larger loans.
6. **Sales and profit summary** by month; a projected balance sheet for bigger loans.
7. **Quotations** for any equipment you will buy.
8. **Scheme papers:** vending certificate for PM SVANidhi; a project report for Stand-Up India or PMEGP.

Lenders also check your age, credit score and that you are not in default anywhere. NBFCs often want one to three years in business; government schemes accept new units.

## How a lender judges a small shop

An officer looking at a ₹5 lakh request from a kirana asks: is the business real, does money flow through it, and does this person repay?

**Your bank statement carries the most weight**, because the bank did not get it from you. They look at monthly credits, the average balance, bounces, and cash withdrawals that empty the account. A shop that takes UPI into its own account and deposits cash regularly looks much bigger on paper than one that banks nothing.

**GST returns are the second check.** The turnover in your GSTR-3B should be in the same range as your bank credits. A gap either way needs explaining.

**Your sales records fill the gaps**, especially for an unregistered shop: numbered bills and monthly totals are your best evidence of turnover and margin. Our [profit margin formula](https://billzap.app/blog/profit-margin-formula) guide helps you show profit, not just sales.

**Can you carry the EMI?** Work from profit. Your shop sells ₹4,00,000 a month at a 12% gross margin: ₹48,000. Rent, power, a helper and other costs take ₹30,000, leaving ₹18,000. A ₹14,122 EMI would take 78% of that, which is too tight. A ₹1,50,000 loan over 24 months at 12% (EMI ₹7,061) takes about 39%, leaving room for a bad month. And collect your [udhaar](https://billzap.app/blog/udhaar-management) first: money stuck with customers is money you end up borrowing.

## Red flags: collateral-free loan scams

- **Upfront fees** ("file charge", "insurance", "GST on loan") paid to a personal UPI ID before disbursal.
- **"Pre-approved" government loan letters** on WhatsApp or SMS.
- **Agents promising subsidy or no repayment.**

If you have paid someone, call **1930** or report at cybercrime.gov.in, and tell your bank at once.

## FAQ

### Can I get a business loan without collateral in India?

Yes. Banks must not take collateral on loans up to ₹20 lakh to micro and small enterprises sanctioned or renewed from 1 April 2026, and Mudra, PM SVANidhi, Stand-Up India and CGTMSE-backed loans are collateral-free. You still need a good credit record and proof the business earns.

### What is the maximum collateral-free loan under CGTMSE?

₹10 crore per borrower, since 1 April 2025. The guarantee covers a share of the loan, commonly 75% to 90%, for an annual fee starting at 0.37% on loans up to ₹10 lakh.

### Is the collateral-free limit ₹10 lakh or ₹20 lakh?

₹20 lakh for bank loans to MSEs sanctioned or renewed on or after 1 April 2026, and up to ₹25 lakh at the bank's discretion for units with a good track record. ₹10 lakh applied before that.

### How much loan can I get under PM SVANidhi?

₹15,000 first, ₹25,000 second and ₹50,000 third, each after repaying the previous one, with a 7% a year interest subsidy.

### Is Stand-Up India still available?

Yes. It lapsed in March 2025 and the Cabinet approved its continuation on 11 November 2026, until 31 March 2031, for loans of ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for a first venture.

### Can a shop without GST registration get a collateral-free loan?

Yes. GST is not a condition for Mudra or the RBI rule. Udyam, a shop licence and a bank account showing your sales are usually enough for a small first loan.

## The bottom line

For most shop owners, the cheapest money without collateral is still at the bank: a Mudra or MSE loan up to ₹20 lakh, which the bank may not secure with your property. PM SVANidhi suits street vendors, Stand-Up India suits women and SC/ST founders starting fresh, and CGTMSE stands behind bigger loans. Use app loans only when speed is worth the extra interest, and keep a bank statement and sales record that show money really flowing through your shop.
