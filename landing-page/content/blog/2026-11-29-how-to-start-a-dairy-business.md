Milk is the one product every household buys every day. That steady demand is why so many people want to start a dairy business, and also why it is harder than it looks: margins on liquid milk are thin, milk spoils within hours without cooling, and on a farm the feed bill arrives daily whether the cows are milking or not.

There are two ways in: **sell milk** from a parlour or collection shop, or **produce milk** on a small farm. This guide covers both: costs, licences, GST after the 2025 reform, the schemes that actually help a small unit, a worked monthly P&L for a five-cow farm, and the cold chain.

## Quick answer

- **Choose your model:** a **milk parlour or collection shop** (lower risk, needs location and refrigeration) or a **small dairy farm** of 5 to 10 animals (needs land, fodder, daily labour and veterinary skills).
- **Rough investment:** about **₹2.7 lakh to ₹6.8 lakh** for a parlour and **₹5.5 lakh to ₹9.5 lakh** for a five-cow farm, excluding land, by our estimate.
- **FSSAI:** compulsory. FSSAI's advisory of **11 March 2026** told states to make sure milk producers outside dairy cooperatives, and milk vendors, hold registration or a licence. Most small units need only basic registration.
- **GST:** fresh milk is **exempt**. Since **22 September 2025**, UHT milk and pre-packaged paneer are also **nil**, and **butter, ghee and cheese are 5%** (down from 12%). Ice cream is 5%.
- **Schemes:** the **Kisan Credit Card** (collateral-free up to ₹2 lakh) and state animal husbandry schemes suit small farms; **AHIDF** helps processing units with a 3% interest subvention. Big-ticket central subsidies mostly target larger units.
- **Profit:** on a small farm, **feed is the biggest cost**. Selling milk directly to households instead of to a collection centre can be the difference between a loss and a profit (worked example below).

## Step 1: Pick your model

| | Milk parlour or collection shop | Small dairy farm (5-10 animals) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you do | Buy milk from farmers, a cooperative or a brand; sell milk, curd, paneer, ghee, ice cream | Keep cows or buffaloes; sell milk to households or a collection centre |
| Space | 150-300 sq ft shop on a busy road | Shed, fodder storage, ideally land for green fodder |
| Main risk | Spoilage, adulterated milk, thin margins | Animal illness, feed prices, a dry cow still eating |
| Skills | Retail, hygiene, milk testing | Animal care, breeding, fodder planning |
| Rough start | ₹2.7 lakh to ₹6.8 lakh | ₹5.5 lakh to ₹9.5 lakh plus land |

A third route is a **brand booth or distributorship**, where the brand sets the margin, interiors and rules; ask its local office. **If you have never kept animals, start with the parlour.**

### Where the money comes from

Liquid milk earns a few rupees a litre; curd, paneer, ghee, lassi and ice cream earn more. Turning surplus milk into curd and paneer converts tomorrow's spoilage into today's margin:

| Monthly illustration | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Milk sold | 120 litres a day × 30 | 3,600 litres |
| Milk margin | 3,600 × (₹52 sale − ₹42 cost) | ₹36,000 |
| Less spoilage, 2% of volume | 72 litres × ₹42 | −₹3,024 |
| Products sold (curd, paneer, ghee, ice cream) | ₹90,000 at 30% gross margin | ₹27,000 |
| **Gross profit** | | **₹59,976** |
| Rent, helper, electricity, packing, sundries | ₹15,000 + ₹12,000 + ₹6,000 + ₹3,000 + ₹3,000 | −₹39,000 |
| **Profit before owner's pay and tax** | | **₹20,976** |

This is an illustration, not a forecast; prices vary by area, season and cow or buffalo milk. Our [break-even point](https://billzap.app/blog/break-even-point) guide shows how to find the sales you need.

If you collect milk from farmers, test every can for fat and SNF and pay on a rate chart, as cooperatives do. Keep a daily register of litres and fat per supplier, and pay on a fixed cycle.

### Feed: the number that decides profit

Feed is usually the largest cost on a dairy farm. A common rule of thumb for a crossbred cow: green fodder, some dry fodder (straw or bhusa), mineral mixture, and concentrate of about **1.5 kg a day for maintenance plus roughly 1 kg per 2.5 litres of milk**. Ask your veterinary officer for a ration for your breed.

**Assumptions for the illustration below** (prices are examples; check your local rates):

| Per cow per day | Milking cow (10 litres) | Dry cow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green fodder 25 kg at ₹2.50 | ₹62.50 | ₹62.50 |
| Dry fodder 5 kg at ₹7 | ₹35.00 | ₹35.00 |
| Concentrate at ₹28/kg | 5.5 kg = ₹154.00 | 2 kg = ₹56.00 |
| Mineral mixture | ₹5.00 | ₹5.00 |
| **Feed cost per day** | **₹256.50** | **₹158.50** |

### Worked monthly P&L for a five-cow farm (illustration)

Five crossbred cows, of which four are in milk at any time, averaging 10 litres a day each: 4 × 10 × 30 = **1,200 litres a month**. Family members do part of the work; one paid helper does the rest.

| Line | Working | Sell to a collection centre at ₹38/litre | Sell direct to households at ₹56/litre |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Milk sales | 1,200 litres × price | ₹45,600 | ₹67,200 |
| Dung sold as manure | | ₹1,500 | ₹1,500 |
| **Income** | | **₹47,100** | **₹68,700** |
| Feed, 4 milking cows | 4 × ₹256.50 × 30 | ₹30,780 | ₹30,780 |
| Feed, 1 dry cow | ₹158.50 × 30 | ₹4,755 | ₹4,755 |
| Helper | | ₹8,000 | ₹8,000 |
| Vet, medicines, insemination | | ₹2,000 | ₹2,000 |
| Electricity and water | | ₹1,500 | ₹1,500 |
| Insurance (₹15,000 a year) | | ₹1,250 | ₹1,250 |
| Cans, transport, sundries | | ₹1,500 | ₹1,500 |
| **Costs** | | **₹49,785** | **₹49,785** |
| **Monthly result** | | **−₹2,685** | **₹18,915** |

What the table shows:

- **Cost per litre is about ₹41.49** (₹49,785 ÷ 1,200), of which feed is ₹29.61. A study in an ICAR journal found a similar ₹42 a litre for crossbred cows in the Meerut region.
- **The selling channel decides the result.** At a collection-centre price, this unit roughly breaks even before the owner's own labour. Selling direct to 40 or 50 households turns the same milk into a profit. Collection centres pay by fat and SNF, so better feed and buffalo milk earn more per litre there.
- **To improve it:** grow your own green fodder, buy concentrate in bulk, keep calving intervals short, cull poor yielders, and sell some milk as curd or paneer.
- **Not included:** loan EMIs, calf sales, replacing cows, and the owner's wage.

## Step 5: GST on milk and dairy products

From **22 September 2025**, most dairy products are either nil-rated or at 5%:

| Product | GST before | GST from 22 Sept 2025 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fresh milk and pasteurised milk (not UHT) | Nil | **Nil** |
| UHT milk | 5% | **Nil** |
| Paneer and chhena, pre-packaged and labelled | 5% | **Nil** (loose was already nil) |
| Butter, ghee, dairy spreads | 12% | **5%** |
| Cheese | 12% | **5%** |
| Condensed milk | 12% | **5%** |
| Milk-based drinks and flavoured milk | 12% | **5%** |
| Ice cream | 18% | **5%** |
| Curd, lassi, buttermilk | Nil loose; 5% pre-packaged and labelled since July 2022 | Loose stays nil; confirm the packaged rate with your CA |

What this means for registration and billing:

- **A business that sells only exempt goods**, such as fresh milk, loose curd and loose paneer, **does not have to register** for GST, however large its turnover.
- **Once you sell taxable items** such as ghee, butter or ice cream, the normal [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) applies, and **your exempt milk sales count towards it**. A parlour with ₹36 lakh of milk sales and ₹6 lakh of ghee is over the ₹40 lakh goods limit.
- **Ice cream is an excluded item** for the ₹40 lakh limit, so a parlour that sells ice cream works to the **₹20 lakh** limit.
- **A registered shop selling only exempt items** issues a [bill of supply](https://billzap.app/blog/bill-of-supply), not a tax invoice.
- **Income from a dairy farm** is generally treated as business income, not tax-free agricultural income. Ask your CA about the presumptive scheme for small businesses.

## Step 6: Loans and government schemes

| Scheme | What it offers | Fit for a small unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kisan Credit Card (animal husbandry)** | Credit for feed and running costs; collateral-free agricultural loans, including allied activities, up to **₹2 lakh** from 1 January 2025 (RBI) | Good for small farms; ask your bank |
| **Animal Husbandry Infrastructure Development Fund (AHIDF)** | **3% interest subvention** on loans for dairy processing, value addition and feed plants | For processing units, not a few cows. Temporarily extended beyond March 2026 pending review; check with your bank |
| **National Livestock Mission (entrepreneurship)** | 50% capital subsidy, through SIDBI | Mainly poultry, sheep, goat, pig and fodder units, not a cow dairy; check current guidelines |
| **Rashtriya Gokul Mission (breed multiplication farms)** | Up to 50% capital subsidy, capped at ₹2 crore | Needs a farm of about 200 milch animals; the application portal was closed to new applications in 2024 |
| **State animal husbandry schemes** | Subsidies for 2 to 10 animal units, cattle insurance, fodder | Often the best fit; ask your district animal husbandry office |
| **Mudra loan** | Loans for a parlour's equipment and working capital | See our [Mudra loan](https://billzap.app/blog/mudra-loan) guide |

NABARD's older **Dairy Entrepreneurship Development Scheme** is still quoted widely; check with your bank that it is open before counting on it.

## Step 7: Cold chain and hygiene

Bacteria in raw milk multiply fast in warm weather. A few habits protect your milk and your customers:

- **Chill milk quickly**, to around 4°C, soon after milking or collection. Collection points use bulk milk coolers; a parlour needs a chiller big enough for a full day's milk.
- **Use stainless steel cans**, washed with hot water and detergent after every use.
- **Keep products cold:** curd, paneer and butter in a visi-cooler, ice cream in a deep freezer.
- **Plan for power cuts.** An inverter for the chiller pays for itself the first time it saves a day's milk.
- **First in, first out**, and check expiry on packed products every morning.
- **On the farm:** clean udders before milking, keep the shed dry, and vaccinate on schedule.

## FAQ

### How much money is needed to start a dairy business?

By our estimate, about ₹2.7 lakh to ₹6.8 lakh for a milk parlour, and about ₹5.5 lakh to ₹9.5 lakh for a five-cow farm, excluding land. Cattle and the shed are the biggest costs on a farm; refrigeration and working capital in a parlour.

### Is a dairy business profitable?

It can be, but margins on liquid milk are thin. Feed is the largest cost on a farm, and selling directly to households or as curd and paneer earns far more than a collection centre pays. Work out your cost per litre before buying animals.

### Do I need an FSSAI licence to sell milk?

Yes. FSSAI's March 2026 advisory asks states to ensure milk vendors and milk producers outside dairy cooperatives hold registration or a licence. Most small units need only basic registration, which costs ₹100 a year.

### Is there GST on milk?

No. Fresh and pasteurised milk are exempt, and UHT milk has been nil since 22 September 2025. Pre-packaged paneer is nil, while butter, ghee, cheese and ice cream are taxed at 5%.

### Is there a government subsidy for a dairy farm?

Central schemes mostly target processing units or large breed-multiplication farms. For a small farm, the Kisan Credit Card and your state's animal husbandry schemes are usually the practical options; ask your district animal husbandry office what is open this year.

## The bottom line

The dairy business rewards people who control two things: the cost of each litre and the price they get for it. Start with a parlour if you are new to animals, register with FSSAI, keep milk cold from the first hour, know which products carry GST, and work out your cost per litre before you expand. A small dairy that sells to its own customers and collects every month's dues can pay its way.

*This guide is general information, not financial, legal or tax advice. Prices and scheme terms vary by state and change often; confirm with your bank, veterinary officer and CA before you invest.*
