A GST notice usually arrives as an email saying "a notice has been issued" and nothing else. You log in to the GST portal, find a form code like ASMT-10 or REG-17, and a deadline that may be as short as seven working days. Some you can close yourself in ten minutes; others need a professional.

This guide covers the notices a small shop is most likely to get: what each means, the reply form, the deadline, the documents that settle it, and how to file the reply on the portal.

## Quick answer

- **Find the form code** in the notice. It tells you what the officer wants and which reply form to use.
- **Common deadlines:** REG-03, REG-17 and REG-23: **7 working days**. GSTR-3A: **15 days**. ASMT-10: **30 days**. DRC-01B and DRC-01C: **7 days**. DRC-01 show cause notice: the date in the notice, often 30 days.
- **Reply on the portal:** Services > User Services > View Notices and Orders (or View Additional Notices/Orders for scrutiny and demand notices), open the notice, file the reply in the named form, attach documents, sign with DSC or EVC.
- **Demand periods:** sections **73** (no fraud) and **74** (fraud) cover tax up to **2023-24**. From **2024-25**, both are replaced by **section 74A**.
- **Pay early to cut penalty:** in a non-fraud case, paying tax and interest within 30 days of a section 73 notice, or 60 days of a section 74A notice, means no penalty.
- **Section 128A waiver** for 2017-18 to 2019-20 demands has **closed** for most people (pay by 31 March 2025, apply by 30 June 2025).

## GST notice types: what each one means and how to reply

The time limit runs from the date of the notice unless it says otherwise.

| Notice | What it means | Reply form | Time to reply |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **REG-03** | The officer needs clarification or documents on your new registration application | REG-04 | 7 working days |
| **REG-17** | Show cause why your GST registration should not be cancelled (usually returns not filed for months) | REG-18 | 7 working days |
| **REG-23** | Show cause why your application to revoke a cancellation should not be rejected | REG-24 | 7 working days |
| **GSTR-3A** | You have not filed a return; file it | File the pending return | 15 days |
| **CMP-05** | The officer doubts you are eligible for the composition scheme | CMP-06 | 15 days |
| **ASMT-10** | Scrutiny of your returns found discrepancies (section 61) | ASMT-11 | 30 days, or longer if the officer allows |
| **DRC-01B** | Tax in GSTR-1 is higher than tax paid in GSTR-3B | Part B of DRC-01B | 7 days |
| **DRC-01C** | ITC claimed in GSTR-3B is more than your GSTR-2B shows | Part B of DRC-01C | 7 days |
| **DRC-01A** | Intimation of tax the officer has worked out, before a formal notice | Part B of DRC-01A, or pay in DRC-03 | Date in the intimation |
| **DRC-01** | Summary of a show cause notice demanding tax, interest and penalty | DRC-06 | Date in the notice, often 30 days |

### Registration notices: REG-03, REG-17, REG-23

**REG-03** comes while your registration application is pending, usually asking for a clearer rent agreement, electricity bill or owner's NOC. Upload exactly what is asked in REG-04 within seven working days, or the application is rejected. Our guide to the [GST registration process](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-process) lists the documents that avoid this.

**REG-17** is the serious one, most often issued when returns have not been filed for months. Your registration is usually **suspended from the date of the notice**, so do not issue tax invoices until it is restored. The best reply is compliance: **file every pending return with late fee and interest**, then reply in REG-18 listing the returns filed with their ARNs. If it is already cancelled, file the pending returns and apply for revocation in REG-21 within 90 days of the order. If you want to close the business anyway, see how to [cancel GST registration](https://billzap.app/blog/cancel-gst-registration).

**REG-23** means the officer is not satisfied with your revocation application. Reply in REG-24 within seven working days, with proof that returns are filed and dues paid.

### GSTR-3A: return not filed

Under CBIC Circular 129/48/2019-GST, GSTR-3A follows a missed return and gives you **15 days** to file. Filing before an assessment order is passed withdraws the notice. If you do not file, the officer can assess your tax to the best of his judgement (section 62) in ASMT-13, without further notice. No sales? File a nil return; it takes minutes.

### ASMT-10: the GST scrutiny notice

ASMT-10 is issued under section 61 after an officer compares your returns with each other and with third-party data. Usual findings:

- Tax in [GSTR-1](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-file-gstr-1) is more than tax paid in [GSTR-3B](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-3b).
- ITC claimed in GSTR-3B is more than your GSTR-2B or GSTR-2A shows.
- ITC claimed on bills from suppliers whose registration was later cancelled.
- Turnover not matching e-way bills or your income-tax return, or RCM not paid on rent.

Reply in **ASMT-11** within 30 days. For each point, either show with documents that nothing is short, or pay the tax with interest through DRC-03 and quote the payment. If the officer accepts, the case closes in **ASMT-12**; if not, a show cause notice under section 73, 74 or 74A follows.

### DRC-01A and DRC-01: the demand notices

**DRC-01A** is an optional pre-notice intimation of the tax the officer has worked out, with a chance to pay or object in Part B. It is your cheapest exit: if the demand is right, paying now avoids the show cause notice and, in non-fraud cases, the penalty.

**DRC-01** summarises a formal show cause notice; read the full notice behind it. Reply in **DRC-06** by the date given and ask in writing for a **personal hearing**. The order (DRC-07) cannot go beyond the amounts and grounds in the notice.

### CMP-05: composition eligibility

If the officer thinks you crossed the turnover limit or made inter-state sales, you get CMP-05. Reply in CMP-06 within 15 days with turnover figures and bills; if the reply fails, you leave the scheme and may owe tax at normal rates. The rules are in our [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide.

## Sections 73, 74 and the new 74A: which one applies

The section depends on the **financial year of the tax**, not the date of the notice. Section 74A was inserted by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 and applies from **FY 2024-25 onwards**. Sections 73 and 74 continue for periods up to 2023-24.

| | Section 73 (to 2023-24, no fraud) | Section 74 (to 2023-24, fraud or suppression) | Section 74A (2024-25 onward) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Time limit for the order | 3 years from the due date of that year's annual return | 5 years from that due date | Notice within 42 months of the annual return due date; order within 12 months of the notice (extendable by 6 months) |
| Penalty in the order | 10% of tax or ₹10,000, whichever is higher | Equal to the tax | No fraud: 10% or ₹10,000. Fraud: equal to the tax |
| Pay tax and interest within 30 days of notice (60 days under 74A) | No penalty | 25% penalty | No fraud: no penalty. Fraud: 25% |

Interest at 18% a year under section 50 is payable in every case. Under section 74A, no notice is issued if the tax involved for the year is under ₹1,000.

### Section 128A waiver: is it still open?

Section 128A waived interest and penalty on **section 73 demands for 2017-18, 2018-19 and 2019-20**, if the full tax was paid by **31 March 2025** and the application (SPL-01 or SPL-02) was filed by **30 June 2025**. That window is closed for most people. The one live route: a section 74 case that an appellate authority, tribunal or court directs to be redetermined under section 73, which gets six months from that order to pay and apply in SPL-02 (CBIC Circular 238/32/2024-GST).

## How to reply to a GST notice on the portal

1. **Check it is genuine.** A real notice shows in your gst.gov.in login, and central (CBIC) communications carry a DIN you can verify on the CBIC website. Ignore any "notice" asking you to pay into a bank account.
2. **Open the notice.** Go to Services > User Services > View Notices and Orders. Scrutiny and demand notices sit under View Additional Notices/Orders, in the case folder.
3. **Download** the notice and every annexure, and put the deadline in your phone calendar.
4. **Reconcile** each figure with your bills, returns and ledgers (see the worked example below).
5. **Pay what is genuinely short** through Form DRC-03 and note the ARN.
6. **File** the reply in the form named in the notice, attach PDFs, opt for a hearing if you want one, and submit with DSC or EVC.
7. **Download the acknowledgement** and keep it with the notice.

If you need more time, ask the officer for an adjournment before the deadline, not after.

## Worked example: replying to an ASMT-10 for a mismatch

Ravi, a GST-registered hardware shop owner, gets an ASMT-10 for FY 2024-25: GSTR-1 shows **₹3,24,000** of output tax for the year, GSTR-3B only **₹3,06,000**. Difference: **₹18,000**. Checking month by month, he finds the gap in March 2025.

| Month | Tax in GSTR-1 | Tax paid in GSTR-3B | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| March 2025 | ₹34,000 | ₹16,000 | ₹18,000 |
| April 2025 | ₹22,000 | ₹40,000 | −₹18,000 |

Two bills dated 31 March 2025 for ₹50,000 each at 18% (₹9,000 tax each, ₹18,000 in all) went into March GSTR-1 but were paid in April GSTR-3B, filed on 20 May 2025.

- **Tax:** already paid, one month late. Not short.
- **Interest:** 18% a year on ₹18,000 for the 30 days from 20 April to 20 May: ₹18,000 × 18% × 30 ÷ 365 = **₹266** (rounded).
- **Reply:** in ASMT-11, explain the timing difference, attach both invoices, the March GSTR-1, the April GSTR-3B and the DRC-03 receipt for ₹266 interest, and ask the officer to drop the matter.

The officer can close it in ASMT-12. Left unanswered, it would have become a section 74A demand for ₹18,000 plus interest plus a penalty of ₹10,000, since 10% of ₹18,000 is less than the ₹10,000 minimum.

## Documents to keep ready

| Notice is about | Keep ready |
| --- | --- |
| Registration (REG-03) | Rent agreement or ownership proof, electricity bill, NOC from owner, PAN, Aadhaar, shop photos |
| Cancellation (REG-17, REG-23) | ARNs of all returns filed, challans for tax, late fee and interest |
| Output tax mismatch | Sales bills for the period, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, any [credit notes](https://billzap.app/blog/credit-note-in-gst) issued |
| ITC mismatch | Purchase invoices, GSTR-2B for each month, proof of payment to suppliers, supplier GSTIN status |
| Turnover mismatch | E-way bills, bank statements, income-tax return, a reconciliation sheet |

## Reply format: a structure you can follow

A structure, not legal advice. For large amounts or any fraud allegation, have a CA or advocate draft it.

1. **Heading:** To the proper officer, with the office name.
2. **Reference:** your GSTIN, legal name, notice form and reference number, date, and the period.
3. **Background:** one short paragraph on your business and returns filed for the period.
4. **Point-wise reply:** quote each discrepancy and answer it directly, with the figure, the reason, and the document number that proves it.
5. **Reconciliation table:** month-wise figures as in the example above.
6. **Payments made:** DRC-03 ARN, date and amount for anything admitted.
7. **Request:** that the proceedings be dropped, and a personal hearing before any adverse order.
8. **Enclosures:** a numbered list matching your attachments.
9. **Signature:** name, designation (proprietor or partner), date and place.

## When to reply yourself and when to get a CA

| You can usually handle it | Get a CA or advocate |
| --- | --- |
| GSTR-3A: just file the return | Any DRC-01 show cause notice |
| REG-03: upload the asked document | Section 74 or 74A notice alleging fraud or suppression |
| REG-17 where you only missed returns | Demand above what you can comfortably pay |
| DRC-01B or DRC-01C with a simple timing difference | ITC denied because a supplier was found fake |
| ASMT-10 with one clear, small mismatch | Any order you want to appeal |

Appeals against an order go to the Appellate Authority within three months, after paying the admitted amount and 10% of the disputed tax.

## Why most small-shop notices happen, and how to avoid them

Most small-shop notices come from numbers that do not agree, not fraud:

- **Bills raised but missed in GSTR-1, or reported in a different month** from GSTR-3B. GSTN has been locking the auto-filled tax in GSTR-3B to what GSTR-1 says, with corrections through GSTR-1A, so these gaps now show up faster.
- **ITC claimed before the supplier's bill shows in GSTR-2B.**

## FAQ

### What happens if I do not reply to a GST notice?

The officer decides on what he has: usually an order for the full tax, interest and penalty, then recovery. For REG-17, your registration is cancelled.

### How many days do I get to reply to a GST notice?

7 working days for REG-03, REG-17 and REG-23; 7 days for DRC-01B and DRC-01C; 15 days for GSTR-3A and CMP-05; 30 days for ASMT-10; and the date in a DRC-01 notice.

### How do I reply to an ASMT-10 notice?

File Form ASMT-11 on the portal within 30 days. Answer each discrepancy with figures and documents, pay any admitted shortfall with interest through DRC-03, and quote the payment. If the officer accepts, the case closes in ASMT-12.

### What is the difference between DRC-01A and DRC-01?

DRC-01A is an optional intimation before a formal notice, giving you a chance to pay or object. DRC-01 is the summary of the formal show cause notice, replied to in DRC-06.

### Which section applies to a GST notice for 2024-25?

Section 74A. It replaced sections 73 and 74 for tax from FY 2024-25 onwards. Older years stay under 73 or 74.

### Can I still use the Section 128A GST waiver?

Generally no. Tax had to be paid by 31 March 2025 and the application filed by 30 June 2025. Only section 74 cases redetermined under section 73 get a fresh six-month window.

### Is a GST notice on email or WhatsApp genuine?

Check your GST portal login: genuine notices appear there. Never pay into an account given in a message.

## The bottom line

Read the form code, note the deadline, and log in the day the notice arrives. Registration and non-filing notices close when you do the pending work. Scrutiny notices close with a clear reconciliation and any genuine shortfall paid with interest. Show cause notices deserve a professional, and paying a correct non-fraud demand within the 30 or 60-day window removes the penalty.
