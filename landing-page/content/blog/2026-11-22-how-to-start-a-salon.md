Most guides on starting a salon read like a franchise brochure: "choose a concept, find a location, hire great stylists". They rarely tell you what a haircut actually costs you to deliver, how much GST you charge since the September 2025 rate change, or why your shop rent can quietly become more expensive once you register.

This guide covers those parts in order: the money you need, the licences, GST, staff pay, how to price a service, what a month of profit looks like, and the habits that bring customers back. It works for a two-chair neighbourhood parlour and for a five-chair unisex salon.

## Quick answer

- **Investment:** roughly **₹4 lakh to ₹12 lakh** for a small 2 to 4 chair salon in a rented shop, most of it interiors, equipment, deposit and working capital. A rough planning figure, not a survey number.
- **Licences:** there is **no single "beauty parlour licence"** in India. You usually need **shop act registration**, a **municipal trade or health licence**, and **GST registration** once turnover crosses **₹20 lakh** (₹10 lakh in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura). Udyam is optional.
- **GST on salon services is 5% without input tax credit** (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) since **22 September 2025** (Notification 15/2025-Central Tax (Rate)). It was 18% before. SAC codes sit under group **99972**, such as **999721** for hairdressing and barbers.
- **Products you sell over the counter** carry their own rates: shampoo and hair oil are 5%, many make-up and skincare items are 18%.
- **Staff pay:** most salons use a **fixed salary plus commission**; some rent chairs to stylists.
- **Price each service from cost:** products used + stylist time + a share of overheads, then add your margin and 5% GST.

## Step 1: Pick the format and the location

Decide early what kind of salon you are opening, because it drives the fit-out, staff and prices.

| Format | Who it serves | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Men's salon / barber** | Haircuts, shaves, beard grooming, basic colour | Fewer, faster services; 2 to 3 chairs; high footfall location |
| **Ladies' beauty parlour** | Threading, waxing, facials, hair, bridal | Private cabins for waxing and facials, more product stock |
| **Unisex salon** | Families, couples, younger customers | Separate or screened sections, staff of both genders, wider service menu |
| **Home-based or doorstep** | Regular clients, bridal work | Low rent; kit and travel; good for testing demand |

A **unisex salon** can get more out of the same space because families and couples book together, but it needs more room and staff. If unsure, start with one side and add the other once you have regulars.

For location, pick steady local footfall over a famous road: residential colonies, near schools, gyms and markets, with easy two-wheeler parking. A salon lives on repeat visits, so people within a short ride matter more than passers-by.

## Step 2: Budget the investment

The figures below are a **rough planning estimate** for a 3-chair salon in a rented shop of about 300 to 400 sq ft in a mid-sized town. Equipment prices are typical ranges from online listings (hydraulic salon chairs roughly ₹6,000 to ₹15,000 each, shampoo stations roughly ₹6,000 to ₹18,000). Metro rents, premium brands and spa rooms push every line up.

| Item | Rough range |
| --- | --- |
| Rent deposit (often 3 to 6 months' rent) | ₹60,000 – ₹2,00,000 |
| Interiors: flooring, lighting, mirrors, stations, plumbing, signboard | ₹1,00,000 – ₹3,50,000 |
| 3 hydraulic chairs + 1 shampoo station | ₹25,000 – ₹65,000 |
| Dryers, straighteners, trimmers, steamer, sterilizer, trolleys, tools | ₹40,000 – ₹1,00,000 |
| AC, water heater, inverter | ₹50,000 – ₹1,00,000 |
| Opening product stock (for services and retail shelf) | ₹30,000 – ₹80,000 |
| Licences, CA fees, launch marketing | ₹10,000 – ₹30,000 |
| Working capital: 3 months of rent, salaries and bills | ₹1,00,000 – ₹2,50,000 |
| **Total** | **about ₹4 lakh – ₹12 lakh** |

Do not skip the working capital line. Chairs take months to fill; salaries are due from month one.

## Step 3: Get the licences and registrations

| Registration | Do you need it? | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shop and establishment registration** | Yes, in almost every state, once you have premises or staff | State labour department portal; see [shop act registration](https://billzap.app/blog/shop-act-registration) |
| **Trade licence / health licence** | Usually yes; many municipal bodies list salons and beauty parlours as a licensed trade | Your municipal corporation or council; see [trade licence](https://billzap.app/blog/trade-license) |
| **GST registration** | Compulsory once turnover crosses **₹20 lakh** a year (₹10 lakh in the four north-eastern states above) | GST portal |
| **Udyam registration** | Optional, free; helps with MSME loans and schemes | Udyam portal |
| **Fire NOC** | Only where your state or building rules require it, usually for larger premises | State fire department |

A salon is a **service business**, so the higher ₹40 lakh limit for goods does **not** apply, even if you also sell shampoo and oil. Services and goods together are tested against **₹20 lakh**. See the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) guide. Rules and fees vary by state and city, so confirm them before you sign the lease.

## Step 4: Understand GST on salon services

### 5% without input tax credit, since 22 September 2025

Beauty and physical well-being services (group **99972**) moved from **18% to 5%** from **22 September 2025**, under **Notification 15/2025-Central Tax (Rate)** dated 17 September 2025. The 5% rate comes **without input tax credit**: you cannot claim back the GST you pay on things you use to provide the service, such as products used on clients, equipment or the AC repair bill. CBIC has said this rate is not optional; a salon cannot choose 18% with credit instead. Many pages still quote 18%.

| SAC | Description | GST |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 999721 | Hairdressing and barbers' services | 5% (no ITC) |
| 999722 | Cosmetic treatment, manicure and pedicure | 5% (no ITC) |
| 999723 | Physical well-being services, such as spas and fitness centres | 5% (no ITC) |
| 999729 | Other beauty treatment services | 5% (no ITC) |

See the [SAC code list](https://billzap.app/blog/sac-code-list) for related codes. Inside a state, the 5% is split as **2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST** on the bill.

### Products sold over the counter

Shampoo, conditioner or serum you sell to a customer to take home is a sale of **goods**, taxed at the product's own rate, not at 5% for services. Since 22 September 2025, hair oil, shampoo and toilet soap are 5%; many make-up and skincare products remain at 18%. Check the HSN code on your supplier's bill for each product. Input tax credit on products bought **for resale** is generally available; common costs used for both services and sales need to be split. Ask your CA how to do this.

### Your shop rent

If you are GST-registered and your landlord is not, you pay **18% GST on the rent yourself under reverse charge**. For most shops this is cost-neutral because they claim it back as credit. For a salon, credit is blocked for the service side, so most of that 18% becomes a **real cost**. On ₹30,000 a month rent, that is ₹5,400 a month. Our guide to [GST on rent](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-on-rent) has the details. Build it into your budget before you register.

### Packages and memberships

For services, GST is due when you **receive an advance**. If a customer pays ₹5,000 for a prepaid package in November, the 5% GST on it belongs in November's return, even if the services are used over the next six months.

## Step 5: Staff and commission

Stylists are the product. Most small salons in India use one of three models.

| Model | How it works | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fixed salary** | Monthly pay regardless of sales | Trainees, helpers, a salon still building clientele |
| **Salary + commission** | A lower fixed salary plus a percentage of the services they do, often above a monthly target | Most salons; rewards busy stylists without risking the owner |
| **Chair rental** | A senior stylist pays you a fixed rent for a chair and keeps their own takings | Experienced stylists with their own clients |

**Example of a target-based commission:** a stylist earns ₹15,000 fixed plus 10% of services billed above ₹40,000 a month. If she bills ₹70,000 of services, the commission is (₹70,000 − ₹40,000) × 10% = **₹3,000**, and her pay is **₹18,000**.

Whatever the model, record **which stylist did each service**, so commission is never a matter of memory or argument.

## Step 6: Price a service from its cost

Copying the salon next door is how many parlours end up busy and broke. Price at least your main services from cost. Here is a **hair spa** taking about 45 minutes.

**Your assumptions (example figures):**
- Products used per hair spa: **₹120** (including the GST you paid, since you cannot claim it back)
- Stylist salary ₹18,000 a month; about 125 booked hours a month (26 days × 8 hours, with roughly 60% of the time with clients) → **₹144 per booked hour**
- Fixed overheads: rent ₹30,000 + electricity ₹6,000 + other ₹9,000 = **₹45,000 a month**, spread over 3 chairs × 125 booked hours = 375 hours → **₹120 per booked hour**

| Cost line | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Products | | ₹120 |
| Stylist time | ₹144 × 0.75 hour | ₹108 |
| Overheads | ₹120 × 0.75 hour | ₹90 |
| **Cost per hair spa** | | **₹318** |
| Your price before GST | | ₹600 |
| Profit per service | ₹600 − ₹318 | ₹282 (47% of price) |
| GST 5% (CGST ₹15 + SGST ₹15) | ₹600 × 5% | ₹30 |
| **Customer pays** | | **₹630** |

If you prefer menu prices that **include GST**, divide by 1.05: a menu price of ₹600 means ₹571.43 for the service and ₹28.57 GST. Before you register for GST you charge no GST at all, and your bill must not show any.

The same sum exposes thin services: a 30-minute haircut costs about ₹72 in stylist time and ₹60 in overheads before any product, so a ₹150 price leaves almost nothing.

## Step 7: What a month of profit looks like

An example month for the same 3-chair salon, after it has built up regular clients. All amounts exclude GST collected, which is not your income.

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Services billed | ₹2,40,000 |
| Product sales | ₹20,000 |
| **Total sales** | **₹2,60,000** |
| Salaries: 3 stylists × ₹18,000 + 1 helper ₹10,000 | ₹64,000 |
| Commissions (example: 10% of services) | ₹24,000 |
| Products used in services (8% of services) | ₹19,200 |
| Cost of products sold | ₹14,000 |
| Rent | ₹30,000 |
| RCM on rent, largely a cost for a salon | ₹5,400 |
| Electricity | ₹6,000 |
| Towels, laundry, disposables, internet, marketing | ₹9,000 |
| **Total costs** | **₹1,71,600** |
| **Profit before owner's pay, loan EMI and income tax** | **₹88,400 (34% of sales)** |

Yearly sales here are about ₹31 lakh, so this salon must be GST-registered. And your **salon profit margin** moves mainly with **chair utilisation**: the same rent and salaries spread over more booked hours.

## Step 8: Memberships, packages and repeat visits

Repeat customers are the whole business:

- **Prepaid packages:** five hair spas for the price of four. Cash upfront, and the client returns. Record sessions used.
- **Memberships:** a yearly fee for a flat discount. Check the discount against your cost sheet first.
- **Weekday offers:** a discount on slow afternoons only fills empty chairs without cutting weekend prices.

Remind clients when they are due for a haircut, root touch-up or facial, by WhatsApp, if they have agreed to hear from you.

## Step 9: Hygiene and safety

Clients notice hygiene before skill, and one infection or burn can cost you your reputation in the colony.

- **Fresh towel and neck strip** for every client; disinfect combs and brushes, and sterilise metal tools, between clients.
- **Single-use blades**, opened in front of the client, and disposable wax spatulas: never double-dip.
- **Patch tests** before colour and chemical treatments; ventilation and gloves for chemical work.
- **Sharps** go in a closed container, not the open dustbin.

## FAQ

### How much investment is needed to start a salon in India?

Roughly ₹4 lakh to ₹12 lakh for a small 2 to 4 chair salon in a rented shop, including deposit, interiors, equipment, opening stock and three months of working capital. A home-based setup can start far lower; a premium metro salon can cost several times more.

### Which licence is required for a beauty parlour?

There is no single national beauty parlour licence. You usually need shop act registration, a trade or health licence from your municipal body, and GST registration once turnover crosses ₹20 lakh. Check your state and city portals for the exact list.

### What is the GST rate on salon services?

5% without input tax credit (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) since 22 September 2025, under Notification 15/2025-Central Tax (Rate). It was 18% earlier. Products sold to customers carry their own GST rates.

### Is GST registration compulsory for a salon?

Only once your turnover crosses ₹20 lakh in a financial year (₹10 lakh in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura). The ₹40 lakh goods limit does not apply to salons, even if you sell products too.

### What is a good profit margin for a salon?

It depends mostly on how many of your chair hours are booked. In our example, a 3-chair salon with ₹2.6 lakh monthly sales keeps about 34% before the owner's pay, loan EMI and income tax. Work out your own from a cost sheet rather than relying on industry averages.

### How do salons pay stylists?

Most use a fixed salary plus commission on services, often above a monthly target. Trainees usually get a fixed salary, and some senior stylists rent a chair and keep their own takings.

## The bottom line

A salon is a time business: you sell stylist hours from a fixed number of chairs. Get the licences sorted before you open, register for GST once you cross ₹20 lakh and charge 5% on services, and price your main services from cost so every booking pays its share of rent and salaries. Then spend your effort on the thing that decides profit, filling empty chairs with clients who come back.

*This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. Licence rules and fees vary by state and city, and GST rates change by notification; confirm your case with your CA and local authority.*
