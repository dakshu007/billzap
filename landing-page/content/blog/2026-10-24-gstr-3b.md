It is the 19th of the month. The shop is busy, the accountant has sent a WhatsApp message that says only "3B?", and somewhere in a drawer are last month's purchase bills. Tomorrow is the 20th, and the 20th is the day GST actually has to be paid. That return is **GSTR-3B**, and for most small businesses it is the one deadline that costs money every time it is missed.

This guide explains GSTR-3B in plain language: who files it, the due dates for monthly and quarterly filers (including which states file on the 22nd and which on the 24th), the late fee as it actually applies, how interest is worked out, what each table asks for, and the steps on the GST portal. It ends with the mistakes that cause notices and a short routine that keeps the 20th from being a crisis.

## Quick answer

**GSTR-3B** is the summary return in which you **declare your sales, claim input tax credit and pay the GST** for the period.

- **Who:** every regular GST taxpayer, including those with nothing to report, who file a nil return. Composition dealers do not file it.
- **Monthly due date:** the **20th of the next month**.
- **Quarterly (QRMP) due date:** the **22nd or 24th** of the month after the quarter, depending on your state.
- **Late fee:** **₹50 a day** (₹20 a day for a nil return), capped at **₹2,000** for turnover up to ₹1.5 crore, ₹5,000 up to ₹5 crore and ₹10,000 above that. A nil return is capped at ₹500.
- **Interest:** **18% a year** on tax paid late, counted on the cash you pay after credit.
- **How:** gst.gov.in → Returns Dashboard → GSTR-3B → Prepare Online → check the auto-filled figures → pay → file with **EVC (OTP)** or **DSC**.

## What is GSTR-3B?

GST has two regular returns for a normal taxpayer, and they do different jobs.

**GSTR-1** lists what you sold, invoice by invoice for business buyers. It reports, but it does not pay anything. **GSTR-3B** is where the money moves: you state the totals, set off the input tax credit you are entitled to, and pay the balance in cash.

That makes GSTR-3B the return the tax department watches most closely. It is the one that decides whether the tax collected from your customers actually reached the government.

**Takeaway:** GSTR-1 tells the government what you sold. GSTR-3B pays for it.

## Who has to file GSTR-3B

Every person registered as a **normal taxpayer** files GSTR-3B for every period, whether or not there were sales. A month with no sales, no purchases and no tax still needs a **nil return**.

These registrations do not file GSTR-3B:

- **Composition dealers**, who pay through CMP-08 every quarter and file GSTR-4 once a year. Our guide to the [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) explains how that works.
- Input service distributors, non-resident taxable persons, TDS deductors, TCS collectors and suppliers of online services from outside India, each of whom has a separate return.

**Takeaway:** if you are a regular GST taxpayer, GSTR-3B is due every period, even an empty one.

## GSTR-3B due dates in 2026

You file either monthly or quarterly. Quarterly filing is available under the **QRMP scheme** (Quarterly Return Monthly Payment) to businesses with aggregate turnover up to **₹5 crore** in the previous financial year.

| Filer type | Return period | Due date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly | Each month | 20th of the next month |
| QRMP, Category X states | Each quarter | 22nd of the month after the quarter |
| QRMP, Category Y states | Each quarter | 24th of the month after the quarter |

### Which states file on the 22nd and which on the 24th?

The quarterly date depends on where your business is registered.

| Group | Due date | States and union territories |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Category X** | **22nd** | Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Goa, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh; UTs of Daman and Diu and Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Puducherry, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Lakshadweep |
| **Category Y** | **24th** | Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Haryana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, Assam, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Odisha; UTs of Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, Chandigarh, Delhi |

So a quarterly filer in Tamil Nadu files the July to September return by **22 October**, while one in Uttar Pradesh has until **24 October**.

Dates are sometimes extended by notification. The return for March 2026, for example, was extended from 20 April to 21 April 2026. The Returns Dashboard always shows the current date for your period, so check it there rather than relying on a calendar.

### Paying monthly when you file quarterly

Filing quarterly does not mean paying quarterly. For the **first two months** of each quarter, a QRMP taxpayer pays tax by the **25th of the next month** using challan **PMT-06**, by one of two methods:

- **Fixed sum:** pay 35% of the tax you paid in cash for the last quarter. The portal pre-fills the amount.
- **Self-assessment:** work out the actual tax for the month, after credit, and pay that.

The third month's tax is paid with the quarterly GSTR-3B itself.

**Takeaway:** monthly filers pay by the 20th; quarterly filers file by the 22nd or 24th but still pay something by the 25th of each of the first two months.

## The GSTR-3B late fee, as it actually applies

Search results disagree about the GSTR-3B late fee. Some say ₹200 a day, others give a single cap of ₹5,000. Both numbers are real; they just describe different layers of the law.

The CGST Act sets the late fee at **₹100 a day under CGST**, and the state Acts match it, which is ₹200 a day in total. Government notifications then **reduced** the fee and **capped** it by turnover. The reduced figures are what you actually pay:

| Your situation | Late fee per day | Maximum per return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nil return | ₹20 (₹10 CGST + ₹10 SGST) | ₹500 |
| Turnover up to ₹1.5 crore | ₹50 (₹25 + ₹25) | ₹2,000 |
| Turnover ₹1.5 crore to ₹5 crore | ₹50 (₹25 + ₹25) | ₹5,000 |
| Turnover above ₹5 crore | ₹50 (₹25 + ₹25) | ₹10,000 |

Turnover here is the aggregate turnover of the previous financial year.

The late fee is charged per return and is added automatically to your next GSTR-3B in Table 5.1. It cannot be paid from input tax credit; it is always paid in cash.

A shop with ₹80 lakh turnover that files a monthly return 12 days late pays 12 × ₹50 = **₹600** in late fee. If it files 60 days late, the fee would be ₹3,000 at the daily rate, but the cap for its turnover holds it at **₹2,000**.

**Takeaway:** ₹50 a day is the working rate; the ₹200 figure is the law before the reduction, and the cap depends on your turnover.

## Interest on late payment

The late fee is for filing late. **Interest** is for paying late, and it is separate. Under **Section 50**, tax paid after the due date carries interest at **18% a year**.

Interest is charged on the **net cash liability**: the tax you actually pay in cash after setting off input tax credit, not on your total output tax. It runs from the day after the due date to the day you pay.

### A worked example

A shop's cash payable for September is **₹40,000**, due on 20 October. It pays on 30 October, **10 days late**.

| Step | Working | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual interest on ₹40,000 | ₹40,000 × 18% | ₹7,200 |
| Interest per day | ₹7,200 ÷ 365 | ₹19.73 |
| Interest for 10 days | ₹40,000 × 18% × 10 ÷ 365 | **₹197.26** |

Add the late fee for 10 days, 10 × ₹50 = ₹500, and the cost of being 10 days late is about **₹697**, all of it paid in cash.

**Takeaway:** interest is small for a few days, but it grows with the amount and never stops until you pay.

## The GSTR-3B tables, one by one

GSTR-3B is short. Most small shops touch only three or four of its tables.

| Table | What it asks for | Who fills it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **3.1** | Outward taxable supplies (your sales), zero-rated, nil and exempt supplies, and **inward supplies on which you pay tax under reverse charge** | Everyone with sales; reverse charge if it applies |
| **3.1.1** | Supplies made through e-commerce operators that pay tax on your behalf | Sellers on platforms covered by this rule |
| **3.2** | Inter-state supplies to unregistered persons, composition dealers and UIN holders, shown state by state | Anyone selling across state lines to these buyers |
| **4** | Eligible input tax credit, auto-filled from GSTR-2B, plus reversals and ineligible credit | Everyone claiming credit |
| **5** | Exempt, nil-rated and non-GST inward supplies | Anyone who bought such items |
| **5.1** | Interest and late fee | Filled automatically if you are late |
| **6.1** | Payment of tax: how liability is set off against credit and cash | Everyone with a liability |

For a typical shop the work is in **Table 3.1** (sales), **Table 4** (credit) and **Table 6.1** (payment). The rest are often blank.

**Takeaway:** check Tables 3.1 and 4 against your own books before you pay anything in Table 6.1.

## GSTR-3B and GSTR-1: how they fit together

The two returns are linked, and the portal increasingly treats them as one system.

- **Table 3 is drafted from GSTR-1.** The sales you report in GSTR-1, or in the Invoice Furnishing Facility for quarterly filers, flow into the auto-drafted liability in GSTR-3B.
- **Table 4 is drafted from GSTR-2B.** Your input tax credit comes from what your suppliers reported in their GSTR-1. Our guide to [input tax credit](https://billzap.app/blog/input-tax-credit-gst) explains why your supplier's filing decides your claim.
- **The order matters.** File GSTR-1 first, then GSTR-3B. Under Rule 59(6), the portal can block a GSTR-1 for a period if the GSTR-3B for the previous period has not been filed, so falling behind on one return holds up the other.

Two changes from 2025 tightened this link further, and both are worth knowing:

- From the **July 2025** tax period, the auto-populated liability in Table 3 of GSTR-3B is **locked** and cannot be edited there. If GSTR-1 was wrong, it is corrected through **GSTR-1A** before GSTR-3B is filed.
- A return cannot be filed after **three years** from its due date, a rule the portal began enforcing in 2025. A long-pending return can become impossible to file at all.

So the quality of your GSTR-3B now depends heavily on getting GSTR-1 right first. Our guide on [how to file GSTR-1](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-file-gstr-1) covers that side.

**Takeaway:** fix sales errors in GSTR-1 or GSTR-1A, not in GSTR-3B.

## A worked GSTR-3B for a small shop

Take a shop in one state with this month:

- Sales to customers within the state: **₹2,00,000** taxable at 18%
- Purchases from registered suppliers, all showing in GSTR-2B: input tax of **₹12,000 CGST and ₹12,000 SGST**

| | CGST | SGST | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Output tax on ₹2,00,000 at 18% (9% + 9%) | ₹18,000 | ₹18,000 | ₹36,000 |
| Input tax credit (Table 4) | ₹12,000 | ₹12,000 | ₹24,000 |
| **Paid in cash (Table 6.1)** | **₹6,000** | **₹6,000** | **₹12,000** |

The shop reports ₹2,00,000 and ₹36,000 of tax in Table 3.1, claims ₹24,000 of credit in Table 4, and pays **₹12,000** in cash. CGST credit pays CGST and SGST credit pays SGST; neither can pay the other. Our guide to the [difference between CGST, SGST and IGST](https://billzap.app/blog/cgst-sgst-igst-difference) explains why, and the guide on [how to calculate GST](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-calculate-gst) works through the 18% arithmetic.

**Takeaway:** the cash you pay is output tax minus eligible credit, head by head.

## How to file GSTR-3B on the GST portal, step by step

1. **Log in** at gst.gov.in with your username and password.
2. Go to **Services → Returns → Returns Dashboard**, choose the financial year and the period, and click **Search**.
3. Under GSTR-3B, click **Prepare Online**.
4. **Answer the questionnaire.** It asks whether you have reverse charge supplies, e-commerce supplies, exempt inward supplies and so on, and shows only the tables you need.
5. **Check the auto-filled tables.** Compare Table 3.1 with your sales register and Table 4 with GSTR-2B and your purchase bills. Use the system-generated PDF summary for a side-by-side view.
6. **Enter or confirm** any figures that are not auto-filled, such as reverse charge liability or ineligible credit, and **Save**.
7. Click **Proceed to Payment**. The portal shows your liability head by head and offsets it against your credit ledger in the required order.
8. **Pay any balance in cash** by creating a challan if your cash ledger is short, then offset it.
9. Tick the declaration, choose the authorised signatory and **file with EVC** (an OTP to the registered mobile) **or DSC**. DSC is mandatory for companies and LLPs.

Once filed, GSTR-3B cannot be revised. Corrections go into a later period's return, so the time to be careful is before step 9.

### Filing a nil GSTR-3B

A nil return applies when, for the period, you had **no outward supplies, no input tax credit to claim and no tax liability**. On the portal, answer the first question as "Yes" for a nil return and file. You can also file a nil GSTR-3B by **SMS** from your registered mobile number. A missed nil return still attracts the ₹20-a-day late fee, up to ₹500.

**Takeaway:** check before you pay; a filed GSTR-3B is final.

## Common GSTR-3B mistakes

- **Filing without matching GSTR-1.** If the sales in GSTR-3B do not agree with GSTR-1, the gap shows up in system checks and can trigger a notice.
- **Claiming credit that is not in GSTR-2B.** Credit is allowed only to the extent it appears in GSTR-2B. Claiming more is the most common reason for an ITC demand.
- **Forgetting reverse charge.** Tax on services such as goods transport by a GTA, legal services from an advocate, or other notified supplies is paid by you as the buyer, in cash, through Table 3.1(d).
- **Paying under the wrong head.** Money paid as CGST cannot simply be used for SGST. A payment under the wrong head has to be corrected through the portal's transfer or refund process.
- **Filing late, again and again.** Continued non-filing of returns is a ground on which a GST registration can be cancelled by the department, and it also blocks e-way bills and your buyers' credit.

**Takeaway:** most GSTR-3B trouble starts with a figure nobody compared against the books.

## A routine that keeps the 20th calm

- **By the 5th:** collect all purchase bills for the month and check that your suppliers' invoices are showing in the portal.
- **By the 10th:** close your sales for the month and file GSTR-1 by the 11th (monthly filers).
- **By the 15th:** open GSTR-2B, compare it with your purchase bills, and chase any supplier whose invoices are missing.
- **By the 18th:** work out the cash payable head by head and make sure the money is in the cash ledger.
- **By the 20th:** file GSTR-3B and save the filed PDF.

Quarterly filers can follow the same rhythm against the 13th for GSTR-1 and the 22nd or 24th for GSTR-3B, with PMT-06 payments by the 25th in the first two months.

## FAQ: GSTR-3B

### What is the due date for GSTR-3B?

For monthly filers, the 20th of the next month. For quarterly (QRMP) filers, the 22nd of the month after the quarter in Category X states and the 24th in Category Y states. Dates are sometimes extended by notification, so check the Returns Dashboard.

### What is the late fee for GSTR-3B?

₹50 a day (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST), or ₹20 a day for a nil return. The maximum per return is ₹500 for a nil return, ₹2,000 for turnover up to ₹1.5 crore, ₹5,000 up to ₹5 crore and ₹10,000 above ₹5 crore. The ₹200-a-day figure is the rate in the Act before it was reduced.

### How is interest on late GSTR-3B calculated?

At 18% a year on the tax paid in cash after input tax credit, from the day after the due date until payment. On ₹40,000 paid 10 days late, interest is ₹40,000 × 18% × 10 ÷ 365 = ₹197.26.

### What is the difference between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B?

GSTR-1 reports your sales invoice by invoice for business buyers and does not pay tax. GSTR-3B is a summary return in which you declare totals, claim input tax credit and pay the tax. GSTR-1 is filed first, and its figures draft Table 3 of GSTR-3B.

### Can I revise GSTR-3B after filing?

No. A filed GSTR-3B cannot be revised. Errors are corrected in a later period's return, and sales errors are better fixed through GSTR-1A before GSTR-3B is filed.

### How do I file a nil GSTR-3B?

If you had no sales, no input tax credit and no liability for the period, choose the nil return option on the portal and file with EVC or DSC, or send the nil filing SMS from your registered mobile number.

### Do composition dealers file GSTR-3B?

No. Composition dealers pay tax quarterly through CMP-08 and file an annual return in GSTR-4.

### What happens if I do not file GSTR-3B for months?

Late fees and interest build up, your GSTR-1 for later periods can be blocked, e-way bills may be blocked, and continued non-filing is a ground for cancellation of registration. A return also cannot be filed after three years from its due date.
