## Quick answer

For most single-counter kirana stores, the right tool in 2026 is a **billing app on the phone you already own**, not a desktop POS. Look for:

- **Fast billing** from a saved item list, with **decimal quantities** for loose goods (0.5 kg, 1.25 L).
- **GST invoices** with HSN codes and CGST/SGST or IGST if you are registered, and plain bills if you are not.
- A **UPI QR on the bill**, and **WhatsApp or PDF sharing**.
- **Stock alerts**, a **daily cash close** that splits cash from UPI, and a way to track **unpaid bills**.
- **Offline operation**, a **backup** you control, and the app in **your language**.
- A **GSTR-1 ready report** for your CA, and pricing you understand before the trial ends.

Choose a **desktop POS with a barcode scanner** only when most of your stock is packaged and barcoded and the queue at the counter is the real bottleneck. Choose a **cloud app with sync** only when more than one person bills on more than one device.

## What a kirana counter actually needs

A kirana store is not a supermarket with a smaller floor. The work at the counter has its own shape, and an app built for a different shop will feel wrong within a day.

**Rush hours decide everything.** Mornings and evenings bring most of the day's customers in two short bursts. A bill that takes 90 seconds to make is fine at 3 pm and a lost sale at 7:30. Speed comes from a saved item list you can search by the first few letters, saved regular customers, and as few screens as possible between "add item" and "share bill".

**Loose goods are sold by weight.** Rice, dal, sugar, oil and spices are weighed out by the kilo or the litre. The app must accept quantities like 0.25, 0.5 and 1.75 and multiply them correctly against a per-kg price. An app that only takes whole numbers forces you into workarounds on half your sales.

**Credit is a way of life.** Regular customers buy on udhaar and settle weekly or monthly. You need to know, at any moment, who owes you and since when, and you need a polite way to remind them.

**Payments are mostly UPI.** Customers expect to scan and pay. A QR printed on the bill, carrying the exact amount and pointing at your own account, removes the "how much was it again?" step and the mistyped amount.

**GST rates are mixed on one bill.** After the rate reform of 22 September 2025, most everyday groceries sit at 5% or nil, while items such as some packaged goods, cosmetics or electricals can be at 18%. One basket can carry three rates, and each line must be taxed at its own rate. If you are new to the arithmetic, our guide on [how to calculate GST](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-calculate-gst) works through mixed-rate bills line by line.

**The internet is not reliable.** Many shops sit in basements, back lanes or markets where the signal drops when it is busiest. A billing app that cannot save a bill without a connection will fail at the worst moment.

**The computer is a phone.** Few kirana owners want a PC on the counter. The phone is already there, already charged and already in your hand.

## The must-have checklist

Use this list to test any app before you commit. Install it, add five real items, and run each check with a pretend customer. Ten minutes is enough to know.

| Check | What "good" looks like | Why it matters at a kirana counter |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | A three-item bill in under 30 seconds from a saved list | Rush hours |
| Item search | Finds "toor dal" when you type "toor" | Long catalogues |
| Decimal quantities | 0.5 kg × ₹168 = ₹84.00, exactly | Loose goods |
| GST invoice | GSTIN, HSN per line, rate per line, CGST + SGST or IGST shown separately | Business customers and your returns |
| Bills without GST | A clean bill when you are not registered, or for exempt items | Small and composition shops |
| UPI QR on the bill | Your UPI ID, the exact amount, money goes straight to your bank | Faster, correct payments |
| WhatsApp and PDF | Share in two taps, no account needed on the customer's side | Customers rarely want paper |
| Stock alerts | A warning before an item runs out | Lost sales on fast movers |
| Daily cash close | Totals split by cash, UPI and other modes | Matching the drawer every night |
| Unpaid bills | A list of who owes what, oldest first, with reminders | Udhaar |
| Offline | Bills save with flight mode on | Weak signal |
| Backup | A file you can copy off the phone and restore | A lost or broken phone |
| Your language | Menus in the language you think in | Speed and fewer mistakes |
| Reports for your CA | A month-wise GST summary laid out for GSTR-1 | Return filing |
| Price | What stops working when the free tier or trial ends | No surprises in month two |

Two checks catch most bad fits. **Turn on flight mode and make a bill.** If the app refuses, it is a cloud app, and it will refuse again on a busy evening. **Bill 0.5 kg of something.** If you cannot, or the total is off by a paisa, move on.

## Paper, phone app, desktop POS or barcode POS?

There are four realistic setups for a kirana store. Each one is right for some shops.

| Setup | Upfront cost | Running cost | Speed at the counter | GST and reports | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper register and bill book | A few hundred rupees | Bill books | Fast for simple sales, slow to total | Manual; error-prone at return time | Very small shops not registered for GST |
| Billing app on your phone | Nil (your phone) | Free, or a yearly subscription | Fast with a saved item list | Automatic tax split and GSTR-1 summary | Most single-counter kirana stores |
| Desktop POS software | PC, printer, software licence | Licence or subscription, electricity, repairs | Fast with a trained operator | Strong; often with accounting | Larger stores with a dedicated billing desk |
| POS with barcode scanner | PC or POS terminal, scanner, printer | As above, plus barcode labels for loose items | Fastest for packaged goods | Strong | Mini-marts where most stock is barcoded |

A note on barcodes. Barcode scanning is wonderful when every packet already carries one. In a kirana store, a large share of sales is loose goods weighed at the counter, which have no barcode unless you print and stick labels yourself. For those shops, a well-organised item list on a phone is often as fast as a scanner, and far cheaper.

## What it really costs over a year

The sticker price is rarely the full cost. For a fair comparison, add up everything you will pay in the first year.

**Paper.** Bill books and a register are cheap, but the real cost arrives later: hours of totalling, missed udhaar, and a GST return built from handwriting. If you are registered for GST, mistakes at return time can cost more than any software.

**Phone app.** You already own the phone. Apps fall into two groups: genuinely free apps, and freemium apps whose free tier is limited, with paid plans for features such as multi-device sync, removal of the app's branding or e-way bills. Freemium plans for popular apps commonly run into a few thousand rupees a year. A Bluetooth thermal printer is optional; many customers are happy with a WhatsApp bill.

**Desktop POS.** Budget for a computer or POS terminal, a printer, a UPS for power cuts, the software licence or subscription, and someone to fix it when Windows updates at the wrong time.

**Barcode POS.** Everything in desktop POS, plus a scanner, and, for loose goods, a label printer and the daily work of labelling.

Before paying for anything, write down the one feature you would be paying for. If the honest answer is "sync for a second phone I do not have" or "e-way bills for goods I never move", a free app will do the same job.

## How to switch from a paper register in one evening

You do not need a week, or a consultant. Do this after closing time, and bill with the app from the next morning.

1. **Add your top 50 items.** Walk the shelves or use last month's bill book to pick the 50 items that make up most of your sales. Enter each with its selling price, unit (kg, litre, piece, packet), HSN code and GST rate. If you already have a list in Excel or Google Sheets, export it as CSV and import it instead. Everything else can be added the first time it is sold.
2. **Add your regular customers.** Name and phone number for your regulars and udhaar customers, plus GSTIN for any business buyers.
3. **Set up your shop.** Business name, address, state, GSTIN if registered, and your UPI ID so the QR appears on bills.
4. **Make three test bills.** One with a loose item by weight, one with two GST rates, one for a business customer in another state if you have any. Check the totals against a calculator and the tax split against our guide to the [GST invoice on mobile](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-make-gst-bill-on-mobile).
5. **Make a backup.** Save the first backup file somewhere off the phone, such as your email or a pen drive.
6. **Start the next morning.** Keep the paper bill book under the counter for the first week, just in case. Most shops stop reaching for it within days.

If you are a composition dealer, set the app to make bills without GST and check the wording your bill of supply needs; our guide to the [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) covers it.

## FAQ: Billing apps for kirana stores

### Which is the best billing app for a kirana store?

It depends on your counter. For a single-counter store billing from one phone, a free offline app such as BillZap covers GST bills, UPI QR, stock alerts and a daily cash close. If several people bill on several devices, a synced app such as Vyapar or myBillBook on a paid plan is a better fit. If most of your stock is barcoded and queues are long, consider a POS with a barcode scanner.

### Is there a free billing app for a kirana shop?

Yes. BillZap is free with no paid tier and no invoice limit, and it works offline without an account. Several other popular apps have free tiers, but keep features such as multi-device sync, branding removal or e-way bills for paying users, so check what the free version includes before you rely on it.

### Can a billing app work without internet?

Some can. BillZap stores every bill on the phone and works fully offline, and Vyapar is offline-first on its free version. Cloud apps that keep your data on their servers usually need a connection to save or sync bills. The quickest test is to turn on flight mode and try to make a bill.

### Do I need a barcode scanner for my kirana store?

Usually not. A barcode scanner helps when most of your stock is packaged and barcoded. Many kirana sales are loose goods weighed at the counter, which have no barcode, so a well-organised, searchable item list on a phone is often just as fast and much cheaper.

### Do I need GST billing software if I am not registered for GST?

You do not need GST invoices, but a billing app still helps with clean bills, stock, unpaid bills and the daily cash total. If your turnover grows towards the registration threshold, the same app can start making GST invoices once you have a GSTIN. Composition dealers issue a bill of supply instead of a tax invoice.

### How do I track udhaar in a billing app?

Most billing apps let you mark a bill as unpaid and show a list of customers who owe you, often with reminders on WhatsApp. Dedicated ledger apps such as Khatabook go further, with a running credit book per customer. Many shops use a billing app for bills and a ledger app for long-running credit accounts.

### Can I bill loose items like rice and dal by weight?

Yes, as long as the app accepts decimal quantities. Enter the price per kg once, then bill 0.5 or 1.25 kg as needed, and the app multiplies the quantity by the price and applies the item's GST rate. Test this before you commit to an app.
