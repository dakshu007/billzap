Search "small business ideas in India" and you get lists of 50 or 100 ideas: dropshipping, podcasting, 3D printing, a restaurant. Most give no cost, no licence and no sense of who the idea is for.

This guide covers **17 businesses that run from a shop, a counter or a home kitchen**, the kind you see on every main road in a small town. For each you get a rough setup cost, the licences you will need, a rough margin and who it suits, then a decision table and a checklist for the first 30 days.

## Quick answer

- **Lowest investment (under ₹2 lakh):** tea stall, tiffin service from home, fruit and vegetable cart, tailoring, electrical and mobile repair, home tuition or a small coaching batch.
- **Moderate (₹2 lakh to ₹8 lakh):** stationery and xerox, mobile accessories and recharge, salon, gift shop, cosmetics, readymade garments, cloud kitchen, kirana store.
- **Higher (above ₹8 lakh):** medical store, hardware and paint, a bakery with its own production.
- **Licences most shops need:** Shop and Establishment registration under your state's law, a trade licence where your municipality requires one, **FSSAI** for anything to do with food, a **drug licence and a registered pharmacist** for a medical store, and **GST** only once turnover crosses the limit or you sell to other states.
- **Costs and margins are rough estimates**, not survey figures. Use them to compare ideas, then check with people already in the trade.
- **What matters more than the idea:** location, how much stock you tie up, and whether you can show up every day.

## How to read the numbers in this guide

Every cost and margin below is **our rough planning estimate for a small setup in a tier-2 or tier-3 town**, not data from a study. Rent and deposits can double the cost in a big city. "Gross margin" is the share of the selling price left after the cost of goods, before rent, salaries and electricity; our guide to the [profit margin formula](https://billzap.app/blog/profit-margin-formula) shows the arithmetic. Licences vary by state, and we say so where they do.

## Food businesses

### 1. Kirana or general store

The steadiest shop business and the one with the thinnest margin. You earn on footfall and repeat visits, not on any single item. Stock is the biggest cost, and credit (udhaar) is the biggest risk. Our full guide on [how to start a kirana store](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-start-a-kirana-store) has an itemised budget and a worked monthly profit.

### 2. Bakery and sweets

Own-made cakes, namkeen and mithai carry far better margins than resold packets, but need equipment, a trained hand and strict hygiene. You need **FSSAI** registration, and your municipality may ask for a trade or health licence for the kitchen. Our [FSSAI registration](https://billzap.app/blog/fssai-registration) guide explains the tiers: since 1 April 2026, basic registration at ₹100 a year covers turnover up to ₹1.5 crore.

### 3. Tea stall

Cheap to start and high margin per cup, but each sale is tiny, so you need volume: bus stands, hospitals, markets, office clusters. Expect long hours. FSSAI basic registration applies. If you set up on the street rather than in a shop, ask your municipality about a **certificate of vending** under the Street Vendors Act.

**A quick break-even, with assumed figures:** a cup sells for ₹10 and costs about ₹4 in milk, tea, sugar, gas and cup, leaving ₹6. With ₹8,000 a month in rent and other fixed costs, you need ₹8,000 ÷ ₹6 = about **1,334 cups a month**, or **45 a day**, just to break even. To also take home ₹20,000, you need ₹28,000 ÷ ₹6 = about **4,667 cups**, roughly **156 a day**.

### 4. Tiffin service

One of the best **small business ideas from home**. You cook for office workers, students or elderly people on a monthly plan, paid in advance. You need FSSAI registration even when cooking at home.

### 5. Fruit and vegetables

Very low entry cost and daily cash, but produce spoils, so real margin depends on how little you throw away. Fresh fruit and vegetables carry no GST. Selling food still needs FSSAI registration, and a street cart needs a vending certificate where your city issues them.

### 6. Cloud kitchen

A delivery-only kitchen sold through food apps. Rent is lower than a restaurant's, but the apps take a commission on each order, so price your menu for it. Check each app's current onboarding list; all ask for FSSAI registration.

## Retail shops

### 7. Medical store

Steady demand and repeat customers, but it is the most regulated idea on this list. You need a **retail drug licence (Forms 20 and 21)** from your state drug controller, a **registered pharmacist** working at the shop, and premises that meet the minimum area; Delhi's drugs department, for example, requires at least **10 square metres**. Medicines that need cold storage need a refrigerator. If you or a family member is a pharmacist, it is a strong option. Our sibling guide on [how to start a medical store](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-start-a-medical-store) goes through the licence step by step.

### 8. Stationery and xerox

Works best within walking distance of schools, colleges or courts. Photocopying, printing and lamination add service income. Demand follows the academic calendar, so plan stock around exams and admissions.

### 9. Mobile accessories and recharge

Covers, chargers and screen guards carry good margins; recharges bring people in but earn a small commission. Buy from reliable distributors, because cheap chargers bring returns. Basic phone repair raises income per customer.

### 10. Hardware and paint

High stock cost and a long range of items, but large tickets and loyal contractor customers. Many of your buyers will be contractors and businesses who want GST bills, so most hardware shops register for GST early even if turnover is below the limit. Credit to contractors is the main risk.

### 11. Gift shop

Good margins and seasonal peaks (Diwali, Raksha Bandhan, weddings). The risk is stock that goes out of fashion, so start small and reorder what sells.

### 12. Cosmetics

Margins are healthy and customers return often, but fakes and expired stock are real problems. Buy only from authorised distributors and check expiry dates on every carton. Retailing packed cosmetics generally does not need a separate licence; manufacturing and import do.

### 13. Readymade garments

Won or lost at the buying stage: wrong sizes and styles become dead stock. Start with a narrow range, such as children's wear, before you widen.

## Service businesses

### 14. Tailoring and alterations

Low investment and steady demand for alterations, blouses and school uniforms, and you can start from home. Income is mostly your time, so growth means training another tailor.

### 15. Salon

Needs trained staff and a clean, well-lit space. Repeat customers and wedding season drive income. Check whether your municipality requires a trade licence. Because a salon supplies services, its GST limit is **₹20 lakh** in most states, not ₹40 lakh.

### 16. Electrical and appliance repair

Fans, mixers, coolers, inverters and wiring jobs. A good repair person gets referrals quickly. For wiring work at customers' homes, most states require an electrical licence from the state electrical inspectorate or licensing board; check yours.

### 17. Coaching centre

A teacher with a reputation can start in a rented room for very little. Income is your time multiplied by batch size. Some states now regulate coaching centres, with rules on registration and space; ask your district education office what applies.

## Which idea suits you? Four questions

1. **How much can you put in without borrowing?** Keep at least two months of rent and salaries aside as a buffer. If you need a loan, a [Mudra loan](https://billzap.app/blog/mudra-loan) offers collateral-free credit in four categories: Shishu up to ₹50,000, Kishore up to ₹5 lakh, Tarun up to ₹10 lakh, and Tarun Plus from ₹10 lakh to ₹20 lakh for borrowers who have repaid a Tarun loan.
2. **Do you have a skill or a qualification?** Tailoring, salon, repair and coaching depend on skill; a medical store depends on a pharmacist. If you have neither, pick a retail idea where the skill is buying well.
3. **What does your street lack?** Walk the area at 8 am and 8 pm. A colony with three kiranas and no stationery shop near the school is telling you something.
4. **Can you show up every day?** A tea stall or kirana with irregular hours loses its customers quickly.

**For a small town:** rent is lower, but so is footfall. Everyday needs (kirana, medicines, mobile accessories, repair, tailoring, stationery) travel well to smaller towns; specialised retail usually needs a bigger catchment.

## The money side: margin is not profit

A margin of 40% sounds better than 12%. It is not, if the 12% shop sells ten times as much. Two checks before you choose:

- **Margin versus markup.** If you buy a phone cover for ₹200 and sell it for ₹300, your **markup** is ₹100 ÷ ₹200 = **50%**, but your **margin** is ₹100 ÷ ₹300 = **33.3%**. Suppliers quote one, owners think in the other; mixing them up overprices or underprices everything.
- **Stock turn.** A kirana sells its stock many times a year; a gift shop may hold items for months, and that money is stuck.

## GST: do you need it on day one?

Usually not. Most shops need GST registration only when turnover crosses **₹40 lakh** for goods-only businesses in most states, or **₹20 lakh** if you supply services (salon, tailoring, repair, coaching), with lower limits in a few states. You need it regardless of turnover if you sell goods to another state. Our guide to the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) explains which limit applies to you. You may still register earlier if your customers are businesses that want GST bills.

## Your first 30 days: a checklist

**Week 1: paperwork**

- Open a **current account** in the shop's name (most banks ask for a Shop Act certificate or another business proof).
- File your **Shop and Establishment** registration or intimation on your state labour portal. Our [shop act registration](https://billzap.app/blog/shop-act-registration) guide explains what your state asks for.
- Apply for **FSSAI** on foscos.fssai.gov.in if you sell any food.
- Apply for a **trade licence** if your municipality requires one, and a **drug licence** before you open a medical store.
- Register on the **Udyam** portal (udyamregistration.gov.in). It is free and helps when you approach a bank.

**Week 2: money in**

- Get a **merchant UPI QR** from a payment app or your bank. Put it at the counter, at eye level.
- Set up **billing** on your phone: every sale gets a numbered bill, and every credit sale is recorded on the day.
- Decide your **credit policy** before the first customer asks: who gets udhaar, how much, and for how long.

**Week 3: stock**

- Enter your **opening stock** with buying prices, so you know your margin from the first bill.
- Mark your **fast movers** and set a minimum quantity for each, so you reorder before you run out.
- Get your **weighing scale verified and stamped** if you sell anything by weight.

**Week 4: review**

- Compare **sales against your break-even** figure, and reorder what earned, not what sat on the shelf.
- Chase every **unpaid bill** older than a week.

## FAQ

### Which small business is most profitable in India?

There is no single answer. High-margin ideas such as gift shops or tea stalls can earn less than a thin-margin kirana with heavy footfall. Profit depends on location, volume, stock turn and costs, so compare ideas on likely monthly profit, not margin alone.

### What business can I start with ₹50,000?

A tea stall, a tiffin service from home, a fruit and vegetable cart, tailoring or a small coaching batch can start in roughly that range, by our estimate, if you already have a space or work from home. Keep part of the money aside as working capital.

### Which business is best for a small town?

Everyday-needs businesses: kirana, medical store, mobile accessories and repair, stationery near schools, tailoring and electrical repair. Specialised shops usually need a bigger catchment than a small town provides.

### What licences do I need to open a shop in India?

Usually Shop and Establishment registration under your state's law and a trade licence where your municipality requires one. Add FSSAI for food, a drug licence for medicines, and GST once your turnover crosses the limit or you sell to another state.

### Do I need GST registration to start a small business?

Not usually. Most shops register once turnover crosses ₹40 lakh for goods in most states or ₹20 lakh for services, or earlier if they sell to other states.

## The bottom line

Pick the idea that fits your money, your skills and your street, not the biggest margin on a list. Start smaller than you think, finish registrations in the first week, take UPI from the first sale, and bill everything so you know within a month whether the shop is working.
