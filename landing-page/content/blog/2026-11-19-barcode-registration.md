If you make or pack a product under your own brand and want it on a supermarket shelf or a marketplace, someone will ask for its barcode number. In India, the only body that can give you a genuine barcode starting with **890** is **GS1 India**. Others selling "890 barcodes" are resellers GS1 India does not recognise.

GS1 India revised its fees from **1 October 2026**, and most pages ranking for this search still show the old table. This guide has the current numbers, the documents, how the code is built and printed, the MSME refund, Amazon's exemption route, and what a shop selling loose items should do instead.

## Quick answer

- **Where:** apply to **GS1 India** (gs1india.org). It is the only authorised issuer of barcodes beginning with **890**.
- **Who applies:** the **brand owner**. A job worker or contract packer should ask the brand owner to apply.
- **Cost (from 1 October 2026):** for a business with turnover **up to ₹5 crore**, 100 barcodes for one year cost **₹50,200** in total: ₹27,000 registration + ₹13,000 subscription + ₹7,200 GST + ₹3,000 refundable deposit. Fees rise with turnover and with the number of codes.
- **Documents:** PAN, turnover proof (not needed for a new business), a request letter on letterhead, GST certificate or equivalent proof of status, and a cancelled cheque.
- **Time:** usually **7 to 10 working days** after the form, documents and payment are in.
- **Renewal:** annual or multi-year; late by more than 15 days attracts a **₹1,180** penalty, and the subscription is terminated if still unpaid after another 30 days.
- **Micro manufacturers** registered as MSMEs can claim back **80%** of the fees for the first three years, up to **₹50,650**, from the Ministry of MSME.

## Do you actually need a GS1 barcode?

A barcode is not a general legal requirement in India. It is a commercial one: retailers, marketplaces and distributors use the number to identify your product.

| Your situation | Do you need your own GS1 barcode? |
| --- | --- |
| You manufacture or pack goods under **your own brand** for modern retail or exports | **Yes** |
| You sell your own brand on Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra, Nykaa and similar | **Usually yes**; Amazon has an exemption route (see below) |
| You are a **job worker** packing for someone else's brand | **No**: the brand owner applies and gives you the numbers |
| You run a **kirana or retail shop** reselling branded goods | **No**: the packets already carry the manufacturer's barcode. Never stick your own over it |
| You sell **loose** items (dal by weight, sweets, vegetables) only in your shop | **No**: use in-store codes (see below) |
| You make products sold only at your own counter or locally, with no retailer asking | **Not yet**; wait until a buyer requires it |

The rule of thumb: **one number for each distinct product you sell**, not one for each unit. A 500 g pack and a 1 kg pack of the same masala need two numbers; so do different flavours and a multipack of six. A barcode is also not an HSN code: the barcode identifies your product, while the [HSN code](https://billzap.app/blog/what-is-hsn-code) classifies it for GST. You need both.

## GS1 barcode fees from 1 October 2026

GS1 India charges four things: a one-time **registration fee**, a **subscription fee** for the period you choose, **GST at 18%** on both, and an interest-free, **refundable security deposit** of ₹3,000 (not charged on the 10-year plan). The amount depends on three choices: your annual turnover, the number of codes (100, 1,000, 10,000 or 1,00,000) and the period (1, 2, 3, 5 or 10 years).

### One year, turnover up to ₹5 crore

This is the slab most small brands fall into.

| Number of barcodes | Registration | Subscription (1 yr) | GST 18% | Deposit | **Total** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | ₹27,000 | ₹13,000 | ₹7,200 | ₹3,000 | **₹50,200** |
| 1,000 | ₹30,500 | ₹14,000 | ₹8,010 | ₹3,000 | **₹55,510** |
| 10,000 | ₹35,000 | ₹16,000 | ₹9,180 | ₹3,000 | **₹63,180** |
| 1,00,000 | ₹43,500 | ₹17,750 | ₹11,025 | ₹3,000 | **₹75,275** |

Check the first row: ₹27,000 + ₹13,000 = ₹40,000; GST at 18% is ₹7,200; add the ₹3,000 deposit and you get **₹50,200**.

### 100 barcodes, one year, by turnover

| Annual turnover | Total for 1 year |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹5 crore | ₹50,200 |

### Longer periods cost less per year

For 100 barcodes and turnover up to ₹5 crore:

| Period | Total paid | Per year (excluding deposit) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 year | ₹50,200 | ₹47,200 |
| 2 years | ₹64,006 | ₹30,503 |

The per-year column is the total minus the ₹3,000 deposit, divided by the years: for 3 years, (₹76,278 − ₹3,000) ÷ 3 = **₹24,426**. The registration fee is paid once, so the longer you commit, the more it is spread out. Every figure here is from GS1 India's published "GCP Fee Structure (effective from 1 October 2026)". Use the calculator on GS1 India's registration page for your exact slab before paying.

**Choose the count carefully.** The number of codes cannot be changed later; if you run out, you register again. Count every product, pack size and variant, plus planned launches. A brand with 30 products today and plans for 150 should take 1,000, not 100.

## The MSME refund: 80% back for micro manufacturers

Under a Ministry of MSME scheme, a **micro manufacturing enterprise** registered as an MSME can claim **80% of the one-time registration fee and the recurring fees for the first three years**, up to **₹50,650**, for plans of up to 1,000 barcodes. You need an [Udyam registration](https://billzap.app/blog/udyam-registration) showing you as a micro enterprise. Under the current definition, that means investment in plant and machinery of up to ₹2.5 crore and turnover of up to ₹10 crore.

**How to claim:** pay GS1 India in full first. Then apply to your nearest **MSME Development Institute (MSME-DI)** on the prescribed form (Annexure-A), with the GS1 allotment letter, invoice and payment receipt. The decision is at the MSME-DI's discretion, so treat the refund as likely, not guaranteed.

**Worked example.** A micro food manufacturer with ₹1.2 crore turnover takes 100 barcodes for 3 years:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Registration fee | ₹27,000 |
| Subscription for 3 years | ₹35,100 |
| Fees eligible for refund | ₹62,100 |
| 80% of ₹62,100 | ₹49,680 (under the ₹50,650 cap) |
| Total paid to GS1 India (with GST and deposit) | ₹76,278 |
| **Net cost after refund** | **₹26,598**, of which ₹3,000 is a refundable deposit |

Ask the MSME-DI whether GST is counted in the claim in your case; the example assumes it is not.

## Documents for GS1 barcode registration

GS1 India's current list:

1. **PAN** of the company, or of the proprietor for a proprietorship.
2. **Turnover proof** for the year ending 2025 or 2026: the relevant page of the balance sheet. Not required for a new business.
3. **Request letter** on company letterhead asking for barcode allotment.
4. **Proof of status:**
   - Proprietorship: GST registration certificate.
   - Partnership firm: GST certificate or the partnership deed. If you have not set one up yet, see our guide to [partnership firm registration](https://billzap.app/blog/partnership-firm-registration).
   - Private or public limited company: GST certificate plus the certificate of incorporation or MOA.
   - Society, HUF and others: GST certificate or the society registration certificate.
5. **Cancelled cheque** in the business's name.

## How to register, step by step

1. **Count your products** and pick the block size (100, 1,000, 10,000 or 1,00,000) and the period.
2. **Fill in the registration form** linked from GS1 India's "Register for Barcodes" page. You can download a proforma invoice there.
3. **Pay** online (card, net banking, UPI), by NEFT, or by demand draft.
4. **Upload or send the documents.**
5. **Receive your allotment** in about 7 to 10 working days, along with access to **DataKart**, GS1 India's product database.
6. **Create a number for each product in DataKart**, entering its name, brand, pack size and other details. The barcode is generated there.
7. **Print it on the pack** and get it checked (next section).
8. **Renew on time.** Renewing within 15 days of expiry carries no penalty; after that a ₹1,180 penalty applies for the next 30 days, and then the subscription is terminated. Renewing a 3, 5 or 10-year plan before expiry gets you 3 months' extra validity.

GS1 India does not appoint agents. A consultant can help with paperwork, but the numbers must come from GS1 India in your company's name.

## How an EAN-13 barcode number is built

The retail barcode on most packs in India is an **EAN-13**: thirteen digits, which GS1 calls a **GTIN-13**.

| Part | Example | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GS1 India prefix | 890 | Shows the number was issued by GS1 India (not where the product was made) |
| Your company number | assigned by GS1 India | Together with 890, this is your **GS1 Company Prefix** |
| Item reference | 00 to 99 (with 100 codes) | Your number for each product |
| Check digit | last digit | Calculated from the other twelve, so scanners catch misreads |

The more codes you buy, the shorter your company prefix and the longer the item reference: with 100 codes you get two item digits (00 to 99), with 1,000 you get three.

**Working out the check digit**, with an illustrative number 890765432101 (not a real product):

- Add the digits in odd positions (1st, 3rd, 5th and so on): 8 + 0 + 6 + 4 + 2 + 0 = **20**.
- Add the digits in even positions and multiply by 3: (9 + 7 + 5 + 3 + 1 + 1) × 3 = 26 × 3 = **78**.
- Total: 20 + 78 = **98**. The check digit is what takes it to the next multiple of 10: 100 − 98 = **2**.
- Full barcode: **8907654321012**.

DataKart calculates this for you, but it is a quick way to spot made-up numbers.

## Printing the barcode on your pack

A barcode that will not scan is worse than none. GS1's EAN-13 specifications boil down to:

- **Size:** standard is about **37.29 mm × 25.93 mm** (100%), scalable from 80% to 200%. Do not cut the height to fit a label.
- **Colour:** dark bars (black, dark blue, dark green) on a light background (white is best). **Red bars do not scan**, because scanners use red light.
- **Clear margins:** leave blank space on both sides of the bars. Text or a border touching the bars causes failed scans.
- **Placement:** on a flat part of the back panel, away from folds, seams and curves.
- **Test** a printed sample with a scanner before you print the full run. GS1 India also offers barcode verification.

## Barcodes for Amazon, Flipkart and other marketplaces

Online marketplaces use the barcode number as the product's **GTIN** to match listings and stop duplicates.

**Amazon.in** checks the number you enter. If you have no GTIN, Amazon's help page sets out a **GTIN exemption**:

- **Generic, unbranded products:** select "This product does not have a brand name" and "I don't have a product ID". Approval is automatic.
- **Your own brand without barcodes:** apply for the exemption together with brand name approval; Amazon says it decides within about 48 hours.
- **Brands where Amazon enforces GS1 codes:** no exemption. You must use the brand owner's GTIN or buy from GS1.

The exemption helps you start, but works only on Amazon. Once you sell through supermarkets, quick-commerce apps or a second marketplace, you need a real GS1 number, which retailers can check against GS1's database.

**Flipkart and others** ask for a barcode or EAN in many categories. Check the category rules in your seller dashboard before listing. Our guide to [GST for online sellers](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-for-online-sellers) covers the tax side of selling on marketplaces.

## Beware fake barcode sellers

Websites selling "890 barcodes" for a few hundred rupees each are common. GS1 India says **any company providing barcodes starting with 890 operates without legal authority**, and that buyers as well as sellers face action. A Delhi court has restrained a US-based entity from reselling 890 barcodes, and its Indian website was blocked.

Red flags:

- **Very low prices** and no annual renewal ("lifetime" barcodes).
- **No GS1 Company Prefix** in your company's name, and no DataKart login.
- **Numbers registered to someone else**, or to nobody, when a retailer checks them.

Some resellers sell old numbers from other countries' GS1 systems. They may scan, but they are not registered to you, and many retailers reject them.

## Loose items and in-store barcodes

A kirana, sweet shop or fruit seller does not need GS1 registration for items weighed and packed in the shop. GS1's standards reserve numbers beginning with **2** (prefixes 20 to 29) for **in-store use**. A label-printing weighing scale prints a barcode on each pack that encodes your item code and the weight or price, and your own scanner reads it at the counter.

These codes work only inside your store; an item sold on by someone else needs a real GS1 number. ## FAQ

### How much does barcode registration cost in India?

Under GS1 India's fees from 1 October 2026, 100 barcodes for one year cost ₹50,200 in total for a business with turnover up to ₹5 crore, including 18% GST and a ₹3,000 refundable deposit. Fees rise with turnover, number of codes and period.

### Is a barcode mandatory in India?

Not as a general rule for selling goods. Retail chains, marketplaces and exporters usually require one, and some sectors have their own coding rules. If no buyer is asking, you can wait.

### How long does GS1 India take to allot barcodes?

Usually 7 to 10 working days after the registration form, documents and payment are received.

### Can I sell on Amazon without a barcode?

Yes, for generic products or your own brand, through Amazon's GTIN exemption. Brands for which Amazon enforces GS1 codes cannot use the exemption. The exemption works only on Amazon.

### Are cheap 890 barcodes sold online valid?

No. GS1 India is the only authorised issuer of 890 numbers, and says buyers and sellers of unauthorised ones face action. The numbers will not be registered to your company.

## The bottom line

Get barcodes from GS1 India and nobody else. Count every product and pack size you plan to sell before choosing the block, since it cannot be changed later. Take a longer period if you can, claim the MSME refund if you qualify, and test a printed sample before the full run. If you only resell branded packets or loose goods in your own shop, you need none of this.
