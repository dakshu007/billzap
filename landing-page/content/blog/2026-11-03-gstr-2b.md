Every month, the GST portal prepares a statement that quietly decides how much input tax credit you can take: **GSTR-2B**. If a purchase bill is not in it, you cannot claim the tax on that bill, however genuine the purchase. If a bill you never received is in it and you do nothing, the portal adds that credit to your GSTR-3B anyway.

This guide covers what GSTR-2B is, what changed with the **Invoice Management System (IMS)**, what is true and what is rumour about the portal "blocking" returns in 2026, and how to reconcile purchases against 2B each month, with a worked example.

## Quick answer

- **GSTR-2B** is a **read-only, auto-drafted statement of input tax credit (ITC)**, built from the invoices your suppliers report in their GSTR-1, GSTR-1A or IFF. You do not file it.
- It is generated on the **14th of the next month** (for example, October's GSTR-2B on 14 November). For quarterly (QRMP) taxpayers, it is generated once a quarter, on the 14th of the month after the quarter.
- **ITC as per GSTR-2B:** since 1 January 2022, you can claim credit only for invoices that appear in your GSTR-2B (Section 16(2)(aa) and Rule 36(4)). Table 4 of GSTR-3B is auto-filled from it.
- **GSTR-2A vs 2B:** 2A keeps changing as suppliers file; 2B is fixed for the month and is the one your claim is based on.
- **IMS:** since 1 October 2024, you can **accept, reject or keep pending** each supplier invoice. Anything you leave alone is **deemed accepted**. If you act after the 14th, you must **recompute** GSTR-2B before filing GSTR-3B.
- **Download:** Returns Dashboard, choose the period, then the **GSTR-2B** tile. You can download it in Excel or JSON.

## What GSTR-2B is

GSTR-2B lists, invoice by invoice, the purchases your registered suppliers have reported, and marks each one as credit **available** or **not available**. It also includes IGST paid on imports and credit from an input service distributor. Three things set it apart:

1. **It is read-only.** You cannot add a missing invoice or correct a wrong one. Only the supplier can, by amending their GSTR-1 or filing GSTR-1A.
2. **It is static.** Once generated for a month, it does not change on its own when a supplier files late. A late invoice goes into the next GSTR-2B that is generated after the supplier files.
3. **It drives your GSTR-3B.** The credit in Table 4 of GSTR-3B is auto-filled from it. Our [GSTR-3B guide](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-3b) shows where each figure lands.

### The GSTR-2B generation date and cut-off

The portal's own FAQ sets the schedule:

| You file returns | GSTR-2B is generated | Includes supplier filings made |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Monthly** | 14th of the next month (October 2026 on 14 November 2026) | Monthly GSTR-1/1A filed from 00:00 on the 12th of the month to 23:59 on the 11th of the next month; IFF and quarterly GSTR-1 filed from the 14th to the 13th |
| **Quarterly (QRMP)** | 14th of the month after the quarter (October to December 2026 on 14 January 2027) | Everything filed within the quarter's cut-offs |

So if your supplier files October's GSTR-1 on 11 November, the invoice is in your October GSTR-2B; on 12 November, it moves to November's, generated on 14 December. The filing date decides, not the invoice date.

One more rule since IMS: **GSTR-2B is now sequential.** The portal will not generate next month's GSTR-2B until you have filed this month's GSTR-3B.

## GSTR-2A vs GSTR-2B

Both are built from what your suppliers report, which is why they get confused.

| | GSTR-2A | GSTR-2B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nature** | Dynamic: changes every time a supplier files or amends | Static: fixed once generated for the period |
| **When it updates** | Continuously | Once a month on the 14th (or once a quarter for QRMP), plus any recompute you do through IMS |
| **Basis for your ITC claim** | No, it is for reference | **Yes.** Credit is limited to what appears here, and GSTR-3B Table 4 is auto-filled from it |
| **Shows whether credit is available** | No | Yes, invoice by invoice, with "ITC not available" cases separated |
| **Do you file it?** | No | No |
| **Best used for** | Spotting what suppliers have filed since 2B was generated | Monthly ITC claim and reconciliation; GSTR-9 Table 8A is also worked out from it |

Reconcile against **GSTR-2B** every month; use **GSTR-2A** to see whether a missing supplier has since filed. At year end, the credit figure your annual return compares against also comes from GSTR-2B, as our [GSTR-9 guide](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-9) explains.

## The Invoice Management System (IMS) and how it changes 2B

Before October 2024, every invoice a supplier uploaded flowed straight into your GSTR-2B, right or wrong. IMS, live from **1 October 2024**, puts you in between. As soon as a supplier **saves** an invoice in their GSTR-1, IFF or GSTR-1A, it shows on your IMS dashboard. You can act on it until you file the GSTR-3B for that period.

| Your action | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Accept** | The invoice goes into "ITC available" in GSTR-2B and auto-fills GSTR-3B |
| **Reject** | It goes into "ITC rejected" and does not auto-fill GSTR-3B. The supplier can see you rejected it |
| **Pending** | It stays out of this month's GSTR-2B and waits on the dashboard for a later month, within the Section 16(4) time limit |
| **No action** | **Deemed accepted** when GSTR-2B is generated |

Points that trip people up:

- **The 14th and recompute.** GSTR-2B is still generated automatically on the 14th using whatever actions you have taken by then. If you accept, reject or change anything after the 14th, you must **recompute GSTR-2B** from the IMS dashboard before filing GSTR-3B, or the old figures stay.
- **Doing nothing is a decision.** Because of deemed acceptance, a bogus or wrong invoice you ignore becomes credit in your return.
- **Not everything goes through IMS.** Reverse charge supplies, and invoices where credit is not available because of the Section 16(4) time limit or place-of-supply rules, skip IMS and go straight to the return.
- **Credit notes changed in October 2025.** GSTN's advisory of 8 October 2025 confirmed that ITC still auto-populates from GSTR-2B into GSTR-3B and that GSTR-2B is still generated on the 14th. From the October 2025 period, you can also keep a credit note **pending** for a specified period, and when you accept one, you can reduce the reversal to the credit you actually took.

## Is the portal blocking GSTR-3B if ITC does not match 2B?

You will see posts claiming that "since January 2026 the portal blocks filing if your ITC does not match GSTR-2B, even by a rupee". That overstates what has happened, so it is worth being precise.

- **What is blocked.** Under a GSTN advisory dated 29 December 2025, GSTR-3B is blocked in two specific cases: when the ITC you **re-claim** in Table 4(D)(1) exceeds the balance in your **Electronic Credit Reversal and Re-claimed Statement**, and when the **reverse charge** credit you claim exceeds your **RCM Liability/ITC Statement**. Neither is a general "must equal 2B" lock.
- **What the portal says about Table 4.** The GST portal's GSTR-2B FAQ, as read in November 2026, still says the values auto-populated into GSTR-3B are **editable**, with an edited field highlighted in red and a warning shown. Locking Table 4 has been widely discussed as the next step after the July 2025 lock on sales liability, so check the portal and your CA before each filing season.
- **What the law says regardless.** Whatever the screen allows, Section 16(2)(aa) and Rule 36(4) limit your credit to invoices in GSTR-2B. Claiming more is the most common reason for an ITC notice.

**Rule 36(4) in brief.** The rule once allowed a little extra credit on invoices suppliers had not uploaded (20% from October 2019, then 10%, then 5%). From **1 January 2022** that allowance went, and credit is limited to GSTR-2B. Our guide to [input tax credit](https://billzap.app/blog/input-tax-credit-gst) covers the other conditions, such as paying suppliers within 180 days.

## How to download GSTR-2B

1. Log in at gst.gov.in.
2. Go to **Services > Returns > Returns Dashboard**.
3. Choose the **financial year** and the **return filing period** (the month, or the quarter if you are on QRMP), then click **Search**.
4. On the **GSTR-2B** tile, click **View** for the summary on screen, or **Download** for the full file.
5. Pick **Excel** for reconciliation in a spreadsheet, or **JSON** if your CA's software will import it.

The IMS dashboard, with its **Compute GSTR-2B** button, is under **Services > Returns > Invoice Management System (IMS) Dashboard**.

## How to reconcile purchases against GSTR-2B, month by month

The goal is simple: every purchase bill in your books should be in GSTR-2B, and everything in GSTR-2B should be a bill you actually received.

1. **Keep a purchase register as bills arrive.** Supplier GSTIN, invoice number and date, taxable value and tax. A spreadsheet is enough for a small shop.
2. **Between the 1st and the 13th, open IMS.** Accept invoices that match your register. Reject anything you did not buy or that has the wrong value. Keep pending anything you have not received yet.
3. **On or after the 14th, download GSTR-2B (Excel).** If you acted in IMS after the 14th, recompute first.
4. **Match on supplier GSTIN and invoice number**, then compare the tax amounts.
5. **Sort differences into three lists:** in books but not in 2B (chase the supplier), in 2B but not in books (reject it, or find the missing bill), and amount mismatches (ask the supplier to amend).
6. **Check the "ITC not available" section** for time-barred or wrong place-of-supply invoices.
7. **File GSTR-3B claiming only what is in 2B**, and keep a note of the missing bills to claim next month.

### A worked example: October 2026

A hardware shop in Pune, filing monthly, has five purchase bills for October 2026 in its register:

| Supplier | Taxable value | GST rate | GST on the bill |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | ₹50,000 | 18% | ₹9,000 |
| B | ₹30,000 | 18% | ₹5,400 |
| C | ₹20,000 | 5% | ₹1,000 |
| D | ₹40,000 | 18% | ₹7,200 |
| E | ₹10,000 | 18% | ₹1,800 |
| **Total** | | | **₹24,400** |

Now the IMS dashboard on 10 November:

- **A, B and C** are there and match the bills.
- **D** has not filed GSTR-1 yet.
- **E** uploaded the bill with a taxable value of ₹1,00,000 by mistake, so tax shows as **₹18,000**.
- **F**, a supplier the shop has never bought from, shows an invoice with **₹2,700** of tax.

**If the owner does nothing,** everything on the dashboard is deemed accepted on 14 November, and GSTR-3B would auto-fill:

₹9,000 + ₹5,400 + ₹1,000 + ₹18,000 + ₹2,700 = **₹36,100**

That is ₹11,700 more than the shop's real credit of ₹24,400 (₹36,100 − ₹24,400), and every rupee of it would have to be reversed with interest later.

**What the owner does instead:** accepts A, B and C; **rejects E** so the supplier corrects it; **rejects F**. The GSTR-2B then shows:

₹9,000 + ₹5,400 + ₹1,000 = **₹15,400**

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| ITC claimed in October GSTR-3B | ₹15,400 |
| Supplier D, claim when it appears in a later GSTR-2B | ₹7,200 |
| Supplier E, claim after the corrected bill appears | ₹1,800 |
| **Total, matching the books** | **₹24,400** |

Check: ₹15,400 + ₹7,200 + ₹1,800 = ₹24,400. Nothing is lost; ₹9,000 is only delayed. If the shop owes ₹40,000 of output tax for October, it pays ₹40,000 − ₹15,400 = **₹24,600** in cash now, instead of ₹15,600 had all its suppliers filed on time. That difference of ₹9,000 is the real cost of late suppliers: working capital, not lost credit.

## What to do when a supplier has not filed

1. **Check their filing status.** A [GST number search](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-number-search) shows whether a GSTIN is active and its return filing history.
2. **Send a reminder with the invoice details.** Most delays are oversight; their GSTR-1 was due on the 11th (13th for quarterly filers).
3. **Hold back the GST portion of their payment** until the invoice shows in your GSTR-2B, if your terms allow.
4. **Claim it in the month it appears.** When the supplier files, the invoice lands in your next GSTR-2B and you claim it then. The outer limit for a year's invoices is **30 November** of the following year, or your annual return date if earlier; for FY 2025-26 bills, that is 30 November 2026. Our [GST due dates](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-due-dates) calendar has the dates.
5. **Watch for suppliers who file GSTR-1 but not GSTR-3B.** Under Rule 37A, if a supplier has not filed GSTR-3B for the period by 30 September after the year ends, the buyer must reverse that credit. Persistent defaulters are worth replacing.

## FAQ

### What is the GSTR-2B generation date?

The 14th of the following month for monthly filers. For quarterly (QRMP) taxpayers, it is generated once a quarter, on the 14th of the month after the quarter. If you change anything in IMS after the 14th, recompute GSTR-2B before filing GSTR-3B.

### What is the difference between GSTR-2A and GSTR-2B?

GSTR-2A is dynamic and changes whenever a supplier files or amends. GSTR-2B is a fixed statement for each period and is the one your ITC claim is based on; GSTR-3B Table 4 is auto-filled from it.

### Can I claim ITC that is not in GSTR-2B?

No. Since 1 January 2022, Section 16(2)(aa) and Rule 36(4) limit your credit to invoices that appear in GSTR-2B. If a supplier files late, claim the credit in the month the invoice appears, within the Section 16(4) time limit.

### Is it compulsory to accept or reject invoices in IMS?

No. Invoices you leave alone are deemed accepted when GSTR-2B is generated. But that means wrong invoices become credit in your return, so it is worth rejecting anything you did not buy.

### What happens if I reject an invoice in IMS?

It goes into the "ITC rejected" part of GSTR-2B and does not auto-fill your GSTR-3B. The supplier can see the rejection and correct the invoice through GSTR-1A or a later amendment, after which it shows on your dashboard again.

### Why has my GSTR-2B for this month not been generated?

GSTR-2B is now sequential: it is not generated for a period until you have filed GSTR-3B for the previous period. File the pending GSTR-3B first.

## The bottom line

GSTR-2B is the ceiling on your input tax credit. Act in IMS before the 14th, recompute if you act later, match 2B against your purchase register, and chase missing suppliers instead of claiming ahead of them. Late suppliers tie up your cash; they cost you the credit only if you miss the 30 November deadline.
