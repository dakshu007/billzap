Every shop that gives udhaar has the same notebook: a few pages of names, dates and amounts, some crossed out, many not. The money in that notebook is real. It is stock you have already paid your distributor for, sitting in someone else's kitchen. And the longer it sits, the harder it is to ask for.

Most of what you find online about udhaar is an app listing or a funny reel. This guide is the practical part: what credit actually costs a small shop, the rules that keep it under control, reminder messages you can copy and send on WhatsApp in English or Hinglish, when to stop giving credit, and what the law offers if a customer will not pay at all.

## Quick answer

- **Write every udhaar down the same day**, with the customer's name, phone number, amount, date and a due date. Unwritten credit is the credit you lose.
- **Set a credit limit for each customer** (about one week of their usual buying is a safe start) and a fixed settlement date, such as the 7th of every month.
- **No udhaar for new customers.** Give credit only after a few weeks of cash or UPI purchases.
- **Remind on a schedule:** a friendly note on the due date, a reminder after 7 days, a firm one after 15 days, and a final message that stops further credit. Always include the exact amount and a UPI pay link.
- **Stop credit** when a customer crosses the limit, misses two due dates, or stops answering.
- **If nothing works,** a legal notice, a pre-litigation case at a Lok Adalat (no court fee) or a civil recovery suit are the options. For small amounts, take advice first; the cost can exceed the dues.

## Why udhaar happens, and what it really costs

Udhaar is how neighbourhood shops compete with supermarkets and apps. Salaried families buy on credit through the month and settle after payday. Daily-wage customers settle at the end of the week. Refusing credit to a loyal family can mean losing them to the next shop. So the goal is not zero udhaar. It is udhaar you control.

The trouble is that credit hides its cost. Here is a worked example for a kirana selling ₹15,000 a day.

| Item | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily sales | | ₹15,000 |
| Share sold on udhaar | 20% of ₹15,000 | ₹3,000 a day |
| Average days before customers pay | | 30 days |
| **Money locked in udhaar at any time** | ₹3,000 × 30 | **₹90,000** |
| Cost of that money if borrowed at 18% a year | ₹90,000 × 18% ÷ 12 | ₹1,350 a month |

₹90,000 is roughly six days of the shop's entire sales, permanently out on credit. If you fund your stock with a loan or an overdraft, that money costs you about ₹1,350 every month. If you do not borrow, it is ₹90,000 of stock you cannot buy, which means empty shelves and lost sales.

Bad debts hurt even more. If your gross margin is 15%, a single ₹1,000 that is never paid wipes out the profit on about ₹6,700 of sales (₹1,000 ÷ 0.15). Our guide to the [profit margin formula](https://billzap.app/blog/profit-margin-formula) shows how to work out your own margin.

Cut the average collection time from 30 days to 15 and the money locked in drops to ₹45,000. That is the single biggest lever, and it comes from the rules and reminders below.

## Five rules that keep udhaar under control

### 1. Write it down, the same day

A credit sale that is not written down at the counter will be argued about later. Record the name, phone number, items or bill number, amount, date and due date. Better still, give the customer a bill for every credit purchase, so there is no dispute about what was bought. A [cash memo](https://billzap.app/blog/cash-memo-format) or bill marked "unpaid" does the job.

### 2. Set a credit limit per customer

Decide a maximum for each customer and stick to it. A simple starting point is one week of what that customer normally buys. A family spending ₹4,000 a month gets a limit of about ₹1,000. Raise it only after months of paying on time.

| Customer type | Suggested limit | Settlement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New customer | No credit | Cash or UPI only |
| Regular for 2 to 3 months, pays on time | About 1 week of their buying | Weekly or monthly |
| Long-standing, always pays | Up to 2 weeks of their buying | Monthly, by a fixed date |
| Has missed two due dates | No new credit | Clear dues first |

Also cap your **total** udhaar. Decide the most you can afford to have out on credit (for example, one week of sales) and stop new credit across the board when you hit it.

### 3. Fix a due date, out loud

"Pay when you can" means never. Agree a date when you give the credit: "Saturday", "the 7th", "after salary". Write it on the bill. Customers who know the date in advance rarely feel insulted when you remind them.

### 4. No udhaar for new customers

The customer you do not know is the one most likely to disappear. Ask new customers to pay by cash or UPI for the first few weeks. Most people understand, especially if you say it is shop policy for everyone.

### 5. Separate udhaar from friendship

Relatives and friends are often the worst payers, because asking feels awkward. Treat them with the same limit and the same reminders. A written policy, even a small card at the counter that says "Udhaar settled by the 7th of every month", makes it about the shop, not about them.

## WhatsApp reminder messages you can copy

The best reminder is short, polite, specific and easy to act on. It names the exact amount, the bill date and gives a way to pay in one tap. Replace the words in brackets.

### Day 1: on the due date (friendly)

**English**

> Namaste [Name] ji, hope you are well. Your bill of ₹[amount] from [date] at [Shop name] is due today. You can pay by UPI here: [UPI link]. Thank you for shopping with us!

**Hinglish**

> Namaste [Name] ji. [Shop name] se aapka ₹[amount] ka bill ([date]) aaj due hai. UPI se yahan pay kar sakte hain: [UPI link]. Dhanyavaad!

### Day 7: gentle reminder

**English**

> Hello [Name] ji, a small reminder from [Shop name]. ₹[amount] is still pending from your bill dated [date]. Please pay when convenient this week: [UPI link]. If you have already paid, please ignore this message.

**Hinglish**

> [Name] ji, [Shop name] se ek chhota sa reminder. [date] ke bill ka ₹[amount] abhi baaki hai. Is hafte jab suvidha ho, yahan pay kar dijiye: [UPI link]. Agar pay kar diya hai to yeh message ignore karein.

### Day 15: firm, still polite

**English**

> Dear [Name] ji, ₹[amount] from your bill of [date] is now 15 days overdue. We have to clear our own supplier payments, so please settle it by [date + 3 days]. Pay here: [UPI link]. Please let me know if there is any problem with the bill.

**Hinglish**

> [Name] ji, [date] ke bill ka ₹[amount] ab 15 din se pending hai. Hamein bhi supplier ko payment karna hota hai, isliye kripya [date + 3 days] tak clear kar dein. Pay link: [UPI link]. Bill mein koi galti ho to bataiye.

### Final message: credit paused

**English**

> [Name] ji, ₹[amount] has been pending since [date] despite our reminders. Until this is cleared, we will only be able to serve you on cash or UPI. We value you as a customer and hope to settle this soon: [UPI link].

**Hinglish**

> [Name] ji, [date] se ₹[amount] baaki hai aur reminders ke baad bhi clear nahi hua. Jab tak yeh clear nahi hota, hum sirf cash ya UPI par hi saaman de payenge. Aap hamare purane grahak hain, umeed hai jaldi settle ho jayega: [UPI link].

### What makes these work

- **The exact amount and date.** "Some money is pending" invites "how much?" and a week of delay.
- **A tap-to-pay UPI link.** Every step you remove gets you paid sooner. A link that opens the UPI app with the amount already filled in is best; our guide to the [UPI QR code for your shop](https://billzap.app/blog/upi-qr-code-for-shop) explains how these links work.
- **An easy way out.** "If you have already paid, ignore this" and "tell me if the bill is wrong" keep the tone respectful and catch genuine mistakes.
- **Send in the morning,** not late at night, and never in a group chat. A reminder in a family or society group embarrasses the customer and usually loses them for good.
- **Call after the day 15 message** if there is no reply. A short, calm phone call settles more dues than a fifth text.

## How to ask for payment politely, face to face

Many customers come back to the shop before they pay. That is your best chance.

- **Ask privately,** when other customers are not listening.
- **Lead with the number:** "₹1,240 is pending from last month, shall I add today's ₹180 or will you clear the old one first?"
- **Offer a part payment** if they cannot pay in full. ₹500 today and a date for the rest is better than nothing.
- **Do not add new credit on top of overdue credit.** "Today on UPI, and we will settle the old bill on Saturday" is fair and clear.

## When to stop giving credit

Stop new udhaar for a customer when any of these happens:

1. They cross their credit limit.
2. They miss two agreed due dates in a row.
3. They stop replying to messages or calls.
4. They start buying elsewhere for cash while their balance with you stays unpaid.
5. Your shop's total udhaar crosses the cap you set.

Stopping credit is not the same as stopping the relationship. Keep serving them on cash or UPI and keep the door open. Many customers clear their dues once they realise the credit has stopped.

## Legal options, briefly

Most udhaar is recovered with patience and reminders. For the rare customer who owes a large amount and refuses to pay, these are the usual routes. Treat this as general information, not legal advice; speak to a lawyer before you act.

- **Keep your evidence.** Bills, the written udhaar record, WhatsApp reminders and any part payments all show the debt is real.
- **Legal notice.** A notice from a lawyer demanding payment within a set period often settles the matter on its own.
- **Lok Adalat.** Your District Legal Services Authority can take up a dispute at the pre-litigation stage. There is no court fee, and an award, once both sides agree to it, is final and enforceable like a civil court decree. It works only if the customer agrees to settle.
- **Civil recovery suit.** India has no separate small-claims court for shop dues, so a recovery suit goes to the regular civil court. There is generally a three-year time limit from when the payment fell due, so do not wait years.

Avoid anything that looks like harassment: no public "defaulters" lists with names, no shaming in groups, no threats. Apart from losing the customer, these can land you in legal trouble.

## FAQ

### How do I ask a customer for pending payment politely?

Ask privately, state the exact amount and bill date, and give an easy way to pay, such as a UPI link. Add a line like "if you have already paid, please ignore this". Keep the tone friendly in the first two reminders and firmer only after 15 days.

### What should a payment reminder message on WhatsApp include?

The customer's name, your shop's name, the exact amount, the bill date, a due date and a UPI pay link. Keep it under four lines and send it to the customer directly, never in a group.

### How much credit should a kirana give one customer?

Start with about one week of what that customer normally buys, and only for customers who have shopped with you for a few weeks. Raise the limit slowly for those who always pay on time.

### Should I charge interest on udhaar?

Most kiranas do not, because it sours the relationship for small amounts. A better tool is a firm due date and stopping new credit when it is missed. If you do plan to charge interest, agree it with the customer in advance and in writing.

### What is a good alternative to a paper udhar khata?

A billing app that marks bills unpaid and sends reminders, such as BillZap, or a ledger app such as Khatabook if you need running balances and part payments per customer. Either beats a notebook you cannot search or back up.

### Can I take legal action for unpaid udhaar?

Yes. Options include a legal notice, a pre-litigation case at a Lok Adalat with no court fee, or a recovery suit in civil court, generally within three years of the due date. For small amounts, get advice first, as the cost can exceed what you recover.

## The bottom line

Udhaar is a loan from your working capital, so lend it on purpose. Write every credit sale down, set a limit and a due date for each customer, remind on a fixed schedule with the exact amount and a pay link, and stop new credit when the rules are broken. Done consistently, that halves the money stuck in your notebook without losing the customers who keep your shop going.
