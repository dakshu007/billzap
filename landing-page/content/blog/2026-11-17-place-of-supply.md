Every GST bill answers one question before it shows a single rupee of tax: **where is this supply taxed?** That location is the **place of supply**. Compare it with your own state, and you know whether to charge CGST + SGST or IGST. Get it wrong and you pay the tax again under the right head, then wait for a refund of the wrong one.

For a shop selling over the counter, the answer is nearly always "my own state". It gets harder the moment you ship to another state, bill one party and deliver to another, sell online, or supply a service such as event management, courier or repairs to a building. This guide sets out the rules from the IGST Act in plain language, with a decision table and worked examples.

## Quick answer

- **Place of supply (POS)** is the state or union territory where GST treats a supply as made. It decides the tax type.
- **Supplier's state = POS:** intra-state supply. Charge **CGST + SGST** (or CGST + UTGST).
- **Supplier's state ≠ POS:** inter-state supply. Charge **IGST**. Exports and supplies to SEZs are always inter-state (Section 7(5), IGST Act).
- **Goods** (Section 10): POS is where the **movement ends**; for **bill-to-ship-to**, the **bill-to party's** principal place of business; for goods that do not move (a **counter sale**), **where the goods are delivered**, which is your shop; for goods installed at a site, that site. Since **1 October 2023**, for an **unregistered buyer**, the address recorded on the invoice (Section 10(1)(ca)), and CBIC Circular 209/3/2024-GST says that means the **delivery address** where it differs from the billing address.
- **Services** (Section 12): the **general rule** is the **registered recipient's location**; for an unregistered recipient, their address on record, else the supplier's location. Specific rules override it: **immovable property and hotels** (where the property is), **restaurants, salons and gyms** (where performed), **events** and **transport** (rules below).
- **Services with a foreign leg** follow **Section 13**.
- **Wrong POS:** pay the correct tax and claim a **refund** of the wrong one (Section 77 of the CGST Act, Section 19 of the IGST Act). No interest on the correct tax.

## Why place of supply decides the tax

The IGST Act defines a supply as **inter-state** when the location of the supplier and the place of supply are in different states (Section 7) and **intra-state** when they are in the same state (Section 8). The tax rate does not change: 18% is 9% CGST + 9% SGST or 18% IGST. What changes is **which government receives it**, and so who can claim the credit.

So POS is not about where the buyer lives, where the buyer's GSTIN is, or where payment comes from. It is a legal test, set out separately for each kind of supply. ## Where the rules live

| Section of the IGST Act | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| **Section 10** | Goods, where both supplier and buyer are in India |
| **Section 11** | Goods imported into or exported from India |
| **Section 12** | Services, where supplier and recipient are both in India |
| **Section 13** | Services, where the supplier or the recipient is outside India |

## Place of supply of goods: Section 10

| Situation | Place of supply | Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goods move, by you, the buyer or anyone else | Where the movement **ends** for delivery | 10(1)(a) |
| Bill-to-ship-to: you deliver to a third party on your buyer's instruction | The **bill-to party's** principal place of business | 10(1)(b) |
| Goods do not move (handed over at your premises) | Where the goods are at the time of delivery | 10(1)(c) |
| Goods sold to an **unregistered** person (from 1 October 2023) | The address of that person **recorded on the invoice**; recording the state name counts as recording the address | 10(1)(ca) |
| Goods installed or assembled at a site | The site of installation | 10(1)(d) |
| Goods supplied on board a train, flight or ship | Where the goods are taken on board | 10(1)(e) |

**Counter sales.** When a customer pays at your counter and carries the goods away, delivery happens at your shop, so the place of supply is your state. A traveller from Kerala buying a saree in a Jaipur shop pays Rajasthan CGST + SGST. Do not switch to IGST because the customer says they are from elsewhere.

**Unregistered buyers with two addresses.** Online and phone sales often have a billing address in one state and a delivery address in another. Clause (ca) points to the address recorded on the invoice, and CBIC **Circular 209/3/2024-GST (26 June 2024)** clarified that where the delivery address on the invoice differs from the billing address, the **delivery address** is the place of supply. Rule 46 of the CGST Rules also requires the delivery address, with state name and code, on invoices to unregistered buyers of **₹50,000 or more**. ## Place of supply of services: Section 12

Services are harder, because nothing physically moves. Section 12 starts with a general rule and then overrides it for specific services.

**The general rule, Section 12(2):**

- **Recipient registered:** POS is the **recipient's location**.
- **Recipient unregistered:** POS is the recipient's **address on record**, if you have one; if not, the **supplier's location**.

**The specific rules:**

| Service | Place of supply | Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Services related to immovable property: construction, repairs, architects, interior decorators, renting, **hotel and guest house stays** | Where the **property** is located | 12(3) |
| **Restaurant and catering**, personal grooming, fitness, beauty treatment, health services | Where the service is **actually performed** | 12(4) |
| Training and performance appraisal | Registered recipient's location; otherwise where performed | 12(5) |
| **Admission** to an event, cinema, amusement park | Where the event is held or the park is located | 12(6) |
| **Organising** an event, fair, conference or wedding | Registered recipient's location; otherwise where the event is held | 12(7) |
| **Transport of goods**, including courier | Registered recipient's location; otherwise where the goods are **handed over** for transport | 12(8) |
| **Passenger transport** | Registered recipient's location; otherwise where the passenger **embarks** for a continuous journey | 12(9) |
| Services on board a conveyance | The first scheduled point of departure | 12(10) |
| Telecom, DTH, mobile recharge | Varies: installation address, billing address on record, or where a prepaid recharge is sold | 12(11) |
| Banking and financial services | Recipient's location in the supplier's records; else the supplier's location | 12(12) |
| Insurance | Registered recipient's location; otherwise the recipient's location in the supplier's records | 12(13) |

Two things surprise most businesses. First, a **hotel stay** is taxed where the hotel is, even when a registered company books it, so a Haryana firm's staff staying in Jaipur pay Rajasthan CGST + SGST. Second, **transport of goods** for a registered recipient follows the recipient. Since 1 October 2023, the old proviso that tied the POS to the destination for goods going abroad has gone, so a registered recipient's location is the answer in that case too.

## Services with a foreign leg: Section 13

When either the supplier or the recipient is outside India, Section 13 applies. Its general rule (13(2)) is the **recipient's location**, with specific rules for things such as services on goods physically handed over, immovable property, events and transport.

**Online services from abroad.** Since **1 October 2023**, any **unregistered person** in India who buys online information and database access or retrieval services (OIDAR) from a foreign supplier is a "non-taxable online recipient", whatever the purpose. The foreign supplier registers in India and pays IGST, with POS at the Indian recipient's location under Section 13(12). For a small business, the practical point is this: a foreign software or app subscription bought in your own name carries IGST, and if you are registered and give your GSTIN, the tax may instead come to you under reverse charge. Check the invoice.

## Decision table: CGST + SGST or IGST?

| Your supply | Ask | Typical answer for a shop in your state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter sale of goods | Where were the goods handed over? | Your shop: **CGST + SGST** |
| Goods delivered by you or a courier | Where does delivery end? | Buyer's delivery state: IGST if outside your state |
| Bill-to-ship-to order | Where is the bill-to party? | Bill-to party's state |
| Online sale to an unregistered buyer | What delivery address is on the invoice? | That address's state |
| Service to a registered business | Is there a specific rule (property, event, transport)? If not, where is the recipient registered? | Recipient's state |
| Service to an unregistered customer | Is there a specific rule? If not, do you have their address on record? | Their address; else your state |
| Restaurant meal, haircut, gym | Where was it performed? | Your premises: **CGST + SGST** |
| Repair or design work on a building | Where is the building? | The building's state |
| Export, or supply to an SEZ | Always inter-state | **IGST** (or zero-rated under LUT) |

## Eight worked examples

**1. Counter sale to a visitor.** A Mysuru shop sells a ₹10,000 silk saree (5% GST) to a customer from Tamil Nadu, who takes it away. POS: Karnataka. Tax: CGST ₹250 + SGST ₹250.

**2. Delivery to another state.** A Surat wholesaler sends ₹1,00,000 of fabric (5%) by transporter to a registered retailer in Jaipur. Movement ends in Rajasthan. Tax: IGST ₹5,000. Above ₹50,000 an e-way bill is usually needed; see our [e-way bill rules](https://billzap.app/blog/e-way-bill-rules).

**3. Bill-to-ship-to.** A Bengaluru dealer sells 20 fans worth ₹40,000 (18%) to a Delhi contractor, who asks for delivery to his site in Bengaluru. POS: Delhi, the bill-to party's principal place of business. Tax: IGST ₹7,200, even though the fans never left Karnataka.

**4. Online order, two addresses.** A Delhi seller sells a ₹60,000 laptop (18%) to an unregistered buyer whose billing address is in Delhi but who asks for delivery to Patna. The delivery address on the invoice is in Bihar, so POS is Bihar (Circular 209/3/2024-GST). Tax: IGST ₹10,800. Because the value is over ₹50,000, the invoice must show the buyer's name, delivery address and state name and code.

**5. Chartered accountant, out-of-state client.** A Hyderabad CA bills ₹50,000 (18%) to a client registered in Maharashtra. No specific rule applies, so the general rule gives the registered recipient's location. Tax: IGST ₹9,000.

**6. Interior design for a flat.** A Bengaluru designer charges ₹2,00,000 (18%) to an unregistered owner for a flat in Pune. Interior decoration of immovable property follows the property. POS: Maharashtra. Tax: IGST ₹36,000.

**7. Event management.** A Goa event company organises a conference in Goa for a company registered in Delhi, for ₹5,00,000 (18%). Registered recipient: POS is Delhi, so IGST ₹90,000. If it organised a wedding in Goa for an unregistered Delhi family instead, POS would be Goa, where the event is held: CGST ₹45,000 + SGST ₹45,000.

**8. Courier for a walk-in customer.** A Chennai courier office books a parcel for an unregistered walk-in customer to Kolkata, for ₹500 (18%). Goods handed over in Tamil Nadu, so POS is Tamil Nadu. Tax: CGST ₹45 + SGST ₹45.

## What happens if you get the place of supply wrong?

Tax paid under the wrong head is not shifted across. The law treats it as two separate events:

1. **Pay the correct tax** under the right head. Section 19(2) of the IGST Act and Section 77(2) of the CGST Act say no interest is charged on that amount.
2. **Claim a refund** of the tax wrongly paid, under Section 77 of the CGST Act (for CGST + SGST paid wrongly) or Section 19 of the IGST Act (for IGST paid wrongly). Under Rule 89(1A), the refund claim must be filed within **two years from the date you paid the correct tax**.

**Example.** A Gujarat trader sells ₹1,00,000 of goods (18%) to a buyer in Ahmedabad and charges IGST of ₹18,000 by mistake. The correct tax is CGST ₹9,000 + SGST ₹9,000. He pays that ₹18,000, then claims the ₹18,000 IGST back as a refund. Until the refund arrives, ₹18,000 is stuck, and his buyer's IGST credit is also wrong.

Catch it before the return is filed whenever you can: cancel or issue a credit note against the wrong invoice and a fresh invoice with the right split in the same tax period. ## Place of supply on the invoice and in returns

Rule 46 of the CGST Rules lists what a tax invoice must show. For place of supply, the key items are:

- **Place of supply with the state name**, for every inter-state supply. Most invoices show it on every bill, which is the cleaner habit.
- **The recipient's name, address and delivery address, with state name and code**, for an unregistered buyer where the value is **₹50,000 or more**, or where the buyer asks.
- **CGST and SGST separately**, or IGST, each with rate and amount.

In **GSTR-1**, every invoice carries a POS. B2B invoices are reported one by one with the buyer's GSTIN; inter-state B2C invoices above ₹1 lakh are reported individually; smaller B2C sales go in a summary by state and rate. A wrong state code here is how most POS mistakes surface later. See our [GST invoice format](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-invoice-format) for the full list of invoice fields.

## Common mistakes

- **Deciding by the buyer's GSTIN.** The GSTIN tells you where the buyer is registered, not where the goods were delivered or the event was held.
- **Charging IGST on a counter sale** because the customer is from another state.
- **Ignoring bill-to-ship-to.** The bill-to party decides POS, even when the goods never leave your state.
- **Using the billing address for an online order** when the invoice shows a different delivery address.
- **Expecting a hotel or restaurant in another state to charge IGST.** Property-based and performance-based services stay local.
- **"Adjusting" IGST against CGST + SGST** instead of paying correctly and claiming a refund.

## FAQ

### What is place of supply in GST?

It is the state or union territory where GST treats a supply as made. If it matches the supplier's state, the supply is intra-state and carries CGST + SGST; if not, it is inter-state and carries IGST.

### Which sections decide the place of supply?

Sections 10 to 13 of the IGST Act: Section 10 for goods within India, Section 11 for imports and exports of goods, Section 12 for services within India, and Section 13 where the supplier or recipient is outside India.

### What is the place of supply for a counter sale to a customer from another state?

Your own state, because the goods are delivered at your shop. Charge CGST + SGST, not IGST.

### What is the place of supply in a bill-to-ship-to transaction?

The principal place of business of the party who is billed and who instructs you to deliver to someone else, under Section 10(1)(b). The delivery location does not decide it.

### What is the general rule for place of supply of services?

For a registered recipient, the recipient's location. For an unregistered recipient, their address on record if you have it, otherwise your own location. Specific rules for property, restaurants, events and transport override this.

### What if I charged IGST instead of CGST and SGST?

Pay the correct CGST and SGST without interest, then claim a refund of the IGST under Section 77 of the CGST Act and Section 19 of the IGST Act, within two years of paying the correct tax.

### Is the state code mandatory on a GST invoice?

The place of supply with the state name is required for inter-state supplies, and the state name and code of the recipient and delivery address are required for unregistered buyers on bills of ₹50,000 or more. Showing the state and code on every bill is good practice.

## The bottom line

Place of supply is one question with a handful of answers. For goods: where delivery ends, the bill-to party for bill-to-ship-to, your shop for counter sales, and the delivery address on the invoice for unregistered buyers. For services: the registered recipient's location, unless the service is tied to a property, a performance, an event or transport. Compare the answer with your own state and the tax type follows.
