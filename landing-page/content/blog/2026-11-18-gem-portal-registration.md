Government offices buy paper, toner, furniture, cleaning supplies, uniforms and hundreds of services every day, and much of that buying goes through one website: the **Government e-Marketplace (GeM)** at gem.gov.in. Central ministries and departments are required to buy through GeM whatever is available on it, and many state departments and public sector companies use it too. For a small trader or manufacturer, that makes GeM the most direct way to sell to the government without a middleman.

Signing up is free. What trips sellers up is everything around it: outdated advice about caution money, a vendor assessment only some sellers need, order charges that start at a threshold, and MSE benefits that many traders assume they get but do not. This guide covers all of it, as of November 2026.

## Quick answer

- **GeM portal registration is free.** There is no registration fee and no fee for listing products.
- **No caution money.** GeM has dropped caution money for all sellers, whatever their turnover. Older guides that quote caution money slabs are out of date.
- **Identity:** your Aadhaar with its linked mobile, **or** your personal PAN with any mobile. Aadhaar is no longer mandatory.
- **Business documents:** business PAN, bank account, registered address and income tax return details. **GSTIN is optional** to register, though most bids ask for it. Udyam is optional but unlocks EMD exemption in bids.
- **Vendor assessment** (by RITES Ltd) is mandatory for manufacturers (OEMs) in many categories, with a fee from **₹871.61 plus GST** depending on turnover. Most resellers do not need it to start.
- **Order charges:** nothing on orders up to **₹10 lakh**. Once your orders in a financial year cross **₹20 lakh**, you pay a **₹10,000 plus GST** milestone charge, and **0.30%** on each order above ₹10 lakh (capped at ₹3 lakh).
- **Payment:** GeM terms require the buyer to pay within **10 days of the CRAC** (consignee receipt-cum-acceptance certificate).

## Who can sell on GeM, and who should

Any Indian business can register, from a proprietorship to a company. You can sell **products** (catalogues with a price) and **services** (cleaning, security, vehicle hire, printing and many more).

GeM suits you if you sell items government offices buy regularly (stationery, printer consumables, furniture, electrical goods, housekeeping material, uniforms), can deliver on time to the address the buyer names, and can wait a few weeks for payment without running short of cash.

**How government buyers pick sellers.** Under the General Financial Rules as amended in July 2024, a central government buyer can buy:

| Order value | How the buyer chooses |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹50,000 | Directly from any seller on GeM meeting the requirement |
| Above ₹50,000 up to ₹10 lakh | From the lowest-priced seller among at least three manufacturers' products |
| Above ₹10 lakh | Through a bid or reverse auction on GeM |

So small orders come from your **catalogue price**, and larger ones from **bids**.

## Documents you need before you start

| Business PAN | Yes | For a proprietorship, this is the proprietor's own PAN |

## GeM seller registration, step by step

1. **Open gem.gov.in**, click **Sign Up** and choose **Seller / Service Provider**.
2. **Pick your business type** (proprietorship, partnership, company and so on), read the prerequisites and accept the terms.
3. **Enter the organisation name** exactly as it appears on your business PAN.
4. **Verify your identity** with Aadhaar OTP (on the Aadhaar-linked mobile) or with your PAN, date of birth and a mobile OTP.
5. **Fill in personal details** and confirm you are the key person of the organisation.
6. **Verify your email** with the OTP GeM sends.
7. **Create your user ID and password.** Choose the user ID carefully; it cannot be changed later.

That completes registration. You land on the seller dashboard, but you cannot list products until the profile is complete.

### Complete the seller profile

GeM checks each entry against official databases:

1. **Business PAN**, entered exactly as on the PAN. It cannot be edited afterwards.
2. **Business details**, MSE status (checked against Udyam) and startup status (DPIIT).
3. **Addresses** and GSTIN if you have one.
4. **Bank account** verification.
5. **Declarations:** e-invoice, and Rule 144(xi) of the General Financial Rules (bidders from countries sharing a land border), each confirmed by OTP.
6. **Income tax return details**, matched against your filed return.

Common snag: the Udyam check fails if the name, constitution or PAN on GeM does not match your Udyam certificate exactly. Once the profile is complete, you can add **secondary users** for staff who handle catalogues, bids or orders.

## GeM registration fees and caution money in 2026

| Item | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Seller registration | Free |
| Caution money | **None.** Exempted for all sellers irrespective of turnover |
| Catalogue and product listing | Free |
| Vendor assessment (where required) | From ₹871.61 plus GST, by turnover, paid to RITES |
| Orders up to ₹10 lakh | No transaction charge |
| Milestone charge, once your orders in a financial year cross ₹20 lakh | ₹10,000 plus GST, once a year |
| Orders above ₹10 lakh, after the ₹20 lakh milestone | 0.30% of order value, capped at ₹3 lakh per order |

**About caution money.** Until 2025, sellers kept a turnover-based caution money deposit with GeM before listing or bidding. GeM first reduced it, then exempted all sellers; its own announcements say no caution money is required. Many websites still list the old slabs, and some agents still collect it as a "fee". Do not pay it. Sellers who deposited it earlier can withdraw it from their dashboard.

**Beware of agents.** GeM warns about callers who promise bid wins for payment. You can register yourself, and GeM's helpdesk (1800-419-3436) is free.

## Worked example: what GeM charges a small seller in a year

Rakesh runs a stationery and office supplies business. In one financial year he wins five GeM orders:

| Order | Value | Running total | Charges |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | ₹4,00,000 | ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| 2 | ₹7,00,000 | ₹11,00,000 | Nil |
| 3 | ₹8,00,000 | ₹19,00,000 | Nil |
| 4 | ₹6,00,000 | ₹25,00,000 | Crosses ₹20 lakh: milestone charge ₹10,000 + 18% GST = **₹11,800**. Order is under ₹10 lakh, so no transaction charge |
| 5 | ₹15,00,000 | ₹40,00,000 | Above ₹10 lakh: 0.30% × ₹15,00,000 = ₹4,500 + 18% GST (₹810) = **₹5,310** |

Total charges for the year: ₹11,800 + ₹5,310 = **₹17,110** on ₹40 lakh of orders, or about **0.43%**. A seller whose orders stay under ₹20 lakh in the year pays nothing at all.

Charges fall due soon after the order is awarded, and GeM can suspend an account with unpaid charges, so build them into your price for large orders.

## GeM vendor assessment: who needs it

**Vendor assessment** is a check of a manufacturer's factory, capacity and quality, carried out by **RITES Ltd** on GeM's behalf. It is mandatory for **OEMs (original equipment manufacturers)** in categories where GeM requires it, and needed before you can get your own brand approved in those categories.

| Seller turnover (OEM) | Vendor assessment fee |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹20 lakh | ₹871.61 + GST |
| Above ₹20 lakh up to ₹1 crore | ₹1,743.22 + GST |
| Above ₹1 crore | ₹3,486.44 + GST |

Key points from GeM's Vendor Validation Policy:

- The assessment is expected to take about **15 working days**, though GeM does not guarantee it.
- An eligible OEM can apply for an **exemption** instead, which RITES decides in about **2 working days**.
- The report is valid for **3 years**.

**Resellers** (shops and distributors selling other brands) generally do not need vendor assessment to list. In many categories, though, the brand's OEM must authorise you as a reseller on GeM before your catalogue goes live, so talk to your distributor or brand first. Fees and categories change with each version of the policy, so check the current Vendor Validation Policy on gem.gov.in before you pay.

## GeM for MSMEs: what Udyam actually gets you

GeM shows a lot of "MSE" labels, and the benefits are real, but they depend on **what kind of MSE you are**.

The **Public Procurement Policy for Micro and Small Enterprises Order, 2012** gives MSEs:

- **25% of annual central government procurement** reserved as a target for MSEs, including **4% for MSEs owned by SC/ST entrepreneurs** and **3% for MSEs owned by women**.
- **Exemption from earnest money deposit (EMD)** and free tender documents.
- **Purchase preference:** an MSE quoting within **L1 + 15%** can supply up to **25% of the tender** if it matches the lowest (L1) price, where L1 is not an MSE.

**Example:** a bid for 1,000 office chairs. The lowest bid, from a large company, is ₹2,000 a chair. An MSE manufacturer quotes ₹2,250. The band is ₹2,000 × 1.15 = ₹2,300, so the MSE is inside it. If it agrees to match ₹2,000, it can supply up to 250 chairs, worth ₹5,00,000.

**The catch for shops and traders.** The policy covers goods **produced** and services **rendered** by MSEs. The Ministry of MSME's own FAQ says **traders, distributors and sole agents are excluded** from it. Retail and wholesale traders can hold Udyam registration since July 2021, but for them it brings priority sector lending only. So a stationery shop reselling branded goods should not expect L1 + 15% purchase preference, and should read each bid's terms on EMD and MSE exemptions rather than assume them. A manufacturer or service provider with Udyam gets the full set.

**Other relaxations you will see in bid documents:**

- **"MSE exemption for years of experience and turnover"** and a matching **startup exemption**. The buyer decides whether to allow these in each bid; the bid document says Yes or No.
- **Make in India preference:** local suppliers with higher local content (Class I) get purchase preference over others under the Public Procurement (Make in India) Order.

## When and how GeM sellers get paid

The flow for a typical order:

1. **Order placed** (direct purchase or after a bid). Accept it on GeM.
2. **Deliver** within the delivery period. Late delivery attracts liquidated damages.
3. The consignee inspects and issues the **CRAC** (consignee receipt-cum-acceptance certificate) on GeM.
4. **Submit your invoice** on GeM against the order.
5. **Payment** reaches your bank account. GeM's general terms require the buyer to pay within **10 days of CRAC** and bill submission.

If payment is late, use GeM's payment escalation process, which needs your CRAC and invoice. **Micro and small manufacturers and service providers** also have the MSMED Act: payment within 45 days of acceptance, compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate on delays, and complaints through the MSME Samadhaan portal. Traders are not covered.

## Tips to win your first GeM bids

1. **Price your catalogue sharply.** Small direct purchases go to the lowest qualifying price, so check similar listings first.
2. **Read the ATC (additional terms and conditions) of every bid.** Most rejections come from a missing document or OEM authorisation asked for there, not from price.
3. **Check the experience and turnover criteria**, and upload proof if the bid allows an MSE or startup exemption you qualify for.
4. **Start small and deliver on time.** One late delivery with penalties can wipe out the margin on several orders.
5. **Know your landed cost**: delivery to the consignee, GST, GeM charges and the wait for payment.
6. **Check new bids daily.** Many GeM bids close within a week.

## FAQ

### Is GeM portal registration free?

Yes. There is no fee to register as a seller and no fee to list products. Caution money has also been exempted for all sellers. You pay only vendor assessment (if your category needs it) and order charges once your yearly orders cross ₹20 lakh.

### Do I need GST to register on GeM?

No, GSTIN is optional for registration. But you need it for supplies to another state, and most bids ask for it, so register under GST if you plan to sell seriously on GeM. Our [GST registration process](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-process) guide explains how.

### Is caution money still required on GeM?

No. GeM has exempted all sellers from caution money, irrespective of turnover. If someone asks you to pay caution money to register, treat it as a red flag. Sellers who paid earlier can withdraw it from their dashboard.

### Can a small shop or trader sell on GeM?

Yes. A trader can register and sell as a reseller. In many categories you need the brand's OEM authorisation on GeM. Traders are excluded from the MSE Public Procurement Policy, so do not count on L1 + 15% purchase preference.

### How much does GeM vendor assessment cost?

For OEMs, ₹871.61 plus GST for turnover up to ₹20 lakh, ₹1,743.22 plus GST up to ₹1 crore and ₹3,486.44 plus GST above that, paid to RITES Ltd. Check the current Vendor Validation Policy on gem.gov.in before paying.

### How soon does a GeM seller get paid?

GeM's terms require payment within 10 days of the consignee issuing the CRAC and you submitting the bill. Delays can be escalated through GeM's payment escalation process.

## The bottom line

GeM portal registration costs nothing, needs no caution money, and can be done in an afternoon with your PAN, bank details and tax returns. The real work starts after sign-up: a complete profile, sharp catalogue prices, careful reading of each bid's terms and on-time delivery. Know which benefits you actually qualify for, a manufacturer with Udyam gets far more than a trader, and build GeM's charges and payment cycle into your prices.
