Meena has run a tailoring corner from home for six years. Her customers keep asking where to buy ready kurtas and kids' clothes, and a 300 sq ft shop has come up for rent on the main road near the school. She has about ₹8 lakh between savings and family, and three questions: what kind of clothing shop, how much stock, and how much will it really make?

Most guides that rank for this topic are about launching an online clothing *brand*. This one is for a physical shop, or a shop with an online side: the shop models, a rough budget, sourcing, licences, the GST rule that changed in September 2025, margins, and the stock and exchange decisions that decide whether the cash comes back.

## Quick answer

- **Pick one model first:** family readymade garments, a boutique, a saree and ethnic store, kids wear, or a shop with an online side. Each needs different space, stock and skills.
- **Rough investment:** about **₹7 lakh to ₹15 lakh** for a 300-400 sq ft readymade shop in a tier-2 town, by our estimate. Stock is the biggest line. A home boutique can start far smaller.
- **Licences:** Shop and Establishment registration, a trade licence where your city requires one, Udyam (free, optional) and **GST** once turnover crosses the limit or if you sell to other states.
- **GST on clothes since 22 September 2025:** **5%** on apparel with a sale value up to **₹2,500 per piece**, **18%** above that. The rate depends on each piece's price, not the bill total.
- **Margins:** roughly 30% to 45% of the selling price before discounts on readymade wear, by our estimate. End-of-season sales and unsold sizes are what pull it down.
- **What decides success:** buying the right sizes, festive timing, and turning stock over before the season ends.

## Step 1: Choose the kind of clothing shop

Decide this first; it sets your space, stock and suppliers.

| Model | What you sell | Typical space | Rough start-up | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Readymade garments (family)** | Shirts, jeans, kurtas, nightwear, innerwear for men, women and kids | 300-600 sq ft | ₹7 lakh to ₹15 lakh | Too many sizes and styles for the money |
| **Boutique** | Your own designs, stitching, alterations, a small ready range | 150-300 sq ft, or from home | ₹1.5 lakh to ₹5 lakh | Depends on your skill and a good karigar |
| **Saree and ethnic wear** | Sarees, suits, dress materials, lehengas | 300-500 sq ft | ₹8 lakh to ₹20 lakh | High-value stock that sells slowly outside seasons |
| **Kids wear** | 0-14 years, school basics, festive and party wear | 200-400 sq ft | ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh | Kids outgrow sizes, so the size range is wide |
| **Online plus offline** | Any of the above, also sold on WhatsApp, Instagram or a marketplace | Shop plus packing space | Add ₹50,000 to ₹2 lakh | Returns, photos and courier costs |

All figures here are rough estimates for a tier-2 town and vary widely with city, rent and how much stock you start with.

**Takeaway:** a narrower shop is easier to stock well. A kids-wear shop with deep sizes in 40 styles beats a family store with one piece each of 200 styles.

## Step 3: Source from wholesale markets

Most small shops buy from wholesalers, not factories. India's main clothing hubs each have a speciality:

| Hub | Known for |
| --- | --- |
| **Surat** (Gujarat) | Sarees, dress materials, synthetic and printed fabrics |
| **Tiruppur** (Tamil Nadu) | Cotton knitwear: T-shirts, innerwear, nightwear, kids wear |
| **Ludhiana** (Punjab) | Woollens, hosiery, sweaters and winter wear |
| **Gandhi Nagar, Delhi** | A very large readymade garments wholesale market: jeans, menswear, kids wear |
| **Mumbai** | Wholesale fabric and readymade markets, and many garment manufacturers |
| **Burrabazar, Kolkata** | One of eastern India's biggest wholesale cloth and garment markets |

You do not have to travel for everything. Many wholesalers sell through agents and WhatsApp catalogues, and your nearest city usually has distributors with similar goods at a slightly higher price.

**Practical rules for the first buying trip:**

- **Buy shallow, then reorder.** Take a few sets of many styles rather than many pieces of a few. Reorder the ones that sell.
- **Understand "sets".** Wholesalers often sell a style only as a set of sizes, such as one each of S, M, L and XL, or in a fixed ratio. Ask for the set break-up before you agree.
- **Get a proper invoice.** If you are GST-registered, a tax invoice with the supplier's GSTIN lets you claim input tax credit.
- **Ask about exchange.** Some wholesalers take back slow pieces or replace defects within a period. Get the terms in writing on WhatsApp.

## Step 5: GST on clothing since September 2025

From **22 September 2025**, the GST rate on readymade clothing depends on the sale value of each piece. PIB's factsheet put it simply: ready-made apparel up to ₹2,500 per piece is at 5%, and the earlier limit was ₹1,000. The rate notification uses the words "sale value not exceeding Rs 2500 per piece".

| Item | Before 22 Sept 2025 | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clothes up to ₹1,000 per piece | 5% | **5%** |
| Clothes ₹1,001 to ₹2,500 per piece | 12% | **5%** |
| Clothes above ₹2,500 per piece | 12% | **18%** |
| Most fabrics (cotton, silk, wool, man-made) | 5% | **5%** |

Our full [GST rates list](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-rates-list) has the rates for footwear and other goods.

**The rate follows each piece, not the bill.** A bill can carry both 5% and 18%, and that is correct.

| Item | Taxable value | Rate | GST |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 cotton kurtas at ₹1,200 each | ₹2,400 | 5% | ₹120 (₹60 CGST + ₹60 SGST) |
| 1 blazer | ₹3,200 | 18% | ₹576 (₹288 CGST + ₹288 SGST) |
| **Total** | **₹5,600** | | **₹696** |

The customer pays ₹6,296. Each kurta is ₹1,200, so 5% applies; the ₹3,200 blazer is 18%.

**Three billing points that trip up clothing shops:**

1. **Your sale price decides your rate, not your supplier's.** If you bought a jacket at 5% and sell it for ₹2,900 before GST, you charge 18%. If you clear an 18% piece at ₹1,900 in a sale, the discounted value shown on the bill is what counts, so it is 5%.
2. **Watch tag prices that include GST.** A tag of ₹2,625 including GST works out to ₹2,500 before tax at 5%, so it stays at 5%. Tags above ₹2,950 work out above ₹2,500 even at 18%, so they are clearly 18%. Tags between about ₹2,626 and ₹2,950 fall in a band where neither rate gives a consistent answer. The simple fix is to price outside that band, or ask your CA how to treat it.
3. **Sarees and dress materials need a check.** A plain woven saree is often classified as fabric, which is 5% whatever the price, but sources disagree on embroidered and made-up pieces. Use the HSN code on your supplier's invoice and confirm with your CA.

## Step 6: Understand your margins

Clothing margins look generous on the tag and shrink at season end. Work out margin on the selling price before GST; our [profit margin formula](https://billzap.app/blog/profit-margin-formula) guide explains why margin and markup are different numbers.

**One shirt, worked through.** You buy a shirt for ₹400 plus 5% GST, which you claim back as credit if registered. You tag it at ₹699 including GST.

| Line | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sale value before GST | ₹699 ÷ 1.05 | ₹665.71 |
| Cost | | ₹400.00 |
| Gross profit | ₹665.71 − ₹400 | ₹265.71 |
| Margin on sale | ₹265.71 ÷ ₹665.71 | **39.9%** |
| Markup on cost | ₹265.71 ÷ ₹400 | 66.4% |

Now the whole shop. Rough figures for a small tier-2 store, to show the shape, not promise a result.

| Monthly line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Sales (before GST) | ₹4,00,000 |
| Blended gross margin after discounts and dead stock, say 35% | ₹1,40,000 |
| Rent | ₹30,000 |
| Two staff | ₹30,000 |
| Electricity | ₹6,000 |
| Packaging, marketing, sundries | ₹10,000 |
| **Left before owner's salary, loan repayment and income tax** | **₹64,000** |

Higher sales improve this quickly because the costs barely change. Let a third of the stock go unsold and it collapses.

## Step 7: Stock by size and colour

A style is not one item; it is five sizes in three colours, and you only make money if the right ones are on the rack.

- **Plan a size curve.** In most adult ranges the middle sizes sell most. A starting ratio such as 1 S : 2 M : 2 L : 1 XL is common; adjust it to what your customers actually buy after the first month.
- **Keep core colours deep.** Black, navy, white and denim sell all year; buy trend colours lightly.
- **Track "broken sizes".** A style left with only S and XXL is dead stock even if the rack looks full. Move it to a sale rail early.
- **Count sell-through every week.** Pieces sold ÷ pieces received, per style. Reorder winners while the season is still on.

## Step 8: Plan around the festive and wedding calendar

A large share of the year is sold in a few weeks. Order early; good styles sell out at the wholesaler first.

| Season | What sells | When to buy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Durga Puja, Navratri, Diwali | Ethnic wear, kurtas, sarees, kids' festive wear | 6 to 10 weeks before |
| Wedding season (roughly November to February, varies by region) | Sarees, lehengas, sherwanis, party wear | Before November |
| Winter (north India) | Jackets, sweaters, thermals | September to October |
| Eid, Onam, Pongal and local festivals | Varies by region | 6 to 8 weeks before |
| School reopening | Kids basics, school socks and vests | Before the term starts |
| End-of-season sales (January and July) | Clearance of last season | Plan the discount, not the stock |

## Step 9: Write an exchange policy before day one

Exchanges are part of selling clothes, especially gifts. Decide the rules before the first argument.

- **Exchange within 7 days** with the bill and tags intact.
- **No exchange** on innerwear, altered or stitched-to-measure items, and clearance stock.
- **Store credit rather than cash** where possible.
- **Print it on the bill and display it at the counter**, so there is no argument later.

A "no exchange" sign does not remove your responsibility for genuinely defective goods under consumer law, so replace a torn seam without fuss. If you are GST-registered and refund money on a taxed sale, ask your CA how to record it; BillZap does not create credit notes.

## FAQ

### How much investment is needed to start a clothing shop?

Roughly ₹7 lakh to ₹15 lakh for a 300-400 sq ft readymade garments shop in a tier-2 town, by our estimate, with opening stock the largest part. A home boutique can start with ₹1.5 lakh to ₹5 lakh. Rent and stock depth change the figure a lot.

### What is the profit margin in a cloth shop?

On readymade wear, roughly 30% to 45% of the selling price before discounts, by our estimate. After end-of-season sales and unsold sizes, a blended 30% to 35% is a more realistic planning figure.

### What is the GST rate on clothes in 2026?

5% on apparel with a sale value up to ₹2,500 per piece and 18% above ₹2,500, since 22 September 2025. The rate is decided per piece, so one bill can have both rates.

### Do I need GST registration to open a clothing shop?

Not at the start if you sell only within your state and turnover is below the limit, which is ₹40 lakh for goods in most states. You need it once you cross the limit or sell to other states, and usually to sell on marketplaces.

### Which clothing business is best for a beginner?

One with a narrow range you understand. Kids wear and a focused readymade range are easier to stock well than a full family store. A boutique suits someone with stitching skills.

## The bottom line

A clothing shop is a stock business more than a fashion business. Choose a narrow model, keep a cash cushion, buy shallow, reorder what sells and time buying to the festive calendar. On bills, remember the 2025 rule: 5% up to ₹2,500 per piece, 18% above, judged piece by piece.
