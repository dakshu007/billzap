Search "Mudra loan" and the first page is official: the Mudra website, JanSamarth, UMANG and bank product pages. They tell you the limits and link to a form. What none of them does is help a shop owner decide: **should I borrow, how much, from whom, and what will make the bank say yes?**

This guide is for a kirana, medical, mobile or hardware shop owner thinking about a loan for more stock, a fridge, a counter or a second outlet: the four categories, eligibility, documents, interest, where to apply, what lenders check, and the scams to avoid.

## Quick answer

- **Mudra loans** are business loans under the **Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY)** for non-farm, income-generating small businesses, including shops. They are **collateral-free** and given by banks, NBFCs, MFIs, RRBs and small finance banks, not by MUDRA itself.
- **Four categories:** **Shishu** up to ₹50,000; **Kishore** above ₹50,000 up to ₹5 lakh; **Tarun** above ₹5 lakh up to ₹10 lakh; **Tarun Plus** ₹10 lakh to ₹20 lakh, only for those who have taken and repaid a Tarun loan.
- **Interest is not fixed by the government.** Each lender sets its own rate. There is **no subsidy** under Mudra.
- **Documents:** ID and address proof, proof of the business (such as Udyam, shop licence or GST certificate), and for Kishore and above, bank statements, past accounts or returns, and projections for larger loans.
- **Apply** at your bank branch, or online through **JanSamarth** (jansamarth.in). Never pay an agent.

## What PM Mudra Yojana is, and what it is not

The Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana was launched on **8 April 2015**. MUDRA (Micro Units Development and Refinance Agency) is a **refinancing** institution: it funds and supports the lenders, and the lenders give the loans. That matters, because **you never get a Mudra loan from "Mudra"**. You get it from a bank or other lender, and it is that lender's loan, with its rate and its terms.

The loans are for **income-generating activities in manufacturing, trading and services**, and activities allied to agriculture. Shopkeepers, fruit and vegetable vendors, repair shops, food-service units and small manufacturers are all named as target borrowers in lenders' own scheme documents.

Three things Mudra is **not**:

- **Not a grant or subsidy.** It is a loan you repay with interest.
- **Not for personal use** such as a wedding, a house or a vehicle for the family.
- **Not cheap by default.** The rate depends on the lender and on you.

## The four categories: Shishu, Kishore, Tarun, Tarun Plus

The loan limit under PMMY was raised from ₹10 lakh to ₹20 lakh, effective **24 October 2024**, with the new Tarun Plus category.

| Category | Loan amount | Typical use for a shop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shishu** | Up to ₹50,000 | A small stock top-up, a weighing scale, a second-hand fridge, a cart or kiosk |
| **Kishore** | Above ₹50,000 up to ₹5 lakh | Stocking up for the season, shelving and counters, a visi-cooler, setting up a new shop |
| **Tarun** | Above ₹5 lakh up to ₹10 lakh | A bigger outlet, a wholesale line, a delivery vehicle for the business |
| **Tarun Plus** | Above ₹10 lakh up to ₹20 lakh | Expansion, for borrowers who have **taken and repaid a Tarun loan** |

The names describe the stage of the business: Shishu (infant), Kishore (adolescent), Tarun (young adult). You start small, repay well and move up; Tarun Plus exists only for people who have already repaid a Tarun loan.

**A practical rule:** borrow for what will earn its own repayment. A ₹1.5 lakh loan for stock that turns over every three weeks is a different risk from ₹1.5 lakh spent on a signboard and an interior.

The loan can be a **term loan** (for equipment or setup), a **working capital limit** such as an overdraft or cash credit, or a mix. For working capital, many banks issue a **Mudra card**, a RuPay debit card linked to the overdraft: you draw when you need stock money and repay when sales come in, so interest is charged only on what you actually use.

## Mudra loan eligibility

You can apply if:

- you are an **individual, proprietorship, partnership firm, private or public company**, or other entity running a **non-farm, income-generating** micro or small business;
- the business is in **trading, manufacturing or services** (a shop is trading);
- you are **not a defaulter** with any bank or financial institution and have a **satisfactory credit record**;
- for some activities, you have the skills or experience the business needs.

Both **new and existing businesses** can apply. A new shop will usually find Shishu or a modest Kishore loan easier than a large one, simply because there is no track record yet.

**GST registration is not a condition of the scheme.** Lenders want proof that the business exists; a GST certificate is one such proof, but a free Udyam certificate or shop licence also works. Whether you need GST at all depends on your turnover; see the [GST registration process](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-process) guide if you are close to the limit.

## Mudra loan interest rate and charges

There is **no single Mudra interest rate**. The interest rates are deregulated: each lender charges as per its own policy, and banks have been advised to keep rates reasonable. In practice:

- **Banks** usually price business loans as a benchmark rate plus a margin that depends on your profile.
- **NBFCs and microfinance institutions** usually charge noticeably more than banks.
- **Processing fees:** for **Shishu loans, most banks waive** the processing fee. For Kishore and above, a processing fee and documentation charges are usually as per the bank's policy.
- **Collateral:** none is required. Loans are covered by the government's **Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units (CGFMU)**, which now also covers Tarun Plus. The lender will still take a charge on the stock or equipment bought with the loan.

Always ask for the **all-in cost**: interest rate, whether it is fixed or floating, processing fee, any guarantee or insurance charge, and the foreclosure charge if you repay early.

### What the rate difference means in rupees

Take a **₹2,00,000** Kishore loan repaid over **3 years** (36 EMIs). The two rates below are illustrations only, not offers.

| | Rate 11% a year | Rate 24% a year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly EMI | ₹6,548 | ₹7,847 |
| Total interest over 3 years | ₹35,719 | ₹82,477 |

The same loan costs **₹46,758 more** at the higher rate. So try your own bank first, even if an app promises faster approval.

### Can your shop carry the EMI?

Work it out from profit, not sales. Suppose your shop sells ₹4,00,000 a month at a 12% gross margin: that is ₹48,000 gross profit. Take away rent, electricity, a helper's salary and other costs of, say, ₹28,000, and ₹20,000 is left. An EMI of ₹6,548 takes about a third of that. Possible, but a bad month will pinch. If your figures look like this, a smaller loan or a longer tenure is safer. ## Mudra loan documents

The official checklist differs a little by category.

| Document | Shishu | Kishore, Tarun, Tarun Plus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Proof of identity** (Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID, driving licence or passport) | Yes | Yes |
| **Proof of residence** (recent electricity or phone bill, property tax receipt, Aadhaar, voter ID, passport, bank passbook) | Yes | Yes |
| **Proof of the business** (licences, registration certificates or other documents showing the business's ownership, identity and address) | Yes | Yes |
| **Bank statement of the last six months** from your existing bank, if any | Not listed, but some banks ask | Yes |
| **Last two years' balance sheets** with income tax or sales tax (GST) returns | No | Yes, for existing businesses |
| **Projected balance sheet** (one year for working capital; the loan period for a term loan) | No | Yes, for loans of ₹2 lakh and above |

On top of the list, banks commonly ask for **photographs**, a **quotation** for any equipment or fittings you plan to buy, and a short note on what the money is for.

**Good business proof for a shop:** an [Udyam registration](https://billzap.app/blog/udyam-registration) certificate (free and online), your [shop and establishment registration](https://billzap.app/blog/shop-act-registration) or trade licence, a GST certificate if you have one, and an FSSAI registration for food businesses. Having two of these, with the same name and address as your bank account, makes the file much smoother.

## How to apply for a Mudra loan

You have three routes.

1. **Your own bank branch.** The simplest route for most shopkeepers. Ask for the PMMY or Mudra loan application form for your category, fill it in, attach the documents and submit. The bank where your shop's account already runs has your history, which helps.
2. **JanSamarth portal (jansamarth.in).** The government's common portal for credit-linked government schemes lists PMMY under business loans. You check eligibility and apply online, and a participating lender takes the application forward.
3. **Other lenders.** NBFCs, small finance banks and microfinance institutions also lend under PMMY. Check that the lender is genuine (its own website, a branch you can visit) and compare its rate with a bank's.

The Mudra website (mudra.org.in) and the **Mudra Mitra** app explain the scheme and have sample forms, but MUDRA does not take loan applications from borrowers directly.

## What lenders look at, and how to look good on paper

A bank officer deciding on a small shop's loan wants to answer three questions: does this business exist, does it earn enough, and will this person repay?

**Your credit history.** Your CIBIL score and your record on any past loan, card or phone EMI. Clear small overdues before you apply.

**Your bank statement.** It is the one record the bank trusts most, because it did not come from you. Shops that take UPI into the business account and deposit cash regularly show a steady flow of money in. A shop that takes everything in cash and banks little looks much smaller on paper than it is.

**A record of sales.** This is where most small shops are weakest. A notebook of daily totals is better than nothing; numbered bills with monthly sales and profit totals that agree with the bank statement are much better. If your sales report says ₹4 lakh a month and your bank shows ₹3.5 lakh of UPI and cash deposits, the story holds together.

**Proof the business is real.** Matching names and addresses across Udyam, shop licence, GST (if any) and the bank account. A photo of the shop helps; officers often visit for larger loans.

**A clear use for the money.** "₹2 lakh for Diwali and wedding-season stock, repaid from the extra sales over 24 months" is a better answer than "for business".

## Red flags: Mudra loan scams to avoid

Fraudsters use Mudra's name because people searching for it are often first-time borrowers in a hurry. PIB Fact Check has repeatedly flagged fake "Mudra loan approval" letters and WhatsApp messages. Watch for these:

- **An agent or "consultant" asking for a fee** to get your loan sanctioned. There is no need for one; you apply directly at the bank or on JanSamarth.
- **Upfront charges before disbursal** called "file charge", "insurance", "stamp duty" or "GST on loan", to be paid to a personal UPI ID or account. Genuine charges are deducted or paid at the bank, against a receipt.
- **An approval letter or message on WhatsApp, SMS or social media** saying your "Mudra loan of ₹5 lakh is approved". MUDRA does not lend directly and does not send approvals this way.
- **Look-alike websites** with "mudra" in the address that ask for your bank details, OTP or Aadhaar. The official sites are mudra.org.in and jansamarth.in, and your bank's own website.
- **Promises of a subsidy or "no repayment".** Mudra loans carry no subsidy.

If you have paid someone, report it to the cybercrime helpline 1930 or at cybercrime.gov.in and tell your bank straight away.

## After you get the loan

Pay every EMI on time, ideally by auto-debit from the account your sales go into, and use the money for what you said you would. A clean repayment record is what makes the next loan easier: from Kishore to Tarun, and from Tarun to Tarun Plus.

## FAQ

### What is the maximum Mudra loan amount?

₹20 lakh, under the Tarun Plus category, available only to borrowers who have taken and repaid a Tarun loan. For everyone else the ceiling is ₹10 lakh under Tarun.

### What is the Mudra loan interest rate?

There is no fixed rate. Interest rates are deregulated and each bank, NBFC or MFI charges as per its own policy. Compare at least two lenders and ask for the all-in cost.

### Do I need collateral or a guarantor for a Mudra loan?

No collateral is required. The loans are covered by the Credit Guarantee Fund for Micro Units. The lender will usually take a charge on the stock or equipment bought with the loan.

### Can I get a Mudra loan without GST registration?

Yes. GST is not a condition of the scheme. You need proof that the business exists, which can be an Udyam certificate, a shop licence or another registration. Register for GST only if your turnover or business type requires it.

### Can a new shop get a Mudra loan?

Yes, Mudra loans are for both new and existing businesses. A new shop with no track record will usually find Shishu or a smaller Kishore loan easier, and should bring a clear plan and quotations for what it will buy.

### Is there any subsidy on a Mudra loan?

No. Mudra loans carry no subsidy. Anyone promising a Mudra subsidy or a loan you do not have to repay is not genuine.

### Where can I apply for a Mudra loan online?

On the JanSamarth portal (jansamarth.in), or on your bank's own website. Do not apply through links received on WhatsApp or SMS.

## The bottom line

A Mudra loan is an ordinary business loan with two helpful features: no collateral and a clear path from small to larger loans. Start with your own bank, borrow for something that earns its repayment, keep the EMI well within your monthly profit, and never pay an agent. The single best thing you can do before applying is to have a few months of clean, numbered sales records that match your bank statement.
