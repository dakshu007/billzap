A stationery shop is one of the few small businesses where the customers are guaranteed to come back. Every school year needs new notebooks, every exam needs pens and geometry boxes, every office runs out of A4 paper, and every college student needs something printed by nine the next morning. The work is steady, the stock does not spoil, and the shop can start small.

The catch is that margins on fast-moving items are thin, and the money is made in the right location, the right product mix and a busy xerox counter. GST also changed in September 2025: notebooks, pencils and erasers became tax-free, while pens stayed at 18%. This guide covers the setup in order, with a labelled budget and margin maths you can adapt to your own town.

## Quick answer

- **Location decides everything.** The best spots are within walking distance of schools, coaching centres, colleges or office clusters, ideally on the route students and staff walk.
- **Investment:** roughly **₹2 lakh to ₹6 lakh** for a small shop with one photocopier, most of it opening stock and the machine. Our labelled budget below adds up to about ₹4 lakh.
- **Registrations:** shop and establishment registration, a municipal trade licence where your city requires one, GST once you cross the limit, and Udyam (optional, free).
- **GST since 22 September 2025:** exercise books and notebooks, pencils, crayons, sharpeners, erasers, and printed maps, charts and globes are **nil**. Geometry, mathematical and colour boxes are **5%**. Pens, A4 paper and most office items are **18%**.
- **Xerox and printing is a service**, taxed at **18%**. Add it and your GST registration limit drops from ₹40 lakh to **₹20 lakh** of total turnover.
- **Seasons:** the biggest rush is around school reopening (April for CBSE schools, June for most state boards), with smaller peaks before exams and festivals.
- **Margins:** thin on branded pens and notebooks, better on unbranded items, gifts, art supplies and print services. A blended gross margin in the range of 20% to 30% is a reasonable target, not a guarantee.

## Step 1: Choose your location and format

The location sets your customers, and your customers set your stock. Decide which of these shops you are opening:

| Format | Where | Main customers | What sells most |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **School-gate shop** | Within 200 to 500 metres of one or more schools | Parents and children | Notebooks, pencils, geometry boxes, art and craft items, school-list kits |
| **College and coaching shop** | Near colleges, hostels, coaching centres | Students | Xerox, printouts, spiral binding, registers, pens, files, project material |
| **Office-area shop** | Commercial streets, near courts and government offices | Offices, advocates, small businesses | A4 paper, files, toner, stamps, registers, printing |
| **Neighbourhood stationery and gifts** | Residential markets | Families | Stationery plus gift wrap, greeting cards, toys, small gifts |

**Before you sign a lease**, spend two mornings and one evening counting footfall at the spot during school opening and closing times. Note how many stationery shops already serve the area and what they lack: no xerox, no colour printing, closed early, poor stock of art supplies. That gap is your opening.

A school-gate shop can work in **100 to 200 square feet**. A shop with a xerox counter needs space for the machine, a binding table and a queue, so plan for **150 to 300 square feet**.

## Step 2: Registrations and licences

A stationery shop needs no special product licence. The usual set:

| Registration | Needed? | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Shop and establishment** | Yes, in most states, within a set time of opening | State labour department portal. See our [shop act registration](https://billzap.app/blog/shop-act-registration) guide |
| **Trade licence** | Where your municipal body requires one | City corporation or municipality |
| **GST** | Compulsory once turnover crosses the limit; voluntary before | gst.gov.in |
| **Udyam** | Optional, free | udyamregistration.gov.in. See our [Udyam registration](https://billzap.app/blog/udyam-registration) guide |
| **Current account** | Practical necessity | Any bank, using two of the registrations above as business proof |

**The GST limit for a stationery shop.** A shop selling only goods within its state uses the **₹40 lakh** limit in most states. The moment you add **xerox, printing, binding or lamination**, you supply services too, and the limit for your **whole turnover** becomes **₹20 lakh** (₹10 lakh in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura). Nil-rated sales such as notebooks still count towards the limit. Our guide to the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) explains the rules.

Below the limit, many small shops stay unregistered and charge no GST. If you register, small shops can consider the composition scheme, which also allows a limited amount of services (up to 10% of the previous year's turnover or ₹5 lakh, whichever is higher). Whether it suits a shop with a large xerox counter is a question for your CA; see our [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide.

## Step 3: Budget the setup

This is an **illustrative budget** for a 200 sq ft shop near two schools in a tier-2 town, with one used photocopier. Rents, deposits and stock levels vary widely by city; replace each line with your own quotes.

| Item | Illustrative amount |
| --- | --- |
| Rent deposit (assumed 3 months at ₹12,000) | ₹36,000 |
| Shelving, glass counter, display racks | ₹60,000 |
| Signboard, lights, fan, minor civil work | ₹25,000 |
| Photocopier (used, multifunction) | ₹70,000 |
| Printer for colour prints, laminator, spiral binding machine | ₹30,000 |
| Computer or laptop for printouts | ₹25,000 |
| Opening stock | ₹1,25,000 |
| Registrations, first month's rent, miscellaneous | ₹24,000 |
| **Total** | **₹3,95,000** |

Check: 36,000 + 60,000 + 25,000 + 70,000 + 30,000 + 25,000 + 1,25,000 + 24,000 = ₹3,95,000.

A school-gate shop without a xerox counter can start well under ₹2 lakh. A larger shop with new machines, a big art-supply section and gifts can cross ₹6 lakh. Many photocopier dealers also rent machines on a per-copy or monthly basis, which cuts the upfront cost.

## Step 4: Decide your product mix

Stock what your format sells, in depth, before you add range. A sensible starting mix for a school-and-college shop:

| Category | Examples | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **School basics** | Notebooks, long books, drawing books, pencils, erasers, sharpeners, scales | Fast moving, thin margin, must never run out |
| **Writing** | Ball pens, gel pens, refills, markers, highlighters | Branded sells on name; keep a cheaper unbranded line |
| **Geometry and art** | Geometry boxes, crayons, colour pencils, sketch pens, watercolours, chart paper | Higher margin; peaks around projects |
| **Paper and files** | A4 reams, registers, files, folders, envelopes | Office and college demand |
| **Craft and project** | Glue, fevicol, glitter, thermocol, foam sheets, project files | Strong in exam and project seasons |
| **Services** | Xerox, printouts, colour prints, binding, lamination, scanning | Highest margin per rupee of space |
| **Seasonal and gifts** | Rakhi, Diwali cards, gift wrap, small gifts, calendars, diaries | Fills slow months |

**Get the school lists.** Before each new session, many schools hand parents a list of notebooks and supplies. Ask parents or teachers for these lists early, and make ready "class kits" in bags. They sell in minutes during reopening week and save you counter time.

## Step 5: GST rates on stationery after September 2025

The GST rate change effective **22 September 2025** made the core school items tax-free but left pens where they were. Many websites still show the old 12% rates.

| Item | GST rate from 22 Sept 2025 |
| --- | --- |
| Exercise books, notebooks, graph books | **Nil** (was 12%) |
| Pencils, crayons, pastels | **Nil** (was 12%) |
| Pencil sharpeners | **Nil** (was 12%) |
| Erasers | **Nil** (was 5%) |
| Printed maps, charts, atlases, globes | **Nil** (was 12%) |
| Geometry boxes, mathematical boxes, colour boxes | **5%** (was 12%) |
| Pens (ball, gel, fountain), markers | **18%** |
| A4 and copier paper (uncoated, not for exercise books) | **18%** (was 12%) |
| Xerox, printing and similar services | **18%** |

For other items, such as files, glue, staplers, school bags and diaries, follow the HSN and rate on your supplier's bill. Most sit at 18%, but confirm each one rather than guess. Our [SAC code list](https://billzap.app/blog/sac-code-list) covers the service codes; xerox and printing shops commonly use SAC 998595 or a code under heading 9989, so confirm the right one with your CA.

**One thing nil-rated goods change for a registered shop:** GST you pay on expenses shared between taxable and nil-rated sales (shop rent under reverse charge, for example) has to be partly reversed instead of claimed in full. Your CA will handle this in your returns.

### A mixed bill at the counter

A parent buys 5 notebooks at ₹60, a pen pack at ₹59 and a geometry box at ₹126, all at MRP including GST:

| Item | Price | Taxable value | GST |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 notebooks × ₹60 (nil) | ₹300 | ₹300 | Nil |
| Pen pack (18%) | ₹59 | ₹50.00 | ₹9.00 (CGST ₹4.50 + SGST ₹4.50) |
| Geometry box (5%) | ₹126 | ₹120.00 | ₹6.00 (CGST ₹3 + SGST ₹3) |
| **Total** | **₹485** | **₹470** | **₹15** |

Three rates on one bill is normal for a stationery shop, which is why per-item rates set up once in your billing app save mistakes at the counter.

## Step 6: Run the xerox and print counter well

For many stationery shops, the photocopier earns more profit per square foot than any shelf. A rough way to see what yours earns, using **assumed figures** you should replace with your machine's real costs:

| Assumption (per A4 black-and-white page) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Paper: a 500-sheet ream at ₹300 | ₹0.60 |
| Toner, drum and maintenance (assumed) | ₹0.30 |
| **Cost per page** | **₹0.90** |
| Price charged (assumed) | ₹2.00 |
| **Contribution per page** | **₹1.10** |

At 400 pages a day for 26 days, that is 10,400 pages and 10,400 × ₹1.10 = **₹11,440** a month towards rent and salaries, before electricity. Busy shops near colleges and courts do far more in exam and filing seasons.

Practical points:

- **Keep a log or counter reading** every evening. Copies are easy to give away without a bill.
- **Price colour prints, binding and lamination separately.** They carry better margins than plain copies.
- **Charge GST on services if you are registered**, at 18%, even when the customer buys nil-rated notebooks on the same bill.
- **Offer WhatsApp printing**: customers send files to the shop number and collect later. It cuts queues at peak times.

## Step 7: Plan around the school calendar

| When | What happens | What to stock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **March to April** | New session in CBSE and many private schools | Notebooks, class kits, covers, labels |
| **June to July** | Most state-board schools and colleges reopen | Same as above, plus college registers and files |
| **August** | Raksha Bandhan, Independence Day | Rakhis, flags, craft items |
| **September to November** | Projects, half-yearly exams, Diwali | Chart paper, project files, gift wrap, greeting cards |
| **December to January** | New year | Diaries, calendars, planners |
| **February to March** | Board and annual exams | Pens, pencils, exam pads, clear files, admit card printouts |

Academic calendars vary by state and board, so check the reopening dates of the schools near you and order **four to six weeks ahead**. Distributors run short in reopening weeks, and the shop that has stock gets the year's loyal customers.

## Step 8: Buy wholesale, not retail

- **Wholesale stationery markets** exist in most big cities, usually in the old market area. Visit two or three wholesalers before committing and compare prices on your top 30 items.
- **Brand distributors** for notebooks and pens often deliver weekly and offer schemes on volume. Ask about returns of damaged stock and credit days.
- **Buy deep, not wide**, on fast movers like A4 paper, notebooks and pens, and buy small quantities of new or seasonal items until they prove themselves.
- **Keep the purchase bills.** They support your stock records and, if you are registered, your input tax credit.

## Step 9: Understand margins

Margins on stationery are not uniform. As a rough guide, branded notebooks and pens carry thinner margins because the MRP is printed and widely known, while unbranded items, art supplies, gift items and print services carry more. These ranges vary by brand, city and volume, so measure your own: our [profit margin formula](https://billzap.app/blog/profit-margin-formula) guide shows how.

**Worked monthly example (illustrative):**

| Line | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Product sales | ₹2,40,000 |
| Gross margin on products at an assumed 22% | ₹52,800 |
| Xerox and print contribution (from the example above) | ₹11,440 |
| **Total gross profit** | **₹64,240** |
| Rent | ₹12,000 |
| One helper's salary | ₹12,000 |
| Electricity, internet, phone | ₹4,500 |
| Packing, repairs, miscellaneous | ₹3,000 |
| **Net profit before owner's drawings and tax** | **₹32,740** |

Check: 52,800 + 11,440 = 64,240; expenses 12,000 + 12,000 + 4,500 + 3,000 = 31,500; 64,240 − 31,500 = ₹32,740.

Reopening months can be three or four times a slow month, so judge the shop on a full year, not one season.

## FAQ

### How much investment is needed to start a stationery shop?

Roughly ₹2 lakh to ₹6 lakh for a small shop, depending on rent, stock depth and whether you buy a photocopier. A school-gate shop without a xerox counter can start below ₹2 lakh.

### Is a stationery shop profitable?

It can be, if the location has steady student or office footfall. Margins on branded notebooks and pens are thin, so profit comes from volume, unbranded and art items, gifts and a busy xerox and print counter.

### What is the GST on notebooks and pencils?

Nil since 22 September 2025, along with sharpeners, erasers, crayons and printed maps and charts. Geometry boxes are 5%, and pens and A4 paper are 18%.

### What is the GST on xerox?

Photocopying and printing are services taxed at 18%. If you offer them, your GST registration limit is ₹20 lakh of total turnover, not ₹40 lakh.

### Do I need a licence to open a stationery shop?

No special product licence. You need shop and establishment registration in most states, a trade licence where your municipality requires it, and GST registration once you cross the limit.

### When is the best time to open a stationery shop?

Open six to eight weeks before the schools near you reopen, so you are stocked and known before the biggest rush of the year.

## The bottom line

Pick a spot students and office staff walk past, start with deep stock of the basics, add a xerox and print counter, and plan the year around school reopening. Know your GST rates since September 2025, nil for notebooks, pencils and erasers and 18% for pens and paper, and remember that services pull your registration limit down to ₹20 lakh. Track margin by item, and judge the shop over a full school year.
