Two brothers taking over their father's shop, two friends opening a hardware store: most small businesses with more than one owner in India start as a **partnership firm**. It is cheap to set up and needs no annual filing with any registrar.

The catch is that a partnership is only as good as its deed, and an unregistered firm cannot go to court to recover money owed to it. This guide covers what to put in the deed, how to register with your state's Registrar of Firms, what it costs, how the firm is taxed, and when an LLP makes more sense.

## Quick answer

- **Law:** the **Indian Partnership Act, 1932**. Minimum 2 partners, maximum **50**.
- **Steps:** agree terms → write a **partnership deed** on stamp paper → apply to your state's **Registrar of Firms** (in many states, Form No. 1) with the deed and partners' documents → get the registration certificate → apply for the firm's **PAN**, bank account and, if required, **GST**.
- **Is registration compulsory?** No. But under **Section 69**, an unregistered firm cannot sue anyone to enforce a contract, and partners cannot sue each other under the deed. Register.
- **Cost:** stamp duty on the deed (fixed by each state) plus a small registration fee. Examples: Tamil Nadu charges a **₹200** registration fee; Maharashtra's stamp duty is **1% of capital** above ₹50,000, **capped at ₹50,000**.
- **Tax:** the firm pays **30%** on its profit, plus **12% surcharge** if income exceeds ₹1 crore, plus **4% cess**. Salary and interest to working partners are deductible within limits (interest up to **12%** a year).
- **Liability:** partners are personally liable for the firm's debts without limit. If that worries you, look at an LLP.

## Registered vs unregistered partnership

A partnership exists the moment two people agree to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all (Section 4). Registration does not create the firm. It records it, and the record matters a great deal.

| | Registered firm | Unregistered firm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can the firm sue a customer or supplier to enforce a contract? | Yes | **No** (Section 69(2)) |
| Can a partner sue the firm or other partners for rights under the deed? | Yes | **No** (Section 69(1)) |
| Can it claim a set-off based on a contract in a case filed against it? | Yes | **No** (Section 69(3)) |
| Can others sue the firm? | Yes | **Yes** |
| Can partners sue for dissolution or for accounts of a dissolved firm? | Yes | Yes (an exception in Section 69(3)) |
| PAN, GST, bank account | Yes | Possible, but some banks and agencies insist on the certificate |
| Proof of the firm's existence for loans, tenders, GS1 barcodes, contracts | Registration certificate | Deed only |

In practice: if a customer owes your unregistered firm ₹4 lakh and refuses to pay, you cannot file a civil suit to recover it. You can register later (the central Act sets no deadline, though some states charge more for late registration), but registering after a dispute starts is too late for that dispute.

## What the partnership deed must contain

A partnership deed (also called the partnership agreement) is the firm's rulebook. Where it is silent, the Act's default rules apply, and those are often not what partners expect: for example, profits are shared **equally** and partners get **no salary and no interest** on capital unless agreed.

### Basic clauses

1. **Name of the firm.** The name cannot use words such as Crown, Emperor, Empire, Imperial, King, Queen or Royal, or words suggesting government sanction, without the state's written consent (Section 58(2)).
2. **Principal place of business** and any branches.
3. **Nature of business.**
4. **Partners' full names, ages and addresses.**
5. **Date of commencement** and duration: a fixed term, a particular venture, or "at will" (until any partner gives notice).
6. **Capital contribution** of each partner, in cash or kind.
7. **Profit and loss sharing ratio.**
8. **Drawings:** how much each partner may withdraw, and whether interest is charged on drawings.
9. **Bank accounts:** who signs, and whether one or two signatures are needed.
10. **Admission, retirement and death of a partner,** and how a retiring partner's share is valued and paid.
11. **Dissolution** and how assets are distributed.
12. **Books of account** and the financial year.
13. **Disputes:** usually arbitration.

### Tax clauses: the ones that save money

Salary and interest paid to partners reduce the firm's taxable profit **only if the deed says so**:

- **Remuneration** is deductible only for **working partners**, only if the deed authorises it, and only if the deed states the **amount or the method** of working it out (for example "as per the limits under the Income-tax Act, divided equally among working partners").
- **Interest on capital** is deductible only if the deed authorises it, and only up to **12% a year, simple interest**.
- Name which partners are **working partners**. Payments to sleeping partners are not deductible.

"Salary as mutually agreed" saves no tax. Write the method.

## Partnership firm registration fees and stamp duty

There is no single all-India fee, because the Registrar of Firms is a state office and stamp duty is a state subject. Your cost has three parts:

| Cost | Who fixes it | What it depends on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stamp duty** on the deed | Your state's Stamp Act | Usually the capital contributed |
| **Registration fee** to the Registrar of Firms | Your state's rules under the 1932 Act | Usually a small fixed fee |
| Notary, typing, professional help | Market | Optional if you file yourself |

Two verified examples:

- **Maharashtra:** stamp duty under Article 47 of the Maharashtra Stamp Act is **₹500** where the capital contribution is up to ₹50,000, and **1% of the contribution** above that, **capped at ₹50,000**. The cap was ₹15,000 until the October 2024 amendment; many pages still show the old figure. Registration is done online through the Registrar of Firms portal (rof.mahaonline.gov.in).
- **Tamil Nadu:** firms register with the Registration Department (tnreginet.gov.in); the registration fee listed on the state's single window portal is **₹200**.

**Worked example (Maharashtra):** two partners bring ₹2,50,000 each, so the capital is ₹5,00,000. Stamp duty is 1% × ₹5,00,000 = **₹5,000**, well under the ₹50,000 cap. For a ₹60 lakh firm, 1% would be ₹60,000, so the cap applies and the duty is **₹50,000**.

For other states, check your state's Stamp Act schedule (the partnership article) and your Registrar of Firms website before buying stamp paper. Stamp duty is due whether or not you register.

## How to register a partnership firm, step by step

1. **Agree the terms** with your partners: capital, profit ratio, roles, salaries, exits.
2. **Choose a name** and check it is not already used locally or as a trademark.
3. **Draft the deed** with the clauses above, print it on stamp paper of the right value (or pay by e-stamping), and have every partner sign it, with witnesses. Many states also want it notarised.
4. **Fill in the application for registration**, called **Form No. 1** in many states. Section 58 requires it to state the firm's name, principal place of business, other places of business, the date each partner joined, partners' names and permanent addresses, and the firm's duration. **Every partner signs and verifies it.**
5. **Attach documents**, typically: the deed (original or certified copy), PAN and address proof of each partner, proof of the business premises (rent agreement or ownership papers plus a utility bill), and a no-objection letter from the owner if the premises are rented or owned by someone else.
6. **Pay the fee** and submit, online or at the Registrar's office, depending on the state.
7. **Get the certificate.** The Registrar records the firm in the Register of Firms (Section 59) and issues a certificate.
8. **Keep it updated.** Report changes in partners, name, address or branches to the Registrar (Sections 60 to 63). And stamp the deed correctly: an under-stamped deed can be impounded, with a penalty, when produced in court.

## After registration: PAN, bank account, GST and other licences

- **PAN:** the firm needs its own PAN, separate from the partners'. Apply online with the deed and registration certificate.
- **Bank account:** open a current account in the firm's name. Banks usually ask for the deed, the firm's PAN, the registration certificate and the partners' KYC.
- **GST:** the firm registers under its own PAN once turnover crosses the limit (₹40 lakh for goods and ₹20 lakh for services in most states), or earlier if it sells online or across states. ## How a partnership firm is taxed

A firm is a separate taxpayer from its partners. It pays a flat rate and gets no basic exemption limit or slabs:

| Item | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Income tax on the firm's total income | **30%** |
| Surcharge, only if total income exceeds ₹1 crore | **12%** of the tax |
| Health and education cess | **4%** of tax plus surcharge |

Since 1 April 2026, India's **Income-tax Act, 2025** has replaced the 1961 Act and renumbered its sections. The rules for firms, including the limits on partners' salary and interest in old **section 40(b)**, carry over with the same amounts. Ask your CA for the new section references when you draft the deed.

**Limits on partners' remuneration** (in force from FY 2024-25):

| Book profit | Maximum deductible remuneration for all working partners together |
| --- | --- |
| First ₹6,00,000, or a loss | ₹3,00,000 or 90% of book profit, whichever is **higher** |
| Balance above ₹6,00,000 | 60% |

**Interest on capital:** up to 12% a year, simple.

**In the partners' hands:** salary and interest from the firm are taxable as business income at the partner's own slab rates. A partner's **share of the firm's profit is exempt**, because the firm has already paid tax on it. Since 1 April 2025, the firm also deducts **TDS at 10%** on salary, remuneration, commission, bonus or interest paid to a partner above ₹20,000 in a year.

### Worked example

Ravi and Sunita run a hardware shop as a firm. Each has ₹10 lakh capital, the deed allows 12% interest on capital, and both are working partners with remuneration "to the maximum allowed under the Income-tax Act, shared equally".

| Step | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net profit before partners' interest and salary | | ₹15,00,000 |
| Interest on capital | ₹20,00,000 × 12% | ₹2,40,000 |
| Book profit | ₹15,00,000 − ₹2,40,000 | ₹12,60,000 |
| Remuneration limit on first ₹6,00,000 | higher of ₹3,00,000 or 90% × ₹6,00,000 = ₹5,40,000 | ₹5,40,000 |
| Remuneration limit on balance | 60% × ₹6,60,000 | ₹3,96,000 |
| Maximum remuneration | ₹5,40,000 + ₹3,96,000 | ₹9,36,000 |
| Firm's taxable income | ₹12,60,000 − ₹9,36,000 | ₹3,24,000 |
| Tax at 30% | | ₹97,200 |
| Cess at 4% | | ₹3,888 |
| **Firm's tax** | | **₹1,01,088** |

Without the salary and interest clauses, the firm would pay 30% plus cess on the whole ₹15,00,000: ₹4,50,000 + ₹18,000 = **₹4,68,000**. Ravi and Sunita instead each receive ₹4,68,000 remuneration plus ₹1,20,000 interest, ₹5,88,000 each, taxed at their own slab rates, which for most small business owners is far below 30%. Under the new regime's rebate, a partner whose total income stays within ₹12 lakh may owe little or nothing, but confirm the current position with your CA.

## Partnership vs LLP

A **limited liability partnership** under the LLP Act, 2008 combines a partnership's flexibility with a company's limited liability.

| | Partnership firm | LLP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Law | Indian Partnership Act, 1932 | LLP Act, 2008 |
| Registered with | State Registrar of Firms (optional) | Ministry of Corporate Affairs (compulsory) |
| Separate legal entity | No | Yes |
| Partners' liability | **Unlimited**, joint and several | **Limited** to agreed contribution (except fraud) |
| Number of partners | 2 to 50 | Minimum 2, no maximum |
| Set-up cost | Stamp duty and a small fee | Higher: MCA fees, digital signatures, LLP agreement stamp duty |
| Annual filings with a registrar | None | Statement of accounts (Form 8) and annual return (Form 11) every year, with late fees for delay |
| Statutory audit | Only tax audit, when turnover crosses the tax-audit limit | Also compulsory if turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh |
| Income tax | 30% + surcharge + cess | Same |
| Partner salary and interest limits | Apply | Apply |
| Closing down | Simple dissolution | Formal striking off or winding up |

A partnership firm can later be converted into an LLP under the LLP Act, so starting as a firm does not lock you in.

**Choose a partnership firm** for a shop or small trading business where the partners trust each other, borrowing is modest and you want low running costs. **Choose an LLP** when the business takes on large credit or contracts, outside investors may join, or partners want their personal property protected from the business's debts.

If you are going it alone, a firm is not possible at all; see [sole proprietorship registration](https://billzap.app/blog/sole-proprietorship-registration).

## FAQ

### Is registration of a partnership firm compulsory?

No, but an unregistered firm cannot sue third parties to enforce contracts, and partners cannot sue each other under the deed (Section 69). Register to keep those rights.

### What is the fee for partnership firm registration?

It depends on the state. Stamp duty on the deed is the main cost; for example, Maharashtra charges 1% of capital above ₹50,000, capped at ₹50,000. The registration fee itself is small, such as ₹200 in Tamil Nadu.

### What documents are needed to register a partnership firm?

The signed and stamped partnership deed, the application form signed by all partners, each partner's PAN and address proof, and proof of the business premises with a no-objection letter from the owner if it is not yours.

### How much tax does a partnership firm pay?

30% of its taxable income, plus 12% surcharge if income exceeds ₹1 crore, plus 4% cess. Salary and interest to working partners, within the limits and authorised by the deed, reduce the taxable income.

### Can a partnership firm be registered after it has started?

Yes. The central Act sets no deadline, though some states charge more for late registration. Registration does not cure a dispute that began before it.

### What is the difference between a partnership and an LLP?

An LLP is a separate legal entity registered with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, and partners' liability is limited. A partnership firm is not separate from its partners, who are personally liable without limit, but it is cheaper and has no annual registrar filings.

## The bottom line

Write the deed with salary, interest and exit clauses, pay your state's stamp duty, and register with the Registrar of Firms before you need a court, not after. Get the firm's own PAN, bank account and GST, keep partners' money separate, and file the firm's return every year. If unlimited liability is a real risk, compare the LLP before you sign.
