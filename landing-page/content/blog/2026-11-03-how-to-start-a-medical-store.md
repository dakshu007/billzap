A medical store looks like any other shop from the outside: a counter, shelves, a billing screen. Underneath, it is one of the most tightly regulated small businesses in India. You cannot open one without a drug licence, you cannot get the licence without a registered pharmacist, and once you are open, a drugs inspector can walk in and ask for your purchase bills, your prescription records and a separate register for certain antibiotics.

Most guides cover either the licence or the money. This one does both, in order: who can run a medical store, the licence forms, what official state sites say about area and fees, the other registrations, a labelled budget, how margins work and the records that keep you out of trouble.

## Quick answer

- **You need a retail drug licence** from your state's Drugs Control Department. You apply in **Form 19**; the licences are issued in **Form 20** (general medicines) and **Form 21** (Schedule C and C1 drugs such as injectables, insulin and vaccines). Most stores take both.
- **A registered pharmacist is compulsory.** The owner need not be one, but you must employ a pharmacist registered with the State Pharmacy Council (D.Pharm or B.Pharm), and prescription medicines must be dispensed under their supervision.
- **Area:** at least **10 square metres** (about 108 sq ft) for a retail licence, and **15 square metres** (about 161 sq ft) if you also want a wholesale licence.
- **Fees vary by state.** Tamil Nadu charges ₹1,500 for Form 20 alone and ₹3,000 for Forms 20 and 21 together; Kerala lists ₹3,000. The licence stays valid as long as you pay a **retention fee every five years**.
- **Other registrations:** GST (compulsory above ₹40 lakh turnover in most states), shop act, Udyam (optional), and FSSAI if you sell food or nutrition products.
- **Rough investment:** ₹5 lakh to ₹12 lakh for a small neighbourhood store, most of it stock.
- **GST on most medicines is 5%** since 22 September 2025; 33 life-saving drugs are nil.

## Step 1: Sort out the pharmacist first

This is the step that decides everything else, so do it before you sign a lease.

Under the Drugs Rules, 1945, any medicine supplied on a doctor's prescription must be dispensed **by or under the personal supervision of a registered pharmacist**, meaning someone registered under the Pharmacy Act, 1948. In practice, every state asks for the pharmacist's details with your licence application. Delhi, for example, asks for their degree or diploma, their **Delhi Pharmacy Council registration**, an appointment letter and an affidavit.

You have two routes:

| Route | What it means | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **You are the pharmacist** | You hold a D.Pharm or B.Pharm and are registered with your State Pharmacy Council | Saves a salary every month, which can be the difference between a thin and a healthy profit |
| **You employ one** | The licence is in your firm's name; the pharmacist is named on it | If the pharmacist leaves, you must apply to change the registered pharmacist on the licence |

Most states also want the pharmacist to declare, by affidavit, that they work at your store. A pharmacist "lent" on paper while working elsewhere is a common reason licences get suspended.

## Step 2: Choose premises that pass inspection

Before granting the licence, a drugs inspector visits the shop. The rules on premises are short but strict:

- **Minimum area:** **10 square metres** for a retail licence in Form 20, Form 21 or both. If you want retail and wholesale (Forms 20B and 21B) from the same premises, **15 square metres**. Delhi's department and Tamil Nadu's guidelines both state these figures.
- **Independent, commercial premises.** States check ownership or rent papers, and some check land use. Delhi will not license a shop on a residential plot that does not meet its master plan rules.
- **Proper storage.** Insulin, many vaccines and several injectables are labelled "store at 2 to 8°C", so you need a **refrigerator**. Tamil Nadu asks for the purchase bill of a refrigerator or air conditioner with the application. Many medicines are labelled "store below 25°C" or "30°C", so in hot regions an air conditioner is close to essential.
- **A layout plan.** You submit a site plan or blueprint of the premises, signed as your state requires.

Location matters as much as compliance: a store near a clinic gets steady prescription traffic, while one in a residential lane lives on repeat medicines for chronic patients.

## Step 3: Apply for the drug licence (Form 19)

### Which licence do you need?

| Licence | Lets you sell | Apply in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Form 20** | Retail sale of allopathic drugs other than those in Schedules C, C1 and X | Form 19 |
| **Form 21** | Retail sale of Schedule C and C1 drugs (biologicals and similar: injectables, insulin, vaccines, sera) | Form 19 |
| **Form 20B / 21B** | Wholesale of the same | Form 19 |
| **Form 20A / 21A** | A restricted retail licence for a limited range of drugs | Form 19A |
| **Form 20F** | Retail sale of Schedule X (certain narcotic and psychotropic) drugs | Form 19C |
| **Form 20C** | Retail sale of homoeopathic medicines | Form 19B |

A normal chemist takes **Forms 20 and 21** together. Schedule X needs a separate licence and much stricter records; most new stores skip it at first.

### Documents most states ask for

- The application in Form 19 and the fee receipt
- Proof of the firm: proprietor ID, or partnership deed or company documents
- Ownership papers or a rent agreement for the premises, plus the site plan
- Pharmacist's qualification certificate, State Pharmacy Council registration, appointment letter and affidavit
- An affidavit that the owners have not been convicted under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act
- Purchase bill of the refrigerator (Tamil Nadu asks for this explicitly)

### Where to apply

Many states now take applications through **ONDLS**, the Online National Drugs Licensing System; Kerala's standard operating procedure, for example, routes retail and wholesale applications through it. Others, such as Delhi, run their own online system. Start at your state Drugs Control Department's website and follow the link it gives.

### Fees and validity

| State (official source) | Fee for a retail licence |
| --- | --- |
| Tamil Nadu (Drugs Control guidelines) | ₹1,500 for Form 20; ₹3,000 for Forms 20 and 21; ₹6,000 for retail and wholesale together |
| Kerala (Drugs Control SOP, January 2026) | ₹3,000 |

Other states differ, so check your own. The licence does not expire on a fixed date: it remains valid as long as you pay a **retention fee**, equal to the grant fee, before the end of **every five years**. Kerala's SOP adds a late fee of **2% of the licence fee per month**, for up to six months. Kerala also sets **30 days** to process an application; timelines in other states vary.

## Step 6: Understand margins, then do the monthly maths

Medicine margins are not set the way grocery margins are.

- **Price-controlled (scheduled) medicines:** the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority fixes ceiling prices under the Drug (Prices Control) Order, 2013, and the formula allows a **16% margin to the retailer** on the price to retailer. You cannot sell above the ceiling price.
- **Other branded medicines:** prices are set by the company. A government committee report noted that retailers' associations commonly insist on about **20%**, more than the 16% the DPCO formula assumes.
- **Generics, OTC products, personal care and nutrition:** margins are usually higher and vary by brand and distributor.

So a blended gross margin of **roughly 15% to 20% of sales** is a reasonable planning figure; treat it as an estimate and replace it with your own numbers after three months.

**A worked example (estimates, sales before GST):**

| | Owner employs a pharmacist | Owner is the pharmacist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales: ₹20,000 a day × 30 days | ₹6,00,000 | ₹6,00,000 |
| Gross margin at 18% | ₹1,08,000 | ₹1,08,000 |
| Rent | ₹20,000 | ₹20,000 |
| Pharmacist's salary | ₹25,000 | ₹0 |
| Helper | ₹12,000 | ₹12,000 |
| Electricity (fridge and AC) | ₹6,000 | ₹6,000 |
| Expiry and breakage write-off (1% of sales) | ₹6,000 | ₹6,000 |
| Software, internet, sundries | ₹3,500 | ₹3,500 |
| **Total expenses** | **₹72,500** | **₹47,500** |
| **Monthly profit before tax** | **₹35,500** | **₹60,500** |

Check: ₹1,08,000 − ₹72,500 = ₹35,500, and ₹1,08,000 − ₹47,500 = ₹60,500. The two levers are plain from the table: whether you are the pharmacist yourself, and how tightly you control expiry losses.

## Step 7: The registers and billing rules you must follow

### Every sale needs a proper bill

Rule 65 of the Drugs Rules requires every retail sale to be made against a **cash or credit memo** showing your shop's **name, address and drug licence number**, a **serial number**, and the **name and quantity** of each medicine, with carbon or office copies kept as records. Pharmacies also commonly print the **batch number and expiry date** on each line, which makes recalls and returns traceable.

### Prescription sales and the Schedule H1 register

- **Prescription records.** Sales on a doctor's prescription are recorded with the date, the prescriber's and patient's names, the medicine and quantity, and for Schedule C drugs the manufacturer, batch number and expiry. The rules allow a prescription register or a serially numbered cash memo book carrying the same details.
- **Schedule H1 register.** Since 2013, every sale of a **Schedule H1** drug (a list that includes certain newer antibiotics, anti-TB drugs and some habit-forming medicines) must be entered in a **separate register** at the time of sale: name and address of the prescriber, name of the patient, name of the drug and quantity. Keep it for **three years**; inspectors ask for it.
- **Purchase records.** Keep supplier bills serially and in date order, showing the supplier's licence number, the drug, quantity, batch number and manufacturer.

### GST on medicines

After the GST changes of **22 September 2025**, most medicines are taxed at **5%** (down from 12%), **33 life-saving drugs** are **nil**, and items such as diagnostic kits and glucometer strips are also at 5%. Cosmetics and some personal-care items you stock may sit at other rates, so check each HSN in our [GST rates list](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-rates-list).

Medicines are sold at MRP, which **includes** GST, so the bill works the tax backwards. A strip with an MRP of ₹84 at 5% GST is ₹80 taxable value plus ₹4 GST (₹84 ÷ 1.05 = ₹80), split as ₹2 CGST and ₹2 SGST.

## FAQ

### Can I open a medical store without a pharmacy degree?

Yes. The owner does not need to be a pharmacist, but you must employ a registered pharmacist (D.Pharm or B.Pharm, registered with the State Pharmacy Council) who is named on the licence and supervises dispensing.

### What is Form 19 in a drug licence?

Form 19 is the application form for a licence to sell drugs by retail or wholesale. The retail licences themselves are issued in Form 20 (general allopathic drugs) and Form 21 (Schedule C and C1 drugs).

### How much area is required for a medical shop?

At least 10 square metres (about 108 sq ft) for a retail licence in Form 20, Form 21 or both, and at least 15 square metres (about 161 sq ft) if you want retail and wholesale licences together.

### What is the drug licence fee for a medical store?

It depends on the state. Tamil Nadu charges ₹1,500 for Form 20 alone and ₹3,000 for Forms 20 and 21 together; Kerala lists ₹3,000. Check your state Drugs Control Department's site for the current amount.

### Does a drug licence need renewal?

Not on a fixed expiry date. It stays valid if you pay a retention fee, equal to the licence fee, before the end of every five years. Paying late attracts a late fee.

### How much does it cost to start a medical store?

Roughly ₹5 lakh to ₹12 lakh for a small neighbourhood store, with opening stock the largest part. City, rent and stock range change this a lot, so build your own budget from local quotes.

### Is GST registration compulsory for a medical store?

Only once turnover crosses ₹40 lakh a year in most states (₹20 lakh in a few north-eastern states). Many stores register earlier because distributors and institutional buyers prefer it.

## The bottom line

Do things in order: secure a registered pharmacist, find at least 10 square metres that will pass inspection, apply in Form 19 for Forms 20 and 21, then add GST, shop act and the rest. After opening, the business lives on two disciplines: stocking what local doctors prescribe without letting it expire, and keeping bills and registers an inspector can check on any day.
