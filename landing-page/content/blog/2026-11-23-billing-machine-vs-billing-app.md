Ask a dealer for a "billing machine for shop" and you will be shown anything from a ₹4,000 keypad box with a paper roll to a ₹50,000 touch-screen counter with a cash drawer. Ask a younger shop owner and they will say they bill on their phone. Both can be right. The wrong choice is the one that does not fit how many bills you make, what your bills must show under GST, and how much you want to spend.

This guide lays out the four real options, what each typically costs today, what GST rules expect on the paper that comes out, and which setup suits which size of shop.

## Quick answer

- **Four options:** a **keypad billing machine** with a built-in printer, an **Android POS** (handheld or counter) with a built-in printer, a **PC or touch POS** with a separate printer, scanner and cash drawer, or a **billing app on your phone**, with or without a small printer.
- **Typical prices from current listings (late 2026):** keypad billing machines about **₹3,000 to ₹25,000**; Android handheld POS with printer about **₹12,000 to ₹30,000**; touch POS systems about **₹17,000 to ₹55,000** plus accessories; a 58 mm Bluetooth thermal printer about **₹1,800 to ₹5,500**. A phone app can cost **nothing** if you already own an Android phone.
- **GST rules care about the bill, not the machine.** A registered shop's tax invoice must show the details in **Rule 46** (GSTIN, serial number, HSN, tax split and so on). Many basic keypad machines cannot print all of them well.
- **Rule of thumb:** a small shop with tens of bills a day does well with a **phone app**; a busy counter with hundreds of barcoded items needs a **PC or Android POS with a scanner**.

## The four types of billing setup

### 1. Keypad billing machine with built-in printer

The classic "billing machine": a calculator-style keypad, a small LCD screen and a built-in 2 or 3 inch thermal printer. You store item codes and prices, punch them in and it prints a slip.

- **Good at:** speed for a fixed list of items, no phone or internet needed, very simple for staff.
- **Weak at:** small screens make item entry and edits slow; many models have limited room for HSN codes, tax splits and customer GSTIN; reports are basic; getting data out for your CA often means a cable and the dealer's software.

### 2. Android POS (handheld or counter)

An Android device with a built-in thermal printer, sold with billing software. Handheld versions are popular with restaurants, vegetable vendors and delivery counters.

- **Good at:** touch screen, full item lists, often barcode support and UPI QR on the slip.
- **Weak at:** you are tied to the bundled software and its subscription, if any; repairs go through the seller.

### 3. PC or touch POS

A computer or all-in-one touch terminal running billing software, with a separate 80 mm thermal printer, a barcode scanner and a cash drawer. This is what supermarkets and large stores use.

- **Good at:** high volume, barcodes, big inventories, several counters.
- **Weak at:** cost, space, power backup, and software licences; overkill for a small shop. See our guide to the [POS machine for shop](https://billzap.app/blog/pos-machine-for-shop) for the card-payment side, which is a separate device from your bank.

### 4. Billing app on your phone

A billing app on the Android phone you already carry, sharing bills as PDF on WhatsApp and printing when a customer wants paper.

- **Good at:** near-zero cost, full GST bill format, UPI QR on the bill, reports and customer lists in your pocket.
- **Weak at:** one phone means one counter; typing items is slower than scanning barcodes for shops with hundreds of items; printing depends on a printer your phone supports.

## What GST rules require on the bill

Whatever prints the bill, the rules apply to the content. Our guide to the [bill format](https://billzap.app/blog/bill-format) covers this in full; the short version:

| Your status | Heading | Must show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registered, charging GST | **Tax Invoice** (Rule 46) | Your name, address and GSTIN; serial number up to 16 characters, unique for the year; date; buyer's GSTIN if registered; HSN code; description, quantity, value; rate and amount of CGST and SGST, or IGST; place of supply for inter-state sales; signature or digital signature |
| Registered, composition or exempt goods only | **Bill of Supply** (Rule 49) | Same basics, no tax amounts |
| Not registered | **Bill** or cash memo | No GST of any kind |

Points that trip up billing machines:

- **HSN digits:** with previous-year turnover up to ₹5 crore, at least **4 digits** are required on B2B bills (optional on B2C); above ₹5 crore, 6 digits on all bills. See [what is HSN code](https://billzap.app/blog/what-is-hsn-code).
- **Buyer GSTIN** on B2B bills, so your customer can claim credit.
- **Consolidated bill:** for sales below ₹200 to unregistered customers who do not want a bill, Rule 46 lets you issue one consolidated tax invoice at day end. Most shops still give individual bills.
- **E-invoicing** applies only to B2B invoices of businesses whose turnover has crossed ₹5 crore; see the [e-invoice limit](https://billzap.app/blog/e-invoice-limit). Most small shops are below it.
- **Record keeping:** GST records must be kept for about six years. Thermal slips fade over time, so keep a digital copy or the machine's data, not just a box of curling paper.

## Thermal printer for billing: what to check

If you choose an app and want paper bills, a thermal printer is the usual add-on.

- **Width:** 58 mm (2 inch) is compact and cheap; 80 mm (3 inch) prints wider tables and is better for itemised GST bills.
- **Connection:** Bluetooth, USB or Wi-Fi. Many cheap Bluetooth thermal printers only work with their own app or apps that support them directly.
- **Test before you buy.** Ask the seller to print from **your** phone and **your** billing app, or buy from a seller with an easy return policy.
- **Paper:** keep spare rolls; thermal prints fade, so share a PDF copy too.

## FAQ

### What is the price of a billing machine for a shop?

Typical listings in late 2026 show basic keypad billing machines from about ₹3,000 to ₹8,000, better keypad models up to about ₹25,000, Android handheld POS with printer about ₹12,000 to ₹30,000, and touch POS systems about ₹17,000 to ₹55,000 before accessories.

### Is a billing machine compulsory for a shop?

No. GST law does not require any particular machine. It requires a correct bill: a tax invoice with the Rule 46 details if you charge GST, a bill of supply if you are registered but do not charge tax, and a plain bill if you are not registered.

### Can I use my mobile as a billing machine?

Yes. A billing app on an Android phone can make complete GST bills, share them on WhatsApp and print to a supported printer. It suits shops with a manageable number of bills a day at one counter.

### Which thermal printer is best for billing?

For a small counter, a 58 mm printer is cheap and portable; for itemised GST bills, 80 mm is easier to read. The most important check is whether it works with your phone and your billing app, so test it before buying.

### Do I need a POS machine to accept card payments?

Yes, card payments need a card terminal from a bank or payment company, which is different from a billing machine. UPI payments only need a QR code. See our guide to the [UPI QR code for shop](https://billzap.app/blog/upi-qr-code-for-shop).

### Do thermal bills fade?

Yes, thermal paper fades over time, especially in heat and sunlight. Keep a digital copy of every bill, because GST records must be kept for about six years.

## The bottom line

A billing machine is only a way to produce a correct bill. For most small Indian shops, the phone already in your pocket does the job at no cost, with WhatsApp bills, UPI QR and reports a keypad machine cannot match. Buy hardware when your counter genuinely needs it: hundreds of barcoded items, several counters, or staff who must not use a phone. Either way, check that what comes out of it is a complete GST bill.
