A customer picks out a ₹1.4 lakh order, scans your QR, and the payment fails with "limit exceeded". It is not your QR or your bank. UPI limits sit on the **payer's** side: the customer's bank and the type of payment decide how much can move.

This guide puts every current limit in one table, checked against NPCI circulars, then explains what they mean for a shop: how much a customer can pay in one go, whether you have a receiving cap, and where the new merchant charges stand.

## Quick answer

- **Normal UPI payments** (to a person or a shop): up to **₹1 lakh** per transaction, and most banks also cap the day at **₹1 lakh** across all your UPI apps.
- **₹5 lakh per payment:** tax payments, hospitals and educational institutions, IPO applications and RBI Retail Direct.
- **From 15 September 2025** (NPCI circular OC 185B): ₹5 lakh per payment and **₹10 lakh a day** to verified merchants in capital markets, insurance, travel, collections and GeM; credit card bills ₹5 lakh per payment, ₹6 lakh a day; jewellery ₹2 lakh per payment, ₹6 lakh a day.
- **UPI Lite:** ₹1,000 per payment, ₹5,000 balance. **UPI 123PAY** (feature phones): ₹10,000 per payment.
- **New users:** usually ₹5,000 in the first 24 hours after registering on a phone.
- **Count:** most banks allow about **20 UPI payments** in 24 hours; some apps show 10.
- **Receiving:** NPCI sets no daily cap on what a shop can receive. Each customer's own limit is what stops a large payment.
- **MDR:** the 0.4% merchant charge on payments above ₹2,000, due on 15 October 2026, was postponed. As of late November 2026 no start date has been notified.

The ₹1 lakh is shared: if a customer has sent ₹70,000 to family today, only ₹30,000 is left for your shop. And every number is a **ceiling**; NPCI lets banks set lower limits, and some smaller banks do.

## Per day, per transaction and per count: three different limits

- **Per transaction** is the largest single payment allowed. For ordinary payments it is ₹1 lakh.
- **Per day** is the total across all payments in a rolling 24 hours, across **every UPI app** linked to that bank account. Using PhonePe and then Google Pay does not double it.
- **Count** is the number of payments. About 20 a day is the figure most banks and apps quote. NPCI's September 2026 consolidated circular withdrew its old 2018 circular that set a 10-payment P2P cap, so check your own bank's figure rather than relying on a number from an old article.

NPCI also caps **balance enquiries at 50 per app per day**, the limit staff who check the balance after every sale hit first.

## The ₹5 lakh and ₹10 lakh categories, explained

NPCI raised limits for specific categories in steps, and this is where most pages online are out of date.

**Older ₹5 lakh categories:** hospitals and educational institutions (December 2023), IPO applications and RBI Retail Direct, and tax payments (OC 185A, September 2024).

**September 2025 additions.** NPCI circular **OC 185B, dated 28 August 2025**, told banks and apps to apply higher limits by **15 September 2025**:

| Category | Per payment | 24-hour total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capital markets (brokers) | ₹5 lakh | ₹10 lakh |
| Insurance premiums | ₹5 lakh | ₹10 lakh |
| Government e-Marketplace (EMD payments) | ₹5 lakh | ₹10 lakh |
| Travel | ₹5 lakh | ₹10 lakh |
| Collections (loan EMIs and similar) | ₹5 lakh | ₹10 lakh |
| Credit card bill payments | ₹5 lakh | ₹6 lakh |
| Jewellery | ₹2 lakh | ₹6 lakh |
| FX retail through BBPS | ₹5 lakh | ₹5 lakh |
| Digital account opening, term deposits | ₹5 lakh | ₹5 lakh |
| Digital account opening, initial funding | ₹2 lakh | ₹2 lakh |

**The catch: "verified merchant".** The higher limits apply only to merchants their bank has tagged as **Verified Merchants**, shown with a green tick in the payer's app. NPCI's consolidated circular of 18 September 2026 says a merchant qualifies by belonging to listed segments (capital markets, insurance, GeM, credit card bills, collections, tax, IPO and government merchants, among others) **or** by having a group turnover of at least **₹50 crore**. A neighbourhood jeweller or electronics shop usually meets neither test, so its customers stay at the ordinary ₹1 lakh. **P2P transfers were not raised**: sending money to a person is still ₹1 lakh.

## PhonePe, Google Pay and Paytm daily limits

The apps do not set a separate amount limit of their own. Your **bank** sets it, within NPCI's ceiling, and it applies across apps. So the PhonePe daily limit, the Google Pay daily limit and the Paytm daily limit are all the same number for the same bank account: ₹1 lakh for most banks.

Where apps differ is in the extras:

- **UPI Lite daily spend.** NPCI fixes ₹1,000 per payment and a ₹5,000 balance. The daily spend varies: Google Pay's help page says ₹5,000 in total a day, while some other apps allow ₹10,000 because you can top up again.
- **UPI Circle** (letting a family member pay from your account): partial delegation is usually capped at ₹5,000 per payment and ₹15,000 a month.
- **iPhone vs Android for new users:** Google Pay's help page says iOS users get ₹5,000 per 24 hours for the first 3 days, up to ₹15,000, instead of a single 24-hour window.

Your bank's own app shows your exact limit under UPI settings, and several banks let you lower it, a good fraud control for a staff phone.

## UPI Lite, UPI 123PAY and biometric payments

**UPI Lite** is an on-device wallet for small payments without a PIN: up to **₹1,000 per payment** and **₹5,000** held at a time (NPCI OC 169A). For a kirana, most customer payments are small enough to go through Lite, which is why they succeed even when the customer's bank server is slow. NPCI's 2026 consolidated circular asks apps to switch Lite on for new users, with consent, and to use it for payments under ₹500, so expect more of your small payments to arrive this way.

**UPI 123PAY** lets feature-phone users pay through IVR calls, missed calls or sound-based payments. The per-payment limit went up from ₹5,000 to **₹10,000** from 1 January 2025.

**Biometric payments**, with fingerprint or face instead of the PIN, started in October 2025 with a ₹5,000 cap. NPCI circular OC 226A, reported as dated 3 July 2026, raises this to **₹10,000** from 7 August 2026. Above that, the customer enters the PIN as usual.

## Is there a UPI limit for merchants receiving money?

No daily cap on receiving is set by NPCI for a shop. A busy store can receive ₹10 lakh in a day through UPI, as long as each customer stays within their own limit. What can bite instead:

- **The customer's limit.** A ₹1.4 lakh sale cannot be paid as one ordinary UPI payment.
- **Your account type.** Small merchants with expected UPI receipts up to about ₹1 lakh are placed in NPCI's "P2PM" (person-to-person merchant) category, meant for the smallest sellers. If your receipts outgrow it, expect your payment app or bank to move you to a regular merchant account, with fuller KYC.
- **A savings account doing a shop's job.** Banks watch for heavy business-like credits on savings accounts. A current account or a merchant account under your shop name avoids questions. ### Worked example: a ₹1.4 lakh order

Ravi runs an electrical shop. A customer buys an inverter, battery and wiring for **₹1,40,000**. The customer has already paid ₹15,000 by UPI to a school today. The bank's daily limit is ₹1 lakh.

| Step | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Customer's daily UPI limit | ₹1,00,000 |
| Already used today | ₹15,000 |
| Can still pay Ravi by UPI today | ₹85,000 |
| Balance on the bill | ₹1,40,000 − ₹85,000 = ₹55,000 |

For the ₹55,000, Ravi can take an **IMPS or NEFT** transfer (IMPS goes up to ₹5 lakh), a **card**, or **UPI tomorrow** once the 24-hour window resets. He should not split the sale into two bills to dodge the limit: one sale, one invoice for the full value, marked unpaid until the balance arrives.

## UPI charges for merchants: where the 0.4% MDR stands

On **15 September 2026** NPCI published a framework bringing back a merchant discount rate on some UPI payments, the first since MDR on UPI was removed in 2020. It was due on **15 October 2026**. On **13 October** NPCI postponed it until "immediately after Diwali", with the date to be notified later.

As announced, the framework looks like this:

| Payment | Charge to the merchant |
| --- | --- |
| Any merchant payment up to ₹2,000 | Nil |
| Small merchants (P2PM) receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month via UPI QR | Nil |
| Ordinary merchant payment above ₹2,000 | 0.4%, capped at ₹300 |
| Railways, fuel, telecom, insurance, above ₹2,000 | Flat ₹5 |
| Capital markets, above ₹2,000 | 0.02%, capped at ₹300 |
| Person-to-person transfers | Nil |

**Status in November 2026:** Diwali has passed and **no start date has been notified**. Mid-November reports from business media say the government is weighing merchant objections, that the rollout could slip beyond the 2027 state elections, and that a revised plan with a flat ₹5 on business-to-business payments is under discussion. Meanwhile NPCI notified a **UPI Vyapaar Saksham** scheme on 11 November 2026, meant to fund QR codes and soundboxes for small merchants. Treat any start date you see on social media with caution until your bank or payment app tells you.

**What it would cost a shop.** Take a shop that receives ₹6 lakh a month through UPI, of which ₹1.2 lakh comes in payments above ₹2,000.

| Payment | 0.4% MDR |
| --- | --- |
| ₹2,000 | Nil (not above ₹2,000) |
| ₹5,000 | ₹20 |
| ₹25,000 | ₹100 |
| ₹75,000 | ₹300 (cap reached) |
| ₹1,00,000 | ₹300 (capped; 0.4% would be ₹400) |

For that shop: ₹1,20,000 × 0.4% = **₹480 a month**, on ₹6 lakh of receipts, or 0.08% overall. Small, and NPCI rules bar adding it to the customer's bill.

## Two rules that protect your counter

- **No more P2P collect requests.** Since 1 October 2025, a person can no longer send a "collect" request to another person. A request asking you to enter your PIN to "receive" money is a scam: you never enter a PIN to receive.
- **Check the payment, not the screenshot.** Confirm on your own app or soundbox before the customer leaves.

## What to do when a payment fails because of a limit

"Daily limit exceeded" means the customer's 24-hour total is used up; "per transaction" means one payment is too large. Take part now and the rest by IMPS, NEFT or after the 24-hour window, try UPI Lite for small amounts if the bank is timing out, and remember a customer on a new phone may be in the ₹5,000 first-day window. Keep the bill unpaid until the money is in your account.

## FAQ

### What is the UPI transaction limit per day?

₹1 lakh for ordinary payments to people and shops, at most banks, across all your UPI apps together. Some banks set lower limits. Payments for taxes, hospitals, education, IPOs and verified merchants in categories like insurance and capital markets allow ₹5 lakh per payment, with up to ₹10 lakh a day in some categories.

### Can I send ₹5 lakh through UPI?

Only to eligible payees: tax payments, hospitals, educational institutions, IPO applications, RBI Retail Direct, and verified merchants in categories such as capital markets, insurance, travel and credit card bills. Sending ₹5 lakh to a person or an ordinary shop is not possible; that stays at ₹1 lakh.

### Is there a limit on how much a shop can receive through UPI?

NPCI sets no daily cap on what a merchant receives. The limit is on each payer. Small merchants in the P2PM category may be moved to a regular merchant account as receipts grow, and banks may question heavy business credits into a savings account.

### What is the UPI Lite limit?

₹1,000 per payment and ₹5,000 held in the Lite balance at a time. The total you can spend in a day depends on the app, commonly ₹5,000 or ₹10,000.

### How many UPI transactions can I make in a day?

About 20 in 24 hours at most banks, though some apps quote 10. Your bank's app shows the exact figure.

### Will customers pay extra for UPI above ₹2,000?

No. The proposed 0.4% MDR is charged to the merchant, not the customer, and merchants may not add a surcharge. As of late November 2026 it has not started, and no date has been notified.

## The bottom line

For everyday shop payments, one number matters: **₹1 lakh a day per customer**, shared across all their UPI apps. The ₹5 lakh and ₹10 lakh limits are real but apply to specific categories and verified merchants, not to the typical neighbourhood shop. You can receive as much as your customers can send, and the 0.4% MDR on payments above ₹2,000 is still on hold. When a large sale hits the limit, take part by UPI, the rest by IMPS or tomorrow, and keep one bill for the full amount.

*Limits change by NPCI circular and bank policy. This guide reflects circulars and reports available in November 2026; confirm your own limit in your bank's app.*
