You open a small shop, and within a week three people tell you three different things. The bank wants a "shop act licence" before it will open a current account. A neighbour says you need a "gumasta". An agent on Instagram offers to get "Shop Establishment Registration" for ₹1,999 in 24 hours. Are these the same thing, do you actually need it, and should you pay an agent?

Usually they are the same thing, you may or may not need it depending on your state and your staff, and you rarely need an agent. This guide explains what shop act registration is, why the answer changes from state to state, what the rules say in the states where we could check them, the documents, the fees and the steps to apply yourself on the official portal.

## Quick answer

**Shop act registration** (also called a **shop and establishment certificate**, **shop act licence**, or **gumasta licence** in Gujarat and some other places) is registration of your shop under your **state's Shops and Establishments Act**. That law governs working hours, holidays, leave and wages of the people who work there.

- **It is a state law.** Each state has its own Act, its own thresholds and its own portal. There is no single all-India shop act.
- **Small shops often need only an intimation, not a registration.** In **Maharashtra**, a shop with **fewer than 10 workers** files a one-time online intimation. In **Gujarat**, the registration threshold was raised to **20 workers** in December 2025. In **Tamil Nadu**, registration is for establishments with **10 or more** workers.
- **Some states register everyone.** **Karnataka** charges ₹405 even with no employees, ₹810 for 1 to 9 and ₹5,400 for 10 to 19.
- **Apply on your state's labour department portal.** It is usually quick, and the government fee is small or nil.

## What is shop act registration?

Every state has a law that regulates shops and commercial establishments: opening and closing hours, weekly holidays, overtime, leave and the records an employer must keep. Registering under it tells the labour department that your establishment exists and who is responsible for it.

The certificate you get is called different things in different places:

- **Shop Act licence**: the common name in Maharashtra.
- **Gumasta licence**: the common name in Gujarat, especially Ahmedabad, and in some other states.
- **Shop and establishment certificate** or **S&E registration**: the formal name almost everywhere.

It is **not** a trade licence. A trade licence comes from your municipal corporation and is about the premises and the trade being carried on there. It is also not GST, not FSSAI and not Udyam. A kirana shop may need several of these, and we will come back to the full list.

## Do you need it? It depends on your state

This is the part most guides skip. The law, the threshold and the process change from state to state, and several states have relaxed their rules for small shops in recent years. Here is what we could confirm from official portals and notifications. For any state not listed, check the **state labour department's website** before paying anyone.

| State | Law | What a small shop needs | Where to apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maharashtra | Maharashtra Shops and Establishments (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 2017 | **Fewer than 10 workers:** one-time online **intimation in Form F**. **10 or more:** registration in Form A | Aaple Sarkar / Maharashtra labour department (MCGM portal in Mumbai) |
| Gujarat | Gujarat Shops and Establishments (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 2019 | An ordinance of **16 December 2025** raised the registration threshold from 10 to **20 workers**. Renewal of registration has been abolished | Gujarat labour department / your municipal corporation |
| Karnataka | Karnataka Shops and Commercial Establishments Act, 1961 | Registration, with fees by staff count: **₹405** with no employees, **₹810** for 1 to 9, **₹5,400** for 10 to 19 | e-Karmika portal |
| Tamil Nadu | Tamil Nadu Shops and Establishments Act, 1947 | Registration in **Form Y** for establishments with **10 or more workers**, within **six months** of starting | Tamil Nadu labour department |
| Delhi | Delhi Shops and Establishments Act, 1954 | Registration in **Form A**, within **90 days** of starting. New companies get it through the SPICE+ incorporation form | Delhi labour department (labourcis.nic.in) |
| Telangana | Telangana Shops and Establishments Act, 1988 | Online registration with the labour department | Telangana labour department |
| Other states | Each state's own Act | Check your state labour department portal | State labour department |

Two things follow from that table:

1. **A one-person kirana in Maharashtra or Gujarat does not need a full registration.** Maharashtra wants a one-time online intimation. In Gujarat, a shop below 20 workers is outside the registration requirement. Anyone telling you otherwise is either out of date or selling something.
2. **"Workers" usually includes everyone employed in the shop**, including helpers and part-timers, not only salaried staff. Count honestly. If you cross the threshold later, you will have to register then.

The rules have changed often enough (Maharashtra in 2017, Gujarat in 2019 and again in 2025) that a page written a few years ago may be wrong today. Check the date on anything you read, including this guide.

## Why it is worth having, even when it is optional

Even where the law only asks for an intimation, having the acknowledgement or certificate in hand is useful:

- **Opening a current account.** Banks ask a proprietor for proof that the business exists. The shop act certificate or intimation is one of the documents they most commonly accept.
- **Business proof elsewhere.** Payment apps, suppliers, wholesale distributors and lenders often ask for proof that your shop exists. This is one of the simplest documents to show.
- **Alongside GST.** When you apply for [GST registration](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-process), the proof that matters for your place of business is the rent agreement, electricity bill or property tax receipt. A shop act certificate is useful supporting evidence, especially for a proprietor.
- **Staying on the right side of inspectors.** If you employ people, the Act requires you to keep records of their hours, holidays and wages. Being registered (or intimated) is the first step to showing you comply.

## Documents you will usually need

The exact list varies by state, but most portals ask for:

- **Owner's identity proof:** Aadhaar and PAN.
- **Owner's photograph.**
- **Proof of the shop's address:** rent agreement, electricity bill or property tax receipt. If you rent, some states also ask for the landlord's no-objection letter.
- **Business details:** shop name, nature of business, date you started, opening hours, weekly holiday and the number of workers (men, women, young persons).
- **For partnerships and companies:** partnership deed, or certificate of incorporation and authorisation for the person applying.
- **A photo of the shop front with the name board**, in some states.

Keep scanned copies (JPG or PDF, usually under 1 MB each) ready before you start. Most rejections are about blurred documents or an address that does not match the proof.

## How to apply online, step by step

The flow is broadly the same across state portals:

1. **Go to your state's labour department portal.** In Maharashtra that is Aaple Sarkar or the labour department site, in Karnataka e-Karmika, in Delhi labourcis.nic.in. Search for "[your state] labour department shops and establishments", and check that the address ends in **.gov.in** or **.nic.in**.
2. **Create a login** with your mobile number and email.
3. **Choose the right form.** Intimation (for example Form F in Maharashtra) if you are below the threshold, or registration (Form A in Maharashtra and Delhi, Form Y in Tamil Nadu) if you are above it.
4. **Fill in the shop and owner details** exactly as they appear on your documents.
5. **Upload the documents** and **pay the fee** online, if your state charges one.
6. **Download the acknowledgement or certificate.** Some portals issue it as soon as the online form is complete. Others route it to an inspector first.
7. **Display it at the shop**, where the law requires that, and keep a copy with your business papers.

**Deadlines** vary as well: Delhi gives 90 days from starting and Tamil Nadu six months, while Maharashtra ties the intimation to the start of business. Do it in the first week and you will not have to think about it again.

## Fees, renewal and the agent question

**Government fees** are set by each state. They are often nil for an intimation and small for a registration. Karnataka's ₹405 to ₹5,400 by staff count is a typical example of the higher end for small shops.

**Renewal** used to be a yearly or multi-year chore in most states. Several have abolished it or made registration valid for life. Gujarat, for example, has abolished renewal under its 2019 Act. Check your state's current rule, and diarise a renewal date if it still has one.

**Agents and look-alike sites.** Many sites with official-sounding names charge a service fee on top of the government fee. They are private businesses, and some do little more than fill in the same online form you can fill in yourself. If you use one, know what you are paying for. If you can upload a photo on WhatsApp, you can usually do this yourself on the official portal in 20 minutes.

## Common mistakes

- **Paying for a registration your state no longer requires.** In Maharashtra and Gujarat, a small shop's obligation is lighter than many agents suggest.
- **Applying on a private look-alike site** thinking it is the government portal.
- **Mismatched names and addresses** between the application, the rent agreement and the bank account. Use the same spelling everywhere, including on your bills.
- **Not updating it when things change**: new address, new owner, or crossing the worker threshold. Most states have a separate "notice of change" form.
- **Forgetting the compliance side.** If you employ people, registration is the start. The Act also covers working hours, weekly holidays, leave and wage records.

## FAQ: Shop act registration

### Is shop act registration mandatory for a small shop?

It depends on your state and the number of workers. In Maharashtra, a shop with fewer than 10 workers only files a one-time online intimation in Form F. In Gujarat, the registration threshold is 20 workers since December 2025. In Tamil Nadu, registration is for establishments with 10 or more workers. Karnataka and Delhi register shops more broadly. Check your state labour department portal.

### Is a gumasta licence the same as shop act registration?

Yes. "Gumasta licence" is the common name for registration under the Shops and Establishments Act in Gujarat and some other states. "Shop act licence" is the common name in Maharashtra. They are the same type of registration under different state laws.

### What are the shop act licence fees?

They are set by each state. Intimations are often free. Registration fees in Karnataka, for example, are ₹405 with no employees, ₹810 for 1 to 9 employees and ₹5,400 for 10 to 19. Private agents charge their own service fee on top of the government fee.

### What documents are needed for shop act registration?

Usually the owner's Aadhaar and PAN, a photograph, proof of the shop's address (rent agreement, electricity bill or property tax receipt), business details such as the start date and number of workers, and for firms or companies, the partnership deed or incorporation documents.

### How long does shop act registration take?

On most state portals, an intimation or registration with complete documents is issued quickly, often the same day or within a few working days. Where an inspector reviews the application first, it can take longer.

### Do I need to renew my shop act licence?

It depends on the state. Several states have abolished renewal or made registration valid for life. Gujarat, for example, has abolished renewal under its 2019 Act. Where renewal still applies, the portal shows the expiry date on your certificate.

### Is shop act registration the same as GST registration?

No. Shop act registration is a state labour law registration about your establishment and its workers. GST registration is a tax registration that depends on your turnover. Many shops need both, and the shop act certificate is useful supporting proof when you apply for GST.

## The bottom line

Shop act registration is a **state** matter, and the right answer depends on **where your shop is and how many people work there**. In several states, a small shop needs only a one-time intimation, not a full registration. Apply yourself on the official labour department portal, keep your name and address consistent across every document, and update the registration when anything changes.
