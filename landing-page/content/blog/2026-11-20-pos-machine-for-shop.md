"Do you take card?" is a question every shop owner hears, and the answer usually starts with a call from a bank or fintech sales agent offering a POS machine. The device is the easy part. The real cost is in the monthly rental, the merchant discount rate (MDR) taken from every card payment, and the GST on both, and those differ widely between offers.

This guide explains the four kinds of POS device available to an Indian shop, who provides them, what you will actually pay each month, what the RBI allows banks to charge on debit cards, what changes with NPCI's new UPI MDR, and how to work out whether a card machine is worth it for your counter.

## Quick answer

- **Four options:** a **card machine** (EDC terminal: swipe, chip and tap), an **Android POS** (card machine plus billing app and printer), a **soundbox** (announces UPI payments; some models also take tap cards), and **tap-on-phone** (an app that turns an NFC Android phone into a contactless card reader).
- **Costs:** a one-time fee or monthly rental for the device, **MDR** on each card payment, and **18% GST** on both.
- **Debit card MDR is capped by the RBI:** up to **0.40%** (max ₹200 a transaction) for merchants with turnover up to ₹20 lakh, and **0.90%** (max ₹1,000) above that.
- **RuPay debit cards and UPI** have carried **zero MDR** since January 2020. NPCI has announced a **0.4% MDR on UPI payments to merchants above ₹2,000** (capped at ₹300 from ₹75,000), with small merchants exempt; as of late November 2026 its start date is still to be notified.
- **Credit cards:** no RBI cap; typically **about 1.5% to 2.5%**, more for premium, corporate and international cards.
- **No surcharge:** you cannot add the MDR to the customer's bill.
- **Settlement:** card money usually reaches your account the next working day.
- **Small kirana with mostly UPI?** A UPI QR and a soundbox are usually enough. Take a card machine when customers regularly pay large amounts by card.

## The four types of POS machine

| Type | What it accepts | Best for | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Card machine (EDC terminal)** | Swipe, chip-and-PIN, tap; most also show a UPI QR | Any shop with regular card customers | Rental, MDR, minimum-usage conditions |
| **Android POS** | Cards, UPI, often wallets; runs apps; built-in printer | Restaurants, pharmacies, apparel and electronics shops that want billing and payments in one device | Higher price or rental; you may be tied to the provider's billing app |
| **Soundbox** | UPI QR payments, announced aloud; some "card soundbox" models also take tap cards | Kiranas, tea stalls, fruit and vegetable sellers with many small UPI payments | Monthly rental adds up; most models do not take chip-and-PIN |
| **Tap-on-phone (SoftPOS)** | Contactless cards, phones and watches tapped on an NFC Android phone | Delivery, home services, market stalls, low card volume | Contactless only; payments above the tap limit need a PIN, which some apps handle on screen |

**EDC** stands for electronic data capture, the banking term for a card terminal. A contactless "tap" payment needs no PIN up to **₹5,000** per transaction (the RBI's limit since January 2021); above that, the customer enters a PIN.

## Who provides POS machines

You get a POS device from an **acquirer**: a bank, or a payment company working with banks. Broadly:

- **Banks** (most large public and private banks) offer card terminals to current account holders. Settlement goes straight into your account with that bank, and the branch can sometimes negotiate rates for high-volume shops.
- **Fintech and payment companies** such as **Paytm, PhonePe, Pine Labs, Razorpay, BharatPe and Mswipe** offer card machines, Android POS, soundboxes or tap-on-phone apps, often bundled with a QR, loans or a billing app. Pine Labs is long established in large-format retail; Paytm and PhonePe are widely known for soundboxes; Razorpay and others sell Android POS and online payments together.

No single provider is cheapest for every shop. What decides it is **your mix of payments**: how much comes in by UPI, debit card, RuPay and credit card, and the size of a typical bill. Get written quotes from two or three providers and compare them with the worked example below.

Prices, offers and waivers change often, so treat any figure you read online, including these, as a starting point for negotiation, not a quote.

## MDR charges explained

The **merchant discount rate** is the fee deducted from each payment before it reaches you. It is shared between your bank or provider, the customer's bank and the card network. The rules differ sharply by payment type.

| Payment type | MDR | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Debit card (Visa, Mastercard), shop turnover up to ₹20 lakh** | Up to **0.40%**, max **₹200** per transaction (0.30% for QR-based card payments) | RBI cap since 1 January 2018 |
| **Debit card, shop turnover above ₹20 lakh** | Up to **0.90%**, max **₹1,000** per transaction (0.80% for QR-based) | RBI cap since 1 January 2018 |
| **RuPay debit card** | **Zero** | Government rule since 1 January 2020 |
| **UPI, payments up to ₹2,000** | **Zero** | Continues under NPCI's new framework |
| **UPI, payments above ₹2,000** | **Zero today; 0.4%** once NPCI's framework starts (max ₹300 from ₹75,000) | Small merchants exempt (see below) |
| **Credit card** | No cap; **typically about 1.5% to 2.5%** | Set by your agreement; premium, corporate and international cards cost more |
| **Credit card on UPI (such as RuPay credit)** | Not free; ask your provider | Outside the zero-MDR rule |

### The new UPI MDR, in brief

On 15 September 2026, NPCI announced a **0.4% MDR on select person-to-merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000**, capped at **₹300** per payment for amounts of ₹75,000 and above. Payments of ₹2,000 or less stay free, and person-to-person transfers stay free. **Small merchants**, including street vendors, receiving up to **₹1 lakh a month** through UPI QR codes in the person-to-person-merchant category continue at **zero MDR** on all their payments. The government says about 96% of merchant UPI payments will remain unaffected.

It was due to start on **15 October 2026**. On 13 October, NPCI deferred it until after Diwali, with the date to be notified separately; as of late November 2026, no start date has been announced. Check your provider's notices or NPCI's website before you plan around it. Our guide to [UPI transaction limits](https://billzap.app/blog/upi-transaction-limit) covers the other UPI rules.

### Can you add the card charge to the bill?

No. The RBI's 2017 directions on debit card MDR require acquiring banks to ensure that merchants **do not pass the MDR on to customers**, and card network rules and merchant agreements generally prohibit surcharges on credit cards too. A "2% extra on card" notice at the counter can lead to a complaint and the loss of your terminal. Build card costs into your prices instead.

## Settlement: when the money reaches you

- **Card payments** usually settle to your bank account the **next working day** (T+1). Many providers offer same-day or instant settlement for an extra fee.
- **UPI payments** to a QR linked directly to your bank account arrive almost at once. On some fintech QRs, money is settled to your bank in batches; check the schedule.
- **Chargebacks:** if a cardholder disputes a payment, the amount can be held or reversed. Keep the charge slip and your bill until the dispute window passes.
- **Reconcile weekly:** match settlements in your bank statement against the provider's report. Short settlements and missed payments are easier to fix within days than months.

## Worked example 1: a mobile and accessories shop

Turnover ₹4,00,000 a month (₹48 lakh a year, so above ₹20 lakh), with this mix:

| Payment type | Amount | MDR | Charge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UPI | ₹2,00,000 | 0% today | ₹0 |
| Debit card (Visa/Mastercard) | ₹80,000 | 0.90% (the RBI maximum) | ₹720 |
| RuPay debit | ₹20,000 | 0% | ₹0 |
| Credit card | ₹60,000 | 2% (assumed) | ₹1,200 |
| Cash | ₹40,000 | | ₹0 |
| **Total** | **₹4,00,000** | | **₹1,920** |

Add GST of 18% on the MDR: ₹1,920 × 18% = **₹345.60**. Assume a card machine rental of ₹500 plus ₹90 GST = **₹590** (illustrative; use your quote). Monthly cost: ₹1,920 + ₹345.60 + ₹590 = **₹2,855.60**, or about **0.71%** of sales.

**When the UPI MDR starts:** if ₹1,20,000 of the UPI money comes in payments above ₹2,000 (common for phones and accessories), that adds ₹1,20,000 × 0.4% = ₹480, plus ₹86.40 GST, taking the monthly cost to **₹3,422**. If the shop is GST-registered as a regular taxpayer, the ₹345.60 and ₹86.40 of GST, and the GST on rental, can be claimed back as input tax credit.

## How to choose the right POS machine for your shop

1. **Look at last month's payments.** How much came by UPI, card and cash, and what is the typical bill size?
2. **If cards are rare and bills small**, stay with UPI QR plus a soundbox.
3. **If customers regularly pay large amounts by card**, get a card machine or tap-on-phone.
4. **If you also need billing and printing at the counter**, compare an Android POS against a separate billing app plus card machine; our [billing machine vs billing app](https://billzap.app/blog/billing-machine-vs-billing-app) comparison helps.
5. **Get written quotes** with rental, MDR by card type, GST, minimum-usage conditions and exit terms. Compare the monthly total for your own mix, as in the examples.
6. **Check settlement time** and how disputes are handled.
7. **Ask about RuPay**: it carries zero MDR, so a provider that pushes your customers to RuPay or UPI costs you less.

**Documents** usually asked for: PAN of the business or owner, a bank account with a cancelled cheque, owner's KYC, proof of the shop (GST certificate, shop act licence or trade licence), and a photo of the shop. Each provider's list differs slightly.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing only the rental.** On a busy counter, MDR is the bigger number.
- **Ignoring minimum-usage charges** and paying a penalty every slow month.
- **Adding a card surcharge**, which the rules do not allow.
- **Not reconciling settlements**, so short payments go unnoticed.
- **Keeping two or three soundboxes and machines from different providers**, each with its own rental.

## FAQ

### What are card swipe machine charges in India?

You pay rental or a one-time device fee, MDR on each card payment and 18% GST on both. Debit card MDR is capped by the RBI at 0.40% (max ₹200) for merchants with turnover up to ₹20 lakh and 0.90% (max ₹1,000) above that. Credit cards typically cost about 1.5% to 2.5%.

### Is there MDR on UPI payments?

Not today. NPCI has announced a 0.4% MDR on merchant UPI payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300, with payments up to ₹2,000 and small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month through UPI QR exempt. Its start was deferred to after Diwali 2026 and the date is still to be notified.

### Is there MDR on RuPay debit cards?

No. RuPay debit card payments have carried zero MDR since 1 January 2020.

### Can a shopkeeper charge extra for card payments?

No. MDR must not be passed on to customers. Include the cost in your prices instead.

### Which is the best POS machine in India?

There is no single best one. For mostly small UPI payments, a QR with a soundbox is enough. For regular card customers, compare card machines from your bank and two or three payment companies on total monthly cost for your own payment mix, settlement time and contract terms.

### How long does POS settlement take?

Card payments usually settle the next working day. Instant or same-day settlement is often available for an extra fee.

### What is an EDC machine?

EDC (electronic data capture) is the banking term for a card terminal: the device that reads a card by swipe, chip or tap and sends the payment for approval.

## The bottom line

Start from your own payment mix, not the sales pitch. If most customers pay small amounts by UPI, a QR and perhaps a soundbox cost little, and UPI up to ₹2,000 stays free even under NPCI's new framework. If card customers bring in real sales, get written quotes, compare the full monthly cost including MDR and GST, never surcharge, and reconcile settlements every week.

*Rates and rules are from RBI and NPCI announcements as reported up to November 2026. Credit card MDR and device costs vary by provider; confirm with yours before signing.*
