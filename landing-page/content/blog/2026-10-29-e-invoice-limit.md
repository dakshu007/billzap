Ask three websites about the e-invoice limit and you can get three answers: ₹100 crore with a 7-day window, ₹10 crore, or ₹5 crore. All three were true at some point. E-invoicing under GST arrived in phases, each phase lowered the turnover limit, and a separate 30-day reporting rule was added later with its own, different limit. Old articles never got updated, so the history and the current rule sit side by side in search results.

This guide settles it. It gives the limit that applies today, explains how to work out whether your business has crossed it, lists the businesses that are exempt whatever their turnover, and explains the 30-day rule. It also says plainly what most small shops want to know: if you sell to walk-in customers and your turnover is under ₹5 crore, e-invoicing does not apply to you.

## Quick answer

- **The e-invoice limit is ₹5 crore.** A GST-registered business must issue e-invoices if its **aggregate annual turnover (AATO) exceeded ₹5 crore in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards**. This has applied since **1 August 2023** (Notification 10/2023-Central Tax).
- **Only B2B, B2G and export invoices** need an e-invoice, along with credit and debit notes on them. **Sales to consumers (B2C) do not.**
- **Turnover is counted across the whole PAN**, adding up every GSTIN registered under it, not one branch at a time.
- **Once you cross ₹5 crore in any year, the rule stays with you**, even if turnover falls later.
- **30-day rule:** if your AATO is **₹10 crore or more**, the invoice portal will not accept an invoice older than **30 days** from 1 April 2025. Below ₹10 crore there is no such time limit for now.
- **Exempt whatever their turnover:** SEZ units, insurers, banks and financial institutions including NBFCs, goods transport agencies, passenger transport services, cinema admission tickets, and government departments and local authorities.
- **A typical kirana or retail counter shop** under ₹5 crore, selling mainly to consumers, **does not need e-invoicing**.

## What an e-invoice actually is

"E-invoice" sounds like any bill made on a computer or phone. Under GST it means something narrower.

An e-invoice is an invoice you prepare in your own software and then **report to a government-authorised Invoice Registration Portal (IRP)**. The IRP checks it, gives it a unique **Invoice Reference Number (IRN)**, digitally signs it and returns a **signed QR code**. Only the invoice that carries that IRN and QR code counts as a valid tax invoice for a supply that falls under e-invoicing.

Three things follow from that:

1. **You still make the invoice yourself.** The IRP does not create it; it registers it.
2. **The IRN is a 64-character hash** built from your GSTIN, the financial year, the document type and the invoice number. The same invoice number cannot be registered twice in one financial year.
3. **The data flows onwards automatically.** Registered e-invoice details are passed to the GST system and fill in the matching tables of your [GSTR-1](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-file-gstr-1), and Part A of an [e-way bill](https://billzap.app/blog/e-way-bill-rules) can be generated from the same data.

A PDF bill you share on WhatsApp is an electronic invoice in the everyday sense. It is not an "e-invoice" in the GST sense unless it has an IRN and the signed QR code.

## How the e-invoice limit came down

This is the history that confuses search results. Each phase lowered the AATO limit:

| Phase | Mandatory from | AATO above |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 October 2020 | ₹500 crore |
| 2 | 1 January 2021 | ₹100 crore |
| 3 | 1 April 2021 | ₹50 crore |
| 4 | 1 April 2022 | ₹20 crore |
| 5 | 1 October 2022 | ₹10 crore |
| 6 | 1 August 2023 | **₹5 crore** (current) |

No further cut has been notified since August 2023. You may read that the limit "will soon" fall again. Until a notification is issued, the limit is ₹5 crore.

## How to check if the e-invoice limit applies to you

Work through these four questions in order.

### 1. Has your AATO exceeded ₹5 crore in any year since 2017-18?

Aggregate turnover is the total value of all taxable supplies, exempt supplies, exports and inter-state supplies made by **all GSTINs under your PAN**. It leaves out the GST itself and inward supplies taxed under reverse charge.

Check every financial year from 2017-18 to the last completed one. If even one year is above ₹5 crore, you are covered from the year after that. A dip below ₹5 crore later does not take you out.

**Example:** a wholesaler had turnover of ₹5.3 crore in 2021-22 and ₹4.6 crore in each year since. It is still covered, because one year since 2017-18 was above ₹5 crore.

### 2. Are you in an exempt category?

If you are an SEZ unit, an insurer, a bank, an NBFC or other financial institution, a goods transport agency, a passenger transport service provider, a cinema selling admission tickets, or a government department or local authority, e-invoicing does not apply however large your turnover is. SEZ **developers** are not exempt; SEZ **units** are.

### 3. Is the supply B2B, B2G or an export?

E-invoicing covers:

- **B2B** tax invoices to GST-registered buyers.
- **B2G** supplies to government buyers with a GSTIN.
- **Exports**, including deemed exports and supplies to SEZs.
- **Credit notes and debit notes** issued against such invoices.

It does **not** cover B2C sales to unregistered buyers, bills of supply, or delivery challans. Those carry on as normal invoices.

### 4. Is your AATO ₹10 crore or more?

If yes, the 30-day reporting limit below also applies.

## The 30-day e-invoice rule

This is the rule people mix up with the limit itself. It does not decide **whether** you e-invoice; it decides **how late** you can register an invoice.

- From **1 November 2023**, businesses with AATO of **₹100 crore or more** could not report an invoice to the IRP more than **30 days** after its date.
- From **1 April 2025**, GSTN lowered this to AATO of **₹10 crore or more**.
- Businesses with AATO **under ₹10 crore** have **no reporting time limit** for now, though they still have to register the invoice before they issue it.

**How it works in practice:** an invoice dated 1 April cannot be reported after 30 April. The IRP simply rejects it, so no IRN is created. The restriction covers invoices, credit notes and debit notes. The date that counts is the date on the document, not the date you upload it.

If you are a ₹10 crore+ business, the practical fix is to generate the IRN at the moment you make the invoice, not in a batch at month end.

Some older articles still describe a "7-day" rule for ₹100 crore businesses. That was a proposal that was replaced; the rule that took effect was 30 days.

## What happens if you skip an e-invoice you needed

For a supply that falls under e-invoicing, an invoice without an IRN is **not a valid invoice** under the GST rules. That has real consequences:

- **Your buyer cannot claim input tax credit** on it, and will usually ask you to fix it or stop buying from you.
- **Penalty under Section 122:** failing to issue a proper invoice can attract a penalty of **₹10,000 or the tax involved, whichever is higher**, per invoice.
- **Penalty under Section 125:** smaller lapses, such as a wrong or incomplete e-invoice, can attract a general penalty of up to **₹25,000**.
- **E-way bill and transport trouble:** goods moving on an invoice that should have been e-invoiced can be detained.

There is no paid "late e-invoice" option. If you are under ₹10 crore and missed one, register it as soon as you notice. If you are ₹10 crore or more and the 30 days have passed, the IRP will refuse it, so speak to your CA about correcting the transaction.

## How to generate an e-invoice

1. **Register on an IRP** using your GSTIN. The government's own portal is run by NIC, and several private IRPs are also authorised.
2. **Prepare the invoice** in your accounting or billing software in the standard e-invoice schema (INV-01), with GSTINs, HSN codes, values and tax.
3. **Upload it** to the IRP: by hand as a JSON file, through an offline tool, or directly from software that has an API connection.
4. **Receive the IRN and signed QR code**, and print them on the invoice you give the buyer.
5. **Cancel within 24 hours if needed.** An e-invoice can be cancelled on the IRP within 24 hours of generation. After that, correct it with a credit note or change it in GSTR-1 before filing.

## If you are exempt, say so

Exempt businesses and businesses below the limit often get asked by large buyers, "Where is the IRN?" Two simple habits help:

- **Print a one-line declaration** on your invoice, for example: "E-invoicing is not applicable to us as our aggregate turnover has not exceeded ₹5 crore in any financial year." Exempt categories can name their category instead.
- **Use the declaration facility on the e-invoice portal**, where a business can record that it is not liable to e-invoicing. Buyers can then check it.

Buyers' accounts teams are cautious because an invoice without a required IRN costs them their ITC. A clear declaration usually settles the question.

## FAQ

### What is the current e-invoice limit under GST?

₹5 crore of aggregate annual turnover. If your AATO exceeded ₹5 crore in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards, you must issue e-invoices for B2B, B2G and export supplies. This has applied since 1 August 2023.

### Is e-invoicing required for B2C sales?

No. E-invoicing covers supplies to registered persons (B2B), government buyers (B2G) and exports. Bills to ordinary consumers stay normal tax invoices. The GST Council has recommended a voluntary B2C e-invoicing pilot for some sectors, but it is not mandatory.

### Is the e-invoice limit checked per GSTIN or per PAN?

Per PAN. Add up the turnover of every GSTIN registered under the same PAN across all states. If the total crossed ₹5 crore in any year since 2017-18, every one of those GSTINs must e-invoice its B2B supplies.

### My turnover fell below ₹5 crore. Can I stop e-invoicing?

No. The test is whether turnover exceeded the limit in **any** financial year since 2017-18. Once you crossed it, e-invoicing continues even if later years are lower.

### What is the 30-day rule for e-invoices?

From 1 April 2025, businesses with AATO of ₹10 crore or more cannot report an invoice, credit note or debit note to the IRP more than 30 days after its date. Businesses under ₹10 crore have no such limit at present.

### Who is exempt from e-invoicing?

SEZ units, insurance companies, banks and financial institutions including NBFCs, goods transport agencies, passenger transport services, cinema admission tickets, and government departments and local authorities. They are exempt whatever their turnover.

## The bottom line

The e-invoice limit is ₹5 crore of PAN-wide turnover in any year since 2017-18, and it applies only to B2B, B2G and export invoices. The 30-day rule is a separate limit for businesses at ₹10 crore and above. If you are under ₹5 crore, or you sell to consumers, carry on issuing proper GST invoices and keep your numbering clean. That is all the law asks of you.
