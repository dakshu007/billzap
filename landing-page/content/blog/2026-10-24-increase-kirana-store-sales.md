Mrs Iyer has bought her milk from Suresh's store every morning for six years. One Saturday she walks past with a trolley bag from the new supermarket on the main road. She waves, a little guiltily. Suresh waves back and wonders what else he has already lost without noticing: the shampoo she now buys there, the festival sweets, the monthly rice.

Most kirana stores do not lose customers in one go. They lose a few items at a time, to an app here and a supermarket there. The good news is that most of the fixes are cheap, and many cost nothing but attention. This guide gives 15 ideas sized for a single-counter store, with what each costs, how hard it is, and how to tell whether it worked. Start with the first one, because it makes every other one measurable.

## Quick answer

- **Your sales are two numbers multiplied:** **bills per day × average bill value**. Grow either one and sales grow. Track both every week.
- **Fix the basics first:** never run out of milk, bread, eggs and staples; put higher-margin items at eye level; keep an impulse shelf by the counter.
- **Make it easy to buy from you:** **WhatsApp orders**, home delivery within a short radius, every **UPI** app accepted, and a **Google Business Profile** so "kirana near me" finds you.
- **Give people a reason to come back:** combos and festival packs, a simple stamp card, and stocking what regulars ask for.
- **Protect cash:** a limited **udhaar** (credit) policy with reminders, so your money funds stock instead of sitting in other people's pockets.
- Most of these cost **little or nothing**. The hardest part is doing them every week.

## Start here: the two numbers that make up your sales

### 1. Know your bills per day and your average bill

Every rupee you take in comes from two things: how many bills you make, and how much each bill is worth on average.

**Sales = bills per day × average bill value**

Take a store that makes 120 bills a day at an average of ₹250. That is ₹30,000 a day.

| Change | Bills a day | Average bill | Daily sales | Extra per day | Extra per 30 days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Today | 120 | ₹250 | ₹30,000 | — | — |
| Average bill up ₹20 | 120 | ₹270 | ₹32,400 | ₹2,400 | ₹72,000 |
| 10 more bills a day | 130 | ₹250 | ₹32,500 | ₹2,500 | ₹75,000 |
| Both together | 130 | ₹270 | ₹35,100 | ₹5,100 | ₹1,53,000 |

Twenty rupees more per bill is one packet of biscuits. Ten more bills a day is ten more people. Neither sounds dramatic, yet together they add over ₹1.5 lakh of sales a month in this example. At a 12% blended margin, the ₹72,000 from the bigger average bill alone is about ₹8,640 of extra gross profit a month.

Every idea below works on one of these two numbers. Some bring more people through the door; others make each visit a little bigger.

**Takeaway:** you cannot improve what you do not count. Note bills per day and average bill value every week before trying anything else.

## Ideas that make each visit bigger

### 2. Never run out of the fast movers

A customer who comes for milk and finds none does not just skip the milk. They go to the next store, and buy their bread and eggs there too. Do that twice and the habit moves.

List your 20 fastest-moving items: milk, curd, bread, eggs, atta, rice, dal, oil, sugar, tea, salt, onions and the best-selling soaps and biscuits in your area. Set a minimum quantity for each and reorder before you reach it, not after.

**Takeaway:** a stock-out costs you the whole bill, not one item. Protect your top 20 above everything else.

### 3. Put the right items at eye level

Customers pick up what they see first. The shelf at eye level is your most valuable space, so give it to items that earn you more: personal care, packaged snacks, ready-to-cook foods, branded spices.

Keep the staples people come for, like atta and rice, towards the back or the far end. Customers will walk past other shelves to reach them, and some of what they pass ends up on the bill.

Do not overload the shelves with choice. In a well-known study by Iyengar and Lepper (2000), a tasting table with 24 jams attracted more shoppers, but only about 3% of them bought, compared with about 30% when 6 jams were shown. For a small store, two or three good options in a category usually sell better than eight.

**Takeaway:** eye level for what earns, the back for what people need anyway, and fewer, better choices on each shelf.

### 4. Build an impulse shelf by the counter

The counter is where people wait, and waiting people look around. Keep small, cheap, useful things within reach: chocolates, biscuits, chewing gum, batteries, small shampoo sachets, matchboxes, lighters, phone chargers, pens.

These are low-value items, so they rarely put anyone off. They are also exactly the ₹10 to ₹30 additions that lift the average bill.

**Takeaway:** the space by the counter should earn on every bill. Refresh it every couple of weeks so regulars notice something new.

### 5. Sell combos and festival packs

A combo saves the customer a decision. A "monthly ration kit" with atta, rice, dal, oil, sugar and tea at a fair price brings in a big bill at once and locks in the month's staples.

Festivals are the best moments. Ahead of Diwali, Pongal, Eid, Onam, Durga Puja or Christmas, put together what your area actually buys: dry fruit boxes, sweets, puja items, baking supplies. Display them at the front a week early.

Keep combos honest: a real saving, clearly priced, made from items you would sell anyway.

**Takeaway:** combos raise the average bill and make your store the first stop for the month's shopping and the festival list.

### 6. Make loose staples and honest weighing your edge

Supermarkets sell staples in fixed packs. You can sell 750 grams of dal, half a kilo of sugar, or rice of a quality you have checked yourself. That flexibility matters to people buying on a budget.

Back it with trust. Use a weighing scale that has been verified and stamped by your state's Legal Metrology department, weigh in front of the customer, and never charge above the MRP on packaged goods.

**Takeaway:** sell what a pack cannot: the exact quantity people want, weighed fairly in front of them.

## Ideas that bring more people through the door

### 7. Take orders on WhatsApp and deliver nearby

Many of your regulars already message you. Make it official: put your WhatsApp number on the board, on bags and on every bill, and offer free delivery within a short radius above a minimum order.

You do not need an app or a delivery fleet. A helper on a bicycle or scooter during quiet hours is enough. Start with a small area, say a few hundred metres, and a minimum that covers the trip.

Delivery wins the customers who are busy, elderly, or simply do not want to step out. It also competes directly with quick-commerce apps on their own ground, without their fees.

**Takeaway:** a phone number and a reliable helper can turn your store into the neighbourhood's delivery service.

### 8. Use WhatsApp Business, with permission

The free WhatsApp Business app lets you set business hours, a short catalogue of items with prices, quick replies for common questions, and labels for customers.

For offers, use broadcast lists. They only reach people who have saved your number, which keeps them permission-based. Send one useful message a week at most: a festival pack, fresh stock that arrived, a weekend offer. More than that and people mute you.

**Takeaway:** one helpful message a week to people who chose to hear from you beats daily forwards to everyone.

### 9. Get found on Google

When people move into an area, or search for something at 9 pm, they type "kirana store near me" or "grocery store near me". A free Google Business Profile puts your store on the map in those results.

Add accurate opening hours, your phone number, clear photos of the shopfront and shelves, and the fact that you deliver if you do. Ask happy regulars to leave a review. Reply to reviews politely, including the critical ones.

**Takeaway:** fifteen minutes on a Google Business Profile can bring you customers who did not know you existed.

### 10. Accept every UPI app, without friction

Many customers now carry no cash at all. If paying is awkward, they go where it is easy.

Display one clear UPI QR code that works with every UPI app, keep it at the counter where the customer can scan it without leaning over, and use a payment sound box or notifications so you confirm payment instantly. Printing the UPI QR on bills also helps with deliveries and customers who settle later.

**Takeaway:** never let payment be the reason someone walks out. Make UPI as easy as handing over a note.

### 11. Add services that bring footfall

Small services bring people in who then buy something: mobile and DTH recharges, electricity and other bill payments, courier pick-up and drop, help with LPG booking. Some stores also act as a parcel collection point.

Only offer what you are allowed to, through recognised providers, and keep it simple enough that it does not slow the counter at rush hour.

**Takeaway:** every service visit is a chance for a sale. Pick one or two that suit your area.

### 12. Explore selling online, carefully

You can also reach customers online through apps connected to ONDC, the government-backed open network for digital commerce, or through local delivery platforms. This can bring orders from people who never walk past your shop.

Go slowly. Check the commission, who handles delivery, and how orders and returns are managed before you sign up. Keep your stock counts accurate, because an online order you cannot fulfil hurts your rating.

**Takeaway:** online listings can add customers, but only once your stock and delivery basics are solid.

## Ideas that bring people back

### 13. Keep a simple loyalty habit

You do not need software for loyalty. A stamp card that gives a small gift after ten visits or ten orders, a free packet of tea on a customer's birthday, or remembering a regular's usual order all work.

Keep the reward small and the rule simple, so it does not eat your margin. What matters is that people feel recognised.

**Takeaway:** a printed stamp card and a good memory are a loyalty programme.

### 14. Listen, and stock what regulars ask for

Every time a customer asks for something you do not have, you have learned something. Keep a "requests" notebook at the counter and write it down: the item, the brand, how often it was asked for.

Once a week, read it. If three people asked for the same brand of ghee, stock a few. Tell the customer who asked first that it has arrived. That one message often brings them back for more than the ghee.

**Takeaway:** your customers are telling you what to stock. Write it down and act on it weekly.

### 15. A clean, bright, well-labelled store

People judge a store in the first few seconds. Good lighting, clean floors, dust-free shelves, front-facing products and clear price labels all tell the customer that the stock is fresh and the prices are fair.

Price labels matter more than they seem. A customer who has to ask the price of everything buys less. Clear labels also make your helper faster at the counter.

**Takeaway:** clean and labelled looks trustworthy. It costs a few hours a week, not money.

## Protect the cash that funds your stock

Selling more only helps if the money comes back. **Udhaar** (credit) is where many kirana stores leak cash: the sales are made, but the money sits with customers while you pay distributors on time.

Set rules and stick to them:

- Credit only for regulars you know.
- A limit per customer and a cap on total credit outstanding.
- A fixed settlement date each month.
- A record of every credit sale, and a polite reminder on WhatsApp when payment is due.

**Takeaway:** every rupee stuck in udhaar is a rupee not on your shelves.

## What to do this week

You do not need all 15 ideas at once. Start with these, which cost almost nothing:

1. Count bills per day and work out your average bill for the last seven days.
2. Write your top 20 fast movers and a minimum stock level for each.
3. Move one higher-margin category to eye level.
4. Set up a small impulse shelf at the counter.
5. Put your WhatsApp number on the board and start a requests notebook.
6. Create or update your Google Business Profile with hours, phone and photos.
7. Write down your udhaar rules and the total currently outstanding.

Next week, add one combo and one idea from the "bring more people" list.

## Measure it

Try one or two changes at a time, and give each at least two weeks. Then compare:

- **Bills per day**, averaged over the week.
- **Average bill value**: total sales divided by number of bills.
- **Stock-outs**: how many times a top-20 item ran out.
- **Udhaar outstanding** at the end of each week.

If bills per day went up, your footfall ideas are working. If the average bill went up, your placement and combos are. If neither moved, try something else rather than doing more of the same.

## FAQ: increasing kirana store sales

### How can I increase sales in my kirana store quickly?

Start with the cheapest changes: never let your top 20 items run out, move higher-margin products to eye level, add an impulse shelf by the counter, and put your WhatsApp number on the board for orders. Track bills per day and average bill value each week to see what is working.

### How do I increase footfall in my shop?

Make your store easy to find and easy to use: a Google Business Profile with accurate hours and photos, WhatsApp ordering with nearby delivery, every UPI app accepted, and services like recharges or bill payments that bring people in. Never running out of basics keeps the regulars you already have.

### How can a kirana store compete with supermarkets and quick-commerce apps?

By doing what they find hard: being close, opening early and late, selling loose staples in any quantity, delivering without a minimum fee in a small radius, and knowing customers by name. Combine that with clean shelves, clear prices and reliable stock.

### Is WhatsApp marketing useful for a small shop?

Yes, if it is permission-based and occasional. Use the free WhatsApp Business app for a catalogue and quick replies, and broadcast lists to send a useful offer or stock update about once a week to people who have saved your number.

### Should a kirana store offer home delivery?

In most neighbourhoods, yes, within a short radius and above a minimum order that covers the trip. A helper on a bicycle or scooter during quiet hours is enough to start. It keeps busy and elderly customers buying from you instead of an app.

### How do I increase the average bill value?

Place higher-margin items at eye level, keep small add-ons by the counter, offer combos and monthly ration kits, and prepare festival packs ahead of time. Even ₹20 more per bill adds up: at 120 bills a day it is ₹2,400 a day.

### Does giving credit (udhaar) increase sales?

It can build loyalty, but uncontrolled credit drains the cash you need for stock. Give credit only to regulars, with a limit per customer, a total cap, a monthly settlement date and a written record, and send polite reminders when it is due.

## The bottom line

Kirana sales rarely jump because of one big idea. They grow because a store stops running out of milk, puts the right things at eye level, answers WhatsApp, shows up on Google, makes UPI effortless and keeps its credit under control.
