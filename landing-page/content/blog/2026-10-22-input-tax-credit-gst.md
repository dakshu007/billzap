Every GST-registered shop pays GST twice in a sense: once to its suppliers when it buys stock, and once to the government on what it sells. **Input tax credit**, or ITC, is what stops that from being double taxation. The GST you paid on your purchases is set off against the GST you collected on your sales, and you pay only the difference. For most traders, it is the single biggest number in the monthly return.

It is also the number most often lost. A credit can disappear because a supplier filed late, because a bill was not paid within 180 days, because the item was on the blocked list, or because the claim was made after the deadline. This guide explains how input tax credit in GST works, the conditions you must meet before claiming it, what is blocked, the order in which credit is used, and a month-end routine that keeps it safe, with worked examples in rupees.

## Quick answer

- **What it is:** the GST you paid on business purchases, set off against the GST you collected on sales. You pay only the difference in cash.
- **Example:** output tax of ₹12,600 on sales, minus ₹9,000 of eligible input tax on purchases, leaves **₹3,600** to pay.
- **Conditions (Section 16):** you hold a **tax invoice**, you have **received** the goods or services, the supplier has **filed** the invoice so it shows in your **GSTR-2B**, the supplier has **paid** the tax, and you **file GSTR-3B**.
- **Pay your supplier within 180 days** of the invoice date, or reverse the credit with interest until you do.
- **Deadline:** claim by **30 November** after the end of the financial year, or when you file the annual return, whichever is earlier.
- **Blocked items** under Section 17(5), such as most cars, food and personal use, never give credit.
- **Composition dealers cannot claim ITC** at all.

## What is input tax credit in GST?

GST is charged at every stage of a sale, but it is meant to be a tax on the value added at each stage, not on the full price again and again. Input tax credit is the mechanism that makes this work.

- **Output tax** is the GST you charge customers on your sales.
- **Input tax** is the GST you paid suppliers on goods and services you use in your business.
- **GST payable** for the period is output tax minus eligible input tax credit.

A worked example, for a shop that buys and sells goods at 18%:

| | Value | GST at 18% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stock bought from a registered supplier | ₹50,000 | ₹9,000 (input tax) |
| Sales to customers | ₹70,000 | ₹12,600 (output tax) |
| **GST payable for the period** | | **₹12,600 − ₹9,000 = ₹3,600** |

With credit, the shop pays tax only on its own value addition: 18% of ₹20,000, which is ₹3,600. If you need the arithmetic behind these figures, our guide on [how to calculate GST](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-calculate-gst) works through it step by step.

Credit is available on **inputs** (stock and materials), **input services** (rent, transport, telephone, accountancy) and **capital goods** (machinery, computers, counters), as long as they are used in the course of business and are not on the blocked list.

## Who can claim input tax credit?

Only a person **registered under GST as a regular taxpayer** can claim ITC. Three groups cannot:

- **Unregistered businesses.** If you are below the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) and have not registered, the GST on your purchases is simply a cost.
- **Composition dealers.** The composition scheme trades a low flat rate on turnover for giving up input tax credit entirely. Our guide to the [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) shows when that trade is worth it.

**ITC when you first register.** Under Section 18(1), a business that registers can claim credit on the stock it holds on the day before registration, provided it has the purchase invoices. The claim is made in form **ITC-01** within 30 days of becoming eligible.

## The conditions to claim ITC (Section 16)

Section 16(2) of the CGST Act sets the conditions, and **all of them** must hold for each invoice. Missing one is enough to lose the credit.

| Condition | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| **(a) A valid document** | You hold a tax invoice or debit note from a registered supplier, or a bill of entry for imports. It must carry your GSTIN. |
| **(aa) It shows in GSTR-2B** | Since 1 January 2022, the supplier must have reported the invoice in their GSTR-1 or IFF, so it appears in your GSTR-2B. |
| **(b) Goods or services received** | You have actually received them. Goods sent to a third party on your direction count as received. |
| **(ba) Not restricted** | Since 1 October 2022, the credit must not be marked as restricted in your GSTR-2B. |
| **(c) Supplier has paid the tax** | The supplier has actually paid the GST to the government. If not, your credit must be reversed with interest, and can be re-availed when they pay. |
| **(d) You have filed your return** | You have filed GSTR-3B for the period. |

A few further rules sit alongside these:

- **Goods received in lots.** When an invoice is delivered in instalments, the credit is available only when the **last lot** arrives.
- **No double benefit on capital goods.** If you claim depreciation on the tax part of a capital good for income tax, you cannot also claim ITC on it.
- **Business use only.** Credit is for goods and services used or intended to be used in your business.

### GSTR-2B is now the rule, not a guide

Under **Rule 36(4)**, since 1 January 2022 you can claim credit only to the extent it appears in your **GSTR-2B**, the monthly auto-generated statement of purchases your suppliers have reported. The old allowance that let businesses claim an extra 20%, then 10%, then 5% on top of what suppliers had uploaded is gone.

In practice, your supplier's filing decides your credit. An invoice uploaded late is claimed in the month it appears in GSTR-2B; one never uploaded cannot be claimed at all.

### The Invoice Management System (IMS)

Since **October 2024**, the GST portal has an **Invoice Management System**. Each invoice your suppliers report appears there, and you can **accept** it, **reject** it, or keep it **pending**. Accepted invoices flow into your GSTR-2B; rejected ones do not; pending ones can be carried forward to a later month.

The useful habit: before GSTR-2B is generated each month, accept the invoices you have genuinely received and reject anything you do not recognise. A wrong invoice is far easier to fix here than after the credit is claimed.

## The 180-day payment rule

Credit is given on the understanding that you will pay your supplier. **Rule 37** enforces that: if you do not pay the supplier the value of the supply **plus the tax** within **180 days** of the invoice date, you must reverse the credit in proportion to the amount unpaid, with interest, in your GSTR-3B.

Example: you buy goods worth ₹1,00,000 plus ₹18,000 GST, an invoice of ₹1,18,000. By day 180 you have paid ₹59,000, half the invoice.

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Invoice value | ₹1,18,000 |
| Paid within 180 days | ₹59,000 (50%) |
| ITC claimed | ₹18,000 |
| ITC to reverse (50% of ₹18,000) | **₹9,000**, plus interest |

The reversed ₹9,000 is not lost for good. Once you pay the balance, you can re-claim it, and the normal time limit does not apply to that re-claim. The rule does not apply to tax paid under reverse charge.

## Time limit for claiming ITC

Under **Section 16(4)**, the credit on an invoice must be claimed by the earlier of:

- **30 November** following the end of the financial year to which the invoice belongs, or
- the date you file your **annual return (GSTR-9)** for that year.

So an invoice dated **15 February 2026** (financial year 2025-26) must be claimed by **30 November 2026**, or earlier if you file GSTR-9 for 2025-26 before that date. A credit not claimed by then is lost, even if every other condition was met.

A supplier who reports a 2025-26 invoice only in December 2026 has cost you that credit.

## Blocked credits: Section 17(5)

Some purchases never give input tax credit, whatever the paperwork. **Section 17(5)** lists them. The ones a shop or small business is most likely to meet:

| Blocked item | Notes and exceptions |
| --- | --- |
| **Motor vehicles** for carrying up to 13 people, including the driver | Allowed if you use them for further supply of vehicles, transporting passengers, driving training, or transporting goods. Repairs, maintenance and insurance of a blocked vehicle are blocked too. |
| **Food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery** | Allowed only if you use them to make the same kind of supply, or the law requires you to provide them to employees. Snacks and tea for the shop are blocked. |
| **Club, health and fitness centre membership** | Blocked. |
| **Life and health insurance** | Same exceptions as food and catering. |
| **Travel benefits for employees on vacation**, such as leave travel concession | Blocked. |
| **Construction of immovable property** on your own account, including works contracts | Blocked, except for plant and machinery. A new shop building or a major renovation does not give credit. |
| **Goods or services for personal use** | Blocked. A phone or TV bought for home is not a business purchase. |
| **Goods lost, stolen, destroyed or written off, or given as gifts or free samples** | Blocked. If you already claimed credit and the goods are later lost, it must be reversed. |
| **Tax paid under the composition scheme** | Blocked. |
| **Tax paid because of fraud or wilful misstatement** (demands under Section 74) | Blocked. |

**Exempt supplies.** If you sell both taxable and exempt goods, the credit on common purchases has to be split, and the share relating to exempt sales reversed under **Rules 42 and 43**. A shop selling mostly taxable goods with a few exempt lines should ask its accountant to work out the proportion.

## How input tax credit is used: the utilisation order

Credit sits in your electronic credit ledger under three heads: IGST, CGST and SGST (or UTGST). It cannot be used in any order you like. **Sections 49 and 49A, read with Rule 88A,** set the order:

| Credit of | Use first against | Then against | Never against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **IGST** | IGST | CGST and SGST, in any order | — |
| **CGST** | CGST | IGST | SGST |
| **SGST / UTGST** | SGST / UTGST | IGST | CGST |

Two rules matter most. **IGST credit must be used up first**, and **CGST credit can never pay SGST, nor SGST credit pay CGST.** Our guide to the [difference between CGST, SGST and IGST](https://billzap.app/blog/cgst-sgst-igst-difference) explains why the three heads exist.

### A worked example

A shop has these figures for the month:

| | IGST | CGST | SGST | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Output tax (liability) | ₹10,000 | ₹6,000 | ₹6,000 | ₹22,000 |
| Input tax credit available | ₹14,000 | ₹3,000 | ₹2,000 | ₹19,000 |

1. **IGST credit first, against IGST.** ₹10,000 of the ₹14,000 pays the IGST liability in full. ₹4,000 of IGST credit is left.
2. **The remaining IGST credit against CGST and SGST.** Say ₹2,000 goes to each. CGST liability falls to ₹4,000 and SGST to ₹4,000.
3. **CGST credit against CGST.** ₹3,000 brings CGST down to **₹1,000**, paid in cash.
4. **SGST credit against SGST.** ₹2,000 brings SGST down to **₹2,000**, paid in cash.

| | IGST | CGST | SGST | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paid from IGST credit | ₹10,000 | ₹2,000 | ₹2,000 | ₹14,000 |
| Paid from CGST credit | — | ₹3,000 | — | ₹3,000 |
| Paid from SGST credit | — | — | ₹2,000 | ₹2,000 |
| **Paid in cash** | **₹0** | **₹1,000** | **₹2,000** | **₹3,000** |

The total cash payment is ₹22,000 − ₹19,000 = **₹3,000**. The portal works out the set-off in GSTR-3B; knowing the order explains why a large CGST credit cannot reduce an SGST bill.

### The 1% cash rule for larger businesses

**Rule 86B** applies to businesses whose taxable supplies exceed **₹50 lakh in a month**. They cannot pay more than 99% of their output tax from credit: at least 1% has to be paid in cash. There are exceptions, such as businesses that paid more than ₹1 lakh of income tax in each of the last two years, those receiving certain refunds, and government bodies and public sector undertakings. Most small shops are well below the threshold.

## How to claim ITC in GSTR-3B

Input tax credit is claimed in your monthly or quarterly **GSTR-3B** return.

1. **Act on IMS.** Accept, reject or keep pending the invoices your suppliers have reported.
2. **Check GSTR-2B.** Once it is generated for the period, compare it with your own purchase register. Note invoices missing from it and follow up with those suppliers.
3. **Open GSTR-3B.** Table 4 (eligible ITC) is auto-populated from GSTR-2B.
4. **Review and adjust.** Remove anything blocked under Section 17(5), and enter reversals, such as unpaid bills past 180 days or credit relating to exempt supplies, in the reversal rows of Table 4.
5. **Offset and file.** The portal sets the credit against your liability in the utilisation order; pay any balance in cash and file with OTP or digital signature.

Your own sales side matters here too. The liability in Table 3 of GSTR-3B is auto-filled from your GSTR-1, and from the July 2025 return period this auto-filled figure is locked, so corrections are made by amending GSTR-1 through GSTR-1A rather than by editing GSTR-3B. ## A month-end ITC routine for a small shop

A short routine each month protects most of your credit:

- **Insist on a GST invoice with your GSTIN** for every business purchase. A cash memo or an invoice without your GSTIN gives no credit. Check a new supplier's GSTIN is valid and active before you start buying; our guide to [GST number search](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-number-search) shows how.
- **Act on IMS and match GSTR-2B** against your purchase register before filing.
- **Chase suppliers who have not filed.** Their late GSTR-1 delays or kills your credit.
- **Pay suppliers within 180 days**, or plan for the reversal if you cannot.
- **Keep blocked items out** of your claim: staff food, cars, personal purchases, gifts.
- **Claim within the time limit**, and do a final sweep of the year's missing invoices before 30 November.
- **Keep invoices and payment proof** for at least six years from the annual return due date, the period the law requires.

## FAQ: input tax credit

### What is input tax credit in GST?

It is the GST you paid on goods and services used in your business, which you can set off against the GST you collected on your sales. If you collected ₹12,600 and paid ₹9,000 on eligible purchases, you pay ₹3,600 in cash.

### What are the conditions to claim ITC?

You must hold a tax invoice or debit note, have received the goods or services, the invoice must appear in your GSTR-2B, the supplier must have paid the tax to the government, and you must file GSTR-3B. You must also pay the supplier within 180 days, and the purchase must not be blocked under Section 17(5).

### Can I claim ITC if the invoice is not in GSTR-2B?

No. Since 1 January 2022, credit is limited to what appears in GSTR-2B. If the supplier reports the invoice later, you can claim it in the month it appears, as long as you are still within the time limit.

### What is the time limit to claim input tax credit?

The earlier of 30 November following the end of the financial year and the date you file the annual return for that year. An invoice dated in February 2026 must be claimed by 30 November 2026.

### On which purchases is ITC not available?

Items blocked under Section 17(5): most motor vehicles for up to 13 people, food and beverages, catering, club memberships, most insurance, employee holiday travel, construction of buildings, personal-use goods, goods lost or given away free, tax paid under composition and tax demanded for fraud. Credit is also not available to composition dealers.

### What happens if I do not pay my supplier within 180 days?

You must reverse the input tax credit in proportion to the unpaid amount, with interest, in GSTR-3B. Once you pay the supplier, you can re-claim the credit, and the normal time limit does not apply to that re-claim.

### In what order is input tax credit used?

IGST credit is used first, against IGST and then against CGST and SGST in any order. CGST credit is used against CGST and then IGST; SGST credit against SGST and then IGST. CGST credit can never pay SGST, and SGST credit can never pay CGST.

### Can a composition dealer claim input tax credit?

No. The composition scheme charges a low flat rate on turnover in exchange for giving up input tax credit. A composition dealer also cannot charge GST on its bills, so its customers cannot claim credit on those purchases either.

## The bottom line

Input tax credit turns GST into a tax on the value you add instead of on the full price. It is also conditional: a valid invoice with your GSTIN, a supplier who reports and pays on time, payment within 180 days, nothing from the blocked list, and a claim before the deadline. Miss one, and the credit becomes a cost.

The routine that protects it is short: accept invoices in IMS, match GSTR-2B, chase late suppliers and pay your own on time. And on your sales side, give your buyers clean invoices they can claim on.
