Most small shop owners in India file their income tax return on **ITR-4**, also called **Sugam**. It is the short form for people who choose **presumptive taxation**: instead of keeping full books and proving every expense, you declare a fixed share of your turnover as profit, 8% of cash sales and 6% of digital sales, and pay tax on that.

This year there is an extra layer of confusion. The **Income-tax Act, 2025** came into force on 1 April 2026 and renumbered almost every section, so pages now mix "Section 44AD" and "Section 58" without saying which applies when. This guide sorts that out, then walks through eligibility, the arithmetic for a real kirana, the deadlines and the traps.

## Quick answer

- **ITR-4 (Sugam)** is for resident individuals, HUFs and partnership firms (not LLPs) with total income up to **₹50 lakh** who declare business income on a presumptive basis.
- **Presumptive income for a shop:** at least **8% of turnover received in cash** and **6% of turnover received digitally** (UPI, bank transfer, account payee cheque) by the return due date. You may declare more.
- **Turnover limit:** **₹2 crore**, or **₹3 crore** if cash receipts are no more than **5%** of total receipts.
- **Which law:** income of **FY 2025-26** (AY 2026-27, returns filed in 2026) is under the **Income-tax Act, 1961**, Section 44AD. Income from **1 April 2026** is under the **Income-tax Act, 2025**, where the same scheme sits in **Section 58**.
- **Deadline for FY 2025-26:** **31 August 2026** for non-audit business returns (ITR-3 and ITR-4). Missed it? A **belated return** can be filed until **31 December 2026**, with a late fee of up to ₹5,000.
- **Tax:** the new regime is the default; with the rebate, income up to **₹12 lakh** pays no tax. You still have to file if income is above **₹4 lakh**.

## Which law applies to your return?

| | FY 2025-26 (filed in 2026) | Tax year 2026-27 (filed in 2027) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Law | Income-tax Act, 1961 | Income-tax Act, 2025 |
| Year label | Assessment year 2026-27 | "Tax year" 2026-27 (no more AY/PY) |
| Presumptive scheme | Section 44AD | Section 58 (merges 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE) |
| Return filing section | Section 139 | Section 263 |
| Rebate | Section 87A | Section 156 |
| New tax regime | Section 115BAC | Section 202 |
| Form | ITR-4, notified under Income-tax Rules 1962 | New forms under Income-tax Rules 2026, to be notified |

The Income Tax Department's e-filing help pages confirm the split: income earned in FY 2025-26 is reported for AY 2026-27 under the 1961 Act, and the forms for tax year 2026-27 will be notified under the new rules before that year's due dates. Commentary on Section 58 so far says the 6% and 8% rates and the ₹2 crore and ₹3 crore limits carry over unchanged; the drafting and section references are what changed.

## Who can file ITR-4

You can use ITR-4 if **all** of these are true:

- You are a **resident** individual, HUF or partnership firm (not an LLP).
- Your **total income** is **₹50 lakh or less**.
- Your business income is declared under **44AD** (shops and traders), **44ADA** (specified professionals) or **44AE** (goods vehicles).
- Your other income is simple: salary or pension, interest, up to **two house properties** (raised from one for AY 2026-27), long-term capital gains on listed shares or equity funds under Section 112A up to **₹1.25 lakh**, and agricultural income up to **₹5,000**.

You **cannot** use ITR-4 if you:

- have total income above ₹50 lakh;
- are a **director** of a company, or held **unlisted equity shares** at any time in the year;
- have **foreign assets** or foreign income, or are a non-resident or "not ordinarily resident";
- have capital gains beyond the 112A allowance, or losses to carry forward;
- want to declare profit **below** 8% or 6% (that needs ITR-3, with books and possibly an audit).

A shopkeeper with one shop, a savings account, an FD and maybe a rented-out flat usually fits ITR-4.

## How presumptive taxation under 44AD works

The idea is simple: the law presumes your profit is a share of turnover, and in return you are excused from keeping detailed books of account.

| Part of turnover | Presumed profit |
| --- | --- |
| Received in cash | 8% |
| Received by UPI, bank transfer, account payee cheque or other prescribed electronic mode, **by the due date of the return** | 6% |

Points most pages skip:

- **"Digital" is about how the money arrived, not how the bill was made.** A credit sale in March that the customer pays by UPI in May still counts at 6%, as long as the money arrives before the return's due date.
- **6% and 8% are floors.** If your real profit is higher, you declare the higher figure.
- **No separate expenses.** Rent, salary, electricity and depreciation are all treated as already allowed inside the 6% or 8%. You cannot deduct them again.
- **The ₹3 crore limit** needs cash receipts of no more than 5% of total receipts for the year. For a typical kirana with a lot of cash at the counter, the ₹2 crore limit is the one that applies.
- **Lock-in.** If you use 44AD for a year and then declare a lower profit in any of the next five years, you lose the scheme for five years after that, and must keep books and get an audit if your income is above the basic exemption limit.
- **Advance tax:** presumptive taxpayers pay all their advance tax in one go, by **15 March**, if the year's tax is ₹10,000 or more.

Presumptive income tax is separate from GST. A shop on the [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) can still use 44AD for income tax, and a shop under the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) can too; the two schemes do not depend on each other.

## Worked example: a kirana with ₹60 lakh turnover

Suresh runs a kirana in Nagpur. For FY 2025-26:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Total sales | ₹60,00,000 |
| Received by UPI and bank | ₹36,00,000 |
| Received in cash | ₹24,00,000 |
| Savings bank interest | ₹12,000 |

**Step 1: presumptive business income**

| Part | Working | Income |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digital receipts | ₹36,00,000 × 6% | ₹2,16,000 |
| Cash receipts | ₹24,00,000 × 8% | ₹1,92,000 |
| **Business income** | | **₹4,08,000** |

**Step 2: total income and tax (new regime)**

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Business income | ₹4,08,000 |
| Savings interest | ₹12,000 |
| **Total income** | **₹4,20,000** |
| Tax: 5% on (₹4,20,000 − ₹4,00,000) | ₹1,000 |
| Rebate (income up to ₹12 lakh) | −₹1,000 |
| **Tax payable** | **Nil** |

Suresh pays no tax, but he **must still file**, because his income is above ₹4 lakh, the basic exemption limit under the new regime. Filing also gives him a record of income that banks ask for when he applies for a loan.

**What if he took everything in cash?** ₹60,00,000 × 8% = ₹4,80,000 of business income, ₹72,000 more than the mixed case. Still no tax here, but at higher turnover the difference is real money. **What if his real profit is lower than ₹4.08 lakh?** Kirana margins are thin, so that is possible. He can declare the lower actual figure only by filing ITR-3 with books of account. If he used 44AD in any of the previous five years, dropping below 6% or 8% also triggers the five-year lock-in, and a tax audit if his income is above ₹4 lakh. ### A bigger shop where tax is payable

A store with **₹2.4 crore** turnover: ₹2.3 crore received digitally and ₹10 lakh in cash. Cash is 4.17% of receipts, under 5%, so the **₹3 crore** limit applies and the store can use 44AD.

| Part | Working | Income |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digital receipts | ₹2,30,00,000 × 6% | ₹13,80,000 |
| Cash receipts | ₹10,00,000 × 8% | ₹80,000 |
| **Business income** | | **₹14,60,000** |

| New regime slab | Taxable amount | Tax |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to ₹4 lakh | ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹4-8 lakh at 5% | ₹4,00,000 | ₹20,000 |
| ₹8-12 lakh at 10% | ₹4,00,000 | ₹40,000 |
| ₹12-16 lakh at 15% | ₹2,60,000 | ₹39,000 |
| **Total** | ₹14,60,000 | **₹99,000** |
| Health and education cess at 4% | | ₹3,960 |
| **Tax for the year** | | **₹1,02,960** |

Income is above ₹12 lakh, so there is no rebate. The full ₹1,02,960 is due as advance tax by 15 March.

## New tax regime slabs and rebate

For FY 2025-26, and unchanged for 2026-27 by Budget 2026:

| Income | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹4 lakh | Nil |
| ₹4-8 lakh | 5% |
| ₹8-12 lakh | 10% |
| ₹12-16 lakh | 15% |
| ₹16-20 lakh | 20% |
| ₹20-24 lakh | 25% |
| Above ₹24 lakh | 30% |

- **Rebate:** up to **₹60,000**, so tax is nil on total income up to ₹12 lakh (with marginal relief just above it). The rebate does not cover tax on special-rate income such as capital gains on shares.
- **No standard deduction** for business income. The ₹75,000 standard deduction is for salary and pension only.
- **Cess:** 4% on the tax.
- **Old regime:** someone with business income can opt for the old regime by filing **Form 10-IEA** before the due date, and that choice can be reversed only once in a lifetime. Most small shopkeepers without large 80C or home loan deductions are better off in the new regime; check both with your CA.

## What ITR-4 asks a shop owner to fill in

ITR-4 is short, but it is not just one number. Have these ready:

- **Gross turnover, split into digital and cash receipts.** Your bank statements are the record for the digital part.
- **GSTIN and the turnover reported in your GST returns,** if you are registered. The department compares the two, so the turnover in your ITR should agree with your [GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-3b) for the year, or you should know why it differs.
- **Financial particulars as on 31 March:** your capital, loans, sundry creditors (what you owe suppliers), stock-in-trade, sundry debtors (what customers owe you), and cash and bank balances. No balance sheet is required, but these figures are mandatory.
- **Bank account details** for the refund, and for AY 2026-27, more detail on bank balances and investments than before.
- **Advance tax and TDS paid,** matched with Form 26AS and the AIS.

A simple daily [cash book](https://billzap.app/blog/cash-book-format) makes the cash figure easy, and a stock count at year-end gives you the stock figure.

## Due dates, late fees and what to do now

| Return | Due date (FY 2025-26) |
| --- | --- |
| ITR-1, ITR-2 | 31 July 2026 |
| ITR-3, ITR-4 (non-audit business) | **31 August 2026** |
| Audit cases | 31 October 2026 (check the portal for extensions) |
| Belated return | **31 December 2026** |
| Revised return | 31 March 2027 (fee if after 31 December 2026) |

The 31 August date for non-audit business returns came in with the Finance Act 2026; until last year it was 31 July. If you have not filed yet:

- **File a belated return by 31 December 2026.** The late fee under Section 234F is **₹5,000**, or **₹1,000** if total income is up to ₹5 lakh. Interest at 1% a month applies on any unpaid tax.
- **After 31 December**, the route left is an **updated return (ITR-U)**, with additional tax on top.
- **Remember advance tax** for FY 2026-27: if your tax for the current year will be ₹10,000 or more, it is due in full by **15 March 2027**.

## What changes from tax year 2026-27

For income earned from 1 April 2026, you will file in 2027 under the Income-tax Act, 2025. What changes for a shopkeeper:

- **Section numbers:** presumptive income is **Section 58**; books of account and audit move to Sections 62 and 63; returns are under **Section 263**.
- **"Tax year" replaces "previous year" and "assessment year".** Your return for April 2026 to March 2027 will be for tax year 2026-27.
- **Rates and limits:** 6% and 8%, ₹2 crore and ₹3 crore, carried over as commentary currently reads them.
- **Eligibility:** commentators note that Section 58 tests disqualification at the level of the taxpayer rather than the business, so a person with one ineligible activity should check with a CA before assuming the scheme still fits.
- **Forms:** new ITR forms will be notified under the Income-tax Rules 2026. Expect the same structure, but check the form name and fields when they come out.

## FAQ

### Who should file ITR-4?

Resident individuals, HUFs and partnership firms (not LLPs) with total income up to ₹50 lakh who declare business income on a presumptive basis under Section 44AD, 44ADA or 44AE. Most small shopkeepers, traders and kirana owners fit.

### What is the 6% and 8% rule in Section 44AD?

Your business income is presumed to be at least 8% of turnover received in cash and 6% of turnover received through banking channels or UPI by the return due date. You may declare a higher figure, but not a lower one without keeping books.

### What is the turnover limit for ITR-4?

₹2 crore under Section 44AD, or ₹3 crore if cash receipts are no more than 5% of total receipts in the year. Total income must also be ₹50 lakh or less to use ITR-4.

### What is the last date to file ITR-4 for FY 2025-26?

31 August 2026 for non-audit cases. A belated return can be filed until 31 December 2026 with a late fee of ₹5,000, or ₹1,000 if total income is up to ₹5 lakh.

### Do I need to keep books of account if I file ITR-4?

Not the detailed books required under Section 44AA. But you must report turnover, cash and bank balances, stock, debtors and creditors, so keep your bills, bank statements and a simple cash book.

### Is Section 44AD still valid after the new Income-tax Act?

For income of FY 2025-26, yes: those returns are filed under the 1961 Act. From tax year 2026-27, the same scheme continues as Section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

### Can a GST composition dealer file ITR-4?

Yes. GST composition and income tax presumptive taxation are separate schemes. A composition dealer within the 44AD limits can file ITR-4.

## The bottom line

For a shop with turnover under ₹2 crore and a simple life outside the business, ITR-4 is the easiest return in Indian tax: declare at least 6% of digital and 8% of cash receipts as profit, pay tax under the new regime, and skip the full books. Get more of your sales into UPI and the bank and your presumed profit falls. If you have not filed for FY 2025-26, the belated return window closes on **31 December 2026**.
