A new wholesale customer asks for a GST bill and reads out a 15-character number. A new supplier sends an invoice with a GSTIN printed at the top. In both cases the number decides who gets input tax credit, and in both cases a single wrong character, or a registration that was cancelled last year, turns into a problem at return time. A **GST number search** takes about a minute and settles it before the bill is made.

This guide shows how to check a GST number for free on the official GST portal, how to search by PAN, and how to use the e-way bill portal's taxpayer search. It explains what each of the 15 characters of a GSTIN means, how to read every field the search returns, what the different statuses mean, and the warning signs of a fake or wrong GSTIN.

## Quick answer

- **Where to check:** go to **gst.gov.in → Search Taxpayer → Search by GSTIN/UIN**, type the GSTIN and the captcha, and click Search. It is free and needs no login.
- **What you see:** the legal name, trade name, registration date, constitution of business, taxpayer type, jurisdiction, place of business, **status** and the **return filing history**.
- **What to look for:** status should be **Active**, the legal or trade name should match the bill, and the state code should match the address.
- **GSTIN format:** 15 characters. **2-digit state code + 10-character PAN + entity number + "Z" + check character.**
- **Search by PAN:** the portal's **Search by PAN** option lists every GSTIN registered under that PAN, state by state.
- **Why it matters:** a wrong or cancelled GSTIN on a B2B invoice means the buyer's input tax credit fails, and a fake GSTIN on a supplier's bill puts **your** credit at risk.

## What a GSTIN is

A GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number) is the number a business receives when it registers under GST. It is issued with the registration certificate and must appear on every tax invoice the business issues. On a B2B invoice the buyer's GSTIN must appear too.

A GSTIN is tied to a PAN and a state. One business with one PAN that is registered in three states has three GSTINs, one per state. A business can also hold more than one registration in the same state (for separate business verticals), and each gets its own GSTIN.

Because a GSTIN is public information, anyone can check it. You do not need the other party's permission, an account or any software.

## The 15 characters of a GSTIN

Every GSTIN follows the same structure. The example below, **27ABCDE1234F1Z5**, is illustrative and not a real business.

| Position | In the example | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | **27** | State code of the registration (27 is Maharashtra) |
| 3–12 | **ABCDE1234F** | PAN of the business or proprietor |
| 13 | **1** | Entity number: which registration this is under the same PAN in the same state (1 to 9, then letters) |
| 14 | **Z** | Fixed as "Z" for normal taxpayers |
| 15 | **5** | Check character, calculated from the first 14 characters to catch typing errors |

A few state codes you will see often:

| Code | State | Code | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 07 | Delhi | 29 | Karnataka |
| 09 | Uttar Pradesh | 32 | Kerala |
| 19 | West Bengal | 33 | Tamil Nadu |
| 24 | Gujarat | 36 | Telangana |
| 27 | Maharashtra | 37 | Andhra Pradesh |

The PAN in the middle has its own pattern: five letters, four digits, one letter. The fourth letter of a PAN shows the type of holder, for example **P** for an individual, **C** for a company and **F** for a firm. So a GSTIN whose PAN part says "company" but whose invoice says "Sharma Traders, proprietor" deserves a second look.

The structure alone tells you a lot before you search. A GSTIN starting with 33 belongs to a registration in Tamil Nadu. If the invoice address is in Pune, something is wrong.

## How to check a GST number on the GST portal

The official GST portal is the source every other tool draws on, and it is free.

1. Open **www.gst.gov.in**. You do not need to log in.
2. From the top menu, choose **Search Taxpayer**, then **Search by GSTIN/UIN**.
3. Type the 15-character GSTIN exactly as printed. Letters are capitals.
4. Type the characters shown in the captcha image.
5. Click **Search**.

If the GSTIN exists, the portal shows the registration details. If it does not, you get a message that the GSTIN is invalid or that no record was found. Retype it once, carefully, before you conclude anything: most "invalid" results are a single mistyped character, which is exactly what the check character at the end is designed to catch.

## How to search a GST number by PAN

Sometimes you know the business's PAN but not its GSTIN, or you want to see every state in which it is registered.

1. On gst.gov.in, choose **Search Taxpayer → Search by PAN**.
2. Enter the 10-character PAN and the captcha.
3. Click **Search**.

The portal lists every GSTIN registered under that PAN, with the state and the status of each. Click a GSTIN to see its full details. This is also a quick way to find the right GSTIN when a customer has branches in more than one state: the invoice should carry the GSTIN for the state of the branch you are billing.

## Other places to check a GSTIN

**Search Composition Taxpayer.** Also under Search Taxpayer on gst.gov.in. It tells you whether a business has opted for the composition scheme. A composition dealer cannot charge GST on its bills, so if a supplier registered under composition sends you a bill with GST on it, ask questions before you pay. Our guide to the [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) explains why.

**E-way bill portal.** The e-way bill system (ewaybillgst.gov.in) has its own **Taxpayer Search**, also free with a captcha. It is useful if you already work on that portal to move goods.

**Private lookup tools.** Many accounting and payment companies run free GST number search pages that pull data from the official system. They are convenient, and some add search by business name. Treat any result from them as a lead and confirm it on gst.gov.in, especially before a large order.

**Search by business name.** The official portal does not offer a free-text search by business name to the public. If you have only a name, ask the business for its GSTIN, or use a private tool to find candidates and then verify the GSTIN itself on the portal.

## How to read the search result

The result page on gst.gov.in shows a set of fields. Labels may vary slightly as the portal is updated, but the content is the same.

| Field | What it tells you | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Legal name of business | The name registered against the PAN | Should match the name on the invoice or the person you are dealing with |
| Trade name | The shop or brand name, if different | Often the name on the signboard |
| Effective date of registration | When GST registration took effect | A very recent date on a large deal is worth noting |
| Constitution of business | Proprietorship, partnership, company, LLP and so on | Should fit the business you know |
| GSTIN/UIN status | Active, cancelled, suspended and so on | Should be **Active** for a current B2B invoice |
| Taxpayer type | Regular, composition and others | A composition dealer cannot charge GST |
| Administrative office / jurisdiction | The state and centre tax offices the business falls under | Should be in the same state as the state code |
| Principal place of business | The registered address | Should match the address on the invoice |
| Nature of business activities | Wholesale, retail, manufacturing, services and so on | Should fit what they sell you |
| Return filing details | Which returns were filed, for which periods, and when | Regular, recent filings are a good sign |

The return filing table is worth a minute. A supplier who has not filed GSTR-1 for months is a supplier whose invoices may never reach your GSTR-2B, and your input tax credit depends on that. Our guide on [how to file GSTR-1](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-file-gstr-1) explains why the supplier's return matters to the buyer.

## What each GSTIN status means

| Status | Meaning | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | The registration is valid | Fine to use on invoices |
| **Cancelled** | The registration has been cancelled, either on the taxpayer's own application or by the tax officer (suo motu) | Do not put it on new B2B invoices; ask the business for its current status |
| **Suspended** | The registration is in the process of cancellation, often after an application or a notice | Hold large transactions until it is resolved |
| **Inactive / provisional** | Older cases, mostly from the migration of pre-GST registrations | Ask the business to confirm its current registration |

A cancelled GSTIN is the most common problem. The business may still be trading and may still print the old number on its bills, but tax invoices issued on a cancelled registration do not give the buyer valid input tax credit.

## Why verifying a GSTIN matters

**When you sell to a registered business.** The buyer's GSTIN goes into Table 4 of your GSTR-1. If it is wrong, the GST portal rejects the entry or the invoice lands in someone else's records, and your customer cannot claim the credit. They will come back to you for a corrected invoice, and you will have to amend your return. Checking the GSTIN once, when you first save the customer, prevents all of that. Our guide to the [GST invoice format](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-invoice-format) shows where the buyer's GSTIN sits on the bill.

**When you buy from a supplier.** Your input tax credit depends on the supplier being genuinely registered, filing returns and reporting your invoice. A fake or cancelled GSTIN on a purchase bill means the credit you claimed can be denied, with interest. Our morning guide to [input tax credit](https://billzap.app/blog/input-tax-credit-gst) covers the conditions in full.

**When the state decides the tax.** The state code in the GSTIN and the place of supply on the bill decide whether the sale is intra-state or inter-state, and so whether you charge CGST and SGST or IGST. A wrong state is a wrong tax split. See [CGST, SGST and IGST explained](https://billzap.app/blog/cgst-sgst-igst-difference).

## How to spot a fake or wrong GSTIN

Run through this list whenever a new GSTIN comes your way:

1. **The state code does not match the address.** A GSTIN starting with 07 (Delhi) on a bill from a shop in Chennai is wrong.
2. **The PAN part is not a valid PAN pattern.** Characters 3 to 12 should be five letters, four digits and one letter.
3. **The PAN does not match the business.** If you know the business's PAN, it should appear in characters 3 to 12.
4. **The 14th character is not "Z"** on what is supposed to be a normal taxpayer's GSTIN.
5. **The portal says invalid or finds no record**, even after you have retyped the number carefully.
6. **The legal or trade name differs** from the name on the invoice or the person you are dealing with.
7. **The status is cancelled or suspended.**
8. **Returns have not been filed for months**, visible in the return filing table.
9. **GST is charged by a composition dealer**, which the composition search will reveal.

Any one of these is a reason to pause. Ask the business to explain, and do not claim input tax credit on its invoices until it is resolved.

## Common mix-ups

**GSTIN vs PAN.** A PAN is a 10-character income tax number. A GSTIN contains the PAN but adds the state code in front and three characters at the end. You cannot use a PAN where a GSTIN is required.

**GSTIN vs ARN.** When a business applies for GST registration, it receives an ARN (Application Reference Number) to track the application. An ARN is not a GSTIN and cannot be used on invoices. The GSTIN comes only after the registration is approved.

**GSTIN vs UIN.** A UIN (Unique Identification Number) is issued to bodies such as embassies and UN organisations. It looks similar and can be searched in the same place, but it is not a normal business registration.

**TDS and TCS registrations.** Government departments that deduct tax and e-commerce operators that collect tax also hold GSTIN-like registrations, with a different letter in the 14th place. If you see one on a supplier's bill for goods, ask what it is.

**Your own GSTIN.** It is printed on your GST registration certificate (Form REG-06) and visible in your dashboard when you log in to gst.gov.in. If you are not yet registered, our guide to the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) explains when you need to be.

## FAQ: GST number search

### How can I check a GST number online for free?

Go to gst.gov.in, choose Search Taxpayer, then Search by GSTIN/UIN. Type the GSTIN and the captcha and click Search. It is free, needs no login, and shows the business's name, status, address and return filing history.

### How do I verify if a GSTIN is genuine?

Search it on gst.gov.in and check four things: the status is Active, the legal or trade name matches the bill, the state code matches the address, and returns are being filed. Also confirm that characters 3 to 12 match the business's PAN.

### Can I search a GST number by PAN?

Yes. On gst.gov.in, choose Search Taxpayer, then Search by PAN. It lists every GSTIN registered under that PAN, by state, with the status of each.

### Can I find a GST number by business name?

The official portal does not offer a public search by business name. Ask the business for its GSTIN, or use a private lookup tool to find candidates and then verify the GSTIN itself on gst.gov.in.

### What is the format of a GSTIN?

Fifteen characters: a 2-digit state code, the 10-character PAN, an entity number for registrations under the same PAN in the same state, the letter Z by default, and a check character calculated from the first 14.

### What happens if a wrong GSTIN is on an invoice?

The buyer cannot claim input tax credit on it, and the entry in the seller's GSTR-1 is rejected or goes to the wrong taxpayer. The invoice has to be corrected and the return amended, so it is far cheaper to check the GSTIN before the first bill.

### What does a cancelled GSTIN mean?

The business's GST registration has ended, on its own application or by the tax officer's order. A cancelled GSTIN should not appear on new tax invoices, and invoices issued under it do not give the buyer valid input tax credit.

### Is a GST number public information?

Yes. Anyone can search a GSTIN on the GST portal and see the business's registered name, address, status and return filing history. That is what makes checking a customer or supplier possible without their help.
