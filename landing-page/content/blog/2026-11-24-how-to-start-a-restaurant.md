Most guides on opening a restaurant give you twelve licences and a line that says "costs vary". That is not enough to sign a lease on. You need to know which licences apply to you, what GST does to your costs, how much money goes in before the first customer sits down, and how many plates a day you must sell to stop losing money.

This guide covers all of that for a small, 30-seat family restaurant, including the April 2026 FSSAI changes and a monthly profit sheet you can redo with your own numbers.

## Quick answer

- **Pick a format and a location first:** dhaba, quick service, casual dining or cafe. Rent should be a small slice of realistic sales; many operators aim for about 10% to 12%.
- **Licences:** FSSAI **basic registration** if food turnover is up to **₹1.5 crore** (₹100 a year, since 1 April 2026), shop and establishment registration, the municipal **health trade licence**, a **fire NOC** where your state's rules or your seating require it, signage permission, and an **eating house licence** in cities that still ask for one. A **liquor licence** is a separate, slower and costlier process with the state excise department.
- **GST:** register once turnover crosses **₹20 lakh** (₹10 lakh in some special category states). Restaurant food is **5% without input tax credit** (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST), AC or not. You cannot claim back GST on equipment, rent or supplies.
- **Budget:** an example 30-seat restaurant in a rented space needs roughly **₹22 lakh to ₹40 lakh** including a working capital buffer, before any liquor licence.
- **Food cost:** keep it near **30% to 35%** of menu price. In our worked example, 70 covers a day at ₹300 earns about **₹96,000** a month before the owner's salary and tax, and break-even is about **55 covers a day**.

## Step 1: Choose the format

| Format | Typical setup | Average bill per person | What decides success |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dhaba or family eatery** | Simple seating, short menu, high volume | Low | Speed, consistency, price |
| **Quick service (QSR)** | Counter ordering, small seating, takeaway | Low to medium | Location footfall, fast kitchen |
| **Casual dining** | Table service, 25 to 60 seats, full menu | Medium | Food quality, service, repeat families |
| **Cafe** | Beverages, snacks, long seating time | Medium | Ambience, beverage margins |

Not sure you can fill seats? A delivery-only kitchen is the cheaper test; see [how to start a cloud kitchen](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-start-a-cloud-kitchen).

Decide the cuisine and the **average bill per person** early; every later number follows from it.

## Step 2: Pick the location

Before you sign:

- **Count the footfall** at 1 pm and 8 pm, weekday and weekend.
- **Check the zoning.** Premises not approved for commercial use can block the trade licence and fire NOC.
- **Services:** a route for the exhaust duct, water, drainage with a grease trap, three-phase power and space for commercial LPG cylinders.
- **Parking and visibility** from the main road.
- **Rent as a share of sales.** Work out realistic sales first (Step 8), then decide the rent.
- **The landlord's GST status.** If you register for GST and the landlord is not registered, you pay 18% GST on the rent under reverse charge, and as a restaurant you cannot claim it back (more in Step 4).

## Step 3: Get the licences

Start the slow ones (fire NOC, health trade licence) while the interiors are being built.

| Licence or registration | Who needs it | Issued by | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **FSSAI basic registration** | Food turnover up to **₹1.5 crore** | FSSAI, on FoSCoS | ₹100 a year; registrations from 1 April 2026 have no renewal |
| **FSSAI State licence** | Turnover above ₹1.5 crore up to ₹50 crore | FSSAI, on FoSCoS | Central licence above ₹50 crore |
| **Health trade licence** | Almost every eating place | Municipal corporation or local body | Name, fee and inspection differ by city |
| **Shop and establishment registration** | Every commercial establishment | State labour department | Covers working hours and staff records |
| **Fire NOC** | Depends on state fire rules: seating (more than 50 seats is a common trigger), floor area, building height or LPG storage | State fire services | Some cities want approval of the layout before fit-out |
| **Eating house licence** | Some cities only | Police or municipal body | Delhi dropped the police eating house licence in June 2025 and moved licensing to local bodies; ask your city |
| **Signage permission** | Signboards facing a public road, in many cities | Municipal advertisement department | Illuminated and large boards usually need it |
| **GST registration** | Turnover above ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh in some special category states) | gst.gov.in | Free; see Step 4 |
| **Liquor licence** | Only if you serve alcohol | State excise department | Separate track, see below |

**FSSAI changed on 1 April 2026.** Basic registration now covers turnover up to **₹1.5 crore**, up from ₹12 lakh, though many guides still quote the old limits. See our [FSSAI registration](https://billzap.app/blog/fssai-registration) and [shop act registration](https://billzap.app/blog/shop-act-registration) guides.

You may also meet State Pollution Control Board consent in some states and copyright licences for recorded music. Ask your municipal office for its checklist first.

### The liquor licence is a separate project

The licence comes from the **state excise department**; fees and conditions vary widely by state, and it can take months. Alcohol is **outside GST**: it carries excise and VAT, so liquor and food are billed separately. A restaurant serving alcohol cannot use the composition scheme. Most first-time owners open without liquor and add it later.

## Step 4: Understand GST before you price the menu

- **Registration:** compulsory above **₹20 lakh** aggregate turnover (₹10 lakh in some special category states), the services limit.
- **Rate:** restaurant service, dine-in, takeaway or delivery, is **5% without input tax credit**, SAC 996331. AC or non-AC makes no difference. Only restaurants inside specified hotel premises (rooms above ₹7,500 a night) charge 18% with credit.
- **Swiggy and Zomato orders:** the app pays the 5% under Section 9(5). You do not charge GST again on those orders.
- **On the bill:** food ₹840 for a table of two becomes ₹840 + CGST 2.5% ₹21 + SGST 2.5% ₹21 = **₹882**.

Our full guide to [GST on restaurant food](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-on-restaurant-food) covers hotel restaurants, service charge and bars.

**What "without input tax credit" really costs you.** A shop claims back the GST on its equipment and supplies; a restaurant at 5% cannot. GST on your burners, furniture, interiors, packaging and repairs is a cost. And **rent:** if your landlord is not registered and you are a regular GST restaurant, you pay **18% on the rent under reverse charge**, in cash, and cannot claim it back. On ₹70,000 rent that is **₹12,600 a month** of pure cost. See our guide to [GST on rent](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-on-rent); composition taxpayers are outside this reverse charge since 16 January 2025.

**Composition scheme.** A restaurant not serving alcohol, with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh in special category states), can pay **5% of turnover** itself, charge no GST and issue a bill of supply. See our [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide.

## Step 5: Budget for the setup

Here is an **example** for a 30-seat casual restaurant in a rented ground-floor space in a tier-2 city, with rent of ₹70,000 a month. Your figures will differ by city and fit-out; get at least three quotes for each big item.

| Item | Example range |
| --- | --- |
| Security deposit (3 to 10 months' rent, by city) | ₹2.1 lakh to ₹7 lakh |
| Interiors, furniture, lighting, washroom | ₹4 lakh to ₹8 lakh |
| Kitchen equipment (burners, tandoor, refrigerator, chiller, steel work, prep tables) | ₹4 lakh to ₹7 lakh |
| Exhaust hood, ducting and fire safety (extinguishers, gas leak detector) | ₹75,000 to ₹1.5 lakh |
| Licences and professional fees (no liquor) | ₹30,000 to ₹1 lakh |
| Crockery, cutlery, utensils | ₹75,000 to ₹1.5 lakh |
| Phone or tablet, printer, CCTV | ₹30,000 to ₹60,000 |
| Signboard | ₹30,000 to ₹1 lakh |
| Opening stock of groceries, gas, packaging | ₹75,000 to ₹1.5 lakh |
| Launch marketing | ₹50,000 to ₹1 lakh |
| Working capital buffer (about 2.5 to 3 months of fixed costs) | ₹8 lakh to ₹10 lakh |
| **Total** | **about ₹22 lakh to ₹40 lakh** |

Owners cut the working capital line and regret it: salaries, rent and electricity start on day one, full tables do not. And GST on everything in this table is a cost, because a 5% restaurant gets no credit.

## Step 6: Plan the kitchen and cost every dish

**Food cost percentage** tells you if a menu can make money:

**Food cost % = Ingredient cost of the dish ÷ Menu price × 100**

An example, with illustrative prices; use your own purchase bills:

| Dish | Menu price (₹) | Ingredient cost (₹) | Food cost % |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paneer butter masala | 280 | 102 (paneer 150 g at ₹420/kg = ₹63, butter and cream ₹18, gravy base ₹14, spices, oil, garnish ₹7) | 36.4% |
| Dal tadka | 180 | 27 | 15.0% |
| Jeera rice | 160 | 22 | 13.8% |
| Butter naan | 60 | 9 | 15.0% |

The paneer dish runs above target. At 32% it would be priced ₹102 ÷ 0.32 = **₹318.75**, about ₹320. Or keep it at ₹280 as a draw and let dal, rice and breads carry the margin.

Waste, staff meals and portion creep push the **actual** food cost above plate cost. Check it monthly: (opening stock + purchases − closing stock) ÷ food sales. A big gap means waste or leakage. Keep the menu short at the start, built on a few gravies and bases.

## Step 8: A worked monthly profit sheet

Same 30-seat restaurant, a regular GST registrant with an unregistered landlord. **Assumptions:** 70 covers (diners) a day, average bill ₹300 per person before GST, 30 days, food cost 32%.

| Line | Working | Amount (₹) | % of sales |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Food sales (excluding GST)** | 70 × ₹300 × 30 | **6,30,000** | 100% |
| GST collected at 5% (paid to the government, not income) | ₹6,30,000 × 5% | 31,500 | |
| Food cost | 32% of sales | 2,01,600 | 32.0% |
| **Gross profit** | | **4,28,400** | 68.0% |
| Staff salaries | Step 7 | 1,27,000 | 20.2% |
| Rent | | 70,000 | 11.1% |
| GST on rent under reverse charge (no credit) | ₹70,000 × 18% | 12,600 | 2.0% |
| Electricity and water | | 40,000 | 6.3% |
| Commercial LPG | | 25,000 | 4.0% |
| Packaging and disposables | | 8,000 | 1.3% |
| Marketing | | 10,000 | 1.6% |
| Repairs, cleaning, pest control | | 12,000 | 1.9% |
| Internet, phone, sundries | | 8,000 | 1.3% |
| **Operating expenses** | | **3,12,600** | 49.6% |
| **Operating profit** | ₹4,28,400 − ₹3,12,600 | **1,15,800** | 18.4% |
| Depreciation on equipment and interiors | | 20,000 | 3.2% |
| **Profit before owner's salary and income tax** | | **95,800** | 15.2% |

**Break-even.** Treat everything except food as fixed: ₹3,12,600 + ₹20,000 = **₹3,32,600** a month. Each rupee of sales leaves 68 paise after food cost. Break-even sales = ₹3,32,600 ÷ 0.68 = **₹4,89,118**, which is ₹4,89,118 ÷ ₹300 = about **1,631 covers a month, or about 55 a day**. Our guide to the [break-even point](https://billzap.app/blog/break-even-point) explains the method.

**Why food cost matters so much.** If waste and portion creep push food cost from 32% to 38%, food cost becomes ₹2,39,400, which is ₹37,800 more. Profit falls from ₹95,800 to **₹58,000**, a cut of almost 40%, with the same number of customers.

## Step 9: Launch, then watch the numbers daily

1. **Soft-open for a week** to friends and neighbours, and fix slow dishes and service gaps before the public launch.
2. **Get on Google Maps** with photos, the menu and correct timings, and collect reviews from happy tables.
3. **Bill every order** and close the day: cash, UPI, bills and expenses should reconcile every night.
4. **Count high-value stock weekly** (paneer, ghee, oil, meat): that is where leakage hides.
5. **Add delivery apps once the kitchen is steady**, with app prices set to cover commissions.

## FAQ

### What licences are required to start a restaurant in India?

FSSAI registration, the municipal health trade licence, shop and establishment registration, a fire NOC where your state requires it, signage permission, GST above ₹20 lakh turnover, an eating house licence where your city asks for one, and a liquor licence only if you serve alcohol.

### How much does it cost to start a small restaurant in India?

For an example 30-seat restaurant in a rented space in a tier-2 city, roughly ₹22 lakh to ₹40 lakh, including the deposit, fit-out, kitchen equipment, licences and about three months of working capital. Liquor licences and prime locations cost much more.

### What is the GST rate for restaurants?

5% without input tax credit (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) for almost every restaurant, AC or non-AC. Only restaurants in hotels with room tariffs above ₹7,500 a night charge 18% with credit. Swiggy and Zomato pay the 5% on orders through them.

### Which FSSAI registration does a small restaurant need?

Since 1 April 2026, basic registration covers food businesses with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore, at ₹100 a year. Above that, up to ₹50 crore, you need a State licence.

### What food cost percentage should a restaurant aim for?

Most operators aim for about 30% to 35% of the menu price. Cost each dish from your purchase bills and check the actual monthly food cost against sales; a gap means waste or leakage.

### Can a restaurant opt for the composition scheme?

Yes, if it does not serve alcohol and turnover is up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh in special category states). It pays 5% of turnover itself, charges no GST and issues a bill of supply.

## The bottom line

Starting a restaurant in 2026 is paperwork and arithmetic. The paperwork: FSSAI (₹100 a year for most small restaurants), health trade licence, shop act, fire NOC where required, and GST above ₹20 lakh. The arithmetic: food cost near 30% to 35%, rent near a tenth of sales, three months of working capital and a known break-even number of covers.
