Once a month or once a quarter, every regular GST-registered business tells the government what it sold. That statement is **GSTR-1**. For a shop owner it is often the most stressful part of GST, not because the form is long, but because it asks for numbers that were never kept in one place: every bill, every buyer's GSTIN, every HSN code, every cancelled invoice.

This guide explains how to file GSTR-1 in 2026: who files it and when, which tables a small shop actually needs (most need only four or five of the fifteen), what the late fee is, how to file a nil return, what GSTR-1A is for, and the exact steps on the GST portal. It ends with a month-end routine that turns filing from an evening's work into a few minutes.

## Quick answer

**GSTR-1** is the return of **outward supplies**, meaning your sales. To file it:

- **Who:** every regular GST taxpayer. Composition dealers do not file GSTR-1; they file CMP-08 quarterly and GSTR-4 annually.
- **When:** by the **11th of the next month** for monthly filers, or by the **13th of the month after the quarter** for QRMP (quarterly) filers with turnover up to ₹5 crore.
- **Where:** gst.gov.in → Services → Returns → Returns Dashboard → GSTR-1 → Prepare Online.
- **What most shops fill:** Table 4 (sales to registered buyers), Table 7 (consumer sales), Table 12 (HSN summary) and Table 13 (documents issued), plus Table 9 if you issued credit notes.
- **Late fee:** ₹50 a day (₹20 a day for a nil return), capped at ₹2,000 for turnover up to ₹1.5 crore.
- **Filing:** generate the summary, preview it, and file with **EVC (OTP)** or **DSC**.

## What is GSTR-1?

GSTR-1 is a statement of every taxable supply you made in a tax period: what you sold, to whom, at what value and with how much tax. It does not pay any tax. Tax is paid through **GSTR-3B**, the summary return filed after it.

GSTR-1 still matters more than its "statement only" label suggests, for two reasons:

1. **It feeds your buyers' input tax credit.** When you report a B2B invoice in GSTR-1, it appears in your buyer's **GSTR-2B**, which is what they use to claim credit. A late or wrong GSTR-1 holds up their credit, and they will call you about it.
2. **It is checked against GSTR-3B.** The tax you declare in GSTR-1 should match the tax you pay in GSTR-3B for the same period. Large gaps trigger notices.

The portal also expects GSTR-1 to be filed before GSTR-3B for the same period. A missed GSTR-1 holds up the rest of your month's compliance.

## Who has to file GSTR-1

Every person registered as a **normal taxpayer** files GSTR-1, including those with no sales in the period, who file a nil return.

These registrations do **not** file GSTR-1:

- **Composition dealers**, who file CMP-08 every quarter and GSTR-4 once a year. See our guide to the [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme).
- Input service distributors, non-resident taxable persons, persons deducting TDS or collecting TCS, and suppliers of online information services from outside India. Each has its own return.

## GSTR-1 due dates in 2026

You file either monthly or quarterly. Quarterly filing is available under the **QRMP scheme** (Quarterly Return Monthly Payment) to businesses with aggregate turnover up to **₹5 crore** in the previous financial year.

| Filer type | Return period | Due date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly | Each month | 11th of the next month |
| QRMP (quarterly) | Each quarter | 13th of the month after the quarter |
| IFF (optional, QRMP only) | First and second month of the quarter | 1st to 13th of the next month |

For a quarterly filer, the GSTR-1 dates for the four quarters fall on **13 July, 13 October, 13 January and 13 April**. When the government extends a date, it does so by notification and the portal shows the revised date on the Returns Dashboard, so check there before you assume a deadline.

### What IFF is for

The **Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF)** exists because of a QRMP filer's B2B buyers. If you file quarterly, your buyers would otherwise wait up to three months to see your invoices in their GSTR-2B. IFF lets you upload just the **B2B invoices** of the first and second months of the quarter, so your buyers can claim credit monthly.

IFF is optional. If you sell only to consumers, you can ignore it and file one GSTR-1 a quarter. If you have regular B2B customers, they will usually ask you to use it.

## GSTR-1 tables: the four most small shops need

The GSTR-1 form has a long list of tables, which is why it looks frightening. Most kirana, retail and small trading businesses touch only a handful of them.

| Table | What goes in it | Does a small shop need it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **4** B2B | Sales to GST-registered buyers, invoice by invoice, with their GSTIN | Yes, if you sell to any registered business |
| **5** B2C Large | Inter-state sales to unregistered buyers with invoice value above ₹1 lakh | Rarely |
| **6** Exports | Exports and supplies to SEZ units | Only if you export |
| **7** B2C Small | All other sales to consumers, totalled by state and rate | Yes, almost always |
| **8** Nil, exempt, non-GST | Sales of nil-rated, exempt and non-GST goods | Yes, if you sell exempt items |
| **9** Amendments, credit and debit notes | Corrections to past invoices; credit and debit notes (9B) | Yes, if you issued credit notes |
| **11** Advances | Tax on advances received and adjusted | Rarely for shops |
| **12** HSN summary | Sales grouped by HSN code with quantity, value and tax | Yes, always |
| **13** Documents issued | Invoice serial number ranges, count and cancelled invoices | Yes, always |

So in practice, a typical shop's GSTR-1 is **Table 7 + Table 12 + Table 13**, with **Table 4** added for business customers and **Table 9** for credit notes. Everything else is usually blank.

### Table 4: B2B invoices

Every invoice to a registered buyer goes here individually: the buyer's **GSTIN**, invoice number and date, invoice value, place of supply, taxable value by rate, and the tax. A wrong GSTIN is the most common error in GSTR-1. It sends the credit to the wrong business, or nowhere, and your buyer cannot claim it until you amend it.

Whether the tax is CGST and SGST or IGST depends on the place of supply. Our guide to the [difference between CGST, SGST and IGST](https://billzap.app/blog/cgst-sgst-igst-difference) explains how to tell.

### Table 5: B2C Large

This table covers **inter-state** sales to **unregistered** buyers where the invoice value is **above ₹1 lakh**. The threshold was **₹2.5 lakh** until it was lowered to ₹1 lakh in 2024, and many guides still quote the old figure. Intra-state consumer sales never go here, however large; they go in Table 7.

### Table 7: B2C Small

All other consumer sales are reported as **totals**, not invoice by invoice: taxable value and tax, grouped by **place of supply** and **GST rate**. For a shop that sells over the counter, this is where most of the month's turnover lands.

### Table 12: HSN summary

Table 12 groups your sales by **HSN code**, with the unit (UQC), total quantity, taxable value and tax for each. If your previous-year turnover was up to ₹5 crore, report at least **4 digits**; above ₹5 crore, at least **6 digits**.

From the April 2025 return period, the portal splits Table 12 into separate **B2B** and **B2C** tabs, and HSN codes are chosen from a **drop-down list** rather than typed freely. A missing or invalid code now stops you at validation. Our guide to [HSN codes](https://billzap.app/blog/what-is-hsn-code) explains how to find the right code for each item.

### Table 13: Documents issued

Here you report the **serial number ranges** of invoices, credit notes and other documents issued in the period, the total count, and how many were **cancelled**. If your invoices run from SLS-1001 to SLS-1180 and two were cancelled, that is what this table says. Gaps in numbering with no cancellations recorded are what draw questions.

### Table 9: amendments, credit and debit notes

Credit notes (for returns or reductions after the bill was issued) and debit notes go in **Table 9B**, split between registered and unregistered buyers. Corrections to invoices reported in an earlier period, such as a wrong GSTIN or a wrong value, are made through the amendment tables, not by editing the old return.

## How to file GSTR-1 on the GST portal, step by step

1. **Log in** at gst.gov.in with your username and password.
2. Go to **Services → Returns → Returns Dashboard**.
3. Choose the **financial year** and the **return filing period** (the month, or the quarter for QRMP filers), then click **Search**.
4. On the GSTR-1 tile, click **Prepare Online**. If you have a JSON file from your billing software or the GST offline tool, use **Prepare Offline** to upload it instead.
5. **Fill the tables** that apply to you: open each tile, add the invoices or totals, and **Save**. The portal saves each table separately, so you can stop and come back.
6. Click **Generate GSTR-1 Summary**. The portal totals everything you entered. Allow a minute for it to refresh.
7. Click **Preview** to download the draft as a PDF. Check the totals against your sales register and against what you plan to declare in GSTR-3B.
8. Tick the **declaration** and select the **authorised signatory**.
9. Click **File Statement** and sign with **EVC** (an OTP sent to the registered mobile number) or **DSC** (digital signature). DSC is mandatory for companies and LLPs; proprietors and partnerships usually use EVC.
10. Download the **acknowledgement** (ARN) for your records.

Once filed, a GSTR-1 cannot be edited. Corrections go through amendment tables in a later period, or through GSTR-1A, explained below.

### Filing a nil GSTR-1

If you made **no outward supplies** in the period, you still file. On the GSTR-1 tile, tick **File Nil GSTR-1**, then file with EVC or DSC. A nil GSTR-1 can also be filed by **SMS** from the registered mobile number. A nil return is quick, but it is not optional; skipping it attracts the nil late fee.

## GSTR-1 late fee

The late fee is charged per day of delay, and it is capped per return based on your turnover.

| Previous-year turnover | Late fee per day | Maximum per return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to ₹1.5 crore | ₹50 (₹25 CGST + ₹25 SGST) | ₹2,000 |
| ₹1.5 crore to ₹5 crore | ₹50 | ₹5,000 |
| Above ₹5 crore | ₹50 | ₹10,000 |
| Nil return, any turnover | ₹20 (₹10 CGST + ₹10 SGST) | ₹500 |

The fee is the smaller cost. The bigger ones are your buyers' delayed credit, and the fact that you cannot file GSTR-3B for the period until GSTR-1 is in.

### The three-year limit

Under changes made by the Finance Act 2023, a return cannot be filed after **three years from its due date**, and the GST portal began enforcing this in 2025. A very old GSTR-1 that was never filed may simply not be fileable any more, which leaves the gap permanently on your record. File every period, even a nil one, on time.

## GSTR-1A: fixing a GSTR-1 you have already filed

**GSTR-1A**, available since **July 2024**, is an optional form that lets you **amend or add** details for the **same tax period** after you file GSTR-1 but **before you file GSTR-3B** for that period. If you notice a missed invoice or a wrong GSTIN the day after filing GSTR-1, GSTR-1A lets you correct it in the same month, rather than carrying the error into the next period's amendment tables.

Once GSTR-3B for the period is filed, the GSTR-1A window for that period closes, and corrections go through the regular amendment tables.

## Before you file: a pre-filing checklist

Run through this before you open the portal:

- [ ] Every invoice for the period is in your sales register, and the numbering has **no unexplained gaps**.
- [ ] **Cancelled** invoices are marked as cancelled, not deleted.
- [ ] Each B2B buyer's **GSTIN** has been checked (on the portal's search taxpayer page, or by your billing app's format check).
- [ ] Each B2B invoice has the right **place of supply**, and so the right split between CGST + SGST and IGST.
- [ ] Every item line carries an **HSN code** with enough digits for your turnover.
- [ ] **Credit notes** for returns and discounts after billing are recorded.
- [ ] Your totals by rate match what you will pay in **GSTR-3B**.

## A month-end routine that makes GSTR-1 easy

Most of the effort in GSTR-1 is not on the portal; it is finding and cleaning the data. A short routine at the end of each month removes most of it:

1. **Close the books on the 1st.** On the first day of the month, stop editing last month's bills. Corrections from now on are credit or debit notes.
2. **Export the month.** Take the sales summary from your billing software: taxable value and tax by rate, B2B invoices with GSTINs, HSN summary, and the invoice number range.
3. **Reconcile with cash and bank.** Compare total sales with what came into the drawer, the UPI account and the bank. A big gap usually means a bill that was never made, or one made twice.
4. **Send it to your CA, or file it yourself**, well before the 11th or 13th. Filing on the last day is when the portal is slowest.
5. **Keep the ARN.** Save the acknowledgement with the month's export, so every return has its evidence next to it.

If your bills are made in software that records the HSN code, rate and GSTIN with every invoice, steps 2 and 3 take minutes. If they are handwritten, this is the month to change that. ## Common GSTR-1 mistakes

- **Reporting a B2B sale as B2C.** If the buyer gave you a GSTIN, the invoice belongs in Table 4. Lumping it into Table 7 costs the buyer their credit.
- **A wrong or mistyped GSTIN.** The credit goes nowhere. Validate GSTINs when you add a customer, not at month-end.
- **Wrong place of supply.** CGST + SGST charged on an inter-state sale, or IGST on a local one. The tax was paid to the wrong government and needs correcting.
- **Using the old B2C Large threshold.** Inter-state consumer invoices above ₹1 lakh go in Table 5, not only those above ₹2.5 lakh.
- **Table 12 not matching the rest of the return.** The HSN totals should add up to the same value and tax as the other tables.
- **Missing Table 13.** It is easy to forget, and it is where missing invoice numbers show.
- **Wrong rates after September 2025.** From 22 September 2025, most goods moved to 5% or 18%, with 40% for luxury and sin goods. An item list set up earlier may still carry 12% or 28%. Our guide on [how to calculate GST](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-calculate-gst) has the current slabs.

## FAQ: filing GSTR-1

### What is the due date for GSTR-1?

The 11th of the following month for monthly filers. For quarterly filers under the QRMP scheme, the 13th of the month after the quarter ends: 13 July, 13 October, 13 January and 13 April. Check the Returns Dashboard for any extension notified by the government.

### What is the late fee for GSTR-1?

₹50 per day of delay (₹25 CGST and ₹25 SGST), capped at ₹2,000 per return for turnover up to ₹1.5 crore, ₹5,000 for ₹1.5 crore to ₹5 crore, and ₹10,000 above ₹5 crore. For a nil return, it is ₹20 per day, capped at ₹500.

### Do I have to file GSTR-1 if I had no sales?

Yes. File a nil GSTR-1 by ticking "File Nil GSTR-1" on the portal, or by SMS from your registered mobile number. Not filing attracts the nil late fee and keeps the period open on your record.

### Can I edit GSTR-1 after filing?

No. A filed GSTR-1 cannot be edited. Before you file GSTR-3B for the same period, you can correct or add details through GSTR-1A. After that, corrections go through the amendment tables in a later GSTR-1.

### What is the difference between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B?

GSTR-1 lists your sales, with invoice-level detail for B2B sales. GSTR-3B is a summary return in which you declare total sales, claim input tax credit and pay the tax. GSTR-1 is filed first, and the two should agree.

### Which GSTR-1 tables does a small shop fill?

Usually Table 7 (consumer sales, totalled by state and rate), Table 12 (HSN summary) and Table 13 (documents issued), plus Table 4 for sales to GST-registered buyers and Table 9 for credit notes. Most other tables stay blank for a typical shop.

### What is IFF in GST?

The Invoice Furnishing Facility lets a quarterly (QRMP) filer upload B2B invoices for the first and second months of a quarter, between the 1st and 13th of the next month, so registered buyers can claim input tax credit every month instead of every quarter. It is optional.

### Do composition dealers file GSTR-1?

No. Composition dealers pay tax quarterly through CMP-08 and file an annual return in GSTR-4. They do not file GSTR-1 or GSTR-3B.
