Search "quotation format" and you land in template galleries: dozens of coloured layouts to download, most of them blank. A blank layout does not tell you what to write in the terms box, whether to add GST, how long the price should hold, or what happens when the customer says yes. Those are the parts that decide whether a quotation wins the order or causes an argument later.

This guide covers them. It explains what a quotation is and what it is not under GST, the fields to include, two filled-in samples you can copy (a GST quotation with every total checked, and a simple one for a shop that is not registered), the terms worth adding, and how the quote turns into a tax invoice.

## Quick answer

A good quotation format has four parts:

- **Header:** your shop name, address, phone, GSTIN (if registered), the word **Quotation**, a quotation number, date and a **valid until** date.
- **Customer:** name, address and, for a business customer, their GSTIN.
- **Items:** description, HSN code, quantity, rate, amount and the GST rate for each line, then the subtotal, **estimated GST** and estimated total.
- **Terms:** validity, delivery time, payment terms (advance and balance), what is included, warranty, and that GST will be charged at the rate in force on the invoice date.

A quotation is an offer, not a tax document. **No GST is payable on a quotation.** GST applies when you make the sale and issue the tax invoice.

## What is a quotation?

A quotation (or "quote") is a written price offer you give a customer **before** they buy: these items, at these prices, on these terms, for this long. Shops use it for bigger or custom orders: an electrical shop fitting out a café, a hardware store supplying a builder, a furniture shop quoting a school, a caterer quoting a wedding.

Three things make it different from a bill:

1. **Nothing has been sold yet.** No goods have moved and no money is due.
2. **It has an expiry date.** Your prices hold only for the validity period you state.
3. **It is an offer.** When the customer accepts it, by signing, replying on WhatsApp or placing the order, it can bind you to those prices and terms. Write terms you are willing to stand by.

### Is GST payable on a quotation?

No. GST is charged on a **supply**, and a quotation is not one. It is not a tax invoice, does not need to follow the invoice rules (Rule 46 of the CGST Rules), is not reported in GSTR-1 and creates no tax liability. GST becomes payable when you supply the goods or services and issue the tax invoice.

That is why a well-made quotation shows GST as an **estimate**: you are telling the customer what the tax will be if they buy at today's rates, not charging it.

Two related points:

- **Advance against a quotation.** If the customer pays an advance to confirm an order for **goods**, a regular GST-registered shop does not pay GST on that advance (Notification 66/2017-Central Tax); the tax is due when the invoice is issued. An advance for **services** is different: GST is due when you receive it, and you issue a receipt voucher.
- **Not registered, or on the composition scheme?** Then you cannot charge GST on your final bill, so do not show any GST on the quotation either. Quote the plain price.

## What to include in a quotation format

| Section | Fields | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Header | Shop name, address, phone, GSTIN, the title "Quotation" | Customer knows who is quoting and that you are registered |
| Reference | Quotation number, date, valid until | Lets you track it and stops old prices being claimed months later |
| Customer | Name, address, GSTIN for a business buyer | Needed later for the tax invoice |
| Items | Description, brand or model, HSN or SAC, quantity, unit, rate, amount | Clear specs avoid "I asked for the 5-star model" disputes |
| Tax | GST rate per line, estimated GST, CGST/SGST or IGST | Customer sees the full cost up front |
| Totals | Subtotal, estimated GST, estimated total | One number to compare with other quotes |
| Terms | Validity, delivery, payment, inclusions and exclusions, warranty, price change clause | Sets expectations before money moves |
| Sign-off | Name, signature, "For [shop name]" | Shows who authorised the price |

**Use a separate number series** for quotations, such as Q/26-27/0087. Your tax invoices must be numbered in one unbroken sequence, and most quotes never become sales. Mixing the two would leave gaps in your invoice numbers.

## Sample 1: GST quotation format (registered shop)

An electrical and appliance shop in Indore quoting a café in the same city, so GST is shown as CGST and SGST. Rates are those in force since **22 September 2025**: air conditioners and refrigerators at **18%**, steel kitchen utensils at **5%**, installation at **18%**.

**SHARMA ELECTRICALS & APPLIANCES**
45, MG Road, Indore, Madhya Pradesh · GSTIN: 23ABCDE1234F1Z5

**QUOTATION** · No. **Q/26-27/0087** · Date: 05-11-2026 · **Valid until: 20-11-2026**
To: Chai Corner Café, Vijay Nagar, Indore · Customer GSTIN: (if registered)

| # | Item | HSN/SAC | Qty | Rate (₹) | Amount (₹) | GST |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Split AC 1.5 ton, 3-star inverter | 8415 | 2 | 32,000.00 | 64,000.00 | 18% |
| 2 | Refrigerator 260 L, double door | 8418 | 1 | 24,500.00 | 24,500.00 | 18% |
| 3 | Stainless steel kitchen utensil set | 7323 | 4 | 2,250.00 | 9,000.00 | 5% |
| 4 | AC installation with 3 m copper pipe | 9987 | 2 | 1,500.00 | 3,000.00 | 18% |
| | **Subtotal** | | | | **1,00,500.00** | |

| Estimate | Amount (₹) |
| --- | --- |
| Taxable value | 1,00,500.00 |
| CGST 9% + SGST 9% on ₹91,500 | 8,235.00 + 8,235.00 |
| CGST 2.5% + SGST 2.5% on ₹9,000 | 225.00 + 225.00 |
| **Estimated GST** | **16,920.00** |
| **Estimated total** | **1,17,420.00** |

**Terms:**
1. Prices valid until 20-11-2026. After that, prices are subject to change.
2. GST will be charged at the rates in force on the date of the tax invoice.
3. Delivery within 3 working days of order confirmation.
4. Payment: 50% advance with order, balance on installation.
5. Installation includes 3 m copper pipe per AC; extra pipe and stand charged separately.
6. Warranty as per manufacturer.

For Sharma Electricals & Appliances (signature)

**Check it yourself.** The 18% lines are ₹64,000 + ₹24,500 + ₹3,000 = ₹91,500. 9% of ₹91,500 is ₹8,235, so CGST and SGST are ₹8,235 each, ₹16,470 in all. The utensils are ₹9,000 at 5%: 2.5% is ₹225 each side, ₹450 in all. Total GST is ₹16,470 + ₹450 = ₹16,920, and ₹1,00,500 + ₹16,920 = **₹1,17,420**. If the café were in another state, the same ₹16,920 would appear as IGST. Our guide on [GST rates](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-rates-list) lists current rates for common items.

## Sample 2: Simple quotation format for a shop not registered under GST

A furniture shop below the GST registration limit quoting a coaching centre. No GSTIN, no tax lines.

**GUPTA FURNITURE**
8, Sadar Bazaar, Jhansi, Uttar Pradesh

**QUOTATION** · No. **Q-0142** · Date: 05-11-2026 · **Valid until: 15-11-2026**
To: Bright Future Coaching, Civil Lines, Jhansi

| # | Item | Qty | Rate (₹) | Amount (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Plastic chair with arms, moulded | 20 | 650.00 | 13,000.00 |
| 2 | Folding table 4 × 2 ft | 4 | 2,400.00 | 9,600.00 |
| 3 | Delivery within city | 1 | 500.00 | 500.00 |
| | **Total** | | | **23,100.00** |

Rupees twenty-three thousand one hundred only. Not registered under GST; no GST is charged.
Terms: valid 10 days; delivery in 2 days from order; full payment on delivery.

Check: 20 × ₹650 = ₹13,000; 4 × ₹2,400 = ₹9,600; ₹13,000 + ₹9,600 + ₹500 = ₹23,100.

You can paste either sample into Word or Google Docs: copy the text, then use **Insert, Table** for the item lines. Save it as a template with your shop's details filled in, so each new quote only needs the customer, items and dates. Export to PDF before sending, so the customer cannot edit the prices.

## Quotation vs estimate vs proforma invoice vs tax invoice

These four documents get used interchangeably, but they do different jobs.

| | Quotation | Estimate | Proforma invoice | Tax invoice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Firm price offer | Approximate cost | Advance copy of the final bill | The actual bill for a sale |
| When | Before the order | Before the order, when exact cost is unknown | After the order is agreed, before supply | When goods are supplied or service is done |
| Price fixed? | Yes, for the validity period | No, can change | Yes | Yes |
| GST payable? | No | No | No | **Yes** |
| Reported in GSTR-1? | No | No | No | **Yes** |
| Buyer can claim ITC on it? | No | No | No | **Yes** |
| Typical use | Appliances, furniture, bulk orders | Repairs, renovation, custom work | Arranging payment, bank loans, import paperwork | Every taxable sale |

The practical difference between a quotation and an estimate: a **quotation** says "this is the price", an **estimate** says "it will cost about this". Use an estimate when you cannot know the exact cost upfront, such as a repair job.

A **proforma invoice** looks like the final bill but is not one. Customers sometimes need it to arrange payment or approval. The real tax invoice still has to be issued when you supply. ## From quotation to tax invoice

When the customer accepts, three things happen:

1. **Confirm in writing.** A signed copy, a WhatsApp "OK, go ahead" or a purchase order. Note the quotation number on it.
2. **Supply the goods** within the delivery time you promised.
3. **Issue the tax invoice** with the same items and prices, from your invoice number series, with GST at the rate in force on the invoice date.

That last point is why the "GST at rates in force" clause matters. When rates changed on 22 September 2025, air conditioners moved from 28% to 18%. A quote made in early September at 28% would generally have been invoiced at 18% if supplied after the change. Rates can move either way; the clause protects both of you.

If the customer is not registered under GST, or is buying for personal use, the invoice goes to them as a consumer. If they are a business with a GSTIN, put it on the invoice so they can claim credit. For more on the final document, see our guide to [bill format](https://billzap.app/blog/bill-format).

## How to follow up on a quotation

Most quotes are lost to silence, not to a cheaper competitor. A simple routine:

- **Send it the same day**, as a PDF on WhatsApp or email, with a one-line message naming the total and the validity date.
- **Follow up after two or three days**: "Did you get a chance to look at the quotation? Happy to adjust the model if needed."
- **Follow up again two days before it expires.** A real deadline is the most honest reason to call.
- **Revise with a new number**, such as Q/26-27/0087-R1, if the customer asks for changes. Keep the old one so you know what was offered.
- **Keep a simple register**: quote number, customer, amount, sent date, status (accepted, lost, expired). After a few months it tells you which items you win on and which you lose on price.

## Common quotation mistakes

- **No validity date.** A customer comes back after three months expecting the old price, and the price has gone up.
- **Showing GST when you are not registered or are on the composition scheme.** You cannot charge it on the bill, so do not quote it.
- **GST shown as final, not estimated.** If rates change before supply, the invoice will differ.
- **Using old rates.** If your Word template still has 12% or 28% columns, check each item; most moved to 5% or 18% in September 2025.
- **Vague items.** "1 AC" invites disputes. Write the brand, capacity, star rating and model.
- **No payment terms.** State the advance and when the balance is due.
- **Using invoice numbers for quotes.** It breaks your invoice sequence.
- **Treating the quotation as the bill.** It is not a tax invoice; the customer cannot claim credit on it and you have not recorded the sale.

## FAQ

### What is a quotation format?

A quotation format is the layout of a written price offer: your shop details, quotation number, date and validity, the customer, an item table with quantities, rates and GST, totals, and terms such as delivery and payment.

### Is GST charged on a quotation?

No. A quotation is an offer, not a supply, so no GST is payable on it. Show GST as an estimate so the customer knows the full cost, and charge it on the tax invoice when you make the sale.

### How long should a quotation be valid?

Long enough for the customer to decide, short enough that your costs do not move: 7 to 30 days is typical. For items whose prices change often, such as metals or electronics, keep it to a week or two.

### What is the difference between a quotation and an invoice?

A quotation is sent before the sale and offers a price; an invoice is issued at the sale and asks for payment. Only the invoice carries GST, is reported in GSTR-1 and lets a business buyer claim input tax credit.

### Is a quotation legally binding?

Once the customer accepts it on its terms, it can bind both sides on the prices and terms stated. Until then it is an offer you can withdraw, and it lapses after the validity date.

## The bottom line

A quotation that wins orders is clear about four things: exactly what you will supply, what it will cost including estimated GST, how long the price holds, and how payment and delivery work. Keep quotes on their own number series, do not show GST if you cannot charge it, and follow up before the validity date. When the customer says yes, the quotation's job is done; the tax invoice is the document that counts for GST.
