Search "home business ideas for women" and you get long lists that mix serious options with vague ones ("become an influencer"), plus scheme lists copied from site to site for years, including schemes no bank seems to offer any more. Few tell you what you will spend, which licence you need, or how to price your first order.

This guide covers 16 businesses women across India run from home today, whether you have two free hours a day or a full day, and ₹2,000 or ₹1 lakh to start. Each has a rough cost, the skill it needs, the paperwork and where to find customers. Then come the government schemes we could confirm as current in November 2026, a worked pricing example and a plan for your first 30 days.

## Quick answer

- **Lowest cost to start (under ₹10,000):** home tuition, tailoring (if you own a machine), mehendi, small-batch pickles and masala, online reselling, writing or translation.
- **Steady, repeat income:** tiffin service, tuition, day care, tailoring.
- **Higher earnings per order:** custom cakes, bridal mehendi and makeup, boutique wear, festive hampers.
- **Licences:** anything you cook and sell needs **FSSAI basic registration** (₹100 a year for turnover up to ₹1.5 crore since 1 April 2026). Most other home businesses need no licence to start. **Udyam** registration is free and helps with loans. GST is needed only above the turnover limit or for goods sold to other states.
- **Schemes that are live:** **Mudra** loans, **Stand-Up India** (₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for women's first ventures, renewed to March 2031), **PM Vishwakarma** for artisans such as tailors and garland makers, **CGTMSE** with 90% guarantee cover for women-led units, and **SHG bank loans** under DAY-NRLM.

## How to choose: four honest questions

1. **How many hours, and when?** A tiffin service runs twice a day, every day. Tuition fills evenings. Baking and tailoring bunch up before festivals and weddings.
2. **How much space?** Day care needs a safe, open room; baking a counter and an oven; freelance work only a phone or laptop.
3. **What do people already ask you for?** If neighbours ask you to stitch blouses or bake birthday cakes, that is your first market.
4. **What can you afford to lose?** Start small enough that a slow first month does not hurt the household budget. Buy equipment after orders, not before.

## Food businesses from home

**1. Tiffin service.** The most reliable home food business: customers pay monthly and eat daily. Start with 5 to 10 tiffins for people you know, fix a weekly menu and take payment in advance. The hard part is the same taste at the same time, every day.

**2. Home bakery.** Custom cakes, brownies, cookies and eggless options sell well in apartment societies. An OTG oven, tins and piping tools are the main costs, and photos are your shop window. To grow into a counter, see [how to start a bakery](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-start-a-bakery).

**3. Pickles, papad and masala.** Long shelf life lets you sell to relatives in other cities and to local shops. Packaged food must carry the product name, ingredients, net quantity, your FSSAI number, and manufacture and best-before dates.

**4. Festive sweets and snacks.** Take advance orders with part payment two to three weeks before the festival, so you buy exactly what you need.

**The licence for all four:** **FSSAI basic registration** on foscos.fssai.gov.in (Form A, ₹100 a year, no renewal for registrations issued from 1 April 2026). Our [FSSAI registration](https://billzap.app/blog/fssai-registration) guide walks through it.

## Clothing, beauty and crafts

**5. Tailoring and blouse stitching.** Blouses, suits, alterations, uniforms and fall-pico work run all year, with peaks before weddings and festivals. Women tailors may qualify for **PM Vishwakarma** (see schemes below).

**6. Boutique from home.** Buy sarees, suits or kurtis from a wholesale market or weavers and sell to people who trust your taste. Stock ties up money, so start with a tight range and reorder what sells.

**7. Beauty services and mehendi.** Threading, facials, bridal makeup and mehendi at home or the client's home. A recognised course builds trust; hygiene brings clients back.

**8. Handmade jewellery, candles and crafts.** Terracotta jewellery, diyas, candles and crochet sell at festivals, melas and online. Price your time, not only materials.

## Teaching and care

**9. Home tuition.** Almost no cost, and parents rarely switch a tutor who gets results. Start with one subject and one age group, and charge at the start of the month.

**10. Music, dance, yoga or art classes.** Batches of 6 to 10 in your living room or online. A small annual recital or exhibition helps keep students.

**11. Day care or creche.** Working parents pay well for safe, reliable care. Rules differ by state and city, so ask your municipal office first, keep a first-aid kit, and keep written consent and emergency contacts for every child.

## Selling and digital work

**12. Online reselling.** Share products from a reselling app or a wholesaler's catalogue in your WhatsApp groups and add your margin. Easy to start, but margins are thin and returns common. **GST note:** since 1 October 2023, small sellers of goods on e-commerce platforms selling only within their own state can do so without GST registration, under conditions such as enrolment on the GST portal. Selling goods to another state needs registration at any turnover; see the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) guide.

**13. Writing, translation and design.** Translation and content for local businesses, schools and coaching centres, or designing menus, invitations and posters.

**14. Social media for local shops.** Offer salons, sweet shops and clinics a monthly package: 12 posts, festival greetings and an up-to-date Google Maps profile.

**15. Front-room general store.** Daily needs for your lane from the front room. It needs money for stock and discipline over credit, plus shop and establishment registration in most states and FSSAI if you sell packaged food.

**16. Gift hampers and festive packing.** Diwali hampers, trousseau packing and return gifts. Seasonal but large orders; take 50% advance.

- **Building and colony groups:** your first 20 customers almost always know you already.

## Government schemes for women entrepreneurs: what is current

Many lists still name schemes such as "Annapurna", "Stree Shakti" or "Mahila Udyam Nidhi" as if any bank will hand you a form. We could not confirm that these are offered today as described, so ask your bank directly. These are the routes we could confirm as running in November 2026:

| Scheme | What you get | Who it suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mudra (PMMY)** | Collateral-free loans: up to ₹50,000 (Shishu), ₹5 lakh (Kishore), ₹10 lakh (Tarun), ₹20 lakh (Tarun Plus) | Any home business that earns; most start with Shishu |
| **Stand-Up India** | ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore for a new (greenfield) enterprise; renewed on 11 November 2026 to 31 March 2031 | Women starting a first, larger venture |
| **PM Vishwakarma** | ₹15,000 toolkit e-voucher, training with ₹500 a day stipend, collateral-free loans of ₹1 lakh then ₹2 lakh at 5% | Tailors, garland makers, basket, mat and broom makers, doll and toy makers, potters and other listed trades |
| **CGTMSE** | Guarantee behind collateral-free bank loans, with **90% cover for women-led enterprises** | Women taking a bank loan for an Udyam-registered unit |
| **DAY-NRLM self-help groups** | Collateral-free bank loans to SHGs (up to ₹20 lakh per group), with interest subvention on smaller loans | Women in or joining an SHG; the "Lakhpati Didi" drive works through these groups |
| **PMEGP** | Subsidy of 25% (urban) or 35% (rural) for women on a new manufacturing or service unit | Food processing, tailoring units; check whether it has been extended beyond March 2026 |

Two to check before counting on them: **PMFME** (35% subsidy up to ₹10 lakh for micro food processing) was last confirmed to September 2026, with a new phase proposed; and the **Budget 2025-26** promise of term loans up to ₹2 crore for 5 lakh first-time women, SC and ST entrepreneurs, which we could not confirm has launched.

PM Vishwakarma applications go through the official portal or a Common Service Centre and are verified locally; only one family member can benefit. ## Worked example: pricing a 1 kg chocolate cake

The commonest mistake is copying the market price and forgetting your own costs.

| Cost item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Ingredients (flour, sugar, cocoa, butter, cream, chocolate) | ₹320 |
| Gas and electricity | ₹40 |
| Cake board and box | ₹45 |
| **Material cost per cake** | **₹405** |
| Your time: 2.5 hours at ₹100 an hour | ₹250 |
| **Full cost per cake** | **₹655** |

At a selling price of **₹850**:

- Margin over materials: ₹850 − ₹405 = **₹445**, or ₹445 ÷ ₹850 = **52.4%** of the price.
- Profit after paying yourself: ₹850 − ₹655 = **₹195** a cake.
- At 20 cakes a month: 20 × ₹445 = **₹8,900** over materials, of which ₹5,000 is fair pay for 50 hours and ₹3,900 is business profit.
- A ₹12,000 OTG oven pays for itself after ₹12,000 ÷ ₹445 = about **27 cakes**.

Charge ₹650 "because the shop does" and you earn less than ₹100 an hour. Our [profit margin formula](https://billzap.app/blog/profit-margin-formula) guide explains markup versus margin.

## Your first 30 days

- **Week 1, test:** pick one business, offer it to 5 people who will tell you the truth, and write down every rupee spent.
- **Week 2, set up:** open a separate bank account or UPI ID, register on Udyam and (for food) FoSCoS, fix a price list as above, and set up a WhatsApp Business catalogue.
- **Week 3, launch:** post in building and colony groups, tell relatives, give the first 10 orders a small launch discount, and ask each customer for a photo or review.
- **Week 4, review:** add up sales, costs and hours. Drop what loses money, raise prices where you always sell out, and set a target for month two.

Keep household and business money separate. Mixed money is how a profitable business looks like a loss.

## FAQ

### Which home business is best for women with low investment?

Home tuition, tailoring, mehendi, small-batch pickles or masala, online reselling and writing or translation can all start under ₹10,000, especially if you already have the skill or equipment.

### Do I need a licence to sell home-cooked food?

Yes. Anyone selling food, including from a home kitchen, needs FSSAI registration: basic registration on FoSCoS for turnover up to ₹1.5 crore, at ₹100 a year.

### Do I need GST registration for a home business?

Not until turnover crosses ₹40 lakh for goods (₹20 lakh in a few states) or ₹20 lakh for services, unless you sell goods to another state.

### What government schemes are available for women entrepreneurs in India?

Mudra loans, Stand-Up India (renewed to 2031), PM Vishwakarma for listed artisan trades, CGTMSE's 90% guarantee for women-led units, PMEGP's higher subsidy for women, and SHG bank loans under DAY-NRLM.

### Can a housewife get a Mudra loan?

Yes, for an income-generating business. Mudra loans are collateral-free and need ID, address proof and proof of the business such as Udyam registration. Most first-time borrowers start with Shishu, up to ₹50,000.

### How do I price a home-made product?

Add material cost, packaging, gas or electricity and a fair hourly rate for your time, then your profit. Check the price covers all of it before matching what shops charge.

## The bottom line

The best home business is one you can do well, repeatedly, in the hours you really have. Start with a skill people already ask you for, spend little until orders come, register on Udyam and FSSAI where needed, price for your time and keep business money separate. When you are ready to grow, Mudra, Stand-Up India, PM Vishwakarma and SHG loans are real, current routes, and none of them needs an agent.
