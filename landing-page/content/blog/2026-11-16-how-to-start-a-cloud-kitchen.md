A cloud kitchen looks like the cheapest way into the food business: no dining hall, no prime frontage, no waiters. The kitchen can sit on a first floor in a back lane. What most guides skip is where the money goes once you are live. On a typical app order, a quarter or more of the food value goes to the platform before you pay for a single onion. A cloud kitchen that does not price its menu around that number loses money on every order it is proud of.

This guide covers the whole path for a small, single-brand kitchen in 2026: the licences (including the April 2026 FSSAI change), how GST works when the app pays it for you, what Swiggy and Zomato actually deduct, a per-order worked example, a sample budget, a monthly profit sheet, packaging and ratings.

## Quick answer

- **Pick one cuisine that travels well** and a kitchen inside a dense delivery area. Delivery radius matters more than rent.
- **Licences:** FSSAI **basic registration** if food turnover is up to **₹1.5 crore** (₹100 a year, since 1 April 2026), shop and establishment registration, the municipal trade or health licence, a **fire NOC where your city or building requires one**, and GST registration when turnover crosses **₹20 lakh** (restaurant service is a service).
- **GST:** restaurant service, which includes delivery kitchens, is **5% without input tax credit**. On Swiggy and Zomato orders, **the app pays that 5% under Section 9(5)**; you charge 5% yourself only on direct orders.
- **Commission:** reported ranges are about **18% to 28%** of the order value, plus about **2%** payment fee and **18% GST** on both. Treat these as typical, not fixed: your contract decides.
- **Target a food cost of roughly 28% to 35%** of the menu price, and price the menu for app deductions, not for a dine-in customer.
- **Build a direct-order channel** (WhatsApp and phone) from day one. A direct order keeps the commission in your pocket.

## Step 1: Choose the model and the menu

There are three common ways to run a cloud kitchen:

| Model | What it is | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Single brand, own kitchen** | One menu, one brand, a rented commercial kitchen | Most first-timers |
| **Multi-brand, one kitchen** | Two or three brands (say biryani, rolls and desserts) from the same kitchen and staff | After the first brand is steady |
| **Home kitchen** | Tiffin, home-style meals or bakes from your own kitchen | Testing an idea with low cost; check local rules on commercial activity at home |

Then decide the menu. A good cloud kitchen menu has three qualities:

1. **It travels.** Biryani, rice bowls, rolls, thalis and curries survive 30 minutes in a box. Dosas, pakodas and crisp fries mostly do not.
2. **It shares ingredients.** Fifteen dishes built on four gravies and three proteins means less waste and faster prep.
3. **It is short.** Twelve to twenty items is plenty for a new brand. Every extra dish adds inventory, waste and confusion for the cook.

Spend a week looking at the apps from your planned kitchen's pin code. Note which cuisines have few options, what the top-rated kitchens charge, and which dishes they push. That is your market research.

## Step 2: Pick the location by delivery radius

Customers see kitchens that can deliver to them quickly. So the location question is not "where is rent cheap?" but "how many offices, hostels and apartment blocks sit within a few kilometres of this kitchen?"

Check before you sign:

- **Water, drainage and ventilation.** You need a proper exhaust and somewhere for the smoke to go.
- **Commercial use.** The premises must be usable as a commercial kitchen. A landlord's flat in a residential society may not qualify, and neighbours complain about smoke.
- **Power load and LPG.** Commercial LPG cylinders and a decent sanctioned load.
- **Access for riders.** Delivery partners should be able to park and pick up quickly. A kitchen on the fourth floor without a lift slows every order.

## Step 3: Get the licences and registrations

Apps will not list you without FSSAI. The rest you need to run legally.

| Licence or registration | Who needs it | Where | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **FSSAI basic registration** | Food turnover up to **₹1.5 crore** | FoSCoS (foscos.fssai.gov.in) | ₹100 a year; from 1 April 2026, no renewal for new registrations |
| **FSSAI State licence** | Turnover above ₹1.5 crore up to ₹50 crore | FoSCoS | Central licence above ₹50 crore |
| **Shop and establishment registration** | Every commercial establishment | State labour department portal | Rules and deadlines vary by state |
| **Trade licence or health trade licence** | Most municipal corporations, for eating houses and kitchens | Your city's municipal portal | Name and fee differ by city |
| **Fire NOC** | Where your state's fire rules or your building require it, often linked to area, floors, seating or LPG storage | State fire services department | Ask the fire department or your municipality before you fit out |
| **GST registration** | Turnover above **₹20 lakh** (₹10 lakh in some special category states); see the note below | gst.gov.in | Free |
| **Udyam registration** | Optional | udyamregistration.gov.in | Useful for MSME loans |

FSSAI basic registration now covers food businesses up to ₹1.5 crore turnover, up from ₹12 lakh before April 2026, so almost every new cloud kitchen needs only the ₹100 tier. Our guide to [FSSAI registration](https://billzap.app/blog/fssai-registration) walks through Form A on FoSCoS. For the other two local registrations, see [shop act registration](https://billzap.app/blog/shop-act-registration) and [trade licence](https://billzap.app/blog/trade-license).

**A note on GST registration.** Section 24 of the CGST Act makes registration compulsory for people who sell through an e-commerce operator, but it leaves out supplies on which the operator itself pays tax under Section 9(5), which is exactly what restaurant service on Swiggy and Zomato is. Many practitioners read that as: a kitchen selling only through apps can stay unregistered below ₹20 lakh. Others disagree, and the apps' onboarding forms may ask for a GSTIN or a declaration. Your app sales count towards the ₹20 lakh either way. Settle this with your CA before you onboard; see our [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) guide for the thresholds.

## Step 4: Understand GST on a cloud kitchen

This is where many new kitchen owners go wrong, so here it is plainly.

- **Rate:** food supplied by a restaurant, including a delivery-only kitchen, is **restaurant service at 5% without input tax credit**. On a bill, that is 2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST. SAC **996331**. AC, non-AC and dine-in do not change it.
- **App orders:** since **1 January 2022**, the app collects and pays the 5% on food ordered through it, under **Section 9(5)** of the CGST Act. You do not charge GST again on those orders and do not pay it from your own pocket. Your CA reports them separately in GSTR-1.
- **Direct orders:** WhatsApp, phone and walk-in takeaway orders are your own sales. If you are registered, you charge 5% on your bill and pay it.
- **No credit:** because your sales are at 5% without ITC, the 18% GST the app charges on its commission and fees, and the GST on your rent, gas and packaging, stays a cost.

The full rate rules, including the 18% exception for restaurants inside hotels, are in our guide to [GST on restaurant food](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-on-restaurant-food).

## Step 5: Know what the apps deduct

Swiggy and Zomato do not publish one commission rate. Each restaurant signs its own terms, and the percentage varies with city, exclusivity, order volume and whether the app's delivery fleet is used. What has been reported in 2026:

| Deduction | Typical size (as reported) | Can you claim it back? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commission | About **18% to 28%** of the order value in most published breakdowns; Bengaluru's hotel association put the range at **8% to 28%** in July 2026 | No |
| Payment collection fee | About **2%** | No |
| GST on commission and fees | **18%** of those charges | No, if you sell at 5% without ITC |
| Discounts you agree to fund | Whatever the campaign says | No |
| Ads and promoted listings | Your choice | No |
| TDS | **0.1%** of the order value (the e-commerce TDS that was section 194-O, carried into the Income-tax Act, 2025) | Yes, against your income tax |

During the July and August 2026 dispute, a Bengaluru hotel association leader said a restaurant doing ₹1 lakh of app sales might receive only ₹40,000 to ₹45,000 after all deductions, discounts and ads included. That is one association's estimate, not a rule, but it shows why the deductions need to be modelled before the menu is priced. Read your own contract and your weekly payout statements line by line.

## Step 6: Engineer the menu around food cost

**Food cost %** is the cost of the ingredients in a dish divided by its selling price. The commonly cited range for quick service and cloud kitchens is roughly **28% to 35%**. Above that, there is little left once the app has taken its share.

### Worked example: one paneer rice bowl on an app

Menu price on the app: **₹300** (food value before the app adds 5% GST for the customer). Assume a 22% commission and a 2% payment fee, both charged on the food value.

| Line | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Menu price | | ₹300.00 |
| Commission | 22% × ₹300 | ₹66.00 |
| GST on commission | 18% × ₹66 | ₹11.88 |
| Payment fee | 2% × ₹300 | ₹6.00 |
| GST on payment fee | 18% × ₹6 | ₹1.08 |
| **Payout from app** | ₹300 − ₹84.96 | **₹215.04** |
| Ingredients (paneer, gravy, rice, garnish) | 30% food cost | ₹90.00 |
| Box, lid, cutlery, bag, sticker | | ₹18.00 |
| **Left to pay rent, staff, gas and you** | ₹215.04 − ₹108 | **₹107.04** |

The same bowl ordered **directly on WhatsApp** at ₹300 + 5% GST (₹315 to the customer, ₹15 paid to the government) leaves ₹300 − ₹90 − ₹18 − ₹40 for your own delivery = **₹152**. That ₹45 gap per order is the whole case for building a direct channel.

### The menu matrix

Once you have a month of sales, sort every dish by two measures: **how often it sells** and **how much it leaves you per order** (price minus ingredients minus packaging minus app deductions).

| | High contribution | Low contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sells a lot** | **Stars**: keep, photograph well, never discount deeply | **Workhorses**: raise the price slightly or cut the portion cost |
| **Sells little** | **Puzzles**: rename, rephoto, move up the menu | **Dogs**: drop them |

## Step 7: Budget the setup

Every kitchen is different, so treat this as an example for a small single-brand kitchen in a rented space in a tier-2 city, not a quote. Replace each line with real prices from your area.

## Step 8: Work out the monthly profit

Using the per-order numbers above, with 40 app orders and 10 direct orders a day for 30 days, at an average order of ₹300:

| Line | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| App orders | 40 × 30 = 1,200 | |
| Direct orders | 10 × 30 = 300 | |
| Sales (food value) | 1,500 × ₹300 | ₹4,50,000 |
| Left after app deductions, food and packaging: app | 1,200 × ₹107.04 | ₹1,28,448 |
| Left after food, packaging and delivery: direct | 300 × ₹152 | ₹45,600 |
| **Total contribution** | | **₹1,74,048** |
| Rent | | ₹30,000 |
| Staff: two cooks and a helper | | ₹55,000 |
| Gas and electricity | | ₹15,000 |
| App ads | | ₹10,000 |
| Cleaning, repairs, sundries | | ₹8,000 |
| **Fixed costs** | | **₹1,18,000** |
| **Profit before your own salary and tax** | ₹1,74,048 − ₹1,18,000 | **₹56,048** |

That is about **12.5%** of sales. Two things move it most: the share of direct orders, and food cost. At 35% food cost instead of 30%, each order loses ₹15 and the month's profit drops by ₹22,500 (1,500 × ₹15) to ₹33,548. Cutting ads or discounts too early, on the other hand, can starve a new brand of orders.

Break-even here is ₹1,18,000 of fixed costs ÷ roughly ₹116 average contribution per order (₹1,74,048 ÷ 1,500) = about **1,017 orders a month**, or 34 a day.

## Step 9: Packaging that survives the ride

Packaging is part of the dish. Customers rate what arrives, not what left the kitchen.

- **Match the box to the food:** compartment boxes for thalis, tall round containers for gravies, vented boxes for anything fried.
- **Seal liquids twice:** a lid plus tamper tape or a sealing machine. One leaked curry costs you a refund and a one-star rating.
- **Follow the plastic rules:** many single-use plastic items have been banned nationally since 1 July 2022, and some states go further. Choose compliant containers.
- **Cost it in.** In the example above, packaging is 6% of the menu price. Track it per dish like an ingredient.
- **Brand it cheaply:** a sticker with your name, WhatsApp number and FSSAI number on every bag turns a one-time app customer into a direct customer.

## Step 10: Ratings, reviews and the first 90 days

Ratings decide how often the app shows you. A new kitchen should obsess over the basics:

1. **Accuracy:** the right items, the right add-ons, cutlery when asked. Use a checklist at the packing station.
2. **Prep time:** keep it short and steady. Late hand-overs hurt more than a slightly smaller portion.
3. **Consistency:** write recipes with gram weights so every cook makes the same dish.
4. **Reply to reviews,** briefly and politely, especially bad ones. Fix the cause, not just the reply.
5. **Grow direct orders:** set up WhatsApp Business with a catalogue and a quick reply for the menu; our guide to [WhatsApp Business for shops](https://billzap.app/blog/whatsapp-business-for-shops) covers the setup.

## FAQ

### How much investment is needed to start a cloud kitchen in India?

It depends on city and size. Our example for a small single-brand kitchen in a tier-2 city comes to about ₹5.4 lakh of setup plus ₹2.4 lakh of working capital. A home kitchen can start for far less; a multi-brand kitchen in a metro needs more.

### Which licence is required for a cloud kitchen?

FSSAI registration is essential (basic registration up to ₹1.5 crore turnover since April 2026). You also need shop and establishment registration, your municipality's trade or health licence, a fire NOC where local rules require it, and GST registration above ₹20 lakh turnover.

### What GST does a cloud kitchen charge?

5% without input tax credit, as restaurant service. On Swiggy and Zomato orders, the app pays that 5% under Section 9(5), so you do not charge it again. On your own direct orders, a registered kitchen charges 5% on its bill.

### How much commission do Swiggy and Zomato take?

There is no single rate. Published 2026 breakdowns mostly quote about 18% to 28% of the order value, plus about 2% payment fee and 18% GST on both. Your contract decides your actual rate.

### Is a cloud kitchen profitable?

It can be, but margins are thin. In our worked example, a kitchen with 50 orders a day at ₹300 makes about 12.5% before the owner's salary. Food cost, the share of direct orders and app discounts decide the outcome.

### Can I start a cloud kitchen from home?

Yes, many people do, with FSSAI basic registration at the home address. Check your housing society's rules and any local permission for commercial activity at home, and keep the kitchen to commercial hygiene standards.

### Do I need GST registration to sell on Swiggy or Zomato?

Below ₹20 lakh turnover, practitioners differ on whether an app-only kitchen must register, because the app pays the GST under Section 9(5). Above ₹20 lakh you must register. Ask your CA before onboarding.

## The bottom line

A cloud kitchen is a margins business. Get FSSAI and the local licences in place, price every dish after app deductions and packaging, keep food cost near 30%, and build direct orders from the first week. Do the per-order sum before you sign the lease, and again every month once you are live.
