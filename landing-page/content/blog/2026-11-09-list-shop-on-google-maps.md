When someone nearby types "medical store near me" or "hardware shop open now", Google shows a map with three shops under it. Those three get the calls, the directions and the walk-ins. Getting your shop into that list starts with a free **Google Business Profile**, the service that used to be called Google My Business.

Most guides stop at "fill in the form and verify". This one also covers what goes wrong after: rejected verification videos, suspensions, and reviews that stop at seven.

## Quick answer

1. **Go to Google Maps or business.google.com** on your phone, signed in with a Google account you will keep, and choose **Add your business**.
2. **Enter your shop name exactly as on the signboard**, pick the closest **primary category** (such as "Grocery store" or "Hardware store"), and place the **pin on your shop's door**.
3. **Add phone, hours and, if you have one, a website** or WhatsApp link.
4. **Verify.** Google chooses the method for you: most shops now get a **video recording** (shop sign, inside, proof you run it). Some get **phone or SMS, email or postcard**. Review can take **up to 5 business days**.
5. **After approval:** add photos, set festival hours, and ask every happy customer for a review, by link or QR code.
6. **It is free.** Google charges nothing to list or verify. Anyone who says they can "guarantee" verification or rank for a fee is selling something Google does not.

## Before you start: what you need

| Item | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A Google account you control (not a staff member's or an agency's) | The owner of the profile is whoever owns this login |
| Shop name exactly as on the board | Name mismatch is a top reason for failed verification |
| Full address with landmark and PIN code | Helps the pin land on the right building |
| A phone number that is answered during shop hours | Google may call or text it; customers will too |
| Opening hours, including weekly off | Shops with hours can show as "Open now" |
| A permanent signboard | Needed for video verification and trust |
| 10 to 15 good photos | Outside, inside, counter, top products |

## Step by step: adding your shop

### 1. Check if your shop is already on Maps

Search your shop name on Google Maps; customers often add shops themselves. If it is there, tap **Claim this business** or **Own this business?** If someone else already manages it, Google lets you request access.

### 2. Create the profile

On Google Maps, tap your profile picture and choose **Add your business** (on a computer, go to business.google.com). Then:

- **Business name:** the real name, nothing extra. "Sharma General Store" is right. "Sharma General Store - Best Grocery Shop in Indore Cheap Price" breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.
- **Category:** pick the most specific match (more on this below).
- **Do you want to add a location customers can visit?** Choose **Yes** for a shop.
- **Address:** enter it, then drag the pin to your entrance.
- **Contact:** phone number and website if you have one.

### 3. Verify

Google then shows the verification method available to you. You cannot pick one that is not offered.

### 4. Complete the profile while verification is pending

Add hours, photos, the description and services. Edits made now go live once you are verified.

## Verification in 2026: video, phone, email and postcard

| Method | What happens | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Video recording** | You record one continuous video on your phone and upload it | The most common method for new shops now |
| **Live video call** | A Google support person checks your shop on a call | Offered in some cases, during listed hours |
| **Phone or SMS** | Code sent by call or text | Phone systems with a menu (IVR) cannot receive the call |
| **Email** | Code sent to an email address Google shows you | Offered when Google already links the business to that email |
| **Postcard** | Code mailed to the shop address | Now uncommon; can take one to two weeks |
| **Instant** | Already verified via Google Search Console | Rare for small shops |

Google says review of a video or other submission can take **up to 5 business days**. Some shops are approved the same day.

### How to record a verification video that passes

Record in one take, without cuts, standing at the shop, during the day. Show three things in order:

1. **Outside:** the street or a nearby landmark, then the **signboard with your shop name**, then the shop front, so Google can match it to the pin.
2. **Inside:** the counter, shelves and stock, the billing area. It should look like the business in your category.
3. **Proof you run it:** unlock the shutter or the cash drawer with your key, open your billing app or bill book, show stock or tools that only staff can reach.

Keep it short, around one to two minutes is plenty. Google's help asks you not to show sensitive information such as bank, tax or ID numbers, or other people's faces, so do not hold your GST certificate or PAN up to the camera unless asked.

### Why verification gets rejected

- Shop name not visible on a signboard, or name on the board differs from the profile
- Video in pieces, or recorded somewhere else
- Pin placed on the road or a different building
- A home address with no visible shop
- Category that does not match what the video shows
- Several profiles for the same shop, or a virtual office or PO box address

After a rejection, Google usually tells you why and lets you record again. Fix the cause first; a second identical video will fail the same way.

## Choosing the right category

The **primary category** is the single biggest choice you make. It decides which searches you can appear in. You can add more categories as secondary (Google allows up to nine), but only the primary one carries real weight.

| Your shop | Good primary category | Possible extra categories |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kirana | Grocery store | Supermarket, Dairy store |
| Chemist | Pharmacy (or Medical supply store) | Chemist |
| Mobile shop | Mobile phone store | Mobile phone repair shop, Electronics store |
| Hardware | Hardware store | Paint store, Plumbing supply store |
| Stationery | Stationery store | Book store, Photocopy shop |
| Bakery | Bakery | Cake shop, Pastry shop |
| Salon | Beauty salon or Hair salon | Hairdresser, Nail salon |

Type a few words and pick from Google's list; you cannot invent a category. Choose what customers search for, not what sounds grand.

## Hours, photos, description and attributes

- **Hours:** set normal hours and the weekly off. Before Diwali, Eid, Holi or a local festival, set **special hours** so the shop does not show "Open" while the shutter is down. Wrong hours cause one-star reviews.
- **Photos:** Google's guidance is JPG or PNG, 10 KB to 5 MB, ideally 720 × 720 pixels or more, well lit and without heavy filters. Upload: logo, a cover photo, the shop front from across the road (helps people recognise it), the inside, the counter, your bestsellers and a team photo. Add new ones every month or two.
- **Description:** up to 750 characters. Say what you sell, the brands you stock, home delivery if you do it, languages spoken, and the landmark. Write it for people; stuffing keywords does not help.
- **Products and services:** add your main product lines with price ranges if they are stable.
- **Attributes:** tick what applies, such as wheelchair access, delivery, in-store pickup and payment options. The options differ by category and region. If UPI is not listed as an option, mention "UPI, cash and cards accepted" in the description.

## Reviews: how to get them, how to reply, and what never to do

Reviews affect both your ranking and whether a searcher picks you over the shop next door. A steady trickle of fresh reviews beats a burst once a year.

### How to ask

- In your profile, use **Ask for reviews** (or **Get more reviews**) to copy your review link.
- Print it as a **QR code** at the billing counter: "Happy with us? Scan to review."
- Send the link with your bill on WhatsApp: "Thank you for shopping with us. If you have a minute, a Google review helps a small shop a lot: [link]."
- Ask in person when someone thanks you. That is when they are most willing.

### How to reply

Reply to **every** review, good and bad, within a few days.

- **Good review:** thank them by name, mention what they bought if you remember. Two lines is enough.
- **Bad review:** do not argue in public. Apologise for the experience, state the fact politely, and offer to fix it: "Sorry the atta packet was damaged. Please bring it in and we will replace it, or call us at the shop."

### What never to do

Google's fake engagement policy prohibits:

- **Buying reviews** or paying anyone to post them
- **Offering a discount, gift or cashback for a review**, even an "honest" one
- **Review gating:** asking only happy customers and steering unhappy ones elsewhere
- Posting reviews for yourself, or getting staff and family to do it
- Asking someone to remove a negative review in exchange for something

Google can remove such reviews and place restrictions on the profile, such as blocking new reviews for a period. A profile with 40 real reviews at 4.4 stars does better over time than one with 200 suspicious ones.

To remove a review that is fake, abusive or off-topic, use **Report review** from the profile. Google decides; a review that is merely negative will usually stay.

## Posts, offers and product updates

Your profile lets you publish **updates**, **offers** and **events** with a photo and a button: new stock, festival offers, home delivery, changed hours. One short post a week shows the shop is active.

Keep offers real and dated. "Wedding-season dry-fruit boxes from ₹499, until 30 November" is better than "Best prices!"

## What decides who shows up first

Google says local results are based on three things:

| Factor | What it means | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Relevance** | How well your profile matches the search | Right primary category, complete description, products listed |
| **Distance** | How far your shop is from the searcher or the place searched | Correct pin; nothing else changes this |
| **Prominence** | How well known and trusted the shop is | Reviews and ratings, replies, photos, mentions on other sites |

In plain words: you cannot move your shop closer, but you can make your profile the most complete and best-reviewed one in your lane. Being open when the search happens also helps, which is why correct hours matter. Consistent name, address and phone number everywhere (JustDial, IndiaMART, your Instagram bio, your bills) helps Google trust the details.

Things that do **not** help, and can hurt: keywords in the business name, fake reviews, multiple listings for one shop, and paying for "Google Maps SEO packages" that promise first position.

## FAQ

### Is it free to add my shop on Google Maps?

Yes. Creating and verifying a Google Business Profile costs nothing. Google's only paid product here is ads, which are optional.

### How long does Google Business Profile verification take?

Google says reviews of video and other submissions can take up to 5 business days. Phone, SMS and email codes are usually quick. Postcards, where still offered, can take one to two weeks.

### Can I list a shop that runs from my home?

Yes, if customers actually visit and there is a sign. If you only deliver or go to customers, set it up as a service-area business and hide the address.

### Do I need a GST number to list my shop on Google?

No. Google does not ask for a GST number to create a profile. You need a real shop that customers can visit, or a real service area.

### How do I get more reviews on Google Maps?

Ask every satisfied customer, share your review link by QR code at the counter and with WhatsApp bills, and reply to every review. Never offer a discount or gift for a review.

### Why is my shop not showing on Google Maps?

The profile may not be verified yet, may be suspended, or may rank below nearby shops for that search. Search your exact shop name first; if it appears, the listing is live and the work is on category, reviews and photos.

## The bottom line

Putting your shop on Google Maps takes an evening: create the profile with your real name and the right category, record a clean one-take video showing your signboard, inside and proof you run the shop, and wait a few days. The part that brings customers takes longer: correct hours, fresh photos, a weekly post, and a habit of asking every happy customer for a review and replying to each one. Do that for three months and your shop will be in the list people see when they search nearby.
