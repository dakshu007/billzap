Search "trade license" and the first page is mostly government portals: one for Odisha, one for Assam, one for Bengaluru, one for Hyderabad. Each is useful if you already know it is the right one, and none of them explains what a trade licence is, whether your shop needs one, or how it differs from the shop act certificate, GST or FSSAI. The agent sites that do explain it usually want ₹1,999 to file the form for you.

This guide fills that gap: what a trade licence is, who needs one, how it fits with your other registrations, the documents, how it works in five big cities we checked, what decides the fee, and why late renewal is the costliest mistake.

## Quick answer

- **A trade licence** is permission from your **municipal corporation, municipality or panchayat** to carry on a particular trade at a particular premises. It is issued under the **state's municipal law**, mainly to control public health, safety and nuisance.
- **Who needs it:** depends on your city. Eating places, food shops and trades with machinery, storage or hazards almost always need one; many corporations require it for ordinary shops as well.
- **It is separate** from shop act registration (labour department), GST (tax) and FSSAI (food safety). A small shop may need all four.
- **Documents:** ID and address proof, proof of the premises (property tax receipt, sale deed or rent agreement with the owner's consent), photo of the shop, and sometimes a site plan or fire NOC.
- **Apply online** on your city's portal, for example the Bengaluru trade licence portal, GHMC in Hyderabad, Greater Chennai Corporation, KMC in Kolkata or the MCD health trade licence portal in Delhi.
- **Fees** vary by city, trade type, area or road, and machinery. **Renewal** is usually yearly (some cities now allow several years at once), and late renewal attracts penalties of **25% to 100%** of the fee in the cities we checked.

## What is a trade licence?

A trade licence is a municipal permission. Your local body, whether a city corporation, municipality or gram panchayat, uses it to know which businesses run where and to make sure the trade is not a danger or nuisance to neighbours. A tea stall frying on gas, a bakery oven, a shop storing paint or fireworks: these are what municipal licensing laws were written for.

The legal basis is the municipal Act of your state or city. In Kolkata, for example, the Kolkata Municipal Corporation issues what it calls a **Certificate of Enlistment** under **Section 199 of the KMC Act, 1980**, with fees set each year in the corporation's schedule of rates. Other cities call it a trade licence, a D&O (dangerous and offensive trades) licence or, in Delhi, a **health trade licence**. The idea is the same.

Because it is local, there is no all-India trade licence, portal or fee.

## Who needs a trade licence?

The honest answer is: it depends on your local body's list. Most lists include:

| Type of business | Usually needs a trade licence? |
| --- | --- |
| Restaurants, dhabas, tea and snack stalls, sweet shops, bakeries | Yes, almost everywhere |
| Shops selling food, meat, fish, milk | Yes in most cities |
| Trades with machinery, ovens, boilers or generators | Yes, often with the horsepower affecting the fee |
| Storage of paint, chemicals, gas, firecrackers, timber | Yes, and often a fire NOC too |
| Salons, gyms, laundries, dry cleaners | Yes in many cities |
| Kirana, general, stationery, mobile, garment shops | In many corporations yes, in some only above a size; check your city |

In a village, the gram panchayat may issue its own licence or NOC under the state's panchayat law. The quickest way to know is to ask at your ward or zonal office, or check your corporation's list of licensed trades.

## Trade licence vs shop act, GST and FSSAI

These four get confused because shops often need them at the same time and agents sell them as a bundle. They come from different departments for different reasons.

| | Trade licence | Shop act registration | GST registration | FSSAI registration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Issued by** | Municipal corporation, municipality or panchayat | State labour department | GST department (Centre and state) | Food safety authority (FoSCoS portal) |
| **Law** | State municipal or panchayat Act | State Shops and Establishments Act | CGST and SGST Acts | Food Safety and Standards Act |

| **Who needs it** | Depends on the local body's list of trades | Depends on state and number of workers | Turnover above the limit, interstate sales, or by choice | Anyone selling food, including packaged food |
| **Renewal** | Usually yearly or for a fixed block of years | Varies by state | None; valid until cancelled | From April 2026, no renewal but an annual fee |

## Documents you will usually need

These come up on almost every portal's checklist:

- **Identity proof** of the owner or partners: Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID.
- **Proof of the premises:** the latest **property tax receipt** if you own it, or a **rent or lease agreement**.
- **Owner's consent or NOC** if the premises is rented, often on a declaration format the portal provides.
- **Photograph** of the shop front, sometimes with the signboard visible.
- **Business proof** if you have it: partnership deed, GST or shop act certificate.
- **Site plan or layout** of the premises, for some trades and larger premises.
- **Trade-specific approvals:** FSSAI registration for food businesses (Delhi now accepts it as a deemed approval for eating places), fire NOC for hazardous storage, pollution board consent for some industrial trades.
- **An affidavit or self-declaration**: Karnataka's Seva Sindhu renewal, for example, asks for ownership proof or a rental agreement plus an affidavit or declaration.

Keep the shop's name and address spelled exactly the same on the licence, the rent agreement, the GST certificate and your bills. Mismatches are the most common reason for queries.

## How to apply for a trade licence online

The steps are similar everywhere:

1. **Find the right portal** for your local body (the city corporation's site, or the state's urban local bodies or single-window portal for smaller towns).
2. **Register** with your mobile number, usually with an OTP.
3. **Fill the application:** owner details, trade type, premises address and ward or zone, area of the premises, and machinery or horsepower if any.
4. **Upload documents** and **pay the fee** online.
5. **Inspection:** a licence or health inspector may visit the shop before approval.
6. **Download the licence**, often digitally signed, and display it at the shop.

## Trade licence fees

There is no standard fee. What we saw across cities is that the fee depends on some mix of:

- **Trade type:** an eating house pays differently from a stationery shop.
- **Size of the premises**, usually in square feet.
- **Location:** zone, ward, or the width of the road the shop faces.
- **Machinery:** horsepower of motors, ovens or generators in some cities.
- **Period:** one year, or a block of years where the city allows it.

For a small shop the fee is usually modest next to rent, but it rises for eating places, larger premises and hazardous trades. An agent's fixed price is their charge plus the government fee; check the government fee on the portal's own schedule first.

## Validity and renewal

Most trade licences run for a year, either the **financial year** (April to March) or the **calendar year** (GHMC, for example). Some cities now let you renew for longer: Bengaluru allows one to five years, and Delhi's health trade licence portal publishes a fee schedule for FY 2025-28 along with provisions for longer validity.

The rule that costs shops money is the **renewal deadline**. Penalties are a percentage of the fee and climb fast:

| City | On time | Late | Very late or no licence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hyderabad (2026 licence) | By 20 Dec 2025 | 25% (21 Dec to 19 Feb) | 50% from 20 Feb; 100% fine for trading without a valid licence |
| Bengaluru (2026 renewal) | By 28 Feb 2026 | 25% (March) | 100% from 1 April |

**A worked example.** Say a shop's annual trade licence fee is ₹4,000. Renewed on time, it pays ₹4,000. Renewed in the 25% window, it pays ₹4,000 + ₹1,000 = **₹5,000**. At 50%, ₹4,000 + ₹2,000 = **₹6,000**. At 100%, ₹4,000 + ₹4,000 = **₹8,000**, double the fee for the same piece of paper.

Put the renewal date in your phone the day the licence arrives, and renew in the first month the window opens.

## Penalties for trading without a trade licence

Municipal Acts give the local body several tools, and the exact amounts depend on your state's law:

- **Fines** on the fee, like GHMC's 100% fine for trading without a valid licence.
- **Notices** from the health or licence inspector, with a deadline to apply.
- **Sealing or closure** of the premises in serious or repeated cases, especially for eating places and hazardous trades.
- **Knock-on problems:** some banks ask for a trade licence before opening a current account or giving a loan.

If you have been trading without one, apply now rather than wait for a notice. A late application costs a penalty; a sealing order costs far more.

## FAQ

### Is a trade licence mandatory for a small shop?

It depends on your municipal body. Eating places and trades with machinery or hazardous storage almost always need one. Many corporations require it for ordinary shops too, while some license only listed trades. Check your city's portal or ward office.

### What is the difference between a trade licence and shop act registration?

A trade licence comes from the municipal body and is about the premises and the trade being safe for the area. Shop act registration comes from the state labour department and is about your workers' hours, holidays and wages. You may need both.

### What are the trade licence fees?

They vary by city and are set by trade type, premises area, location and, in some cities, machinery horsepower. Check the fee on your corporation's portal; an agent's quoted price includes their own charge.

### How long is a trade licence valid?

Usually one year, by financial year or calendar year. Bengaluru now allows renewal for one to five years, and some other cities allow multi-year licences too. The licence or the portal shows your expiry date.

### What happens if I renew my trade licence late?

You pay a penalty on the fee. In Hyderabad it was 25%, then 50%, for the 2026 licence; in Bengaluru 25% in March and 100% from April for the 2026 renewal. Trading without a valid licence can also lead to notices or sealing.

### Do I need a trade licence for GST registration?

No. GST registration asks for proof of the business premises, such as a property tax receipt, rent agreement or electricity bill, but a trade licence is not compulsory for it.

## The bottom line

A trade licence is your local body's permission to run your trade at your address. If you sell food, use machinery or run a shop in a big corporation, assume you need one and check the portal. Use the same name and address as everywhere else, keep it separate from shop act, GST and FSSAI, and renew on time: in the cities we checked, late renewal costs 25% to 100% extra.
