Search "GST on gold" and you get two answers that cannot both be right. Some pages say jewellery is taxed at 3% on gold and 5% on making charges. Others say 3% on the whole bill. Then there is the old gold exchange, where some guides tell buyers GST applies only to the balance they pay, and jewellers' own industry bodies say the opposite.

This guide sorts it out for shop owners and buyers: which rate applies to what, why making charges usually go at 3% and sometimes at 5% or 18%, how an exchange is taxed, what has to be on a jewellery bill, and a worked bill with every rupee checked.

## Quick answer

- **GST on gold is 3%:** 1.5% CGST plus 1.5% SGST within a state, or 3% IGST between states. It applies to gold jewellery, coins, bars and biscuits. Silver and silver jewellery are also at 3%.
- **Making charges on jewellery a jeweller sells are part of the jewellery's price**, so they take 3%, even if the bill shows them on a separate line.
- **Making charges are a separate service only when you give your own gold.** Job work for a registered jeweller is at 5%. Work done directly for an individual customer is generally treated as 18%; confirm with your CA.
- **Old gold exchange:** GST is charged on the full value of the new jewellery. The old gold is a separate purchase that reduces what you pay, not the taxable value.
- **No GST** on gold ETF units or Sovereign Gold Bonds. No new SGB tranche has been issued since February 2024.
- **Import duty is separate:** 15% (10% basic customs duty plus 5% AIDC) since 13 May 2026, with 3% IGST on top.

## Making charges: 3%, 5% or 18%?

This is where most of the confusion lives, and most ranking pages get it half right. The rate depends on one question: **who owns the gold?**

### When the jeweller sells you finished jewellery: 3% on everything

You walk in, pick a chain from the counter and pay. The jeweller is selling you a piece of jewellery. The gold, the labour, the wastage and any stones are all part of that one product. Under GST, that is a single supply of goods, so the full price, making charges included, is taxed at 3%.

Showing making charges as a separate line on the bill does not change this. Jewellers list them separately because buyers want to see them, not because they are a separate supply. The Gem and Jewellery Council's GST FAQs give the same answer.

### When you give your own gold: it becomes a service

If you hand over 10 grams of old gold and ask a goldsmith to make bangles from it, the goldsmith is not selling you gold. They are doing work on goods that belong to you. That is a service under heading 9988, and its rate depends on who you are.

- **Job work for a registered person: 5%.** "Job work" under GST means work on goods belonging to another *registered* person. A karigar making jewellery from a registered jeweller's gold charges 5% on the labour.
- **Work for an individual customer: generally 18%.** An ordinary buyer is not registered, so the work is not "job work" in the legal sense. It falls under the general entry for manufacturing services on goods owned by others, which is 18%. Jewellers' associations, including the GJC in its updated FAQs, read it this way.

Some pages, and some jewellers, still charge 5% to retail customers who bring their own gold. If you run a jewellery shop, settle this with your CA in writing, because the difference between 5% and 18% on labour adds up across a year.

## Old gold exchange: GST is on the full price of the new piece

A customer brings old jewellery worth ₹60,000 and buys a new chain priced at ₹1,45,600 before tax. Is GST on ₹1,45,600 or on the ₹85,600 difference?

On the full ₹1,45,600. The jeweller is selling a new chain at its full value and, separately, buying old gold from the customer. The old gold is a form of payment. It does not reduce the taxable value of the new chain. The GJC FAQ uses the same logic in its example: old jewellery of ₹10,000 plus ₹5,000 cash for a new piece priced at ₹15,000 means GST on ₹15,000.

What about GST on the old gold itself? An individual selling their own household jewellery is not doing it in the course of business, so there is no GST on that purchase and no tax invoice from the customer. The jeweller records it on a purchase voucher with the weight, purity and value.

| | Correct method | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taxable value of new chain | ₹1,45,600 | ₹85,600 |
| GST at 3% | ₹4,368 | ₹2,568 |
| Total of new chain | ₹1,49,968 | ₹88,168 |
| Less old gold | ₹60,000 | (already deducted) |
| Customer pays | **₹89,968** | ₹88,168 |

The mistake undercharges GST by ₹1,800 on one bill. For the jeweller, that is tax payable out of pocket, plus interest, when it is caught in an audit.

**When the jeweller resells old jewellery as it is.** If a registered dealer buys used jewellery from an individual and sells it again without changing its form, the margin scheme in Rule 32(5) of the CGST Rules lets them pay GST only on the margin (selling price minus purchase price), with no input tax credit. The Tamil Nadu Authority for Advance Ruling allowed this in the Best Money Gold case. If the old gold is melted and remade, the margin scheme does not apply and GST is on the full value.

## A worked jewellery bill, line by line

Here is a sample bill for a 22 carat chain sold within the state. The gold rate is illustrative; use the day's board rate.

| Line | Detail | Amount (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Item | 22K gold chain, HSN 7113, HUID shown on bill | |
| Net weight | 10.000 g | |
| Gold value | 10.000 g × ₹13,000 per gram | 1,30,000.00 |
| Making charges | 12% of gold value | 15,600.00 |
| **Taxable value** | | **1,45,600.00** |
| CGST | 1.5% of ₹1,45,600 | 2,184.00 |
| SGST | 1.5% of ₹1,45,600 | 2,184.00 |
| **Invoice total** | | **1,49,968.00** |

Check: ₹1,30,000 + ₹15,600 = ₹1,45,600. 3% of ₹1,45,600 is ₹4,368, split into ₹2,184 each. ₹1,45,600 + ₹4,368 = ₹1,49,968.

If the buyer were in another state, the same ₹4,368 would be charged as 3% IGST instead of CGST and SGST. ## What a jewellery bill should show

A gold bill is a tax invoice first, so it needs everything in the standard [GST invoice format](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-invoice-format): your name, address and GSTIN, a serial invoice number, date, the customer's name, HSN, taxable value, rate and amount of each tax, and the place of supply. On top of that, a good jewellery bill shows:

- **Purity** (for example 22K or 916) and the **HUID** for each hallmarked piece
- **Gross weight, stone weight and net weight** in grams, to three decimals
- **Rate per gram** used for the day
- **Making charges** and any **stone charges** on separate lines, even though they all take 3%
- **Old gold** taken in exchange, with weight, purity and value, shown as a deduction after the GST total
- **Payment mode**, especially when part is cash

For the basic layout of any bill, see our guide to the [bill format](https://billzap.app/blog/bill-format).

## Hallmarking and HUID

Hallmarking is run by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS). It became mandatory for gold jewellery on 23 June 2021 in 256 districts, and BIS has extended it in phases since; its own brief now lists 392 districts. Check BIS's Mandatory Hallmarking Order page if you are not sure your district is covered.

- **Three marks on every piece:** the BIS standard mark, the purity (caratage and fineness, such as 22K916) and a **six-digit alphanumeric HUID** unique to that piece.
- **Permitted grades:** 9K, 14K, 18K, 20K, 22K, 23K and 24K.
- **Exemptions** include articles under 2 grams, kundan, polki and jadau pieces, and some export cases. Jewellers with annual turnover up to ₹40 lakh were exempted when the scheme began.
- **Buyers can check any HUID** in the BIS CARE app using "Verify HUID". It shows the purity and the article type.

Hallmarking is a BIS rule, not a GST rule. It does not change the GST rate, but showing the HUID on the bill ties each invoice to a specific piece, which helps you and the buyer if there is ever a dispute.

## PAN and cash limits on gold purchases

Two income-tax rules matter at the jewellery counter. Both carried over when the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026; only the section numbers changed.

- **Cash of ₹2 lakh or more is not allowed** from one person in a day, for one transaction, or for one event such as a wedding. This was Section 269ST and is now Section 221. The penalty falls on the jeweller who receives the cash and equals the amount received.
- **PAN is required for any purchase above ₹2 lakh**, whatever the payment mode, under Rule 159 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026. A buyer without PAN gives Form 60.

So a ₹1,49,968 bill paid in cash is fine. A ₹2,10,000 bill needs PAN, and at least ₹10,001 of it must come by UPI, card, cheque or bank transfer so the cash stays under ₹2 lakh.

## Gold coins, ETFs, digital gold and imports

- **Gold coins and bars** from a jeweller, bank or refiner: 3% GST on the price. Making charges on coins are part of the price, so 3% applies to the whole amount.
- **Digital gold** bought through apps: 3% GST on purchase, because you are buying physical gold stored in a vault on your behalf.
- **Gold ETFs and gold mutual funds:** no GST on the units, which are securities. Brokerage and fund charges carry 18% GST.
- **Sovereign Gold Bonds:** no GST. The government has not issued a new tranche since February 2024, so you can only buy existing bonds on the stock exchange.
- **Imports:** customs duty on gold was raised to 15% (10% basic customs duty plus 5% Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess) from 13 May 2026. IGST of 3% is then charged on the value including that duty. This is why the Indian price of gold sits above the international price.

## FAQ

### What is the GST rate on gold in 2026?

3%, split as 1.5% CGST and 1.5% SGST within a state, or 3% IGST for an inter-state sale. It applies to gold jewellery, coins, bars and digital gold. The rate did not change in the September 2025 GST overhaul.

### Is GST on making charges 3% or 5%?

When you buy finished jewellery from a jeweller, making charges are part of the jewellery price and take 3%. The 5% rate is for job work done for a registered jeweller on the jeweller's own gold. If you give your own gold to a goldsmith as an individual, the work is generally taxed at 18%.

### Is GST charged on the full value in an old gold exchange?

Yes. GST is on the full value of the new jewellery. The old gold you hand over is a separate sale by you and works as part payment, so it reduces the amount you pay but not the GST.

### Is there GST when I sell my old gold to a jeweller?

No. An individual selling personal jewellery is not doing business, so no GST is charged on that sale. The jeweller records it on a purchase voucher.

### Is there GST on gold coins bought from a bank?

Yes, 3% on the price of the coin, whoever sells it.

### What is HUID on gold jewellery?

A six-digit alphanumeric code unique to each hallmarked piece, stamped along with the BIS mark and purity. You can check it in the BIS CARE app with the "Verify HUID" option.

### Do I need PAN to buy gold?

Yes, for any purchase above ₹2 lakh, whether you pay in cash, by card or by UPI. Separately, a jeweller cannot accept ₹2 lakh or more in cash from one person for one transaction.

## The bottom line

Gold, coins and jewellery are taxed at 3%, and making charges ride along at 3% when the jeweller sells you the finished piece. Making charges become a separate service only when the gold is yours: 5% as job work for a registered jeweller, generally 18% for an individual. In an exchange, GST is on the full price of the new piece. Get those three right on every bill, show the HUID and weights, and keep cash under ₹2 lakh.
