A **bill of supply** is the GST bill you issue when you are registered but **cannot or do not charge tax** on what you sell. It looks like a tax invoice with the tax columns taken out, and it carries a different heading. Two kinds of seller use it every day: shops under the **composition scheme**, and registered businesses selling **exempt** goods or services such as fresh vegetables, eggs or loose grains.

Getting it wrong is common. Composition shops print "Tax Invoice" because their billing book came that way, or show "GST included" on a bill where they are not allowed to collect any tax. This guide sets out who must issue a bill of supply, what Rule 49 requires on it, the wording a composition dealer must print, the combined bill for shops that sell both taxable and exempt items, and filled samples you can copy.

## Quick answer

- **Who issues it:** a **registered** person who (a) is under the **composition scheme**, or (b) supplies **exempt** goods or services (nil-rated, wholly exempt or non-GST). Section 31(3)(c) of the CGST Act.
- **What it must show (Rule 49):** your name, address and GSTIN; a serial number of up to 16 characters, unique for the financial year; date; buyer's name, address and GSTIN if registered; HSN or SAC; description; value after discount; and a signature.
- **No tax** is shown, and the buyer gets **no input tax credit** from it.
- **Composition dealers** must print **"composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies"** at the top.
- **Mixed bill:** a regular shop selling taxable and exempt items to an **unregistered** customer can issue one **invoice-cum-bill of supply** (Rule 46A).
- **Small sales:** Section 31(3)(c) lets you skip a bill of supply where the value is **under ₹200**, subject to prescribed conditions. Issue one if the customer asks.
- **Unregistered shops** do not issue a bill of supply at all; they issue a plain bill or cash memo.

## Who issues a bill of supply

| Seller | Document | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition dealer (trader, manufacturer or restaurant) | **Bill of supply** | Section 10 dealers cannot collect tax from buyers |
| Composition service provider under Section 10(2A) | **Bill of supply** | Same rule |
| Regular registered shop selling **only exempt** goods (fresh vegetables, fruit, eggs, fresh milk, loose grains) | **Bill of supply** | No tax on exempt supplies |
| Regular registered business supplying exempt services | **Bill of supply** | Same |
| Regular shop selling taxable **and** exempt items to an **unregistered** customer | **Invoice-cum-bill of supply** (one document) | Rule 46A |
| Regular shop selling taxable **and** exempt items to a **registered** buyer | Tax invoice for taxable items, bill of supply for exempt items | Rule 46A covers unregistered buyers only |
| Regular registered shop selling taxable goods | **Tax invoice** | Section 31(1) |
| Exporter (zero-rated supply) | **Tax invoice** with the export declaration | Exports are zero-rated, not exempt |
| Shop **not registered** under GST | Plain **bill** or cash memo | Cannot issue any GST document |

"Exempt supply" under Section 2(47) includes nil-rated supplies, wholly exempt supplies and non-taxable supplies. So a fuel pump selling petrol, which is outside GST, is also in bill of supply territory for that sale. Rule 49 adds that an invoice issued under another law for a non-taxable supply (a VAT invoice for petrol or liquor, for example) is treated as the bill of supply.

Not sure which items are exempt today? After the September 2025 rate changes, several items moved to nil, such as packed paneer, UHT milk, roti and paratha. Check our [GST rates list](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-rates-list) before you set up your products.

## What a bill of supply must contain: Rule 49

Rule 49 of the CGST Rules lists eight particulars:

| # | Particular | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Name, address and GSTIN of the supplier | Your trade name as registered |
| 2 | Consecutive serial number, up to 16 characters | Letters, numbers, "-" and "/" only; unique for the financial year |
| 3 | Date of issue | |
| 4 | Name, address and GSTIN or UIN of the recipient, **if registered** | For walk-in consumers, a name is enough or can be left out |
| 5 | HSN code of goods or SAC of services | See the digit rule below |
| 6 | Description of goods or services | |
| 7 | **Value of supply**, after any discount or abatement | The amount the customer pays |
| 8 | Signature or digital signature of the supplier or authorised representative | |

**HSN digits.** Rule 49 applies the proviso of Rule 46 to bills of supply, and that proviso (as set by Notification 78/2020-Central Tax) scales HSN digits with turnover. With aggregate turnover up to ₹5 crore in the previous year, use **4 digits** on bills to registered buyers; on bills to unregistered consumers, the digits may be left out. Above ₹5 crore, use **6 digits** on all bills. Our guide to [what an HSN code is](https://billzap.app/blog/what-is-hsn-code) explains the structure.

What is **not** on a bill of supply: the GST rate, the tax amount, CGST, SGST or IGST columns, and the place of supply. If you see tax columns, it is not a bill of supply.

### The composition dealer's extra wording

**Rule 5(1) of the CGST Rules** requires a composition dealer to:

- print **"composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies"** at the **top** of every bill of supply, and
- display **"composition taxable person"** on the signboard and notices at every place of business.

Composition dealers pay their tax (1% of turnover for traders and manufacturers, 5% for restaurants, 6% for service providers under Section 10(2A)) from their own pocket through CMP-08 every quarter. Showing "GST" on the bill, or adding tax to the price as a separate line, is not allowed. More in our [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide.

## Bill of supply vs tax invoice

| | Bill of supply | Tax invoice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Law | Section 31(3)(c), Rule 49 | Section 31(1) and (2), Rule 46 |
| Issued by | Composition dealers; sellers of exempt goods or services | Regular registered persons selling taxable supplies |
| Heading | "Bill of Supply" | "Tax Invoice" |
| Tax shown | None | Rate and amount of CGST, SGST or IGST, per item |
| Place of supply | Not required | Required for inter-state supplies |
| Particulars | 8 | 17 |
| Buyer's input tax credit | **None** | Yes, if conditions are met |
| Composition wording | Required for composition dealers | Not applicable |
| Reported in | GSTR-1 Table 8 (regular taxpayers' exempt sales); CMP-08 and GSTR-4 (composition dealers) | GSTR-1 Tables 4, 5 or 7 |
| Under ₹200 to a consumer | May be skipped (subject to conditions) | May be skipped; consolidated daily invoice instead |

## Sample 1: bill of supply for a composition kirana

Shree Ganesh Kirana in Indore is a composition dealer. A customer buys four items.

**COMPOSITION TAXABLE PERSON, NOT ELIGIBLE TO COLLECT TAX ON SUPPLIES**

**BILL OF SUPPLY**

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Supplier | Shree Ganesh Kirana, 14 Sneh Nagar, Indore, Madhya Pradesh |
| GSTIN | 23AAAAA0000A1Z5 |
| Bill number | BOS/26-27/1482 |
| Date | 25-11-2026 |
| Customer | Walk-in (unregistered) |

| Sr | Description | HSN | Qty | Rate (₹) | Amount (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Toor dal, 1 kg pack | 0713 | 2 | 140.00 | 280.00 |
| 2 | Sugar, loose (kg) | 1701 | 5 | 44.00 | 220.00 |
| 3 | Sunflower oil, 1 L pouch | 1512 | 2 | 150.00 | 300.00 |
| 4 | Bath soap, 100 g | 3401 | 4 | 38.00 | 152.00 |
| | **Total** | | | | **952.00** |
| | Less: discount | | | | 12.00 |
| | **Value of supply / amount payable** | | | | **940.00** |

Signature: for Shree Ganesh Kirana

**Check:** 280 + 220 + 300 + 152 = **₹952**; less ₹12 = **₹940**. No tax line. The shop itself pays 1% of its turnover as composition tax, which on this sale is ₹9.40 (₹4.70 CGST + ₹4.70 SGST), through the quarterly CMP-08. That ₹9.40 never appears on the customer's bill.

## Sample 2: invoice-cum-bill of supply for a mixed bill

Many regular GST kiranas sell both taxable packed goods and exempt fresh items. Making two bills for one customer is a waste of time. **Rule 46A** (inserted from 13 October 2017) lets a registered person supplying taxable and exempt goods or services **to an unregistered person** issue a single **"invoice-cum-bill of supply"**. A proviso added from 26 December 2022 (Notification 26/2022-Central Tax) makes clear that this single document must still carry the particulars of Rule 46 for the taxable items and Rule 49 for the exempt items.

Lakshmi Stores, a regular GST registrant in Chennai, sells to a walk-in customer:

**INVOICE-CUM-BILL OF SUPPLY** | Lakshmi Stores, GSTIN 33AAAAA0000A1Z5 | No. LS/26-27/2211 | 25-11-2026 | Customer: walk-in (unregistered), Tamil Nadu (33)

| Sr | Description | HSN | Qty | Rate (₹) | Taxable / exempt value (₹) | GST rate | CGST (₹) | SGST (₹) | Total (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Wheat atta, 5 kg (packed, labelled) | 1101 | 1 | 240.00 | 240.00 | 5% | 6.00 | 6.00 | 252.00 |
| 2 | Detergent powder, 1 kg | 3402 | 1 | 120.00 | 120.00 | 18% | 10.80 | 10.80 | 141.60 |
| 3 | Tomatoes (kg) | 0702 | 2 | 30.00 | 60.00 | Exempt | 0.00 | 0.00 | 60.00 |
| 4 | Eggs | 0407 | 12 | 7.00 | 84.00 | Exempt | 0.00 | 0.00 | 84.00 |
| | **Taxable value** | | | | **360.00** | | **16.80** | **16.80** | |
| | **Exempt value** | | | | **144.00** | | | | |
| | **Grand total** | | | | | | | | **537.60** |

**Check:** atta 240 × 2.5% = ₹6.00 each for CGST and SGST; detergent 120 × 9% = ₹10.80 each. Tax total ₹33.60. ₹360 + ₹33.60 + ₹144 = **₹537.60**. Many shops round this to ₹538 with a round-off line of ₹0.40.

If the same customer were **registered**, Rule 46A would not apply: issue a tax invoice for the atta and detergent, and a separate bill of supply for the tomatoes and eggs.

## The ₹200 rule for small sales

Section 31(3)(c) has a proviso: a registered person **may not issue a bill of supply** if the value of the supply is **less than ₹200**, subject to conditions and in a manner to be prescribed. (The equivalent relief for tax invoices is in Section 31(3)(b).)

In practice, treat it this way:

- It is a relief for small counter sales to **unregistered** customers. If a customer asks for a bill, give one.
- The sale is still part of your turnover. Record it, for example in a **daily consolidated bill** or your day's sales summary, so that your books, your CMP-08 or GSTR-1 figures and your cash all match. That is how tax invoices under ₹200 work under Section 31(3)(b), and it is the safest practice for bills of supply too.
- A billing app that numbers every sale makes the question moot: issuing a bill takes seconds.

A plain cash memo for such a sale is fine for your records; our [cash memo format](https://billzap.app/blog/cash-memo-format) guide has a layout.

## Reporting bills of supply

- **Regular taxpayers** report exempt, nil-rated and non-GST sales in **GSTR-1 Table 8**, split by inter-state or intra-state and registered or unregistered buyers. The serial number range of your bills appears in the document summary, Table 13. See our [GSTR-1 filing guide](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-file-gstr-1).
- **Composition dealers** do not file GSTR-1. They declare turnover and pay tax in **CMP-08** by the 18th of the month after each quarter, and file the annual **GSTR-4** by 30 June.
- Keep bills of supply for **72 months** from the due date of the annual return for that year, like all GST records.

## Common mistakes

- **Printing "Tax Invoice" on a composition bill.** The heading must be Bill of Supply.
- **Showing GST on a composition bill**, even as "inclusive of GST". Composition dealers cannot collect tax.
- **Missing the composition wording** at the top.
- **One combined bill to a registered buyer.** Rule 46A is only for unregistered buyers.
- **Restarting numbers every month.** The series must be unique for the whole financial year.
- **Charging tax on items that became nil** in September 2025, or treating taxable items as exempt. Check each product's rate once and save it.
- **An unregistered shop calling its bill a bill of supply.** Only registered persons issue one; an unregistered shop issues a plain bill. See our [bill format](https://billzap.app/blog/bill-format) guide for all three types.

## FAQ

### What is a bill of supply in GST?

A bill issued by a registered person, instead of a tax invoice, when no GST can be charged: by composition dealers, and by sellers of exempt, nil-rated or non-GST goods or services. It shows no tax, and the buyer cannot claim input tax credit on it.

### Who should issue a bill of supply?

Composition dealers for all their sales, and regular registered persons for exempt supplies. An unregistered shop does not issue a bill of supply; it issues a plain bill.

### What are the contents of a bill of supply under Rule 49?

Supplier's name, address and GSTIN; a serial number up to 16 characters, unique for the year; date; recipient's name, address and GSTIN if registered; HSN or SAC; description; value after discount; and signature.

### What must a composition dealer write on the bill?

"Composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies", at the top of every bill of supply. The signboard must also say "composition taxable person".

### Can I issue one bill for taxable and exempt items?

Yes, to an unregistered customer, as an invoice-cum-bill of supply under Rule 46A, with the tax invoice particulars for taxable items and the bill of supply particulars for exempt items. To a registered buyer, issue a tax invoice and a separate bill of supply.

### Is a bill of supply needed for sales under ₹200?

Not necessarily. Section 31(3)(c) lets you skip it for supplies under ₹200, subject to prescribed conditions. Issue one if the customer asks, and keep every sale in your records.

### Can a buyer claim ITC on a bill of supply?

No. A bill of supply carries no tax, so there is no credit to claim, even if the buyer is registered.

## The bottom line

If you are registered and not charging tax, your bill is a **bill of supply**: eight Rule 49 particulars, no tax columns, a year-long number series and, for composition dealers, the line "composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies" at the top. Sell both taxable and exempt items to walk-in customers? One invoice-cum-bill of supply does the job. Set the heading right once, and every bill after it will be right too.
