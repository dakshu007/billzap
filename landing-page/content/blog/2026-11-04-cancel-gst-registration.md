Shops register for GST for many reasons: a supplier insisted, a business customer wanted GST bills, or turnover crossed the limit one good year. Some years later the shop has shrunk or closed, and the owner is still filing two returns a month for nothing.

Surrendering a GST registration is not hard, but most guides stop at the portal clicks. They skip the parts that cost money: the tax on stock still on your shelves, the final return almost everyone forgets, and the bills you print the day after. This guide covers the whole closure, in order.

## Quick answer

- **Who can cancel:** a business that has **closed or been transferred**, one whose turnover is **below the registration limit**, or a **voluntary registrant** who no longer wants GST. The old rule that voluntary registrants must wait one year was removed in January 2018.
- **How:** file **Form GST REG-16** on gst.gov.in (Services > Registration > Application for Cancellation of Registration), ideally **within 30 days** of the event, declaring your closing stock and the tax on it.
- **Pay on closing stock:** pay back the input tax credit on stock and capital goods you still hold, or the tax on their value if higher.
- **Officer's order:** in **REG-19**, normally within 30 days. Meanwhile your registration is **suspended**: no tax invoices, no GST charged.
- **Final return:** file **GSTR-10** within **three months** of the cancellation date or the order date, whichever is later. Late fee: **₹200 a day** (₹100 CGST + ₹100 SGST), up to **₹10,000**.
- **If the officer cancelled you** (for example, for not filing returns), apply for revocation in **REG-21** within **90 days** of the order, extendable by up to 180 days more.
- **Your bills:** once cancelled, you cannot charge GST or call your bill a Tax Invoice.

## Should you cancel at all?

Being registered costs time and fees, but it also does things for you. Check first.

| Situation | Cancel? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shop closed for good | Yes | Returns keep falling due while the GSTIN is active, and late fees build up |
| Turnover well below the limit, all customers are households | Usually yes | You save the returns and, if you file through a CA, the fees |
| Turnover below the limit, but most buyers are businesses | Think twice | They lose input tax credit on your bills and may move to a registered supplier |
| You sell to other states, or through some online marketplaces | Probably not | These can require registration whatever your turnover |
| Turnover near the limit and growing | Probably not | You may have to register again within a year |
| You want to pay less tax but stay registered | No, look at composition | The [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) may suit you better |

Your limit is ₹40 lakh for goods-only businesses in most states but ₹20 lakh for services, and lower in a few states; see our guide to the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit).

## When the law lets you cancel

Section 29(1) of the CGST Act allows cancellation, on your own application or on an application by your legal heirs, when:

1. **The business has closed** or been transferred, sold, merged or demerged, including transfer on the death of the proprietor.
2. **The constitution of the business has changed**, for example a proprietorship becoming a partnership firm with a new PAN.
3. **You are no longer liable to be registered**, typically because turnover has fallen below the limit.
4. **You registered voluntarily and want to opt out.**

**The one-year rule no longer applies.** Rule 20 once barred a voluntary registrant from cancelling within a year of registration. That proviso was **omitted by Notification 03/2018-Central Tax, dated 23 January 2018**, though many websites still quote it. If you registered voluntarily six months ago, you can apply now.

## Before you apply: a short checklist

1. **File every pending return** up to the cancellation date: GSTR-1 and [GSTR-3B](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-3b), or CMP-08 if you are a composition dealer. An application with gaps invites questions. The [GST due dates](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-due-dates) guide lists what was due when.
2. **Choose the date.** Usually the day the shop closed, or a month end so your last GSTR-3B covers a clean period.
3. **Count your stock** on that date, including capital goods (fridge, computer, machinery) on which you claimed credit, and find the purchase bills.
4. **Note your ledger balances.** The amount on closing stock can be paid from your electronic credit or cash ledger.

## How to cancel GST registration: REG-16 step by step

The GST portal's own manual describes this flow.

1. Log in at **gst.gov.in**.
2. Go to **Services > Registration > Application for Cancellation of Registration**.
3. **Basic Details tab:** confirm your address for future correspondence, where notices will reach you after cancellation.
4. **Cancellation Details tab:** choose the reason. The dropdown offers: change in constitution of business leading to change in PAN; ceased to be liable to pay tax; discontinuance or closure of business; transfer of business (amalgamation, merger, demerger, sale, lease); death of sole proprietor; and others.
5. Enter the **date from which you want the registration cancelled**.
6. Enter the **value of stock** held on that date and the **tax on it**, then show how you will pay that amount from your cash and credit ledgers.
7. If the business is being transferred or merged, enter the **GSTIN of the business taking it over**.
8. **Verification tab:** tick the declaration, choose the authorised signatory, enter the place, and submit with **EVC** (OTP) or **DSC**.
9. An **ARN** is generated. Keep it; you will track the application with it.

Rule 20 asks for the application **within 30 days of the event**. If you closed months ago and never applied, apply anyway: every month of delay is another month of returns falling due. ## Tax on closing stock: what you pay back, with an example

When you claimed input tax credit on goods you bought, the law assumed you would sell them and charge GST. If you close with those goods still on the shelf, Section 29(5) makes you pay back that credit.

**What you pay** is the higher of:

- the **input tax credit** on inputs, semi-finished and finished goods held in stock on the day before cancellation, worked out from the purchase invoices (Rule 44), or
- the **output tax** that would be payable on those goods.

For **capital goods**, the credit is reduced for the time you have used them, on a five-year life. Rule 44 works it out month by month for the remaining life. If the tax on the goods' current transaction value is higher, that applies instead.

Without the purchase invoices, you estimate the credit at the **current market price**, and Rule 44 requires that estimate to be **certified by a chartered or cost accountant**.

### Worked example

Sunita runs a gift and stationery shop and is cancelling from 31 December 2026. On that date she holds:

| Item | Purchase value | GST rate | Credit taken |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gift items and stationery | ₹1,00,000 | 18% | ₹18,000 |
| Packed snacks and other goods | ₹60,000 | 5% | ₹3,000 |
| **Stock total** | **₹1,60,000** | | **₹21,000** |

She also has a display fridge bought **2 years and 3 months** earlier (27 months), on which she claimed **₹9,000** of credit.

| Working | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Remaining life of fridge: 60 − 27 months | 33 months |
| Credit to pay back on fridge: ₹9,000 × 33 ÷ 60 | ₹4,950 |
| Credit on stock (from the table) | ₹21,000 |
| **Total to pay with REG-16 / GSTR-10** | **₹25,950** |

If her stock is worth more today than she paid for it, the tax on today's value is the figure to use, because the law takes the higher of the two.

**The practical lesson:** stock you still hold costs you its credit. If you are closing down, a clearance sale in the last weeks, billed with GST, is usually better than paying the reversal on unsold goods. Composition dealers, who cannot claim input tax credit, generally have nothing to reverse.

## What happens after you apply

- **Your registration is suspended straight away.** Under Rule 21A, once you apply for cancellation, the registration is deemed suspended from the date of the application or the date you asked cancellation from, whichever is later. During suspension you **must not issue tax invoices or charge GST**, and you do not have to file GSTR-3B for that period.
- **The order.** If satisfied, the officer issues an order in **REG-19** cancelling your registration from a date the officer decides, within 30 days of your application (or of your reply to any notice). The order directs you to pay any tax, interest or penalty due, including the closing-stock amount.
- **Past dues survive.** Section 29(3) says cancellation does not wipe out tax or obligations for the period before it. A notice about an old year can still reach you.

## GSTR-10: the final return almost everyone forgets

Cancellation is not complete until you file **GSTR-10**, the final return under Section 45.

- **Who files:** everyone whose registration is cancelled, except composition dealers, input service distributors, non-resident taxable persons, TDS deductors, TCS collectors and OIDAR service providers.
- **When:** within **three months** of the effective date of cancellation or the date of the cancellation order, **whichever is later**.
- **What it contains:** the closing stock (inputs, semi-finished and finished goods, capital goods), with and without invoices, and the tax payable and paid on it.
- **Nil is allowed** if you had no stock and no credit to reverse.

**Late fee:** Section 47 sets ₹100 a day under CGST, capped at ₹5,000, and the same under SGST. That is **₹200 a day, up to ₹10,000** in total.

**Example:** Sunita's REG-19 order is dated 20 January 2027, with cancellation effective from 31 December 2026. The later of the two dates is 20 January, so GSTR-10 is due by **20 April 2027**. If she files on 15 May, she is 25 days late: 25 × ₹200 = **₹5,000** in late fee. At 50 days late, she would hit the ₹10,000 cap.

If GSTR-10 is not filed at all, the officer can send a notice and then assess the amount on the best information available.

## When the officer cancels your registration

Under Section 29(2) and Rule 21, the officer can cancel a registration, even from a past date, when, among other reasons:

- a **monthly filer** has not filed returns for a **continuous period of six months**;
- a **quarterly (QRMP) filer** has not filed for **two consecutive tax periods**;
- a **composition dealer** has not filed the annual return for more than **three months** after its due date;
- a **voluntary registrant has not started business within six months**;
- the business is **not operating from the declared address**, or bills were issued without any actual supply.

The officer must first give you a chance to be heard. You get a show cause notice in **REG-17** and have **seven working days** to reply in **REG-18**. If the notice is for non-filing, the simplest reply is to file all pending returns and pay the tax, interest and late fee; the rules then require the officer to drop the proceedings.

### Revocation: getting a cancelled GSTIN back with REG-21

If the officer cancelled your registration on their own, you can apply to have the cancellation revoked in **Form REG-21**:

- **Time limit:** within **90 days** from the date the cancellation order was served. Since August 2023 (Notification 38/2023-Central Tax), this can be **extended by up to 180 days more** by a Joint or Additional Commissioner if you show sufficient cause. The old limit was 30 days.
- **If cancelled for non-filing:** you must first file all pending returns and pay the tax, interest, penalty and late fees in them.
- **After revocation:** you must file all returns for the gap period, from the cancellation date to the revocation order, within **30 days** of the revocation order.
- **The officer decides** within 30 days: **REG-22** if revoked, **REG-05** if rejected.

Revocation is only for cancellations made by the officer. If you applied yourself and change your mind, you register afresh. If the revocation window has closed, the options are an appeal or a new registration; take professional advice.

## What happens to your bills after cancellation

- **Stop charging GST** from the effective date of cancellation, and from the date of your application while it is suspended. Section 32 forbids anyone who is not registered from collecting any amount by way of tax, and any amount collected as tax has to be paid to the government anyway.
- **Do not call the bill a "Tax Invoice".** After cancellation your document is a plain **bill** or **cash memo**.
- **Remove the GSTIN** from your bill heading, letterhead and shop board. A cancelled GSTIN on a bill confuses customers and can mislead a business buyer into claiming credit that is not there.
- **Tell your business customers** the date your registration ends, and **keep your records** for the registered period.

## FAQ

### Can I cancel GST registration within one year of voluntary registration?

Yes. The rule that stopped voluntary registrants from cancelling within a year was omitted from Rule 20 in January 2018. You can apply in REG-16 whenever you no longer need the registration.

### Is there a fee to cancel GST registration?

There is no government fee for filing REG-16. You do have to pay the tax on closing stock, any pending tax, interest and late fees on returns, and a late fee on GSTR-10 if it is filed late.

### What is the last date to file GSTR-10?

Three months from the effective date of cancellation or the date of the cancellation order, whichever is later. After that the late fee is ₹200 a day, capped at ₹10,000.

### My GST registration was cancelled for not filing returns. Can I get it back?

Yes, if you act in time. File all pending returns and pay the dues, then apply for revocation in REG-21 within 90 days of the cancellation order. On sufficient cause, a senior officer can extend that by up to 180 days.

### Can I use my old GSTIN on bills after cancellation?

No. After cancellation you must not charge GST, call your bill a Tax Invoice, or show the cancelled GSTIN as if it were active. Issue a plain bill without tax.

## The bottom line

To cancel GST registration cleanly: file pending returns, count your stock, apply in **REG-16**, stop charging GST the day you apply, wait for the **REG-19** order, and file **GSTR-10** within three months. Sell down what stock you can before the date. If the officer cancelled you instead, the **90 days** for REG-21 still runs out, so move fast. And change your bills the same day.
