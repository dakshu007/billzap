Many pages on GST for online sellers still say three things that are no longer true: that you must register for GST before your first online sale whatever your turnover, that composition dealers cannot sell through e-commerce, and that marketplaces deduct 1% TCS. All three changed between October 2023 and July 2024.

This guide sets out the rules as they stand in November 2026: who needs a GSTIN, how small sellers can sell within their state without one, what the marketplaces actually ask for, how TCS and TDS work, and a worked month of numbers you can compare with your own settlement report.

## Quick answer

- **Default rule:** anyone selling through an e-commerce operator that collects TCS must register for GST, whatever the turnover (section 24 of the CGST Act).
- **Exception since 1 October 2023:** you can sell **goods within your own state** without a GSTIN if your turnover is under the registration limit, using a **GST enrolment number** (Notification 34/2023-Central Tax). Inter-state sales still need a GSTIN.
- **Composition dealers** can sell **goods** (not services) through e-commerce since 1 October 2023, within their state only.
- **TCS:** the platform collects **0.5%** of your net taxable sales (0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST, or 0.5% IGST) since **10 July 2024**. It was 1% before.
- **GSTR-8** is the platform's return, not yours, due by the 10th of the next month. The TCS shows on your GST portal and goes to your cash ledger once you accept it.
- **Income-tax TDS:** **0.1%** of gross sales, under **section 393(1)** of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (it was section 194-O until 31 March 2026).
- **Marketplaces:** Meesho accepts an enrolment number for in-state selling. Amazon and Flipkart generally ask for a GSTIN, except for sellers of exempt goods such as books. Check each platform's current sign-up page.

## Selling online without GST: the enrolment number route

Notification 34/2023-Central Tax exempts small sellers of goods through e-commerce from registration, on conditions:

- you **do not make inter-state supplies**;
- you sell through e-commerce in **only one state**;
- you have a **PAN**;
- you **declare your PAN, business address and state** on the GST portal and get an **enrolment number** before selling;
- your aggregate turnover stays **within the registration limit**.

The platform is allowed to let you sell only after the enrolment number is allotted (Notification 37/2023-Central Tax), and it must restrict your listings to buyers in your state.

**What it means in practice:** you charge no GST, issue no tax invoice, file no GST returns, and claim no input tax credit. Your products are visible only to buyers in your state. The day you want to sell inter-state or your turnover approaches the limit, take regular registration; the enrolment number stops being valid once you register. ## Composition dealers on e-commerce

Since 1 October 2023, a composition dealer can sell **goods** through Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho or ONDC. The limits:

- **Intra-state only.** Composition dealers cannot make inter-state sales, and the platform must block them.
- **Goods only.** Composition dealers supplying services through e-commerce are still excluded.
- **TCS still applies** at 0.5%, and the credit can be used towards your quarterly CMP-08 payment.
- You issue a **bill of supply**, not a tax invoice, and cannot charge GST to the buyer.

For a small seller with mostly local buyers, composition at 1% of turnover can beat regular GST. The trade-offs are in our [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide.

## TCS on e-commerce: how it works

**Who collects:** the e-commerce operator, under section 52 of the CGST Act. Where several apps are involved in one order, as on ONDC, the seller-side app that pays you collects it (CBIC Circular 194/06/2023-GST).

**Rate:** 0.5% of the **net value of taxable supplies**: your taxable sales through that platform in the month, minus returns, excluding GST. Intra-state: 0.25% CGST + 0.25% SGST. Inter-state: 0.5% IGST.

**GSTR-8:** the operator reports the TCS seller by seller in GSTR-8 by the 10th of the following month, and files an annual GSTR-9B. You do **not** file GSTR-8.

**How you claim TCS credit:**

1. Log in to the GST portal and open the TCS and TDS credit received option under Returns.
2. Check the amounts against your settlement reports and **accept** them (or reject wrong entries).
3. Accepted TCS goes to your **electronic cash ledger**, where it pays your GST or can be claimed as a refund.
4. In GSTR-1, report sales made through each platform with its GSTIN, in the table for supplies through e-commerce operators. The guide on [how to file GSTR-1](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-file-gstr-1) shows where.

If TCS is missing, the operator either filed GSTR-8 late or reported the wrong GSTIN. Raise it with seller support; the operator can correct it in a later GSTR-8.

## Income-tax TDS: 0.1% under the new Act

Separately from GST, the operator deducts **income-tax TDS of 0.1%** on the gross amount of your sales. Until 31 March 2026 this was section 194-O of the 1961 Act. Under the Income-tax Act, 2025, it sits in the TDS table of **section 393(1)**. An individual or HUF seller with gross sales through the operator of up to ₹5 lakh in the year, who has given PAN or Aadhaar, is generally exempt. The TDS appears in your annual tax statement and is claimed in your income-tax return; small sellers often file under presumptive taxation (see our [ITR-4 guide](https://billzap.app/blog/itr-4-for-business)).

## Worked example: one month of TCS, TDS and GST

Priya, a regular GST-registered seller in Jaipur, sells phone accessories (18% GST) on a marketplace. In October 2026:

| Sales through the marketplace | Taxable value |
| --- | --- |
| To buyers in Rajasthan (intra-state) | ₹1,20,000 |
| To buyers in other states (inter-state) | ₹3,80,000 |
| Less: returns, all inter-state | −₹30,000 |
| **Net taxable value** | **₹4,70,000** |

**GST TCS collected by the marketplace**

| Head | Working | TCS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CGST | ₹1,20,000 × 0.25% | ₹300 |
| SGST | ₹1,20,000 × 0.25% | ₹300 |
| IGST | ₹3,50,000 × 0.5% | ₹1,750 |
| **Total** | ₹4,70,000 × 0.5% | **₹2,350** |

**Income-tax TDS:** 0.1% of net sales of ₹4,70,000 = **₹470**. (The exact base depends on how the operator treats GST and refunds; check your TDS certificate.)

**Priya's GST for the month**

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Output GST: ₹1,20,000 × 18% (CGST + SGST) | ₹21,600 |
| Output GST: ₹3,50,000 × 18% (IGST) | ₹63,000 |
| **Total output GST** | **₹84,600** |
| Less: input tax credit on purchases | −₹55,000 |
| Balance payable | ₹29,600 |
| Less: TCS credit in cash ledger | −₹2,350 |
| **Fresh cash to pay in GSTR-3B** | **₹27,250** |

TCS is not an extra tax: it is an advance against tax Priya owes anyway. The ₹470 TDS reduces her income-tax bill for the year. If she did not accept the TCS on the portal, she would pay ₹29,600 in cash and leave ₹2,350 sitting unused.

## Common mistakes online sellers make

- **Using an enrolment number and selling inter-state** through your own website or social media. Inter-state supply of goods needs a GSTIN.
- **Sending stock to a warehouse in another state** without registering there.
- **Not accepting TCS credit** on the portal, so it never reaches the cash ledger.
- **Reporting marketplace sales as ordinary B2C** in GSTR-1, without the operator's GSTIN, which creates mismatches with GSTR-8.
- **Ignoring returns** when checking TCS: TCS is on net sales, so heavy returns reduce it.
- **Quoting 1% TCS** in your pricing sheets. It has been 0.5% since July 2024.

## FAQ

### Can I sell on Amazon without GST?

Generally no, unless you sell only GST-exempt goods such as books, in which case Amazon lets you register with PAN. The law allows in-state selling with an enrolment number, but check whether Amazon's current sign-up accepts it.

### Can I sell on Flipkart without GST?

Flipkart generally asks for a GSTIN, except for sellers of exempt categories such as books. Check its seller sign-up for current rules.

### Can I sell on Meesho without GST?

Yes. Since October 2023, Meesho accepts a GST enrolment number, and your products are shown only to buyers in your state.

### What is the TCS rate on e-commerce sales?

0.5% of net taxable value since 10 July 2024: 0.25% CGST plus 0.25% SGST for intra-state sales, or 0.5% IGST for inter-state sales.

### Do I have to file GSTR-8 as a seller?

No. GSTR-8 is filed by the e-commerce operator. You accept the TCS it reports on the GST portal and use it from your cash ledger.

### Can a composition dealer sell on Amazon or Flipkart?

The law has allowed composition dealers to sell goods through e-commerce since 1 October 2023, within their state only. Whether a particular marketplace onboards composition sellers is its own policy.

### Is TCS an extra cost for online sellers?

No. It is an advance payment of your GST. Accept it on the portal and it reduces the cash you pay in GSTR-3B.

## The bottom line

Online selling no longer forces every small seller to register. If you sell goods only within your state and stay under the limit, an enrolment number is enough on platforms that accept it, Meesho being the clearest. Once you sell across states, hold stock in another state or cross the limit, take a GSTIN. Either way, the platform collects 0.5% TCS and 0.1% TDS; both are credits, not costs, as long as you accept and claim them.
