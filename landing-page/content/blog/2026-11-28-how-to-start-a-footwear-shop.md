Rakesh has sold shoes in someone else's shop for nine years. He knows which sizes walk out on a Sunday and which chappals come back with a broken strap. Now a 300 sq ft shop near the bus stand is on offer, and he wants to know what it takes to run his own.

Most pages ranking for this topic are generic checklists or pitches for an online store. But a footwear shop is a size business: every design you buy is really six products, one per size, and the money is made or lost on that spread. This guide covers the budget, sourcing, the two rules that changed recently (per-pair GST and the BIS quality control orders), size planning, a worked margin example and dead stock.

## Quick answer

- **Rough investment:** about **₹6.5 lakh to ₹15 lakh** for a 250-400 sq ft multi-brand shop in a tier-2 town, by our estimate. Opening stock is the biggest line.
- **Where to buy:** **Agra** and **Kanpur** (leather and formal), **Kolkata** (leather and mass footwear), **Kerala** and **Bahadurgarh** (PU, EVA and sports styles), plus **brand distributors**.
- **GST since 22 September 2025:** **5%** on footwear with a sale value up to **₹2,500 per pair**, **18%** above it. The rate goes by each pair's price, not the bill total.
- **BIS:** the 2024 footwear Quality Control Orders require most footwear to carry the BIS Standard Mark. Declared old stock without it can be sold until **31 July 2027** (extended in June 2026). Buy marked stock.
- **Licences:** Shop and Establishment registration, a trade licence where your city needs one, and GST once turnover crosses the limit or if you sell to other states.
- **Margins:** roughly **35% to 50% of the net selling price** on unbranded stock, lower on big brands, by our estimate, before broken sizes and sales pull it down.

## Step 1: Pick the kind of footwear shop

| Model | What you sell | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Family multi-brand** (300-500 sq ft) | Men's, women's and kids' casuals, chappals, school shoes | Too many categories for the money; thin size runs |
| **Chappal and sandal shop** (150-250 sq ft) | Hawai chappals, PU and EVA sandals, slippers | Low ticket size, needs high footfall |
| **Leather and formal** | Office shoes, leather sandals, belts as add-ons | Slow outside wedding and office-buying seasons |
| **Sports and sneakers** | Running shoes, casual sneakers | Brand terms; many pairs cross ₹2,500 and carry 18% GST |
| **Single-brand franchise** | One company's range | Deposit, fixed interiors, the brand's margin and return rules |

**Takeaway:** a shop that always has size 9 in its 30 best men's styles will outsell one with 150 styles and constant "size nahi hai".

## Step 3: Source from the right cluster

India's footwear is made in a handful of clusters, and each is strong in different things.

| Cluster | Known for |
| --- | --- |
| **Agra** (Uttar Pradesh) | Leather shoes and sandals, formal and casual; the Hing Ki Mandi area is a large wholesale market for footwear |
| **Kanpur** (Uttar Pradesh) | Leather footwear and leather goods, including heavier and safety shoes |
| **Kolkata** (West Bengal) | A long-standing leather and footwear centre for eastern India, from leather shoes to mass-market styles |
| **Kerala** (Kozhikode region) | PU, EVA and rubber sandals and chappals in large volumes |
| **Bahadurgarh and Delhi NCR** | Non-leather and sports-style footwear, PU and EVA |
| **Chennai, Ambur and Vaniyambadi** (Tamil Nadu) | Leather footwear, much of it export-grade |
| **Brand distributors** in your nearest city | Branded ranges on the brand's margin and terms |

**How to buy on the first trip:**

- **Buy by size run.** Wholesalers usually sell a design as a "set" covering a size range. Check what the set contains before agreeing a price per pair.
- **Start shallow across designs, deep across sizes,** then reorder what sells.
- **Mix price points.** In a tier-2 town most pairs sell under ₹1,500.
- **Ask about exchange of slow designs or broken sizes,** and get it in writing.
- **Check the BIS mark and invoice** on every lot (Step 5), and inspect sole bonding and stitching.

Brand distributors bring names customers trust and sometimes 15 to 45 days' credit, but the brand sets your margin, usually thinner than on unbranded stock. Most shops mix the two: brands to bring people in, regional labels to make the margin.

## Step 5: GST on footwear and the BIS mark

### GST: 5% or 18%, decided pair by pair

From **22 September 2025**, footwear with a **sale value not exceeding ₹2,500 per pair** is taxed at **5%**, and footwear above ₹2,500 per pair at **18%** (Notification 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate)). Before that, only pairs up to ₹1,000 got the lower rate. Footwear sits under HSN headings 6401 to 6405; see [what is an HSN code](https://billzap.app/blog/what-is-hsn-code) for how to pick yours.

| Pair's sale value (before GST) | GST rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹2,500 | 5% (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) |
| Above ₹2,500 | 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST) |

Three things that trip up shoe shops:

1. **Your selling price decides the rate, not your supplier's.** A pair bought at 18% but sold for ₹2,200 after a discount shown on the bill is 5%.
2. **Tag prices that include GST.** A ₹2,625 tag is ₹2,500 before tax at 5%, so it stays at 5%. A tag above ₹2,950 is above ₹2,500 even at 18%. Tags between about ₹2,626 and ₹2,950 give no consistent answer at either rate, so price outside that band or ask your CA.
3. **Credit can pile up.** Buy premium pairs at 18% and clear them below ₹2,500 at 5%, and your input credit can exceed your output tax. Ask your CA before the season-end sale.

### BIS: buy marked stock

Two Quality Control Orders make the BIS Standard Mark (with a licence number) compulsory for most footwear: the **Footwear made from Leather and other Materials (Quality Control) Order, 2024** and the **Footwear made from All Rubber and all Polymeric Material and its Components (Quality Control) Order, 2024**, in force from **1 August 2024**. They cover everyday items such as sandals and slippers (IS 6721), hawai chappals (IS 10702), sports footwear (IS 15844) and general-purpose shoes (IS 17043).

The date that matters now is the **legacy stock deadline**. Footwear made before the orders applied, and declared to BIS by its maker or importer, could be sold without the mark until 31 July 2026. On **12 June 2026** DPIIT amended both orders (S.O. 3037(E) and S.O. 3038(E)) and **extended this to 31 July 2027**. Reports differ on whether the extension covers every maker or mainly small non-leather units, so treat 31 July 2027 as the outer limit.

What it means for a new shop:

- **Buy only marked footwear.** Look for the BIS mark and licence number (CM/L-) on the pair or box, and verify it in the **BIS Care** app.
- **Do not buy cheap unmarked "old stock".** After the deadline only certified footwear can be sold; unmarked pairs left with you become a loss.
- **Keep purchase bills** naming the manufacturer. If a supplier says a product is exempt, ask to see the clause.

## Step 6: Plan sizes before you plan designs

Size planning is the biggest skill in a shoe shop. Nobody buys a size that does not fit, so a design with only sizes 6, 7 and 11 left is already dead stock.

**An example size run for 12 pairs of one design (UK sizes):**

| Men's size | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Women's size | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | |
| Pairs | 1 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 12 |

This spread is an illustration, not data. Write down every size you sell for the first eight weeks and reshape your next order around your town's real curve. Kids' footwear needs the widest spread, since children change size every few months.

Three rules that keep the run whole:

- **Reorder sizes, not designs.** When size 8 and 9 of a good design sell out, reorder those two sizes at once instead of a full new set.
- **Count by size every week** for your top 20 designs. Our guide to [inventory management techniques](https://billzap.app/blog/inventory-management-techniques) covers ABC grouping and reorder levels.
- **Track walk-outs.** A notebook line saying "asked for size 10 black formal, didn't have" is your best buying signal.

## Step 7: A worked margin example

Take one men's casual design bought from an Agra wholesaler at **₹620 per pair plus 5% GST** (₹651 paid; ₹31 comes back as input credit for a regular GST shop). You price it at **₹1,299 including 5% GST**.

| Per pair at full price | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selling price incl. GST | | ₹1,299.00 |
| Net of GST | ₹1,299 ÷ 1.05 | ₹1,237.14 |
| GST to pay | ₹1,299 − ₹1,237.14 | ₹61.86 |
| Cost (net of credit) | | ₹620.00 |
| Gross profit | ₹1,237.14 − ₹620 | ₹617.14 |
| Margin on net sale | ₹617.14 ÷ ₹1,237.14 | 49.9% |

That 49.9% is the number on paper. Now follow one set of 12 pairs through a season: 9 sell at full price, 2 go in the end-of-season sale at ₹909 (30% off), and 1 is an odd size you clear at ₹399.

| For the whole set of 12 | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 9 at ₹1,299 | 9 × ₹1,237.14 | ₹11,134.29 |
| 2 at ₹909 | 2 × (₹909 ÷ 1.05 = ₹865.71) | ₹1,731.43 |
| 1 at ₹399 | ₹399 ÷ 1.05 | ₹380.00 |
| **Net sales** | | **₹13,245.71** |
| Cost of 12 pairs | 12 × ₹620 | ₹7,440.00 |
| **Gross profit** | | **₹5,805.71** |
| **Real margin** | ₹5,805.71 ÷ ₹13,245.71 | **43.8%** |

Three pairs out of twelve cost six percentage points of margin, more than you would gain by squeezing ₹20 off the purchase price. The [profit margin formula](https://billzap.app/blog/profit-margin-formula) guide explains margin versus markup.

## Step 8: Stop dead stock before it starts

Unsold footwear ages: PU soles can crumble in storage, glue weakens in damp, and colours date.

| Stock age | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Under 90 days | Full price; reorder missing sizes of designs that sell |
| 90 to 180 days | Move to the front, pair with a small offer, ask the distributor about an exchange |
| 180 days to a year | Clear in a planned sale at 25% to 40% off |
| Over a year | Sell at cost or below; the cash is worth more than the shelf |

**Date every lot** on arrival, put broken sizes on one "last pairs" rack at a fixed price, plan around school reopening, weddings, festivals and monsoon (when rubber footwear sells and leather does not), and do not reorder a design until its current set is half sold.

## FAQ

### How much investment is needed to start a footwear shop?

For a 250-400 sq ft multi-brand shop in a tier-2 town, roughly ₹6.5 lakh to ₹15 lakh by our estimate, mostly opening stock. A chappal shop can start for less; a brand franchise usually needs more.

### What is the GST rate on footwear in 2026?

5% on footwear with a sale value up to ₹2,500 per pair and 18% above ₹2,500, since 22 September 2025. The rate is decided per pair on its selling price, so one bill can carry both rates.

### Can I sell footwear without the BIS mark?

Most footwear must now carry the BIS Standard Mark. Declared old stock without it can be sold until 31 July 2027 after the June 2026 extension. A new shop should buy only marked footwear.

### What is the profit margin in a shoe shop?

Roughly 35% to 50% of the net selling price on unbranded footwear, less on big brands, by our estimate. After sales and broken sizes, plan on a blended 35% to 40%.

### Do I need GST registration for a shoe shop?

Only once turnover crosses ₹40 lakh a year for goods in most states (₹20 lakh in some), or if you sell to other states. Below that it is optional.

## The bottom line

A footwear shop is won on sizes, not styles. Pick a narrow model, budget for deep size runs and three months of costs, buy BIS-marked stock, and price with the ₹2,500-per-pair GST line in mind. Then track every size you sell, reorder sizes rather than designs, and clear old stock before it ages.
