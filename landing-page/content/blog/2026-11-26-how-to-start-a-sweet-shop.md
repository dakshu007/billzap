A sweet shop earns a large part of its year in a few weeks: Diwali, Raksha Bandhan, Holi, Eid and the wedding season. Many look busy and still struggle. They price kaju katli by looking at the shop next door, throw away milk sweets every second evening, and learn about FSSAI's best before rule when a food safety officer walks in.

This guide covers format, licences and labelling, GST after the September 2025 rate changes, a budget, two sweets costed per kilo, festival planning, wastage and the counter.

## Quick answer

- **Pick a format:** a **counter that buys from a karigar or wholesaler** (lowest cost), a **shop with its own kitchen** (the usual model), or a **sweet shop with snacks and seating** (adds restaurant service).
- **FSSAI:** since 1 April 2026, **basic registration** (₹100 a year) covers turnover up to **₹1.5 crore**, but a shop that makes its own sweets must also stay within the small-manufacturer **production capacity** limit. Above either, you need a **State licence**.
- **Best before on every tray:** loose sweets on display must show a **"best before" date** on the tray or container, a rule in force since **1 October 2020**. The date of manufacture is optional.
- **GST since 22 September 2025:** mithai **5%** (unchanged), namkeen and bhujia **5%** (down from 12%), cakes, chocolates and ice cream **5%** (down from 18%). Dine-in service is **5% without input tax credit**.
- **GST registration:** above **₹40 lakh** turnover for a shop selling only goods in most states, **₹20 lakh** if you also serve food at tables.
- **Rough investment:** ₹3 lakh to ₹6 lakh for a counter without a kitchen, ₹8 lakh to ₹18 lakh for a small shop with one.
- **Margins:** kaju katli earns more per kilo but a lower percentage than besan ladoo. Cost every item.

These are broad 2026 estimates for a tier-2 city. In a shop with a kitchen, your karigar is your product: if you are not a halwai yourself, agree recipes and a notice period in writing.

## Step 2: Get the licences and the labelling right

### FSSAI: which tier

| Tier (from 1 April 2026) | Annual turnover | Fee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic Registration (Form A) | Up to ₹1.5 crore | ₹100 a year |
| State Licence (Form B) | Above ₹1.5 crore up to ₹50 crore | Set by kind of business |
| Central Licence | Above ₹50 crore | Higher |

A shop that makes its own sweets is a **manufacturer**, so turnover is not the only test. To stay on basic registration, daily production must stay within the capacity limit FoSCoS shows for your kind of business, around **100 kg a day** for most foods, with separate limits where you handle milk. Registrations issued from April 2026 have no renewal, though the annual fee still applies. The full process is in our [FSSAI registration](https://billzap.app/blog/fssai-registration) guide.

### The best before rule for loose sweets

FSSAI's order of **25 September 2020**, in force from **1 October 2020**, requires that for loose, non-packaged sweets, **the tray or container holding them at the outlet must display a "best before" date**.

- **You decide the date**, based on the sweet and local conditions. A rasgulla or kalakand lasts a day or two; a besan ladoo or soan papdi much longer.
- **The date of manufacture is voluntary.**
- **It applies to every tray**, chilled ones included. A tag clipped to each tray, changed when the tray is refilled, is the simplest system.

Never sell past the date, or mix old stock into a fresh tray.

### Other licences and checks

- **Shop act registration** under your state's Shops and Establishments Act, usually within 30 days of opening.
- **Municipal trade or health licence** for food premises, which most cities require.
- **Fire NOC** in many cities for a kitchen with a bhatti and LPG cylinders, and pollution board consent in some states for wood or coal bhattis. Ask your municipal office before signing the lease.
- **Stamped scales**, verified by your state's legal metrology department, and **weigh sweets net of the box**: put the empty box on the scale and press tare first. Charging sweet prices on the box's weight is short weight.
- **FSSAI number on your bills**: the 14-digit number must be printed on bills and cash memos.

## Step 3: Know your GST rates

The GST rate changes of **22 September 2025** cut the rate on most things a sweet shop sells beside mithai. Many guides still show the old rates.

| Item | Before 22 Sep 2025 | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mithai, sweetmeats (barfi, ladoo, peda, rasgulla, halwa) | 5% | **5%** |
| Namkeen, bhujia, mixtures | 12% | **5%** |
| Cakes, pastries, biscuits, cookies | 18% | **5%** |
| Chocolates | 18% | **5%** |
| Ice cream | 18% | **5%** |
| Aerated soft drinks | 28% + cess | **40%** |
| Samosa, chaat, meals served at your tables | 5% no ITC | **5% no ITC** |

**Counter sale versus dine-in.** A box of sweets sold over the counter is a **sale of goods** at 5%, and a regular GST shop can claim input tax credit on its dry fruits, sugar, boxes and equipment. Food served at your tables is **restaurant service**, also 5%, but without input tax credit. If you do both, ask your CA how to split the credit; our guide to [GST on restaurant food](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-on-restaurant-food) covers that side.

**When to register.** A shop selling only goods within the state registers once aggregate turnover crosses **₹40 lakh** in most states; add table service and the limit is **₹20 lakh**. Our [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) guide has the full table. A shop comfortably under the line in August can cross it in October, and registration is due within 30 days of crossing.

**Composition.** With turnover up to ₹1.5 crore, a sweet shop can pay **1% of turnover** as a manufacturer (or **5%** if run as a restaurant) from its own pocket, with no GST on the bill and no input tax credit. Corporate customers often want a tax invoice for festival orders, so compare both; our [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide has a worked comparison.

## Step 6: Plan for festivals and weddings

Diwali 2026 has just gone. **Diwali 2027 falls on 29 October 2027** in most calendars (check your local panchang), so planning starts around August. The **wedding season** runs through the winter, with muhurat dates that vary by region and year.

- **Pre-book dry fruits, ghee and boxes 6 to 8 weeks ahead.** Prices rise into the festival: before Diwali 2026, pistachio and almond prices rose sharply and cashew rose too. Printed boxes take weeks.
- **Make long-life sweets first** (kaju katli, soan papdi, ladoo, namkeen) and milk sweets in the last two or three days.
- **Take pre-orders with an advance.** Corporate and wedding orders need a written quantity, rate, delivery date and advance, with the balance billed on delivery.
- **Cap what you accept.** Your kitchen has a daily limit, and so does FSSAI's capacity test. Turning away the last orders is cheaper than spoiled batches.
- **Watch your turnover**, because the festival peak is when shops cross the GST limit.

## Step 7: Control wastage

Wastage is where sweet shops lose their margin, mostly in milk sweets.

- **Sort the counter by shelf life.** Chhena and milk sweets (rasgulla, rasmalai, kalakand) last a day or two and need the chilled counter. Khoya sweets last a few days; besan, dry-fruit sweets and namkeen much longer.
- **Make milk sweets to yesterday's sales**, not to fill the tray. Keep a daily sheet of made, sold and thrown away for each item; after two weeks you know your real numbers.
- **Never recycle** unsold milk sweets into another sweet. It is a food safety risk, and with the best before rule a legal one too.

## Step 8: Staff, counters and packaging

**Staff.** A small shop with a kitchen usually runs with a **karigar**, one or two **kitchen helpers** and one or two people at the **counter**, plus festival helpers hired early. Counter staff must know every item's price per kilo, its best before date and how to tare a box. Keep the kitchen to FSSAI's hygiene requirements: clean water, covered storage, head covering and clean clothing, and pest control.

**Counters.** A **chilled counter** for milk and chhena sweets and an **ambient glass counter** for dry sweets and namkeen, each tray with a price per kg and a best before tag.

**Packaging.** Use food-grade boxes in standard sizes (250 g, 500 g, 1 kg) with butter paper; boxes printed with your name and phone number bring repeat orders. Sweets packed in front of the customer are loose sales. Boxes packed in advance and displayed sealed are **pre-packaged** goods, which bring in FSSAI labelling and legal metrology declarations such as net quantity, MRP and dates, so get festival pre-pack labels checked.

## FAQ

### Which FSSAI licence does a sweet shop need?

Basic registration (₹100 a year) if annual turnover is up to ₹1.5 crore and, for a shop making its own sweets, daily production stays within the small-manufacturer capacity limit on FoSCoS. Above either, a State licence.

### Is it compulsory to show a best before date on sweets?

Yes, for loose sweets on display. Since 1 October 2020, FSSAI requires the tray or container holding them to show a best before date, decided by the shop. The date of manufacture is optional.

### What is the GST rate on sweets in 2026?

5% on mithai, unchanged by the September 2025 reform. Namkeen is also 5% (down from 12%), and cakes, chocolates and ice cream 5% (down from 18%). Food served at your tables is restaurant service at 5% without input tax credit.

### How much does it cost to open a sweet shop?

About ₹8 lakh to ₹18 lakh for a small shop with its own kitchen in a tier-2 city in 2026, and ₹3 lakh to ₹6 lakh for a counter that buys in. Rent deposits, counters and festival stock are the big items.

### What is the profit margin in a sweet shop?

It differs by item. In the worked examples, kaju katli at ₹1,200 a kg earns about 27% after all costs, and besan ladoo at ₹600 a kg about 34%. Milk sweets can be profitable but carry the most wastage.

## The bottom line

A sweet shop is a manufacturing business with a festival calendar. Get **FSSAI** in the right tier, put a **best before tag on every tray**, charge **5% GST** on mithai and namkeen once registered, and watch the **₹40 lakh** line through October. Cost every sweet per kilo, plan Diwali from August, and write down what you throw away each day.
