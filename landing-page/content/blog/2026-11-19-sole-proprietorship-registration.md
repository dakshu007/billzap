Most people who search for "sole proprietorship registration" expect a form, a fee and a certificate, the way a company gets incorporated. There is no such thing. India has no law that registers a sole proprietorship as such. A proprietorship is simply **you, doing business under a name**, and the "registration" is the set of licences and tax registrations that prove the business exists.

That is good news, because it makes a proprietorship the cheapest and quickest way to start. It also causes the most common problem new owners hit: the bank asks for "two proofs of the firm" to open a current account, and they have none. This guide puts the registrations in the right order, shows exactly what banks ask for, explains how a proprietor is taxed after the Income-tax Act 2025, and compares the proprietorship with a partnership, LLP and company.

## Quick answer

- **There is no single sole proprietorship registration.** The business gets its legal identity from registrations in its name: **Udyam, GST, shop and establishment, trade licence** and others your trade needs.
- **The proprietor's own PAN is the business PAN.** There is no separate PAN for a proprietorship.
- **Fastest start:** Udyam registration (free, online, same day) plus shop and establishment registration in your state.
- **Current account:** banks follow RBI's KYC rules: the proprietor's own KYC **plus any two documents in the firm's name** as proof of business, such as Udyam, shop act, GST certificate or utility bills. A bank may accept one with an extra on-site check.
- **Tax:** business profit is added to the proprietor's own income and taxed at **individual slab rates**. Small businesses can use **presumptive taxation** (6% or 8% of turnover, now **Section 58** of the Income-tax Act 2025, formerly 44AD) and file **ITR-4**.
- **Liability is unlimited.** Your personal assets are on the line for business debts. That is the main reason to switch to an LLP or company later.

## What a sole proprietorship is, in law

A sole proprietorship is a business owned and run by one person, with no separate legal existence:

- **You and the business are the same person** for tax and law. The firm cannot own property, sue or be sued apart from you.
- **The trade name is just a name.** "Sharma Stationers" is Ravi Sharma doing business. Contracts, licences and bank accounts are "Ravi Sharma, proprietor, Sharma Stationers".
- **Profit is your income.** There is no separate tax return for the firm.
- **Liability is unlimited.** If the business owes money it cannot pay, creditors can go after your house, savings and other assets.
- **It ends with you.** It cannot be sold as an entity or passed on as a going concern without a transfer of its assets.

## The registrations that create your proprietorship

Pick the ones your business actually needs. Each one adds a document in the firm's name.

| Registration | Who needs it | Cost | Counts as bank proof? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Udyam** (MSME) | Optional for all; useful for most | Free | Yes |
| **Shop and establishment** | Shops, offices, commercial premises in most states | State fee, often small | Yes |
| **GST** | Above the turnover limit, inter-state sellers, online sellers; voluntary otherwise | No government fee | Yes |
| **Trade licence** | Where your municipal body requires it, depending on trade | Municipal fee | Yes (as a government licence) |
| **FSSAI** | Any food business | From ₹100 a year for basic registration | Yes (as a government licence) |
| **Professional tax** | In states that levy it | State-set | Yes |
| **Import Export Code (IEC)** | Importers and exporters | Small DGFT fee | Yes |

## How to register a proprietorship firm, step by step

1. **Choose a trade name.** Check it is not the same as a well-known brand or registered trademark in your line of business. You can search the trademark registry online.
2. **Get your premises sorted.** A rent agreement or ownership proof for the shop, and ideally an electricity bill you can later move into the firm's name.
3. **Register on Udyam** with your Aadhaar and PAN. Enter the trade name as you want it on everything else.
4. **Apply for shop and establishment registration** (and a trade licence if your city needs one), using the same trade name and address.
5. **Register for GST** if you need to, or voluntarily if your buyers want GST bills. Your GSTIN is built on your personal PAN, with the trade name shown on it.
6. **Open a current account** in the firm's name with two of these documents.
7. **Set up billing and records** from day one: numbered bills, a cash book or app, and receipts for expenses.

Keep the trade name and address **identical** across every registration; mismatches are the commonest reason banks reject documents.

## Opening a proprietorship current account

Banks follow the RBI's Master Direction on KYC. For a sole proprietary firm, the bank does KYC on **you** (Aadhaar or other identity proof, PAN, photograph) and also asks for **any two** of these documents, in the **name of the firm**, as proof of business:

1. Registration certificate issued by the government, including the **Udyam registration certificate**
2. Certificate or licence under the **Shop and Establishment Act** from the municipal or state authority
3. **Sales and income tax returns**
4. **GST** (or older CST/VAT) **certificate**
5. Certificate or registration from sales tax, service tax or **professional tax** authorities
6. **IEC** from DGFT, or a licence or certificate of practice from a statutory professional body
7. The proprietor's **complete income tax return** (not just the acknowledgement) showing the firm's income, acknowledged by the Income Tax Department
8. **Utility bills** such as electricity, water or landline in the firm's name

If you genuinely cannot produce two, the RBI allows the bank, at its discretion, to accept **one** document, provided it carries out **contact point verification** (a visit to the business) and collects other information to confirm the firm exists. Many banks also ask for a rent agreement or other address proof under their own policy.

**Combinations that usually work for a new shop:**

| Situation | Two proofs |
| --- | --- |
| New shop, not on GST | Udyam + shop act registration |
| GST-registered shop | GST certificate + Udyam (or shop act) |
| Home-based business | Udyam + professional tax registration, or Udyam + GST |
| Existing business, filing returns | Udyam + complete ITR showing business income |

## How a proprietorship is taxed

### GST

GST registration is in the proprietor's PAN and the firm's trade name. One PAN gets one GSTIN per state, so if you run two proprietorships in the same state, they share a GSTIN and you add the second as another place of business.

### Income tax

The proprietor files one return as an **individual**. Business profit is added to any other income (rent, interest, salary) and taxed at slab rates. The **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced the 1961 Act from **1 April 2026**. It renumbered the sections but kept the main rules for small businesses.

**New regime slabs for tax year 2026-27** (unchanged by Budget 2026):

| Income | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹4 lakh | Nil |
| ₹4 lakh to ₹8 lakh | 5% |
| ₹8 lakh to ₹12 lakh | 10% |
| ₹12 lakh to ₹16 lakh | 15% |
| ₹16 lakh to ₹20 lakh | 20% |
| ₹20 lakh to ₹24 lakh | 25% |
| Above ₹24 lakh | 30% |

A rebate makes the tax nil for a resident individual whose total taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh under the new regime, and 4% health and education cess applies on any tax payable.

**Presumptive taxation (Section 58, formerly 44AD).** If your turnover is up to **₹2 crore** (or **₹3 crore** where cash receipts are no more than 5% of turnover), you can declare profit at **6% of turnover received digitally** (bank, UPI, cheque) and **8% of the rest**, without keeping full books or getting an audit. You file **ITR-4**; our [ITR-4 for business](https://billzap.app/blog/itr-4-for-business) guide covers the form. You can declare a higher profit if you actually earn more.

### Worked examples

**Example 1: small kirana.** Turnover ₹60 lakh, of which ₹48 lakh by UPI and bank and ₹12 lakh in cash.

| Part | Working | Presumptive profit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digital receipts | 6% × ₹48,00,000 | ₹2,88,000 |
| Cash receipts | 8% × ₹12,00,000 | ₹96,000 |
| **Total** | | **₹3,84,000** |

Income is under ₹4 lakh, so tax is **nil**.

**Example 2: larger trader on presumptive basis.** Turnover ₹1.5 crore, all digital. Profit = 6% × ₹1,50,00,000 = **₹9,00,000**. Slab tax: 5% of ₹4 lakh (₹20,000) + 10% of ₹1 lakh (₹10,000) = ₹30,000, but income is within ₹12 lakh, so the rebate takes it to **nil**.

**Example 3: profitable shop on actual books.** Actual profit ₹18 lakh, no other income.

| Slab | Tax |
| --- | --- |
| ₹4 lakh to ₹8 lakh at 5% | ₹20,000 |
| ₹8 lakh to ₹12 lakh at 10% | ₹40,000 |
| ₹12 lakh to ₹16 lakh at 15% | ₹60,000 |
| ₹16 lakh to ₹18 lakh at 20% | ₹40,000 |
| Tax | ₹1,60,000 |
| Cess at 4% | ₹6,400 |
| **Total** | **₹1,66,400** |

Proprietors with tax payable above the advance tax threshold pay it in instalments; presumptive taxpayers can pay the whole amount by 15 March. Confirm the current section numbers, the books-of-account rules (now Section 62) and the audit limits (now Section 63) with your CA.

## Proprietorship vs partnership vs LLP vs company

| | Sole proprietorship | Partnership firm | LLP | Private limited company |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owners | One | 2 or more | 2 or more designated partners | At least 2 directors and 2 shareholders (one in an OPC) |
| Governing law | None specific | Indian Partnership Act, 1932 | LLP Act, 2008 | Companies Act, 2013 |
| Registration | Through Udyam, GST, shop act | Deed; registration with the state Registrar of Firms optional but advisable | Incorporation with MCA | Incorporation with MCA |
| Separate legal entity | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Liability | Unlimited, personal | Unlimited, joint and several | Limited to contribution | Limited to shares |
| Income tax | Proprietor's slab rates | Flat 30% on firm profit (plus surcharge and cess where applicable); partners' remuneration and interest deductible within limits | Same as partnership | Corporate rates |
| Annual compliance | Owner's ITR, GST returns if registered | Firm's ITR, GST | ITR, annual MCA filings (Form 8 and Form 11), audit above limits | ITR, audit, board meetings, ROC filings |
| Setup cost and time | Lowest, days | Low | Moderate | Highest |

Our guide to [partnership firm registration](https://billzap.app/blog/partnership-firm-registration) covers the deed, stamp duty and Registrar of Firms process if you are bringing in a partner.

**When a proprietorship is the right choice:** one owner, modest risk, profits within the lower slabs, and a business that sells mostly to retail customers.

**When to consider switching:**

- You are taking on **large loans, contracts or liabilities** and want to protect personal assets.
- You are bringing in a **partner or investor**.
- Your **profit is high enough** that the top slab of 30% plus surcharge exceeds what an LLP or company would pay after remuneration.
- Large customers or tenders prefer, or require, a **registered entity**.

## Converting a proprietorship later

A proprietorship cannot be converted into an LLP or company in one filing the way a partnership firm can be converted into an LLP. In practice you:

1. **Form the new entity** (LLP or company).
2. **Transfer the business** to it under a business transfer agreement: stock, fixed assets, receivables, contracts and staff.
3. **Take a new GSTIN** in the new entity's PAN. Unused input tax credit can be transferred to the new entity by filing **Form GST ITC-02** on a transfer of business. Then cancel the old registration.
4. **Update bank accounts, licences, Udyam and supplier and customer records.**

A transfer of a proprietorship to a company can be tax-neutral if conditions on shareholding and continuity are met; these conditions carried into the 2025 Act under new numbering, so plan it with a CA before you move assets.

## FAQ

### How do I register a sole proprietorship in India?

There is no single registration. Get Udyam (free, online) and shop and establishment registration in the trade name, add GST and a trade licence if your business needs them, and open a current account using two of these as proof of business.

### What documents are needed for a proprietorship firm?

The proprietor's PAN, Aadhaar, photograph and address proof, premises proof such as a rent agreement, and then registrations in the firm's name: Udyam, shop act, GST or others. A bank needs any two firm documents for a current account.

### Can I open a current account for a proprietorship without GST?

Yes. GST is only one of the accepted proofs. Udyam plus shop and establishment registration is a common pair for a business that is not on GST.

### How is a sole proprietorship taxed?

Business profit is added to the proprietor's income and taxed at individual slab rates. Small businesses can declare 6% or 8% of turnover under presumptive taxation (Section 58 of the Income-tax Act 2025, formerly 44AD) and file ITR-4.

### Does a proprietorship need a separate PAN?

No. The proprietor's personal PAN is used for the business, including for GST registration and income tax.

### Is a proprietor personally liable for business debts?

Yes. Liability is unlimited, so personal assets can be used to pay business debts. An LLP or private limited company limits liability.

### Can one person own two proprietorships?

Yes, under the same PAN. In one state they share a single GSTIN, with the second business added as an additional place of business.

## The bottom line

A sole proprietorship needs no incorporation: you build its identity with Udyam, shop act registration, GST when needed and any licences your trade requires, using the same trade name and address everywhere. Two of those documents open a current account. You pay tax as an individual, with presumptive taxation available to most small shops. When the risks or profits grow, form an LLP or company and transfer the business to it.
