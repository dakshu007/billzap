A bakery is one of the few food businesses you can start in your own kitchen and grow into a shop. That is also why so many fail quietly: the cakes are good, but nobody priced them properly, the fresh cream stock was thrown away every second evening, and the owner found out about licences when a food app asked for them.

This guide takes the business side in order: which kind of bakery to start, the licences, the GST rates that changed in September 2025, a rough budget, a cake costed line by line, and the wastage habits that separate a profitable bakery from a busy one.

## Quick answer

- **Pick a model first:** a **home bakery** (custom cakes and cookies to order, lowest cost), a **takeaway bakery shop** (bread, buns, puffs, pastries, cakes off the counter) or a **cafe-bakery** (seating, coffee, dine-in).
- **FSSAI is compulsory** for all three. Since 1 April 2026, **basic registration** (₹100 a year, Form A on FoSCoS) covers turnover up to **₹1.5 crore**, as long as your production stays within the small-manufacturer capacity limit.
- **Shop act registration and a municipal trade or health licence** apply to a shop or cafe. A home bakery usually needs only FSSAI to start; check your city's rules.
- **GST:** cakes, pastries, biscuits and cookies are **5%** since 22 September 2025 (they were 18%). Plain bread, pizza bread and khakhra are **nil**. Dine-in service in a cafe-bakery is **5% without input tax credit**.
- **GST registration** is needed once goods turnover crosses **₹40 lakh** in most states (₹20 lakh if you also supply services such as dine-in).
- **Rough investment:** ₹50,000 to ₹2 lakh for a home bakery, ₹6 lakh to ₹15 lakh for a small takeaway shop, ₹15 lakh and up for a cafe-bakery.
- **Margins:** aim to keep ingredients and packaging near **30 to 35%** of the selling price; wastage, rent and staff decide what is left.

## Step 1: Choose your bakery model

| | Home bakery | Takeaway bakery shop | Cafe-bakery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What you sell | Custom cakes, cupcakes, cookies, brownies, to order | Bread, buns, puffs, pastries, cakes, cookies, rusk | Everything a shop sells, plus coffee, sandwiches, seating |
| Space | Your kitchen | 150 to 400 sq ft with a baking area | 500 sq ft and up |
| Staff | You, maybe a helper | 1 to 3 | 4 and up |
| Licences | FSSAI | FSSAI, shop act, municipal trade/health licence | Same, plus fire and signage approvals in many cities |
| GST character | Sale of goods | Sale of goods | Goods at the counter, restaurant service at the table |
| Wastage risk | Low (made to order) | High (daily display stock) | High |
| Rough setup cost | ₹50,000 to ₹2 lakh | ₹6 to ₹15 lakh | ₹15 lakh and up |

The setup figures are broad estimates for a small operation in 2026 and swing widely with city, rent deposit and how much equipment you buy second-hand. Get three quotes for every big item.

**Start small if you can.** A home bakery lets you test recipes, prices and demand before you sign a lease.

## Step 2: Get your licences in the right order

### FSSAI: which tier

Every food business needs FSSAI, including a home baker selling to neighbours. The rules changed on **1 April 2026**:

| Tier | Annual turnover | Fee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic Registration (Form A) | Up to ₹1.5 crore | ₹100 a year |
| State Licence (Form B) | Above ₹1.5 crore up to ₹50 crore | Set by kind of business |
| Central Licence | Above ₹50 crore | Higher |

A bakery **manufactures** food, so there is a second test: production capacity. To stay on basic registration, your daily output must stay within the capacity limit FoSCoS shows for your kind of business (around **100 kg a day** for most foods). A home bakery or a small shop baking a few dozen kilos a day is comfortably inside it. Registrations issued from April 2026 have no renewal, though the annual fee still applies. The full process is in our [FSSAI registration](https://billzap.app/blog/fssai-registration) guide.

Two FSSAI rules that bakeries miss:

- **The 14-digit FSSAI number must be printed on your bills and cash memos.**
- **Loose sweets displayed in trays must show a "best before" date**, under an FSSAI order in force since 1 October 2020. If you sell mithai beside your cakes, label the trays.

### Shop act and municipal licences

A bakery shop or cafe needs registration under your state's **Shops and Establishments Act** (Gumasta in some states), usually within 30 days of opening. See our guide to [shop act registration](https://billzap.app/blog/shop-act-registration). Most cities also want a **trade licence or health licence** from the municipal corporation for food businesses, and some ask for a **fire NOC** for premises with commercial ovens and LPG cylinders. Rules differ by city, so check with your municipal office before you sign the lease.

A **home bakery** usually needs only FSSAI to start. If you put up a board, take walk-in customers or hire staff, your municipality or housing society may expect more.

### GST registration: when it becomes compulsory

| Your bakery | Registration needed above |
| --- | --- |
| Sells only goods (cakes, bread, cookies), within the state | ₹40 lakh a year (most states) |
| Also supplies services, such as dine-in or baking classes | ₹20 lakh a year |
| Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura | ₹20 lakh goods / ₹10 lakh services |

The ₹40 lakh limit is lost if you sell to another state. Our guide to the [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) has the full rules. Food apps and corporate clients may ask for a GSTIN earlier, so many bakeries register voluntarily.

## Step 3: Know your GST rates (they changed in 2025)

The GST rate reform of **22 September 2025** cut most bakery items. Many bakery guides still show 18% on cakes.

| Item | Before 22 Sep 2025 | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bread (plain, branded or not) | Nil | **Nil** |
| Pizza bread | 5% | **Nil** |
| Khakhra, plain chapati, roti, paratha | 5% to 18% | **Nil** |
| Rusk, toasted bread | 5% | **5%** |
| Cakes, pastries, biscuits, cookies | 18% | **5%** |
| Namkeen and bhujia | 12% | **5%** |
| Sweets (mithai) | 5% | **5%** |
| Chocolates | 18% | **5%** |
| Ice cream | 18% | **5%** |
| Aerated soft drinks | 28% + cess | **40%** |
| Dine-in service in a cafe-bakery | 5% no ITC | **5% no ITC** |

**Counter sale versus dine-in.** A cake or a box of cookies sold over the counter to take home is a **sale of goods** at 5%, and a regular GST bakery can claim input tax credit on its flour, butter, packaging and ovens. When a cafe-bakery serves food at its tables, that is **restaurant service** (SAC 996331), also at 5%, but **without input tax credit** on the inputs used. If you run both, ask your CA how to split the credit. The restaurant rules are explained in our guide to [GST on restaurant food](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-on-restaurant-food).

**Composition scheme.** A small bakery with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore can opt for composition: **1% of turnover** for a manufacturer or trader, **5%** for a restaurant, paid from your own pocket with no GST on the bill and no input tax credit. With bakery goods now at 5%, compare both before choosing; our [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide has a worked comparison.

## Step 4: Budget the setup

Rough items for a **small takeaway bakery shop** in a tier-2 city. These are indicative ranges, not quotes.

| Item | Rough cost |
| --- | --- |
| Rent deposit and first month's rent | ₹1 lakh to ₹3 lakh |
| Interiors, counter, shelves, signage | ₹1 lakh to ₹3 lakh |
| Commercial deck oven or convection oven | ₹1 lakh to ₹3 lakh |
| Planetary mixer, dough mixer, small tools, trays, moulds | ₹50,000 to ₹1.5 lakh |
| Display chiller and refrigerator | ₹60,000 to ₹1.5 lakh |
| Work tables, racks, sink, exhaust | ₹40,000 to ₹1 lakh |
| Opening stock of ingredients and packaging | ₹30,000 to ₹60,000 |
| Licences and registration | Under ₹10,000 if you apply yourself |
| Working capital for 2 to 3 months | ₹1 lakh to ₹2 lakh |
| **Total** | **About ₹6 lakh to ₹15 lakh** |

A **home bakery** needs a good oven (an OTG or a countertop convection oven), a stand or hand mixer, tins, a refrigerator with space to spare, packaging and the FSSAI fee: usually **₹50,000 to ₹2 lakh** in all, less if you already own the oven.

Keep working capital aside: the first two or three months rarely cover their own costs.

## Step 5: Cost every product before you price it

Most home bakers price by looking at what others charge. Do the arithmetic first. Here is a **1 kg chocolate truffle cake**, with illustrative ingredient prices you should replace with your own:

| Cost line | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maida | 250 g at ₹50/kg | ₹12.50 |
| Sugar | 250 g at ₹46/kg | ₹11.50 |
| Cocoa powder | 40 g at ₹800/kg | ₹32.00 |
| Eggs | 4 at ₹7 | ₹28.00 |
| Butter | 100 g at ₹550/kg | ₹55.00 |
| Dark compound chocolate | 200 g at ₹350/kg | ₹70.00 |
| Whipping cream | 200 ml at ₹300/litre | ₹60.00 |
| Baking powder, soda, essence | | ₹8.00 |
| **Ingredients** | | **₹277.00** |
| Cake box, base board, candle | | ₹40.00 |
| Gas or electricity | | ₹25.00 |
| Your labour | 1.5 hours at ₹100/hour | ₹150.00 |
| Share of rent and other overheads | | ₹60.00 |
| Wastage allowance | 5% of ingredients | ₹14.00 |
| **Total cost** | | **₹566.00** |

Check the ingredients: 12.50 + 11.50 + 32 + 28 + 55 + 70 + 60 + 8 = ₹277. Total: 277 + 40 + 25 + 150 + 60 + 14 = ₹566.

**Pricing it:**

- A common rule of thumb is to keep **ingredients at about 30% of price**: ₹277 ÷ 0.30 = **₹923**, so ₹900 to ₹950 is the floor.
- An unregistered home baker selling at **₹950** earns ₹950 − ₹566 = **₹384**, a margin of 384 ÷ 950 = **40.4%** after paying herself for her time.
- A GST-registered shop pricing the same cake at **₹999 including 5% GST** has a taxable value of ₹999 ÷ 1.05 = **₹951.43** and GST of ₹47.57 (₹23.79 CGST + ₹23.79 SGST, rounded). Its margin is almost the same as the home baker's.

Leaving out your own labour is the commonest costing mistake in home bakeries. Our guide to the [profit margin formula](https://billzap.app/blog/profit-margin-formula) shows how to work out margin and markup without mixing them up.

## Step 6: Plan for shelf life and wastage

Wastage is where a bakery shop loses its margin. Typical keeping times, which your recipe and storage will change:

| Product | Typical shelf life |
| --- | --- |
| Fresh cream cakes and pastries | 1 to 2 days, refrigerated |
| Bread and buns | 2 to 4 days |
| Puffs and savouries | Same day for best quality |
| Cookies and biscuits in airtight packs | Several weeks |
| Rusk | Several months |

Now look at what two unsold pastries do. A tray of 12 pastries costs ₹25 each to make (₹300) and sells at ₹70 each.

| | All 12 sold | 10 sold, 2 thrown away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales | 12 × ₹70 = ₹840 | 10 × ₹70 = ₹700 |
| Cost | ₹300 | ₹300 |
| Gross profit | **₹540** | **₹400** |

Two pastries in the bin cut that tray's profit by ₹140, about **26%**. Habits that help:

- **Bake fresh cream items in small batches** and push custom orders, which have almost no wastage.
- **Record what you bake and what you throw away** every evening, item by item. After two weeks you will know which items to bake less of.
- **Sell the day's leftovers before closing** at a small discount, or turn yesterday's bread into rusk or croutons where your recipe allows.
- **Label packaged items** with the date of manufacture and best before date, as FSSAI's labelling rules require for pre-packaged food.

## Step 7: Sell, bill and get found

- **Take advances on custom orders.** Half in advance for a celebration cake is normal and protects you from no-shows.
- **Get on Google Maps.** "Bakery near me" and "cake shop near me" are how most new customers find you. See our guide to [listing your shop on Google Maps](https://billzap.app/blog/list-shop-on-google-maps).
- **Show your work.** Photos of real cakes on WhatsApp status and Instagram sell custom orders better than any banner.
- **Bill every sale.** It gives you the sales figures you need to decide what to bake, and it is the record you need if you register for GST.

## FAQ

### Do I need an FSSAI licence for a home bakery?

Yes. Every food business needs FSSAI. A home bakery with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore needs only basic registration, which costs ₹100 a year and is applied for on FoSCoS with Form A.

### How much investment is needed to start a bakery?

Roughly ₹50,000 to ₹2 lakh for a home bakery, ₹6 lakh to ₹15 lakh for a small takeaway bakery shop and ₹15 lakh or more for a cafe-bakery. Rent deposit and the oven are usually the biggest items.

### What is the GST rate on cakes and pastries?

5% since 22 September 2025, down from 18%. Biscuits and cookies are also 5%, rusk is 5%, and plain bread, pizza bread and khakhra are nil.

### Does a home bakery need GST registration?

Only once turnover crosses ₹40 lakh a year in most states if you sell only goods within the state, or ₹20 lakh if you also supply services. Selling to another state makes registration compulsory regardless of turnover.

### What is the profit margin in a bakery business?

It depends on wastage, rent and staff more than on recipes. A well-priced custom cake can leave 35 to 45% after ingredients, packaging and labour, as in the worked example above. Net margins for a shop after all costs are much lower, so track wastage and overheads monthly.

### Can I start a bakery from home legally?

Yes, with FSSAI registration. Check your municipality's rules and your housing society's bylaws if customers will come to your door, and register under the shop act and GST when your setup and turnover require it.

## The bottom line

Pick the model that fits your money and time, get FSSAI before your first sale, and use the post-reform rates: 5% on cakes and cookies, nil on bread. Cost every product, including your labour, and write down what you throw away each evening. A bakery that knows its cost per cake and wastage per day makes money; one that does not is just busy.
