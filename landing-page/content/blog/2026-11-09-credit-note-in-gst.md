A customer sends back four damaged cartons. A buyer points out you billed ₹1,250 a carton when the agreed rate was ₹1,200. The invoice has already gone out, maybe into your GSTR-1, so you cannot tear it up. Under GST the fix is a **credit note**: a separate document that reduces the value or tax of the earlier invoice.

Many pages on this are out of date: some still give a September deadline (changed in 2022), one says "within 30 days" of the invoice (not in the law). Few cover the October 2025 changes in the Invoice Management System (IMS) or the post-sale discount amendment of **1 November 2026**. This guide does, with a worked example and a format to copy.

## Quick answer

- A **credit note in GST** is issued by the **supplier** under **Section 34 of the CGST Act** to reduce an invoice: overcharged value or tax, goods returned, deficient supply, or a post-sale discount.
- **Time limit:** **30 November** after the end of the supply's financial year, or the annual return date if **earlier**. For FY 2025-26 supplies: **30 November 2026**.
- **Format:** no fixed form; **Rule 53(1A)** lists nine particulars, including the original invoice number.
- **Reporting:** **GSTR-1 Table 9B** for the month you issue it. Your output tax falls only if a registered buyer **reverses the matching input tax credit**.
- A **debit note** **increases** value or tax. A **financial credit note** shows no GST: your tax stays the same and the buyer keeps full credit.

## What Section 34 says

Section 34(1) lets a registered supplier issue one or more credit notes for supplies made in a financial year where:

1. the **taxable value or tax charged** in the invoice is more than it should be,
2. the **goods are returned** by the recipient,
3. the goods or services are **found to be deficient**, or
4. a **discount under Section 15(3)(b)** is given after the supply. This ground was inserted by the Finance Act 2026, effective **1 November 2026**.

One credit note can cover several invoices of the same year to the same buyer, as long as it lists each one.

Section 34(2), as substituted from **1 October 2025**, adds the condition: no reduction in output tax if a registered recipient has not reversed the credit it availed, or, in other cases, if the tax was passed on to someone else.

## When to issue a credit note: common shop situations

| Situation | GST credit note? | What it reduces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer returns goods (damaged, wrong size, excess) | Yes | Value and tax of the returned quantity |
| Rate or price billed higher than agreed | Yes | The excess value and its tax |
| Wrong (higher) GST rate charged on the bill | Yes | The excess tax |
| Goods or service found deficient, partial refund agreed | Yes | The refunded value and its tax |
| Post-sale discount, such as a year-end rebate | Yes, from 1 Nov 2026, if the buyer reverses ITC (see below) | The discount and its tax |
| Discount shown on the invoice itself | No | Already out of the value under Section 15(3)(a) |
| Price agreed **after** the bill turns out **higher** | No, that needs a **debit note** | Increases value and tax |

IGST charged instead of CGST and SGST needs more than a credit note; see [CGST, SGST and IGST](https://billzap.app/blog/cgst-sgst-igst-difference). On e-invoicing, credit notes also go to the IRP; see the [e-invoice limit](https://billzap.app/blog/e-invoice-limit) guide.

## The credit note time limit

Section 34(2): declare the credit note in the return for the month it is issued, but **not later than 30 November** following the end of the financial year in which the supply was made, or the date of furnishing the annual return, **whichever is earlier**. The word was "September" until the Finance Act 2022 changed it, with effect from 1 October 2022.

| Original invoice dated | Last date to report a GST credit note |
| --- | --- |
| 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026 (FY 2025-26) | 30 November 2026, or earlier if GSTR-9 for 2025-26 is filed before that |
| 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 (FY 2026-27) | 30 November 2027, or earlier if GSTR-9 for 2026-27 is filed before that |

The deadline follows the **supply's** financial year, not the date of the return: a sale billed on 10 March 2026 and returned on 15 December 2026 is past it. You can still give the money back, but only through a **financial credit note**, with no GST reduction.

## What a credit note must contain: Rule 53(1A)

There is no prescribed form, but Rule 53(1A) of the CGST Rules requires nine particulars: (1) supplier's name, address and GSTIN; (2) nature of the document; (3) a consecutive serial number of up to 16 characters, unique for the financial year; (4) date of issue; (5) recipient's name, address and GSTIN or UIN, if registered; (6) for an unregistered recipient, name, address, delivery address and state name and code; (7) number and date of the original invoice(s) or bill(s) of supply; (8) taxable value, rate and tax credited; (9) signature.

Use a separate series for credit notes, such as CN/26-27/001, not your invoice series. Invoice-side rules are in our [GST invoice format](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-invoice-format) guide.

## Worked example: returns and a rate difference on one credit note

A distributor in Lucknow sells 40 cartons of an item taxed at 18% to a registered retailer in the same state.

**Original invoice INV/26-27/0412, 12 September 2026**

| Item | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taxable value | 40 × ₹1,250 | ₹50,000 |
| CGST 9% | | ₹4,500 |
| SGST 9% | | ₹4,500 |
| **Invoice total** | | **₹59,000** |

Two things go wrong. On 18 September the retailer returns **4 damaged cartons**. And the agreed rate was ₹1,200, not ₹1,250, so the remaining **36 cartons** were billed **₹50 each** too high.

**Credit note CN/26-27/007, 20 September 2026**

| Line | Working | Taxable value | CGST 9% | SGST 9% | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Goods returned | 4 × ₹1,250 | ₹5,000 | ₹450 | ₹450 | ₹5,900 |
| Rate difference | 36 × ₹50 | ₹1,800 | ₹162 | ₹162 | ₹2,124 |
| **Total** | | **₹6,800** | **₹612** | **₹612** | **₹8,024** |

**After the credit note:** taxable value ₹50,000 − ₹6,800 = **₹43,200**; tax ₹9,000 − ₹1,224 = **₹7,776** (which is 18% of ₹43,200); the retailer owes ₹59,000 − ₹8,024 = **₹50,976**.

**Supplier side:** report CN/26-27/007 in the September 2026 GSTR-1, Table 9B. Output tax falls by ₹1,224, provided the retailer reverses that credit.

**Buyer side:** the retailer accepts the credit note in IMS, and ₹1,224 of credit is reduced in GSTR-2B.

## Reporting a credit note in GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B

- **Registered buyer:** GSTR-1 Table **9B**, the "Credit/Debit Notes (Registered)" section, with the buyer's GSTIN, note number and date, and value.
- **Unregistered buyer:** credit notes against large inter-state B2C invoices (above ₹1 lakh, since August 2024) and against exports go in Table 9B "Unregistered". Credit notes against ordinary small consumer sales are netted off in the Table 7 consumer summary. Check how your billing or accounting software files them.
- Corrections to a reported credit note go in Table **9C**; the series used goes in Table **13**.

## What the buyer must do: ITC reversal and IMS

The supplier's tax cut depends on the buyer's credit being reversed, and the GST portal now enforces this through IMS.

- **Accept:** the credit note is taken into the buyer's GSTR-2B and the matching credit is reduced.
- **Reject:** the credit is not reduced, and the supplier's output tax goes back up.
- **Pending (from the October 2025 tax period):** GSTN added an option to keep a credit note pending for **one tax period**, a month for monthly filers or a quarter for QRMP filers. After that it is treated as accepted.
- **Declare the reversal amount (from October 2025):** on accepting, the buyer is asked whether ITC needs to be reduced. "No" if the credit was never claimed; "Yes" with an amount for a partial reversal; "Yes" with no amount reduces the full tax on the note.

Before October 2025 the portal reversed the full amount even if the buyer never claimed it. See our guides to [GSTR-2B](https://billzap.app/blog/gstr-2b) and [input tax credit](https://billzap.app/blog/input-tax-credit-gst).

## Post-sale discounts: what changed on 1 November 2026

Until 31 October 2026, Section 15(3)(b) let a post-sale discount reduce the value only if it was **agreed before or at the time of supply** and **linked to specific invoices**, and the buyer reversed the credit.

The **Finance Act 2026** substituted Section 15(3)(b) with effect from **1 November 2026**. A discount given after the supply is now excluded from the value if the supplier issues a **credit note** and the recipient **reverses the attributable input tax credit, in line with Section 34**. The prior-agreement and invoice-linking conditions are gone, and Section 34(1) now names such discounts as a ground for a credit note.

The buyer's reversal is still the condition and the 30 November deadline still applies. Discounts given before 1 November 2026 follow the old conditions.

## Financial (commercial) credit notes without GST

A **financial credit note**, also called a commercial credit note, records a price reduction **without touching GST**. It shows an amount but no tax, and is not reported in GSTR-1.

Use it when the 30 November deadline has passed, or when you and the buyer agree not to adjust GST (a goodwill amount or early-payment discount, say).

The effect: the supplier's output tax is **not** reduced, and the buyer keeps **full ITC** on the original invoice. CBIC Circular 92/11/2019-GST said this for financial credit notes, and **Circular 251/08/2025-GST** (September 2025) confirmed that a dealer receiving a post-sale discount through a financial credit note need not reverse credit.

**Example:** a supplier sold ₹1,00,000 of goods at 18% (₹18,000 GST) to a retailer during the year and gives a ₹10,000 year-end incentive.

| | GST credit note | Financial credit note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Credit note shows | ₹10,000 + ₹1,800 GST = ₹11,800 | ₹10,000, no GST |
| Supplier's output tax | Falls by ₹1,800 (to ₹16,200) | Stays ₹18,000 |
| Retailer's credit | Reverses ₹1,800 (keeps ₹16,200) | Keeps full ₹18,000 |
| Retailer's account reduced by | ₹11,800 | ₹10,000 |

## Debit note in GST, and credit note vs debit note

Under **Section 34(3)**, the supplier **must** issue a debit note when the invoice **understated** value or tax: a price increase agreed later, short billing, or a lower rate charged by mistake. The law treats a **supplementary invoice** as a debit note.

| | Credit note | Debit note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Issued by | Supplier | Supplier |
| Effect | Reduces value or tax | Increases value or tax |
| Deadline for supplier | 30 November after the FY, or annual return date if earlier | No Section 34 deadline; report in the month issued |
| Buyer's ITC | Must be reversed for supplier's tax to fall | Can be claimed up to 30 November after the FY **of the debit note** (Section 16(4)) |
| GSTR-1 | Table 9B | Table 9B |

**Example:** the agreed price turns out to be ₹1,300, not ₹1,250, on the same 40 cartons. Debit note: 40 × ₹50 = ₹2,000, plus 18% GST of ₹360, total ₹2,360.

**The buyer's "debit note".** A buyer returning goods often sends a commercial debit note ("we have debited your account"). It has no GST effect; the seller still issues a GST credit note.

## FAQ

### What is a credit note in GST?

A document issued by a registered supplier under Section 34 of the CGST Act to reduce the taxable value or tax of an earlier invoice, for returns, overcharging, deficient supply or a post-sale discount. It must quote the original invoice.

### What is the time limit for issuing a credit note under GST?

It must be declared in a return by 30 November following the end of the financial year of the supply, or the date of filing the annual return if that is earlier. For supplies in FY 2025-26, the outer date is 30 November 2026.

### Who issues a credit note, the buyer or the seller?

The seller (supplier). A document a buyer sends when returning goods is a commercial debit note and has no GST effect.

### Does the buyer have to reverse ITC on a credit note?

Yes, if the buyer is registered and had claimed the credit. Otherwise the supplier's output tax is not reduced. From October 2025, the buyer can declare in IMS how much credit to reverse, including nil if none was claimed.

### What is a financial credit note?

A credit note for a price reduction that shows no GST. The supplier's tax is not reduced and the buyer keeps full ITC, as CBIC Circular 251/08/2025-GST confirms for post-sale discounts.

### Is there a time limit for a debit note?

Section 34 sets none for the supplier; it is reported in the return for the month it is issued. The buyer can claim credit on it until 30 November after the end of the financial year in which the debit note is dated.

## The bottom line

Issue a GST credit note with its own number series, quote the original invoice, report it in GSTR-1 Table 9B before 30 November after the year ends, and make sure your buyer accepts it in IMS. From 1 November 2026, post-sale discounts follow the same route. Anything outside that goes on a financial credit note, with the tax left as it was.
