A tea shop looks like the simplest business in India: a burner, a pan, milk, tea and a good spot. The spot matters most, but the arithmetic matters almost as much, and most guides get it wrong. Milk crossed ₹60 a litre in 2026 and a commercial LPG cylinder costs ₹2,820 in Delhi this month, so the "₹3 to ₹5 per cup" figure many pages still quote no longer holds. Price a cup at ₹10 today and you are working for very little.

This guide covers the format, franchise or your own name, licences, a budget, a cup of chai costed line by line, a day's profit and loss, UPI, and the GST line a busy stall can cross without noticing.

## Quick answer

- **Pick a format:** a **street stall or cart** (₹50,000 to ₹1.5 lakh), a **kiosk or small shop** (₹2 lakh to ₹6 lakh) or a **chai cafe with seating** (₹8 lakh to ₹25 lakh and up). These are rough 2026 ranges.
- **Licences:** **FSSAI basic registration** (₹100 a year on FoSCoS) for every format; a **certificate of vending** under the Street Vendors Act, 2014 for a street stall; a **trade licence** and **shop act registration** for a kiosk or cafe.
- **Gas:** use a **commercial LPG cylinder**. Domestic cylinders are not allowed for commercial use.
- **Cost per cup:** about **₹8.25** for a 100 ml cup of milky chai at November 2026 prices. Sell at **₹15** and you keep about ₹6.75 a cup before rent and wages; at ₹10 you keep under ₹2.
- **Break-even:** with ₹800 a day of fixed costs, about **119 cups a day** at ₹15.
- **GST:** serving tea is treated like restaurant service, so registration is needed above **₹20 lakh** a year (₹10 lakh in special category states). A stall selling 300 cups a day is close to that line.
- **Payments:** put a **UPI QR** on the counter. Merchants pay no charge on UPI payments.

## Step 1: Choose your format

| | Street stall or cart | Kiosk or small shop | Chai cafe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Where | Footpath, markets, outside offices, hospitals, colleges | Rented shop, office complex, bus stand | High street, near colleges and IT parks |
| Space | A cart or 20 to 40 sq ft | 40 to 150 sq ft | 300 sq ft and up |
| Menu | Chai, biscuits, rusk, buns | Chai variants, coffee, samosa, bun maska | Full menu, cold drinks, meals |
| Staff | You, maybe a helper | 1 to 3 | 4 and up |
| Licences | FSSAI, vending certificate | FSSAI, trade licence, shop act | Same, plus fire and signage approvals in many cities |
| Rough setup | ₹50,000 to ₹1.5 lakh | ₹2 lakh to ₹6 lakh | ₹8 lakh to ₹25 lakh and up |

**Start smaller than you think.** A stall or kiosk tells you within three months whether enough people pass your spot; a cafe takes a year and costs ten times as much to be wrong about. Stations, malls and office campuses usually allot stalls through tenders, so ask early.

### Franchise or your own brand

Chai franchises promise a known name, a fixed menu, training and supplies. Before you pay anyone, get these in writing:

- **Every fee:** the upfront fee, royalty (a share of sales or a fixed monthly amount), marketing fees and renewal fee.
- **Supply lock-in:** whether you must buy premix, cups and equipment only from the franchisor, and at what price. This often decides your margin more than the royalty.
- **Territory, lock-in and exit:** whether another outlet can open next to you, how long you are bound, and what happens to your fit-out if you leave.
- **Licences in your name:** FSSAI, trade licence and GST for your outlet should be yours.

India has no franchise-specific disclosure law, so the agreement is your only protection. Talk to existing outlet owners without the franchisor present, treat "guaranteed returns" as marketing, and ask the company for its current fees rather than trusting figures on blogs. Your own brand costs nothing, and you control the recipe and the price.

## Step 2: Get your licences

### FSSAI basic registration

Every tea seller needs FSSAI, including a cart. Since **1 April 2026**, **basic registration** covers food businesses with turnover up to **₹1.5 crore**, for **₹100 a year**, on **foscos.fssai.gov.in** using Form A, under a kind of business such as petty retailer of snacks or tea shop. New registrations have no renewal, though the annual fee still applies. Display the certificate and print the 14-digit number on any bills you issue. The full process is in our [FSSAI registration](https://billzap.app/blog/fssai-registration) guide.

### Certificate of vending for a street stall

If you sell from a footpath or other public land, the law that protects you is the **Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act, 2014**. Each city's **Town Vending Committee** surveys street vendors, at least once every five years, and vendors identified in the survey get a **certificate of vending** to vend in a vending zone on the terms and fee set by the city's scheme. The certificate can be suspended or cancelled if you break its conditions.

Without one, a stall on public land can be removed, so ask your municipal corporation how to apply. The certificate, or a letter of recommendation from the urban local body, also opens up **PM SVANidhi** loans. Since the scheme was restructured in August 2025, the first loan goes up to **₹15,000**, the second up to **₹25,000** and the third **₹50,000**, with lending extended to **31 March 2030**.

### Trade licence and shop act for a kiosk or cafe

A tea shop in rented premises needs a **trade licence** (or health licence) from the municipal corporation; our [trade licence](https://billzap.app/blog/trade-license) guide covers the documents. Register under your state's **Shops and Establishments Act**, usually within 30 days of opening. A cafe with seating may need a **fire NOC** and signage permission, depending on the city.

### Commercial gas connection

Get a **commercial LPG connection** from an oil company distributor. Using a domestic cylinder for business is against the LPG supply rules and can lead to seizure. The higher price of commercial gas is built into the cup cost below.

## Step 4: Cost one cup of chai

This is the number your whole business runs on. A 100 ml cup of milky chai, with **illustrative prices** you should replace with your own:

| Cost line | Working | Per cup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Milk | 75 ml at ₹64 a litre | ₹4.80 |
| Tea dust (CTC) | 2.5 g at ₹500 a kg | ₹1.25 |
| Sugar | 8 g at ₹46 a kg | ₹0.37 |
| Ginger, elaichi, masala | | ₹0.30 |
| Gas | ₹2,820 cylinder ÷ about 3,000 cups | ₹0.94 |
| Paper cup | | ₹0.60 |
| **Cost per cup** | | **₹8.26** |

Check it: 4.80 + 1.25 + 0.37 + 0.30 + 0.94 + 0.60 = **₹8.26**.

- **Milk** is 58% of the cost. After the November 2026 increase, toned milk in Delhi-NCR costs about ₹60 a litre and full cream about ₹76; ₹64 assumes a mix or loose dairy milk. Each ₹2 rise in milk adds 15 paise to every cup.
- **Gas:** ₹2,820 is the Delhi price of a 19 kg commercial cylinder from 1 November 2026. Cups per cylinder depend on your burner and how long the pan simmers; 3,000 is an assumption, so count your own first two cylinders.
- **Cups:** plastic cups, glasses and stirrers have been banned nationally since 1 July 2022. Paper cups and kulhads are the usual choice; some states restrict more items.

**What the price does to your margin:**

| Selling price | Cost | You keep per cup | Margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ₹10 | ₹8.26 | ₹1.74 | 17% |
| ₹12 | ₹8.26 | ₹3.74 | 31% |
| ₹15 | ₹8.26 | ₹6.74 | 45% |
| ₹20 (larger cup, 1.5× the cost) | ₹12.39 | ₹7.61 | 38% |

At ₹10 a cup you need nearly four times the customers to earn what a ₹15 cup earns. A smaller "cutting" cup is how many stalls hold a low price; just make the price and the cup size move together.

## Step 5: A day's profit and loss

A street stall near an office complex, open every day, selling **300 cups at ₹15** plus packaged biscuits, rusk and buns. Fixed costs are assumptions: ₹9,000 a month for a small rent or vending fee, and a helper at ₹12,000 a month.

| Line | Working | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tea sales | 300 × ₹15 | ₹4,500 |
| Snack sales | | ₹800 |
| **Total sales** | | **₹5,300** |
| Cost of tea | 300 × ₹8.26 | − ₹2,478 |
| Cost of snacks | about 80% of snack sales | − ₹640 |
| Milk wastage | 3% of ₹1,440 milk cost | − ₹43 |
| Rent or vending fee | ₹9,000 ÷ 30 | − ₹300 |
| Helper | ₹12,000 ÷ 30 | − ₹400 |
| Water, power, cleaning, phone | | − ₹100 |
| **Profit for the day** | | **₹1,339** |

Check it: 5,300 − 2,478 − 640 − 43 − 300 − 400 − 100 = **₹1,339**. Over 30 days that is about **₹40,000 a month**, and it is your wage for 12-hour days, not a return on top of it.

**Break-even:** fixed costs are ₹800 a day. Each ₹15 cup contributes ₹6.74, so you need ₹800 ÷ ₹6.74 = **about 119 cups a day** before you earn anything. At ₹12 a cup it is ₹800 ÷ ₹3.74 = **about 214 cups**. Our guide to the [break-even point](https://billzap.app/blog/break-even-point) explains the method for any shop.

**Watch the GST line.** That stall's sales are ₹5,300 × 365 = **₹19.35 lakh a year**, just under the ₹20 lakh registration limit. A slightly busier stall crosses it.

## Step 6: UPI at the counter

Most chai payments are ₹10 to ₹50, and a missed UPI sale is a lost customer:

- **Use a merchant QR**, not your personal UPI ID, so business receipts stay separate.
- **Get a sound box** that announces each payment; at rush hour you cannot check your phone for every ₹15.
- **Keep the QR at eye level**, laminated against steam and spills.

Our guide to the [UPI QR code for shops](https://billzap.app/blog/upi-qr-code-for-shop) compares the options, and [UPI payment pending](https://billzap.app/blog/upi-payment-pending) covers the customer whose money is debited while your sound box stays silent.

**UPI makes your turnover visible.** In July 2025, Karnataka's commercial tax department sent notices to thousands of small traders, including tea stalls, after UPI data showed receipts above the registration limits; it said traders could reply with records. The lesson is not to refuse UPI, but to know your annual sales and register on time if you cross the limit.

## Step 7: GST, simply

A tea stall, kiosk or cafe serving tea and snacks is supplying **restaurant-type service**, not just selling goods:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| When must I register? | Aggregate turnover above **₹20 lakh** a year (₹10 lakh in Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland and Tripura) |
| Rate if registered (regular scheme) | **5%** (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST), **without** input tax credit |
| Composition option | Pay **5% of turnover** yourself, show no GST on the bill |
| Below the limit | Do not charge GST; issue a plain bill |

Aggregate turnover counts all your sales, including packaged biscuits. Once you cross the limit, apply for registration within 30 days. Our [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) guide explains how turnover is counted, and [GST on restaurant food](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-on-restaurant-food) covers the rules for eating places, including composition.

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to start a tea stall?

About ₹50,000 to ₹1.5 lakh for a street stall or cart in 2026, including the commercial gas connection, utensils, opening stock and two months of working capital. A kiosk costs about ₹2 lakh to ₹6 lakh and a chai cafe ₹8 lakh and up, mostly for rent deposits and fit-out.

### Which licence is needed for a tea stall?

FSSAI basic registration (₹100 a year on FoSCoS) in every case. A street stall should hold a certificate of vending from the Town Vending Committee under the Street Vendors Act, 2014. A kiosk or shop needs a municipal trade licence and shop act registration.

### What is the cost of one cup of tea?

About ₹8.25 for a 100 ml cup of milky chai at November 2026 prices: roughly ₹4.80 of milk, ₹1.25 of tea, ₹0.37 of sugar, ₹0.30 of spices, ₹0.94 of commercial gas and ₹0.60 for a paper cup.

### How much profit does a tea stall make per day?

At 300 cups a day at ₹15, with ₹800 a day of rent, wages and other fixed costs, the worked example makes about ₹1,339 a day, or about ₹40,000 a month, which is the owner's wage.

### Does a tea stall need GST registration?

Only once aggregate turnover crosses ₹20 lakh a year (₹10 lakh in special category states), because serving tea is treated like restaurant service. Below that, do not charge GST. Above it, the rate is 5% without input tax credit, or 5% of turnover under composition.

## The bottom line

A tea shop works when three numbers work: **the cost of a cup** (about ₹8.25 at today's milk and gas prices), **the price** (₹15 gives you room; ₹10 barely does) and **the cups a day** at your spot (about 119 just to cover ₹800 of daily fixed costs). Get FSSAI and, for a stall, the vending certificate before you open, use a commercial cylinder, put a UPI QR on the counter, write down every credit sale, and watch the ₹20 lakh GST line as the stall gets busier.

*Prices in this guide are illustrative November 2026 figures and vary by city and supplier. Licence rules differ by state and city, so confirm with your municipal office, and check GST questions with your CA.*
