A hardware shop looks simple from the road: shelves of pipes, taps, wires, paint tins and boxes of screws. Behind the counter it is one of the harder small businesses to run well. You stock thousands of small items, your best customers are contractors who want credit, a good part of what you sell carries 18% GST, and the bags of cement that bring people in earn almost nothing.

The pages that rank for this topic mostly list the same seven steps: choose a location, get a licence, find suppliers. This guide adds what they leave out: how the money splits across categories, a sample month with real arithmetic, the GST rates after the September 2025 changes, and how to handle contractor credit and stock.

## Quick answer

- **Investment (rough):** about **₹6-15 lakh** for a 300-500 sq ft shop in a small or mid-size town, most of it stock. A paint or building-material focus needs more working capital.
- **Product mix:** plumbing and sanitary, electrical, paints, hand and power tools, fasteners and fittings. Cement and steel are optional and low-margin.
- **Margins (rough):** low single digits on cement and steel, around 8-15% on branded paint, roughly 15-25% on plumbing and electrical, and 25-40% on loose fasteners and small tools. A blended gross margin of 15-20% is a realistic target.
- **GST:** most hardware is **18%**. Cement fell from 28% to **18%** on 22 September 2025. A few items differ: granite and marble blocks are **5%**, and manually operated agricultural hand tools such as spades and hoes are generally exempt.
- **Registration:** GST is compulsory above ₹40 lakh turnover for goods in most states (₹20 lakh in some), but most hardware shops register early because contractors need a GST bill to claim input tax credit.
- **Biggest risks:** unpaid contractor credit and dead stock. Decide your credit rules before you open.

## Pick your format first

"Hardware shop" covers several different businesses. The format decides your investment, your customers and your margins.

| Format | Main customers | Stock character | Margin character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General hardware | Homeowners, plumbers, electricians | Thousands of small items | Higher on small items |
| Sanitary and electrical | Plumbers, electricians, builders | Branded fittings, wires, switches | Medium; brand schemes matter |
| Paint shop | Painters, contractors, homeowners | Fewer SKUs, many shades through tinting | Lower per tin, volume-driven |
| Building material | Builders, contractors | Cement, steel, sand, bricks | Very thin, high turnover, heavy credit |

Most successful town shops start as general hardware with a sanitary and electrical section, then add paint once they have contractor relationships. Building material is a separate business: it needs yard space, transport and much more credit.

## What to stock: the product mix

| Category | Typical items | Who buys | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plumbing | PVC and CPVC pipes, fittings, valves, solvent, tanks | Plumbers, homeowners | Fast-moving; stock all common sizes |
| Sanitary | Taps, showers, WC, wash basins, flush tanks | Builders, homeowners | Display sells; keep a catalogue for big items |
| Electrical | Wires, switches, MCBs, conduit, fans, lights | Electricians, builders | Brand schemes and dealer margins matter |
| Paints | Emulsions, enamels, primers, putty, thinners, brushes | Painters, contractors | Tinting machine lets you sell any shade from base tins |
| Tools | Hammers, pliers, spanners, drills, cutters | Everyone | Good margin on unbranded hand tools |
| Fasteners | Nails, screws, bolts, anchors, hinges, locks | Carpenters, fabricators | Highest margin; sold loose or by weight |
| Building material | Cement, TMT bars, binding wire | Builders | Thin margin; brings footfall, ties up credit |

**A first-month buying list** works best when you ask three local plumbers and electricians what they buy weekly and stock exactly that, in two or three brands at different prices. Add slow items only once you see demand.

**If you sell by weight** (nails, binding wire), your weighing scale must be verified and stamped under the Legal Metrology rules. An unstamped scale is an easy fine.

## Suppliers and dealerships

- **Local distributors** supply most branded plumbing, electrical and paint lines. They usually offer some credit once you have a track record; start with cash and build it.
- **Company dealerships** for paints, pipes and wires come with schemes, display material and better margins, but also sales targets. Paint companies typically offer a tinting machine to dealers on terms that vary by company and town; ask the company's area sales officer directly.
- **Wholesale markets** in the nearest city are where you buy unbranded tools and fasteners at the best price.
- **B2B platforms** online help you compare prices, but check GST invoices and return terms before you order.

Buy from GST-registered suppliers against proper tax invoices. The GST on those purchase bills is your input tax credit, and it comes back against the GST on your sales.

## GST rates for hardware items after September 2025

GST rates were reset on **22 September 2025**. For hardware, the main change was **cement**, which fell from 28% to 18%. Most other hardware was already at 18% and stayed there.

| Item | HSN (heading) | GST rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cement | 2523 | 18% (was 28%) |
| Paints, varnishes, distempers | 3208-3210 | 18% |
| Putty and wall fillers | 3214 | 18% |
| PVC and plastic pipes and fittings | 3917 | 18% |
| Ceramic sanitaryware (WC, basins) | 6910 | 18% |
| Ceramic tiles | 6907 | 18% |
| Taps, valves, cocks | 8481 | 18% |
| Insulated wires and cables | 8544 | 18% |
| Switches, sockets, MCBs | 8536 | 18% |
| Nails, screws, nuts, bolts | 7317, 7318 | 18% |
| Locks and padlocks | 8301 | 18% |
| Hand tools such as pliers, spanners, hammers | 8203-8205 | 18% |
| Manually operated agricultural hand tools (spades, shovels, hoes) | 8201 | Generally exempt (check the exemption entry) |
| Granite and marble blocks, sand-lime bricks | 2515, 2516, 6810 | 5% (was 12%) |

Read the full eight-digit code and description for anything unusual, and check it against the CBIC rate notification; one heading can carry two rates. Our [GST rates list](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-rates-list) explains how to look items up and save the rate once.

## Why contractors care about your GSTIN

A registered contractor who supplies works contract services, or a builder, can claim the GST on materials as input tax credit, but only if your bill is a **tax invoice** that shows **their GSTIN**, the HSN codes and the tax split. Without it, the 18% is a cost to them. That is why:

- **Register under GST even below the limit** if you want contractor business. The [GST registration limit](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-registration-limit) is the legal minimum, not the business decision.
- **The composition scheme is usually a poor fit.** A composition dealer cannot charge GST or issue a tax invoice, so contractors get no credit and will buy elsewhere. It can suit a shop that sells almost entirely to homeowners; see the [GST composition scheme](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme) guide before you choose.
- **Get the buyer's GSTIN right.** A wrong digit blocks their credit, and they will be back at your counter about it.
- **Large deliveries may need an e-way bill.** A consignment above ₹50,000, such as a truck of cement and steel to a site, generally needs one before it moves.

A homeowner building their own house cannot claim the credit, so for them the GST is simply part of the price.

Here is a sample month for a shop selling ₹4 lakh. The margins are **assumptions** from the rough ranges above, not industry statistics; replace them with your own once you have three months of sales.

## Credit to contractors without sinking

Credit is how hardware shops win contractors, and how many of them close. Set rules before the first request:

1. **A credit limit per contractor,** based on how long you have known them and how they paid before. Start low.
2. **A fixed due date,** commonly 15 or 30 days, said out loud and written on the bill.
3. **One bill per site or job,** so disputes are about one bill, not a running total nobody can explain.
4. **No new credit** once a contractor misses two due dates, until the old bills are cleared.
5. **Remind on the due date,** politely, with the exact amount and a way to pay.

## Managing thousands of SKUs

A hardware shop can carry several thousand items, many of them small and similar. The habits that keep it under control:

- **Name items consistently:** type, size, brand, unit. "PVC elbow 1 inch Supreme" and "Elbow PVC 25mm" in the same list is how stock goes missing.
- **One unit per item:** piece, metre, kg or box, and never switch.
- **Count fast-movers often** and slow items once a quarter. A rolling count of one rack a week keeps the whole shop accurate.
- **Set reorder levels** for the items plumbers and electricians ask for daily.
- **Clear dead stock** every season, at cost if needed, before it becomes scrap.

## Licences and registrations checklist

- **Shop and Establishment registration** from your state's labour department.
- **Trade licence** from the municipality, where your city requires one.
- **GST registration,** above the threshold or earlier for contractor business.
- **Udyam registration,** free and optional, useful for loans and schemes.
- **Weighing scale verification** if you sell anything by weight.
- **Fire safety:** paints and thinners are flammable; check with your local fire department whether storage of your size needs a NOC.

## FAQ

### How much investment is needed to start a hardware shop?

Roughly ₹6-15 lakh for a 300-500 sq ft shop in a small or mid-size town, with opening stock the largest part. Paint dealerships and building material need more working capital. Get local rent and supplier quotes before you plan.

### What is the profit margin in a hardware shop?

It varies by category: very thin on cement and steel, around 8-15% on branded paint, roughly 15-25% on plumbing and electrical, and higher on loose fasteners and small tools. A blended gross margin of 15-20% is a realistic target for a mixed shop.

### What is the GST rate on hardware items?

Most hardware, including paints, pipes, sanitaryware, wires, switches, fasteners and hand tools, is 18%. Cement moved from 28% to 18% on 22 September 2025. Granite and marble blocks are 5%, and manually operated agricultural hand tools such as spades are generally exempt.

### Do I need GST registration for a hardware shop?

Legally, once turnover crosses ₹40 lakh for goods in most states, or ₹20 lakh in some. In practice most hardware shops register early, because contractors and builders need a tax invoice with their GSTIN to claim input tax credit.

### Should a hardware shop take the GST composition scheme?

Usually not if you sell to contractors. Composition dealers cannot issue tax invoices, so registered buyers get no credit. It can work for a shop selling only to homeowners.

### How do I get a paint dealership?

Contact the paint company's area sales officer or regional office. Terms for deposits, targets and tinting machines vary by company and town, so compare two or three brands before you sign.

## The bottom line

To start a hardware shop, plan for ₹6-15 lakh, most of it in a wide spread of stock, and keep a cushion of working capital for contractor credit. Stock what local plumbers and electricians actually buy, protect your margin with tools and fasteners, treat cement as a footfall item, register under GST so contractors can claim credit, and put a credit limit and due date on every contractor bill from the first day.

*Investment and margin figures here are rough planning ranges, not quotes. GST rates change by notification; check each item's HSN entry, and confirm registration and tax questions with your chartered accountant.*
