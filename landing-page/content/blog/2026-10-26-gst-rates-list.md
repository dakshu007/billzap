Search "GST rates list" and half the pages on the first screen are still wrong. One large insurer's page still lists ghee, fruit juice and mobile phones under a 12% slab that no longer exists for them. Others give you a neat four-slab chart and stop there, which is no help when a customer is standing at your counter with a tube of toothpaste and a packet of namkeen.

This list is arranged the way a shop is arranged: by aisle. For each common item you get the rate before 22 September 2025, the rate now, and a note where the answer depends on packing, price or brand. Every rate here comes from the government's own notifications and the PIB factsheet on the reform. Where something is genuinely complicated (tobacco, for one), we say so and point you to the official source rather than guess.

## Quick answer

Since **22 September 2025**, GST on goods has four main rates: **0% (nil), 5%, 18% and 40%**. The old 12% and 28% slabs have been emptied for almost every everyday item.

- **Nil:** loose (unbranded) rice, atta, dal, fresh fruit and vegetables, fresh milk, salt, roti and paratha, UHT milk, packed paneer, exercise books, pencils and erasers.
- **5%:** packed and labelled staples, edible oil, sugar, tea, butter and ghee, namkeen, biscuits, chocolates, ice cream, hair oil, soap, shampoo, toothpaste, utensils, most medicines, clothes and footwear up to ₹2,500.
- **18%:** detergent, mobile phones, TVs and ACs, cement, clothes and footwear above ₹2,500, small cars and bikes up to 350cc, most services.
- **40%:** aerated and caffeinated soft drinks, pan masala, cigarettes and tobacco, large cars and bikes above 350cc.
- **Loose versus packed** still decides the rate for staples: loose rice is nil, the same rice pre-packed and labelled is 5%.

## The four GST slabs in 2026 at a glance

| Rate | What it covers, broadly | Split on an intra-state bill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0% (nil or exempt)** | Unprocessed food, fresh produce, unbranded staples, education, healthcare services | No GST line |
| **5%** | Daily-use food and household goods, most medicines, mass-market clothing and footwear | 2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST |
| **18%** | The standard rate: most manufactured goods, consumer durables, most services | 9% CGST + 9% SGST |
| **40%** | Luxury and "sin" goods: soft drinks, tobacco, pan masala, big cars | 20% CGST + 20% SGST |

Two smaller rates still exist for specialised goods: 3% on gold, silver and jewellery, and 0.25% on rough diamonds and precious stones. If you do not run a jewellery shop, you can ignore them.

For a sale to another state, the same total rate is charged as a single IGST line instead of CGST plus SGST. Our guide to the [CGST, SGST and IGST difference](https://billzap.app/blog/cgst-sgst-igst-difference) explains when to use which.

The rule that trips up most kirana owners is the packing rule. A bag of rice weighed out from a sack in front of the customer is nil-rated. The same rice in a sealed, labelled 5 kg bag (anything up to 25 kg that carries the declarations the Legal Metrology rules require) is taxed at 5%. Check the bag, not the grain.

## Aisle 10: Services you might sell

| Service | Before | Now | Input tax credit? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Restaurants (not in a hotel charging over ₹7,500 a night) | 5% | **5%** | No |

The "no input tax credit" column matters. A salon that charges 5% cannot claim back the GST it paid on its chairs, shampoo and rent. That is the trade-off for the lower rate. Our guide to [input tax credit](https://billzap.app/blog/input-tax-credit-gst) covers it in detail.

## The 40% slab and tobacco

The 40% rate covers aerated drinks, large cars and motorcycles above 350cc, yachts, betting and online gaming. Tobacco took longer. Cigarettes, pan masala and other tobacco products stayed at 28% plus compensation cess after September 2025, and moved to **40% from 1 February 2026**, when the compensation cess on them was set to nil. Bidis are taxed at 18%. These products also carry separate levies outside GST, so if you sell them, take the full rate from your distributor's tax invoice and the CBIC notification, not from a blog chart, this one included.

## How to check the rate for any item using its HSN code

No list can cover every product. When you are unsure, work from the HSN code:

1. **Find the HSN code.** It is printed on your supplier's tax invoice next to the item. Our guide on [what an HSN code is](https://billzap.app/blog/what-is-hsn-code) explains how many digits you need.
2. **Look it up on the CBIC rate page** (cbic-gst.gov.in, "GST Goods and Services Rates"). The schedules are arranged by HSN chapter; the four-digit heading takes you to the right row.
3. **Read the description, not just the number.** One heading can carry two rates, for example "pre-packaged and labelled" at 5% and loose at nil.
4. **Match your supplier.** If your distributor bills an item at 5%, and you sell the same item unchanged, your rate is 5% too. If the two differ, ask before you bill.

## How to put the right rate on a bill

A kirana basket almost always mixes rates. Here is a four-item bill from a GST-registered shop selling within its own state:

| Item | Taxable value (₹) | GST rate | CGST (₹) | SGST (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rice 5 kg, loose | 300.00 | Nil | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| Toothpaste | 100.00 | 5% | 2.50 | 2.50 |
| Biscuits | 50.00 | 5% | 1.25 | 1.25 |
| Washing powder 1 kg | 200.00 | 18% | 18.00 | 18.00 |
| **Total** | **650.00** | | **21.75** | **21.75** |

Total GST is ₹43.50, and the customer pays ₹650 + ₹43.50 = **₹693.50**. Notice that the washing powder, less than a third of the bill, carries over 80% of the tax. That is why getting each line's rate right matters more than the bill total.

Three rules keep the bill correct:

- **Rate per line, not per bill.** Each item carries its own rate. Our guide on [how to calculate GST](https://billzap.app/blog/how-to-calculate-gst) shows the line-by-line formula, including how to back GST out of an MRP.
- **Split correctly.** Within your state, half goes to CGST and half to SGST. To another state, the full rate is IGST.
- **Show the rate and HSN on the bill.** Rule 46 requires both on a tax invoice. Our [GST invoice format](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-invoice-format) guide lists every field.

One exception: if you are a [composition dealer](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-composition-scheme), none of these rates go on your bill. You issue a bill of supply with no GST on it and pay a flat rate on turnover instead.

## FAQ

### What are the GST slab rates in 2026?

The main rates on goods are 0%, 5%, 18% and 40%. There are also special rates of 3% on gold and jewellery and 0.25% on rough precious stones. The 12% and 28% slabs no longer apply to everyday goods.

### Is 12% GST removed completely?

For almost all goods, yes: items in the 12% slab moved to 5% or, in a few cases such as clothing above ₹2,500, to 18%. A small number of specific entries and services can still carry other rates, so check the HSN entry for anything unusual.

### Which items moved from 18% to 5% GST?

Hair oil, shampoo, toilet soap bars, toothpaste, toothbrushes, shaving cream, biscuits, cakes, chocolates, ice cream, soups, instant coffee and tea extracts, and thermometers, among others. Salon and gym services also moved from 18% to 5%, without input tax credit.

### What is the GST rate on grocery items?

Loose staples such as rice, atta and dal are nil. The same staples pre-packed and labelled are 5%. Edible oil, sugar, tea and spices are 5%. Packed namkeen, biscuits and chocolates are now 5%. Detergent stays at 18%, and soft drinks are 40%.

### Do I need to change my old MRP stickers?

No. Relabelling old stock was a temporary arrangement around the rate change. What matters is your bill: it must show the current rate for the item, whatever rate was in force when the pack was printed, and customers will expect the lower price.

### Is GST charged on MRP?

MRP includes GST. To find the GST inside it, divide by (1 + rate). A ₹105 item at 5% has a taxable value of ₹100 and GST of ₹5.

### Where can I check the official GST rate for a product?

On the CBIC website's "GST Goods and Services Rates" page, searched by HSN chapter, and in the rate notifications behind it. The GST portal's HSN search shows the description for any code.

## The bottom line

GST in 2026 is simpler than it has ever been: nil for unprocessed staples, 5% for most things a kirana sells, 18% as the standard rate and 40% for sin goods. The mistakes that remain are specific ones: detergent billed at 5%, packed rice billed at nil, an expensive jacket billed at the clothing rate for cheaper pieces. Check each item's HSN code once, save the rate against the product, and every bill after that is right.
