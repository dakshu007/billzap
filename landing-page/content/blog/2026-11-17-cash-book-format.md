Every shop has a cash book, even if it is only the number the owner carries in their head at closing time: "there should be about twenty thousand in the drawer." A written cash book turns that guess into a figure you can check. When the drawer is short, it tells you on which day and after which entry. When your CA asks where ₹15,000 in the bank came from, it shows the deposit.

Most pages for "cash book format" are written for commerce students: Dr and Cr sides, ledger folios and discount columns. This guide is for a shop owner. It shows the formats, a filled week for a kirana store with every balance checked, how to balance the book and match it to the drawer, how to run petty cash, how to set it up in Excel, and the cash limits income-tax law puts on shops.

## Quick answer

- A **cash book** records every rupee of **cash received and paid**, in date order, with a running or closing **balance** that should match the cash in your drawer.
- **Formats:** **single column** (cash only), **double column** (cash and bank, the best fit for a shop that takes UPI), **triple column** (adds discount) and the **petty cash book** for small expenses.
- **Columns:** date, particulars, voucher or bill number, receipt (in), payment (out) and balance.
- **Balancing:** opening balance + receipts − payments = closing balance. Count the drawer; the two must match.
- **Petty cash:** use the **imprest system**: fix an amount (say ₹2,000), spend from it with vouchers, top it up by exactly what was spent.
- **Income-tax limits (Income-tax Act, 2025, from 1 April 2026):** do not receive **₹2 lakh or more in cash** from one person in a day, for one transaction or for one event (**section 186**, the old section 269ST). A cash payment for an expense of **more than ₹10,000** to one person in a day is **not allowed as a deduction** (**section 36(4)**, the old section 40A(3)).

## What a cash book is, and why a shop needs one

A cash book is both a diary and an account. It is a **diary** because entries are made as they happen, day by day. It is an **account** because it carries a balance: the cash you should have.

For a shop, it does three jobs:

1. **Catches leaks.** If the book says ₹8,550 and the drawer has ₹7,900, you know within a day, not at the end of the month.
2. **Explains the bank.** Cash deposits, UPI collections and supplier payments line up, so your CA is not guessing.
3. **Feeds your accounts.** Sales, expenses and drawings flow from it into your profit and your income-tax return.

It sits beside your other records. Bills or [cash memos](https://billzap.app/blog/cash-memo-format) prove each sale; the [stock register](https://billzap.app/blog/stock-register-format) tracks goods; the cash book tracks money.

## Types of cash book

| Type | Columns for money | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Single column** | Cash only | A shop that takes mostly cash and rarely uses the bank |
| **Double column** | Cash and bank | Most shops today: cash at the counter, UPI and NEFT through the bank |
| **Triple column** | Cash, bank and discount | Wholesalers who give or get cash discounts on settlement |
| **Petty cash book** | Small cash expenses, often split by type | Tea, courier, cleaning, auto fares; kept separately from the main book |

## The columns of a cash book

The textbook layout has two sides. The left (**Dr**, receipts) and the right (**Cr**, payments) each have:

| Column | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| **Date** | Day of the entry |
| **Particulars** | Who paid or was paid, and for what: "Cash sales, bills 412 to 447", "Paid Gupta Traders" |
| **Voucher or bill no.** | The bill, receipt or voucher that backs the entry |
| **L.F.** (ledger folio) | Page of the ledger account the entry is posted to; skip it if you do not keep a ledger |
| **Amount** | Cash column; add a Bank column for a double column book, and a Discount column for triple |

Most shops find the **running-balance layout** easier: one table, with **Receipt** and **Payment** side by side and a **Balance** after every line. It holds the same information and shows a mistake the moment it happens. The sample below uses it.

## How to balance a cash book

At the end of each day (or at least each week):

1. **Add the receipts**, including the opening balance.
2. **Add the payments.**
3. **Closing balance = receipts − payments.** In the two-sided format, write it on the payment side as "**Balance c/d**" (carried down), so both sides total the same. Next period, bring it to the receipt side as "**Balance b/d**" (brought down).
4. **Count the cash.** Note shortages and excesses in the book as "cash short" or "cash excess" with the date, rather than quietly adjusting.
5. **Never a credit balance.** A cash book cannot show more cash paid than you had. If the balance goes negative, an entry is missing or wrong.

In the two-sided format, the same week closes like this:

| Dr: Receipts | ₹ | Cr: Payments | ₹ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Balance b/d | 5,000 | Suppliers (Krishna Dairy, Gupta Traders) | 13,000 |
| Cash sales | 65,700 | Bank deposits (contra) | 27,000 |
| Udhaar received | 1,500 | Wages, electricity, petty cash | 6,590 |
| | | Drawings | 4,000 |
| | | **Balance c/d** | **21,610** |
| **Total** | **72,200** | **Total** | **72,200** |

## The double column cash book: cash and bank together

A shop taking UPI has two pots of money: the drawer and the bank. A double column book tracks both, side by side. Here is the same week with the bank added. Opening bank balance: ₹42,000. UPI sales for the week: ₹38,450.

| Particulars | Cash in | Cash out | Bank in | Bank out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening balance | 5,000 | | 42,000 | |
| Cash sales | 65,700 | | | |
| UPI sales (straight to bank) | | | 38,450 | |
| Udhaar received in cash | 1,500 | | | |
| Cash deposited in bank, 10 and 14 Nov (C) | | 27,000 | 27,000 | |
| Suppliers paid in cash | | 13,000 | | |
| Distributor paid by NEFT | | | | 31,600 |
| Shop rent paid by UPI | | | | 18,000 |
| Wages, electricity, petty cash | | 6,590 | | |
| Owner's drawings | | 4,000 | | |
| **Closing balance c/d** | | **21,610** | | **57,850** |
| **Totals** | **72,200** | **72,200** | **1,07,450** | **1,07,450** |

**(C) means contra:** money moved between your own drawer and your own bank. It appears on both sides, once as cash out and once as bank in, and is neither income nor expense. Bank side check: ₹42,000 + ₹38,450 + ₹27,000 = ₹1,07,450 in; ₹31,600 + ₹18,000 + ₹57,850 = ₹1,07,450 out.

Once a month, tick the bank column against your bank statement. Differences are usually bank charges, a UPI refund or a cheque not yet cleared.

## Petty cash book and the imprest system

Small, frequent expenses (tea, courier, phenyl, an auto to the wholesale market) clutter the main book. Keep them in a **petty cash book** on the **imprest system**:

1. Fix an amount that covers a week or two of small spends, say **₹2,000**, and hand it to whoever handles it.
2. Each spend gets a slip or voucher.
3. At the end of the period, total the spends and **top up by exactly that amount**, through one entry in the main cash book.

An **analytical** petty cash book splits spends into columns so the totals go straight into your expense heads:

| Date | Particulars | Voucher | Amount (₹) | Tea | Conveyance | Courier | Stationery | Cleaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 Nov | Imprest balance | | 2,000 | | | | | |
| 9 Nov | Tea for staff, week | PV1 | 280 | 280 | | | | |
| 10 Nov | Phenyl and broom | PV2 | 150 | | | | | 150 |
| 11 Nov | Courier to supplier | PV3 | 120 | | | 120 | | |
| 13 Nov | Register and pens | PV4 | 210 | | | | 210 | |
| 14 Nov | Auto to wholesale market | PV5 | 180 | | 180 | | | |
| | **Total spent** | | **940** | **280** | **180** | **120** | **210** | **150** |
| 15 Nov | Balance before top-up | | 1,060 | | | | | |

The column totals add back to ₹940 (₹280 + ₹180 + ₹120 + ₹210 + ₹150), and the top-up is the ₹940 entry in the main cash book above. The imprest always comes back to ₹2,000, so a missing voucher shows up at once.

## Cash book in Excel or Google Sheets

Set up five columns: **A Date, B Particulars, C Receipt, D Payment, E Balance**.

- Row 2: the opening balance in **E2**.
- **E3:** `=E2+C3-D3`, then drag it down the column.
- Week totals: `=SUM(C3:C30)` and `=SUM(D3:D30)`.
- Check: opening + total receipts − total payments should equal the last balance.
- Add a column F for **category** (Sales, Supplier, Wages, Drawings, Contra) and use `=SUMIF(F:F,"Wages",D:D)` to total any head.
- For a double column book, add Bank receipt, Bank payment and Bank balance columns with the same formula.

Protect the formula column, start a new sheet each month carrying the closing balance forward, and keep a copy off your phone or laptop.

## Cash limits under income-tax law

India's **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced the 1961 Act from **1 April 2026** and renumbered its sections. The cash limits that matter to a shop carry over:

| Rule | Limit | Section (2025 Act) | Old section | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Receiving cash | **₹2 lakh or more** from one person in a day, for a single transaction, or for one event or occasion | **186** | 269ST | Penalty equal to the amount received |
| Paying an expense in cash | **More than ₹10,000** to one person in a day (₹35,000 for payments to transporters for hiring or leasing goods carriages) | **36(4)** | 40A(3) | Expense not allowed as a deduction |
| Taking or repaying loans and deposits in cash | **₹20,000 or more** | 185 and 188 | 269SS and 269T | Penalty |

What this means at the counter:

- **A ₹2.5 lakh gold or furniture sale cannot be taken in cash**, even split across two bills for the same purchase on the same day. The shop receiving it faces the penalty, not the customer.
- **Pay suppliers above ₹10,000 by UPI, NEFT or account payee cheque.** Splitting one day's ₹18,000 payment into two cash payments of ₹9,000 to the same person does not help; the limit counts the day's total. A few narrow exceptions exist under the rules; ask your CA before relying on one.
- **Borrowing from a relative or friend for the shop?** Take it into the bank, not in cash.

Digital receipts also help at tax time. Under the presumptive scheme for small traders (old section 44AD), profit is deemed at **6%** of turnover received through banking channels and UPI, against **8%** for cash. ## Cash or digital: what a cash book cannot do

A cash book only knows what you write in it. UPI and bank payments leave their own trail; cash leaves none. That is why the double column book, with UPI on the bank side, is the honest format for a modern shop: it puts both pots next to each other.

A few habits keep the cash side clean:

- **Bill every sale,** cash or UPI, so the receipts in the book tie to bill numbers.
- **Deposit surplus cash** every few days instead of letting it build up in the drawer.
- **Record udhaar separately** and enter it in the cash book only when the money arrives. Our [udhaar management](https://billzap.app/blog/udhaar-management) guide covers keeping that list tidy.
- **Take UPI with the exact amount** on a QR, so payments match bills; see [UPI QR code for shop](https://billzap.app/blog/upi-qr-code-for-shop).

For GST-registered shops, Section 35 of the CGST Act requires true and correct accounts of supplies, stock and tax at your principal place of business, and records must be kept for 72 months from the due date of that year's annual return. A cash book is part of the trail that supports them.

## FAQ

### What is the format of a cash book?

Date, particulars, voucher or bill number, receipts, payments and balance. The traditional format puts receipts on the left (Dr) and payments on the right (Cr); a running-balance format puts them side by side with a balance after every entry.

### What is the difference between a single and a double column cash book?

A single column cash book records cash only. A double column cash book adds a bank column, so cash sales, UPI collections, bank deposits and bank payments sit side by side. Most shops that take UPI need the double column format.

### What is a contra entry in a cash book?

An entry that moves money between your own cash and your own bank, such as depositing cash or withdrawing it. It appears in both the cash and bank columns and is neither income nor expense. Mark it (C).

### What is the imprest system of petty cash?

You fix a petty cash amount, spend from it with vouchers, and at the end of the period top it up by exactly what was spent, so it returns to the fixed amount every time.

### Can a cash book have a credit balance?

No. You cannot pay out more cash than you have. A negative balance means an entry is missing or wrong.

### How much cash can a shop accept from a customer?

Less than ₹2 lakh from one person in a day, for one transaction or for one event. Receiving ₹2 lakh or more in cash breaks section 186 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (old section 269ST) and invites a penalty equal to the amount.

### Is a cash payment above ₹10,000 allowed?

It is not illegal, but an expense paid in cash of more than ₹10,000 to one person in a day is not allowed as a deduction under section 36(4) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (old section 40A(3)). The limit is ₹35,000 for payments to transporters.

## The bottom line

Pick the double column format if you take UPI, keep petty cash on an imprest, and balance the book against the drawer every night or at least every week. Keep cash receipts under ₹2 lakh per customer per occasion and cash expenses at ₹10,000 or less per person per day. A cash book is only as good as the day it was last balanced.

*This guide is general information, not accounting or tax advice. Section numbers refer to the Income-tax Act, 2025; confirm your own case with your chartered accountant.*
