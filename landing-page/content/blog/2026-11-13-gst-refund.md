A GST refund is tax or credit the law lets you take back in cash. For most small shops it comes up in one of three ways: too much cash paid into the GST portal, IGST paid where CGST and SGST were due, or purchases carrying a higher GST rate than sales. Exporters meet it every month.

The process runs through **RFD-01** and a chain of department forms, and it changed twice in the last year: 90% provisional refunds were extended to inverted duty claims from October 2025, and that extension became law from **1 November 2026**. This guide covers who can claim, time limits, steps, interest, status and why claims fail.

## Quick answer

- **Who gets a refund:** exporters and suppliers to SEZs, businesses with **inverted duty** (input GST rate higher than output), anyone with **excess balance in the electronic cash ledger**, tax paid **under the wrong head** (IGST instead of CGST + SGST, or the reverse), excess tax paid, and a few others.
- **How:** file **Form GST RFD-01** on the GST portal (Services > Refunds > Application for Refund). Exports of goods with IGST paid need no RFD-01: the shipping bill is treated as the refund claim.
- **Time limit:** **2 years from the relevant date** (Section 54). There is **no time limit** for refund of excess cash ledger balance.
- **Timeline:** acknowledgement (**RFD-02**) or deficiency memo (**RFD-03**) within **15 days**; **90% provisional refund** (**RFD-04**) within **7 days** of acknowledgement for exports and, now, inverted duty; final order (**RFD-06**) within **60 days** of a complete application.
- **Interest on delay:** **6% a year** if the refund is not paid within 60 days; **9%** where it arises from an appellate or court order.
- **Minimum claim:** ₹1,000.
- **Status:** Services > Refunds > Track Application Status, with your ARN.

## Who can claim a GST refund

| Situation | Typical claimant | How it is claimed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Export of goods **with payment of IGST** | Exporter | Automatic: shipping bill is the claim, processed by Customs (Rule 96) |
| Export of goods or services **under LUT**, without paying tax | Exporter, IT and service exporters | RFD-01: refund of unused input tax credit |
| Supplies to an **SEZ** unit or developer | Supplier | RFD-01 (with or without payment of tax) |
| **Inverted duty structure** | Manufacturer or trader whose inputs carry a higher rate than outputs | RFD-01: refund of accumulated ITC |
| **Excess balance in electronic cash ledger** | Anyone who paid more cash than needed | RFD-01 |
| Tax paid **under the wrong head** | Business that charged IGST on a local sale, or CGST + SGST on an inter-state one | RFD-01, after paying the correct tax |
| **Excess payment of tax** | Business that paid more than its liability by mistake | RFD-01 |
| Tax paid on a supply **not made** and not invoiced | Business that took an advance and refunded it | RFD-01 |
| **Deemed exports** | Supplier or recipient | RFD-01 |
| Refund arising from an **appeal or order** | Taxpayer who won an appeal | RFD-01 |

A small shop usually meets only three of these: the cash ledger, the wrong head and, since the September 2025 rate cuts, inverted duty.

## Time limit: two years from the "relevant date"

Section 54(1) gives you **two years from the relevant date**, and the relevant date depends on the type of refund:

| Refund type | Two years run from |
| --- | --- |
| Export of goods by sea or air | Date the ship or aircraft leaves India |
| Export of goods by land | Date the goods pass the frontier |
| Export of services | Date of receipt of payment in foreign exchange (or the invoice date, if paid in advance) |
| Supplies to SEZ | Due date of the GSTR-3B for the period of supply |
| Inverted duty structure | Due date of the GSTR-3B for the period in which the claim arises |
| Tax paid under the wrong head | Date you paid the correct tax (Rule 89(1A)) |
| Refund following an appellate order | Date the order is communicated |
| Excess balance in the cash ledger | **No time limit** (CBIC Circular 166/22/2021-GST) |

A claim filed a day late is rejected, however valid.

## How to file RFD-01, step by step

**Before you start**

1. **File GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B** for every period in the claim; the refund is worked out from them.
2. **Validate your bank account.** Refunds are paid through PFMS into a bank account in your registration; fix a failed validation by amending your registration first.
3. **Check your ledgers.** An ITC refund cannot exceed the balance in your electronic credit ledger; a cash refund cannot exceed your cash ledger balance.

**Filing**

1. Log in at gst.gov.in and go to **Services > Refunds > Application for Refund**.
2. Choose the **refund type** (for example "Refund of ITC on Export of Goods & Services without Payment of Tax", "Refund on account of ITC accumulated due to Inverted Tax Structure" or "Excess Balance in Electronic Cash Ledger"), and the **period** from and to. One application can cover several months.
3. Fill in the **statement** the portal gives for that type (invoice-wise details, turnover and ITC figures) and **upload documents**.
4. Choose the bank account and fill in the **declarations**. For an ITC or cash refund, the amount claimed is **debited from your ledger** when you file; if it is later rejected, the officer re-credits it.
5. Submit with **DSC or EVC**. You get an **ARN** (application reference number) by SMS and email. Keep it; it is how you track the claim.

### Documents by refund type

| Refund type | Main documents |
| --- | --- |
| Export of goods under LUT | Statement of invoices with shipping bill numbers and dates, export general manifest details |
| Export of services under LUT | Invoices with BRC or FIRC (proof of foreign exchange received) |
| Inverted duty | Rule 89(5) calculation, list of purchase invoices whose credit is claimed (the credit must appear in GSTR-2B) |
| Wrong head | Challan for the correct tax, details of the invoice on which wrong tax was paid |
| Excess cash ledger | Usually none beyond the application |
| Any refund other than cash ledger | Declaration that you have not passed the tax on (unjust enrichment); a **CA or CMA certificate if the claim exceeds ₹2 lakh** in cases where it applies |

## What happens after you file: the RFD timeline

| Form | What it is | Time limit now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RFD-02 | Acknowledgement that the application is complete | Within 15 days of filing |
| RFD-03 | Deficiency memo: something is missing; you file a fresh application | Within 15 days of filing |
| RFD-04 | Provisional refund of 90% | Within 7 days of RFD-02 (zero-rated supplies and inverted duty) |
| RFD-08 | Show cause notice, if the officer proposes to reject any part | Reply in RFD-09 within 15 days |
| RFD-06 | Final order sanctioning or rejecting the refund | Within 60 days of a complete application |
| RFD-05 | Payment advice; money credited to your bank via PFMS | After RFD-04 or RFD-06 |
| RFD-07 | Order withholding or adjusting the refund against dues | When applicable |

**Proposed faster timeline.** The 57th GST Council meeting in October 2026 recommended cutting the RFD-02 or RFD-03 window to **10 days** and issuing the RFD-04 provisional order within **3 working days** of acknowledgement, without an officer's intervention, for low-risk claims. It was also clarified that once RFD-02 is issued, a deficiency memo cannot be issued later for the same application. Check whether the rule changes have been notified before relying on the shorter limits.

## Provisional refund: 90% up front, now for inverted duty too

Under **Section 54(6)**, refunds on **zero-rated supplies** (exports and SEZ supplies) get 90% provisionally, with the remaining 10% after final verification. What changed:

- **From 1 October 2025**, Rule 91(2), as amended by Notification 13/2025-Central Tax, lets the system assess each claim's risk, so low-risk claims get the 90% without a detailed manual check. Notification 14/2025-Central Tax lists the persons who cannot get it.
- **Inverted duty claims filed from 1 October 2025** were given the same 90% provisional treatment by CBIC **Instruction No. 06/2025-GST** (3 October 2025), as an interim measure.
- **From 1 November 2026**, Section 155 of the **Finance Act, 2026** amends Section 54(6) itself to cover inverted duty claims, brought into force by Notification 03/2026-Central Tax. The same Act amended Section 54(14) so that the ₹1,000 minimum no longer blocks refunds on goods exported with payment of tax.

A claim the system flags as risky is examined in full first.

## Inverted duty refund: the formula with a worked example

Inverted duty means inputs carry a higher rate than outputs, so unusable credit piles up. It became more common after **22 September 2025**, when many finished goods moved to 5% while some inputs stayed at 18%.

The maximum refund under **Rule 89(5)**:

> **Refund = (Turnover of inverted rated supply × Net ITC ÷ Adjusted total turnover) − (Tax payable on inverted rated supply × Net ITC ÷ ITC availed on inputs and input services)**

**Net ITC** means credit on **inputs (goods) only**, not input services or capital goods, as the Supreme Court upheld in VKC Footsteps (2021).

**Example.** A small food manufacturer sells everything at 5%. In a quarter:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Turnover of 5% supplies (= adjusted total turnover) | ₹20,00,000 |
| Output tax at 5% | ₹1,00,000 |
| ITC on inputs (₹10,00,000 of materials and packaging at 18%) | ₹1,80,000 |
| ITC on input services | ₹20,000 |
| Total ITC availed | ₹2,00,000 |

- First part: ₹20,00,000 × ₹1,80,000 ÷ ₹20,00,000 = **₹1,80,000**
- Second part: ₹1,00,000 × ₹1,80,000 ÷ ₹2,00,000 = **₹90,000**
- **Maximum refund: ₹1,80,000 − ₹90,000 = ₹90,000**
- Provisional refund at 90%: **₹81,000**

Check: ₹2,00,000 credit less ₹1,00,000 output tax leaves ₹1,00,000. The ₹10,000 not refunded is the input-services share, which stays in the ledger.

No inverted duty refund is allowed where the output is **nil-rated or exempt**, or for specifically notified goods.

## Interest when the refund is late

Under **Section 56**, if a refund is not paid within **60 days** of the date a complete application was received, interest runs from the day after those 60 days until payment, at **6% a year** (Notification 13/2017-Central Tax). Where the refund flows from an order of an appellate authority, tribunal or court and is not paid within 60 days of the application, the rate is **9%**.

**Example:** a ₹90,000 refund applied for on 1 June. The 60 days end on 31 July. It is paid on 29 October, which is 31 + 30 + 29 = 90 days late. Interest = ₹90,000 × 6% × 90 ÷ 365 = **₹1,331.51**, about ₹1,332.

## Excess cash ledger and wrong head: the small-shop refunds

**Excess cash ledger.** Paid ₹50,000 by challan when GSTR-3B needed ₹30,000? The ₹20,000 sits in your cash ledger. Leave it for next month, move it between heads with **Form PMT-09**, or claim it in RFD-01 under "Excess Balance in Electronic Cash Ledger". There is no two-year limit and no unjust enrichment declaration for this type. Our guide to [GST payment online](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-payment-online) covers challans and the cash ledger.

**Wrong head.** If you charged IGST on a sale within your state (or CGST + SGST on an inter-state one), you cannot simply adjust the two. Pay the correct tax, then claim back the wrong one under **Section 77** of the CGST Act and **Section 19** of the IGST Act, within two years of paying the correct tax. ## How to check your GST refund status

- **Without login:** gst.gov.in > Services > Refunds > **Track Application Status**, enter the ARN.
- **After login:** Services > Refunds > Track Application Status, or **My Applications**, which lists every refund application with its current stage.
- **Bank validation:** the dashboard shows whether PFMS validated your bank account; a failure holds up payment even after sanction.

## Common reasons GST refunds are rejected

- **Time-barred:** filed more than two years after the relevant date.
- **Mismatch between returns:** GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B figures differ, or for IGST-paid exports, invoice numbers or values in the shipping bill do not match GSTR-1.
- **Credit not in GSTR-2B:** the supplier did not report the invoice, so the credit is not supported. See our guide to [input tax credit](https://billzap.app/blog/input-tax-credit-gst).
- **Input services included** in an inverted duty claim.
- **No proof of export payment** for services (BRC or FIRC missing).
- **Wrong refund category** selected, or the period chosen does not match the documents.
- **Bank account not validated** through PFMS.
- **Output exempt or nil-rated**, so the inverted duty route is not available.
- **Unanswered show cause notice.** Reply to RFD-08 in RFD-09 within 15 days and ask for a personal hearing. Our guide to [replying to a GST notice](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-notice-reply) covers how.

If the final order goes against you, you can appeal to the Appellate Authority in **APL-01 within three months** of the order under Section 107.

## FAQ

### How do I claim a GST refund?

File RFD-01 on the GST portal (Services > Refunds > Application for Refund), choose the type and period, upload documents and submit with DSC or EVC. Exports of goods with IGST paid are refunded through the shipping bill.

### What is the time limit for a GST refund?

Two years from the relevant date, which depends on the refund type. There is no limit for excess cash ledger balance.

### How long does a GST refund take?

Acknowledgement within 15 days, 90% provisional refund within 7 days of that for exports and inverted duty, and a final order within 60 days of a complete application. If payment is later, interest at 6% a year applies.

### How do I check my GST refund status?

Go to gst.gov.in > Services > Refunds > Track Application Status and enter your ARN, with or without logging in.

### Can I get a refund of excess balance in my cash ledger?

Yes, through RFD-01 under "Excess Balance in Electronic Cash Ledger", with no two-year limit. You can also keep it or move it between heads with PMT-09.

### Is interest paid on a delayed GST refund?

Yes: 6% a year after 60 days from a complete application, and 9% where the refund arises from an appellate or court order.

## The bottom line

A GST refund is a two-year clock and a chain of forms: RFD-01 from you, RFD-02 or RFD-03 back, 90% in RFD-04 for exports and now inverted duty, and RFD-06 within 60 days. Keep returns and credits matched, validate your bank account first and answer every notice within 15 days. For excess cash and wrong-head refunds, the best fix is paying the right tax on the right bill the first time.
