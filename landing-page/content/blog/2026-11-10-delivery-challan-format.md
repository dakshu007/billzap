Goods do not always leave your shop because you sold them. You send sheets to a fabricator for cutting, a motor to a mechanic for rewinding, ten light fittings to a contractor's site for approval, or stock from your shop to your own godown. There is no sale yet, so there is no invoice. What travels with the goods is a **delivery challan**.

Most pages ranking for "delivery challan format" are template downloads from billing software makers. A template is useful, but it does not tell you whether you are allowed to use a challan at all, what happens when the e-way bill limit is crossed, or what job work adds. This guide covers the law first, then gives a filled sample you can copy.

## Quick answer

- A **delivery challan** is the document for moving goods **without a tax invoice**, under **Rule 55 of the CGST Rules**.
- It can be used only for: **job work**, movement **for reasons other than supply** (repair, approval, exhibition, own godown), **liquid gas** where the quantity is not known at removal, and other cases notified by CBIC. An ordinary sale needs an **invoice**, not a challan.
- **Nine particulars:** challan number (up to 16 characters) and date; consigner's name, address and GSTIN; consignee's name, address and GSTIN; HSN and description; quantity; taxable value; tax rate and amount (only where the movement is for supply); place of supply for inter-state movement; signature.
- **Triplicate:** Original for Consignee, Duplicate for Transporter, Triplicate for Consigner.
- **E-way bill:** still required if the consignment value is above ₹50,000 (or your state's intra-state limit), and for **inter-state job work at any value**.
- **Job work:** inputs must come back within **1 year**, capital goods within **3 years**, and challans are reported in **ITC-04**.

## When GST allows a delivery challan

Rule 55(1) lists the cases. If your movement is not one of them, you need a tax invoice or bill of supply.

| Situation | Challan allowed? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw material sent to a job worker for processing | Yes | Rule 55(1)(b), job work |
| Goods sent for repair or servicing | Yes | Not a supply |
| Goods sent on approval or "sale or return" | Yes | CBIC Circular 10/10/2017-GST: challan at dispatch, invoice when the buyer accepts |
| Stock moved to your own godown or another shop **under the same GSTIN** | Yes | Not a supply |
| Goods sent for an exhibition or trade fair | Yes | Not a supply until sold |
| Liquid gas where the quantity is not known at dispatch | Yes | Rule 55(1)(a) |
| Machine dispatched in parts or lots | Yes, alongside the invoice | Rule 55(5) |
| Transfer to your branch **in another state** (different GSTIN) | **No**, invoice needed | Distinct persons; it is a supply |
| Sale to a customer, with delivery later the same day | **No**, invoice needed | It is a supply |

The common misuse is sending a sale on a challan "for now" and billing at month end. GST requires the invoice on or before removal of the goods for a sale. A challan for a sale is an invoice that was not issued.

## The required fields: Rule 55(1)

There is no prescribed form. Your challan must carry these particulars:

1. **Date and number** of the challan, serially numbered, up to **16 characters**, in one or more series
2. **Name, address and GSTIN of the consigner**, if registered
3. **Name, address and GSTIN or UIN of the consignee**, if registered
4. **HSN code and description** of goods
5. **Quantity** (provisional, where the exact quantity is not known)
6. **Taxable value**
7. **Tax rate and amount** (CGST, SGST, IGST, UTGST or cess), **where the transportation is for supply to the consignee**
8. **Place of supply**, for inter-state movement
9. **Signature**

Point 7 trips people up. For job work, repair or a godown transfer there is no supply, so no tax is shown, but you still show the **value**: the e-way bill needs it, and the officer who stops a truck will ask.

Most businesses also add: vehicle number, transporter name, purpose of movement, expected return date for job work, and a "received by" box. These are not required but help.

## Delivery challan in triplicate

Rule 55(2) requires three copies for the movement of goods:

| Copy | Marked | Who keeps it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original | ORIGINAL FOR CONSIGNEE | Travels with the goods and stays with the receiver |
| Duplicate | DUPLICATE FOR TRANSPORTER | Driver or transporter carries it for checks |
| Triplicate | TRIPLICATE FOR CONSIGNER | Stays in your book |

Get the receiver to sign and stamp the triplicate or return a signed copy. That signature is your proof the goods reached the job worker or customer, and it is what you will need if the goods are not returned on time.

## Delivery challan vs invoice

| | Delivery challan | Tax invoice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Moving goods without a sale | Recording a sale |
| Legal basis | Rule 55 | Section 31, Rule 46 |
| GST charged | No (tax shown only where the movement is for supply) | Yes |
| Buyer can claim ITC from it | No | Yes |
| Reported in GSTR-1 | No (job work challans go in ITC-04) | Yes |
| Copies | Triplicate | Triplicate for goods |
| Converted later | Yes, an invoice is issued if the goods are finally sold | Not applicable |

Many shops also send a **delivery note** with a sale for the customer to sign as proof of delivery. That is fine as a commercial practice, but it is not a Rule 55 challan and it does not replace the invoice. ## Delivery challan and the e-way bill

A challan is not a substitute for an e-way bill. Rule 55(3) says that when goods move on a challan instead of an invoice, they must be declared as Rule 138 requires, which is the e-way bill rule.

- **Value above ₹50,000** for inter-state movement, or above your **state's intra-state limit**: e-way bill needed, even though no sale is happening. The value is the value on the challan.
- **Inter-state job work:** e-way bill needed **at any value**, by the principal or a registered job worker.
- In the e-way bill, choose document type **Delivery Challan** and the right sub-type, such as Job Work, For Own Use, Exhibition or Fairs, Line Sales, or Recipient Not Known.

| Place of supply | Not applicable (intra-state, not a supply) |
| E-way bill | Required only above Maharashtra's intra-state limit; check the current figure |

| Sr | Description | HSN | Qty | Unit | Rate (₹) | Value (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | MS sheet 2 mm | 7208 | 400 | kg | 62 | 24,800 |
| 2 | MS sheet 3 mm | 7208 | 250 | kg | 60 | 15,000 |
| | **Total** | | **650** | **kg** | | **39,800** |

Tax: **not charged**, goods sent for job work under Section 143. Signature of consigner, and a "Received by" box with signature, date and stamp of the job worker.

Check the arithmetic: 400 × ₹62 = ₹24,800; 250 × ₹60 = ₹15,000; total ₹39,800. The value is below ₹50,000, so this intra-state movement does not need an e-way bill in most states. If the same sheets went to a job worker in Gujarat, the e-way bill would be needed even at this value.

For a movement that **is** for supply (such as liquid gas or lot-wise dispatch), add columns for tax rate, CGST and SGST (or IGST) and a total including tax.

## Job work challans and ITC-04

Job work has its own rules under **Section 143** of the CGST Act and **Rule 45**.

- **Send inputs or capital goods on a challan** with the Rule 55 particulars. You keep the input tax credit on them while they are with the job worker.
- **Return time:** inputs within **1 year**, capital goods within **3 years** of being sent. Moulds, dies, jigs, fixtures and tools are outside this time limit.
- **If they do not come back in time,** the law treats them as **supplied** by you to the job worker on the day you sent them, so you must raise an invoice and pay tax, with interest.
- **Goods coming back:** the job worker returns them with their own challan and bills you separately for the job work charges.
- **ITC-04:** the principal reports challans sent and received in Form **GST ITC-04**. Since October 2020, a principal with annual aggregate turnover above **₹5 crore** files it **half-yearly** (by 25 October for April to September and by 25 April for October to March), and one at or below ₹5 crore files it **annually** by 25 April.

| Job work event | Document | Who issues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sheets sent for cutting | Delivery challan | Principal |
| Cut parts returned | Delivery challan | Job worker |
| Job work charges | Tax invoice for the service | Job worker |
| Scrap sold by the job worker | Tax invoice | Job worker if registered, else principal |
| Inputs not returned within 1 year | Tax invoice, deemed supply | Principal |

Keep a simple register of challans sent and received, with balance quantity at each job worker. Our [stock register format](https://billzap.app/blog/stock-register-format) guide has columns you can adapt.

## Goods on approval and lot-wise dispatch

**On approval.** Send the goods on a challan. When the customer decides to keep them, issue the invoice. Section 31(7) says the invoice must be issued when it becomes known the supply has taken place, or six months after removal, whichever is earlier. Goods not returned in six months are treated as sold.

**In lots or parts (Rule 55(5)).** When a machine or an order goes in several vehicles:

1. Issue the **complete invoice before the first lot** leaves.
2. Issue a **delivery challan for each lot**, quoting the invoice number.
3. Send each lot with its challan and a **certified copy of the invoice**.
4. Send the **original invoice with the last lot**.

## Common mistakes

- **Using a challan for a sale** to delay the invoice or the tax.
- **No value on the challan** because "no tax is due". Value is mandatory.
- **One copy only**, with nothing signed coming back.
- **No e-way bill** on inter-state job work below ₹50,000.
- **Branch transfer to another state on a challan** instead of an invoice.
- **Job work goods left with the worker** past one year without raising an invoice.
- **Skipping ITC-04**, which is where an officer first looks.
- **Reusing challan numbers** across years or series.

## FAQ

### What is a delivery challan under GST?

A document issued under Rule 55 of the CGST Rules to move goods without a tax invoice, for job work, for reasons other than supply such as repair or approval, for liquid gas of unknown quantity, or other notified cases.

### Is there a prescribed delivery challan format?

No form, but Rule 55(1) requires nine particulars: number and date, consigner and consignee details with GSTIN, HSN and description, quantity, taxable value, tax where the movement is for supply, place of supply for inter-state movement, and signature.

### Why is a delivery challan made in triplicate?

Rule 55(2) requires three copies: Original for Consignee, Duplicate for Transporter and Triplicate for Consigner, so the receiver, the transporter and you each have a record.

### Is an e-way bill needed with a delivery challan?

Yes, if the value on the challan is above ₹50,000 for inter-state movement or your state's limit for intra-state movement. Inter-state movement for job work needs one at any value.

### Can I sell goods on a delivery challan and bill later?

No. A sale needs a tax invoice at or before removal of goods. A challan is only for the cases in Rule 55, such as goods on approval, where the invoice follows when the sale is confirmed.

### How long can goods stay with a job worker?

Inputs must come back within one year and capital goods within three years. Otherwise they are treated as supplied on the day they were sent, and tax with interest is due.

## The bottom line

A delivery challan is for goods that move without being sold: to a job worker, for repair, on approval, to your own godown. Number it, show the value and HSN, make three copies, get the receiver's signature, and check the e-way bill rule on every movement. For job work, track the one-year and three-year limits and file ITC-04. And the moment a sale actually happens, switch to a tax invoice.
