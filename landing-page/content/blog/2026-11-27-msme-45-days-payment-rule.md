If you supply goods or services to other businesses, you have probably been asked for your Udyam certificate by a buyer's accounts team, or heard a customer say "we have to pay MSMEs in 45 days now." Both come from the same two laws working together: the **MSMED Act, 2006**, which sets the payment deadline, and an **income-tax rule** that takes away the buyer's tax deduction if it misses that deadline.

Most pages still cite only Section 43B(h), a section of the Income-tax Act, 1961, which was replaced from **1 April 2026**. The rule carries on under a new number. This guide gives the current position, dated examples of when a buyer loses the deduction, and what a small supplier can put on its invoices to get paid on time.

## Quick answer

- **The deadline (MSMED Act, Section 15):** a buyer must pay a micro or small supplier within **15 days** of accepting the goods or services, or within the period **agreed in writing**, which can **never exceed 45 days**.
- **The tax rule:** if the buyer pays late, it can deduct the purchase only in the year it **actually pays**. This was **Section 43B(h)** of the 1961 Act (FY 2023-24 to FY 2025-26). From **tax year 2026-27** it is **Section 37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act, 2025**.
- **Who is protected:** **micro and small** enterprises registered on **Udyam** as **manufacturers or service providers**. **Medium enterprises and traders (retail and wholesale) are not covered.**
- **Interest:** a late buyer owes **compound interest with monthly rests at three times the RBI bank rate** (Section 16). With the bank rate at 5.75% since October 2026, that is **17.25% a year**. The interest is not tax-deductible for the buyer.
- **Complaints:** new delayed-payment cases go on the **MSME ODR portal (odr.msme.gov.in)**. MSME Samadhaan stopped taking new cases on 15 October 2025.

## The two laws behind the 45-day rule

People say "the 45-day rule" as if it were one law. It is really two, and each does a different job.

| | MSMED Act, 2006 | Income-tax law |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section | 15 (deadline), 16 (interest), 18 (disputes) | 43B(h) of the 1961 Act; now 37(2)(g) of the 2025 Act |
| What it does | Sets the payment deadline and the penalty interest | Delays the buyer's tax deduction if the deadline is missed |
| Who enforces it | Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC) of your state | Income-tax department, through the buyer's return and tax audit |
| When it bites | From the day after the deadline | At the buyer's year end (31 March), if the dues are still unpaid and late |

The MSMED deadline dates from 2006 but was widely ignored. The tax rule, added by the Finance Act, 2023, gave it teeth by hitting the buyer's own tax bill.

### Section 15: 15 days, or up to 45 days in writing

Section 15 of the MSMED Act works like this:

1. **If there is a written agreement on payment terms**, the buyer pays by the agreed date. The agreed period **cannot exceed 45 days** from the day of acceptance (or deemed acceptance). A purchase order saying "90 days credit" does not stretch the legal limit; for this law it is read as 45.
2. **If there is no written agreement**, the buyer must pay before the "appointed day", which is the day after **15 days** from acceptance.

**Acceptance** matters because the clock starts there. If the buyer does not raise a written objection within 15 days of delivery, the goods or services are treated as accepted (deemed acceptance). If the buyer does object, acceptance is the day the objection is resolved.

### The tax rule: Section 43B(h), now Section 37(2)(g)

Section 43B(h) was inserted by the Finance Act, 2023 and applied from **FY 2023-24**. It said that any sum payable to a micro or small enterprise **beyond the time limit in Section 15** of the MSMED Act is deductible only in the year it is actually paid.

The **Income-tax Act, 2025** came into force on **1 April 2026**. The same rule now sits in **Section 37(2)(g)**, which applies from **tax year 2026-27** (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027). The Ministry of Finance confirmed the correspondence in a Rajya Sabha reply on **21 July 2026**, and said the government is keeping the provision.

One point did not change in the move: most "pay on actual payment" items (such as employer PF dues or bonus) are allowed if paid before the due date of the tax return. **MSME dues do not get that relief.** Paying the supplier in July, before filing the return, does not save the deduction for the year just ended.

## Who the 45-day rule covers, and who it does not

| Supplier | Covered? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Micro manufacturer (for example a small packaging unit), registered on Udyam | **Yes** | Micro enterprise under the MSMED Act |
| Small service provider (for example a printing or repair firm), registered on Udyam | **Yes** | Small enterprise |
| Medium enterprise | **No** | The rule covers only micro and small |
| Retail or wholesale trader with Udyam (kirana, distributor, stockist) | **No** | Traders get only priority sector lending, under the Ministry of MSME memo of 2 July 2021 |
| Manufacturer with no Udyam registration | **In practice, no** | Buyers rely on the Udyam certificate to identify covered suppliers |

The size limits for micro and small enterprises were revised from **1 April 2025**: micro is up to **₹2.5 crore investment and ₹10 crore turnover**, small up to **₹25 crore and ₹100 crore**. Our guide to [Udyam registration](https://billzap.app/blog/udyam-registration) has the full table and the free steps on the official portal.

**On the buyer side**, the tax rule hits any business that claims the purchase as an expense: companies, LLPs, firms and proprietors, whether or not the buyer is itself an MSME. Two kinds of buyer feel little or no tax effect:

- **Buyers on presumptive taxation.** A shop that declares income at a fixed percentage of turnover (Section 44AD under the old Act, **Section 58** of the 2025 Act) does not claim purchases as a separate deduction, so there is nothing to disallow. Our guide to [ITR-4 for business](https://billzap.app/blog/itr-4-for-business) explains that scheme. The MSMED Act deadline and interest still apply to them in law.
- **Capital purchases.** A machine is not claimed as an expense in one go, so the rule mainly bites on raw material, packing and services. Ask your CA about capital items.

## Worked example: when the deduction is lost

**Sharma Foods**, a partnership firm that makes namkeen, buys packaging from **Ravi Packaging**, a micro manufacturer registered on Udyam. They have a written agreement for **45 days** credit. Here are four bills in the tax year 2026-27, which ends on 31 March 2027.

| Bill | Amount | Accepted on | Deadline | Paid on | Deduction in 2026-27? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | ₹2,00,000 | 10 Feb 2027 | 27 Mar 2027 | 25 Mar 2027 | **Yes**, paid on time |
| B | ₹3,00,000 | 1 Mar 2027 | 15 Apr 2027 | 10 Apr 2027 | **Yes**, paid after 31 March but within 45 days |
| C | ₹5,00,000 | 5 Feb 2027 | 22 Mar 2027 | 20 May 2027 | **No**, late and unpaid at year end |
| D | ₹1,00,000 | 1 Dec 2026 | 15 Jan 2027 | 31 Jan 2027 | **Yes**, late but paid within the same year |

- **Bill B:** money owed on 31 March is not automatically disallowed. Paid within the agreed period, it stays deductible in 2026-27.
- **Bill C:** ₹5,00,000 is added back to Sharma Foods' income for 2026-27 and deducted in 2027-28, when it is paid.
- **Bill D:** late, but paid before the year closed, so the deduction is unaffected. The MSMED **interest** is still owed.

**The tax cost of Bill C.** A partnership firm pays 30% tax plus 4% cess, which is 31.2% (ignoring surcharge, which starts above ₹1 crore of income). Adding back ₹5,00,000 means **₹1,56,000 more tax** in 2026-27. The firm gets the deduction next year, so it is a timing cost, but it is cash out of the business for a year.

**The interest cost of Bill C.** Section 16 interest runs from the day after the deadline, 23 March, to payment on 20 May: 59 days. At three times a bank rate of 5.75%, which is 17.25% a year compounded monthly, the interest on ₹5,00,000 comes to about **₹14,000**. Under Section 23 of the MSMED Act, that interest can never be deducted for income tax.

A two-month delay cost the buyer ₹1.56 lakh of tax paid a year early, plus about ₹14,000 of interest if the supplier claims it. That is the supplier's leverage.

## Interest at three times the bank rate

Section 16 of the MSMED Act says a buyer who pays late must pay **compound interest with monthly rests**, from the day after the deadline, at **three times the bank rate notified by the RBI**. Any agreement to the contrary does not override it.

The bank rate moves with RBI policy. After the Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate to 5.50% in October 2026, the bank rate stands at **5.75%**, so the MSMED rate is **17.25% a year**. Check the current bank rate on rbi.org.in when you calculate a claim.

| Overdue amount | Days late | Approximate interest at 17.25% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ₹50,000 | 30 | ₹710 |
| ₹1,00,000 | 60 | ₹2,860 |
| ₹5,00,000 | 59 | ₹14,000 |

(Figures are rounded and use monthly compounding.)

Most micro suppliers never claim this interest for fear of losing the customer. Mentioning it politely on your invoice is often enough to move your bill up the buyer's payment queue.

## If a buyer still does not pay: the ODR portal

Under Section 18 of the MSMED Act, a micro or small supplier can refer a delayed payment dispute to the **Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC)** of its state. The council first tries conciliation and, if that fails, arbitration. The Act asks it to decide within **90 days** of the reference. A buyer who wants to challenge the award in court must first deposit **75%** of the amount awarded (Section 19).

How you file has changed. **MSME Samadhaan**, launched in October 2017, was the portal for years. The **MSME ODR portal** (**odr.msme.gov.in**) went live on **27 June 2025**, offering online negotiation first and then passing unsettled cases to the MSEFC. From **15 October 2025**, **all new references** must be filed there; Samadhaan only keeps older cases running.

You need your **Udyam Registration Number**, plus invoices, the purchase order or agreement, delivery proof and the reminders you sent.

## What a small supplier should put on every invoice

You need to be able to show three things: that you are a covered micro or small enterprise, when the buyer accepted the supply, and the agreed payment period.

1. **Your Udyam Registration Number** (UDYAM-XX-00-0000000) and your category, for example "Micro enterprise, manufacturing". Send a copy of the certificate to each buyer's accounts team once, so they flag you in their system.
2. **A clear due date.** Agree the credit period **in writing** (a purchase order, email or contract the buyer accepts) and keep it at 45 days or less. Print the due date on the bill, not just "credit 30 days".
3. **The delivery date and proof of delivery.** Acceptance starts the clock, so keep signed delivery copies or delivery emails.
4. **A one-line note on late payment**, such as: "Payment due within 30 days of acceptance as agreed. Delayed payment attracts interest under Section 16 of the MSMED Act, 2006."
5. **Correct GST details** if you are registered. A buyer can delay a bill that has a wrong GSTIN or HSN code by raising an objection. Our guide to the [GST invoice format](https://billzap.app/blog/gst-invoice-format) lists every field a tax invoice needs.

Then follow up. A polite reminder a week before the due date and another a day after it does more than any notice. Our guide to [udhaar management](https://billzap.app/blog/udhaar-management) has a simple routine for chasing dues without souring the relationship.

### If you are a trader

Many shop owners register on Udyam expecting the 45-day protection, but neither the MSMED deadline nor the tax rule covers payments to traders. ## FAQ

### What is the MSME 45 days payment rule?

Under Section 15 of the MSMED Act, a buyer must pay a micro or small supplier within the period agreed in writing, which cannot exceed 45 days from acceptance, or within 15 days if nothing is agreed. If the buyer misses it, the income-tax law allows the expense only in the year of actual payment.

### What is the new section for 43B(h) under the Income-tax Act 2025?

Section 37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act, 2025. It applies from tax year 2026-27, which began on 1 April 2026. Section 43B(h) of the 1961 Act covered FY 2023-24 to FY 2025-26.

### Does the 45-day rule apply to traders?

No. Retail and wholesale traders can register on Udyam, but only for priority sector lending. Payments to traders, and to medium enterprises, are outside the MSMED deadline and the tax rule.

### Is the deduction lost if the bill is unpaid on 31 March?

Only if it is also late. If the agreed period (up to 45 days) has not run out and the buyer pays within it, the deduction stays in that year. If it is already overdue and unpaid on 31 March, it moves to the year of payment, and paying before the return due date does not help.

### What interest does a buyer owe for late payment?

Compound interest with monthly rests at three times the RBI bank rate, from the day after the deadline. At the current bank rate of 5.75%, that is 17.25% a year. The buyer cannot claim it as a tax deduction.

### Where do I complain about a delayed MSME payment?

On the MSME ODR portal, odr.msme.gov.in, using your Udyam number. Since 15 October 2025, new cases cannot be filed on MSME Samadhaan.

## The bottom line

The **MSMED Act** sets the deadline (15 days, or up to 45 in writing) and the interest. The **income-tax rule**, now **Section 37(2)(g)** of the 2025 Act, moves a late buyer's deduction to the year of payment. It protects **micro and small manufacturers and service providers on Udyam**, not traders or medium firms. If you are covered, put your Udyam number, a written credit period and a due date on every bill, and chase before the deadline, not after.
